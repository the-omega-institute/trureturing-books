  1. Tangent magnification. The reading of the state P against the contact point o is κ, with the deficit s = 1 − κ = sin²(θ/2) in [0, 1]. The line from the antipode o through P lands on the real axis of the tangent plane at x = tan(θ/2) = √(s/(1 − s)). A short arc of the sphere is magnified Aerr = 2 dx/dθ = 1 + x² = 1/κ times: —. It diverges as s → 1; on the sphere the way stays finite, the chord 2√s never exceeds 2.
  2. Flat addition and multiplication. Two turns about one axis add their angles, and the tangent coordinates compose as (x₁ + x₂)/(1 − x₁x₂); at x₁ = x₂ = 1 the denominator is 0 and the sum is exactly o, while the flat 1 + 1 = 2 stops at —. Off one axis the rotors multiply to (x₁ + x₂ + x₁ × x₂)/(1 − x₁·x₂), with coefficient 1 on the cross product; the flat sum drops x₁ × x₂, and at x = 1 the axis is off by —. Repeated n times, the curved side winds round the great circle, ⟨x₀, xₙ⟩ = Tₙ(cos θ); the flat n·x runs to infinity along the line.
  3. The primes take it back. The radius of the disc is logarithmic: the magenta arrow reaches ln N and the ring of the prime p lies at ln p; every prime factor of N hops back by ln p, and the last hop ends on the unit ball, |N|∞ ∏p |N|p = 1. The bead of ring p stands at N mod p, on the arrow only when p divides N; a prime N is invisible to every older ring and is taken back by its own new ring alone. The product of the primes below N plus one always has a prime divisor outside the list. At N = 1 there is neither arrow nor ring.
  4. Rivets and phase rings. A cage with three edges at every vertex and only pentagons and hexagons needs exactly 12 pentagons by V − E + F = 2, however many hexagons there are; hexagons alone never close into a sphere. Three orthogonal rings carry one prime phase τ ln p each. Reading the pentagons as rivets that answer to primes is an analogy (MODEL). The mean density of the primes is 1/ln x in x and eℓ/ℓ in ℓ = ln x (the prime number theorem).
  5. No return. The orbit with frequencies 1 : 2 : 3 closes after every 2π and runs along one line for ever. The orbit with frequencies ln 2 : ln 3 : ln 5 never comes back to a state it has been in, because the logarithms of the primes are linearly independent over ℚ; it is dense in the three-dimensional phase torus (derivations ④ and ⑤ of the evidence panel). This phase torus is compact and has no boundary; it is not the same object as the closed geodesics of SL₂(ℤ)\ℍ² in the trace formula of Selberg.

理论接口 · 有限和、合成四根与有限零点样本Theory interface · finite sums, a synthetic quartet and a finite line-zero sample

这是数值演示，不是证明。页面只做有限计算：有限个项、有限个素数、十个零点；它既不计算解析延拓，也不给任何误差证书。

  1. 两种有限近似。SN(s) = Σn≤N n−s 是有限部分和，EN(s) = ∏p≤N (1 − p−s)−1 是有限欧拉积；舞台把每一步 n−s = n−σe−it log n 首尾相接，沿 log n 轴抬起，屏上是它在复平面上的投影。σ > 1 时两者都收敛到 ζ(s)（DLMF §25.2；本书 §22.2 以冻结的 Lean 声明引用这两个恒等式），但有限 N 下两者一般不相等：页面只列出它们的差，不称其中任何一个为 ζ(s)，也不对每个 N 作普遍断言。σ ≤ 1 时不画欧拉积。
  2. 有限和不是解析延拓。σ ≤ 1 时狄利克雷级数不收敛，ζ(s) 由解析延拓定义；页面不计算解析延拓。σ = ½、t 取第一个零点纵坐标时，SN 并不接近 0，随 N 增加还在转。
  3. 莫比乌斯函数。μ 由狄利克雷卷积的逆算出：μ(1) = 1，n > 1 时 μ(n) = −Σd|n, d<n μ(d)，于是 Σd|n μ(d) = [n = 1]（狄利克雷卷积与反演见 DLMF §27.5）。Σ μ(n) n−s 只在 σ > 1 绝对收敛到 1/ζ(s)（DLMF 27.4.E5）；页面列出有限乘积 SN·MN 与 1 的距离，σ ≤ 1 时不作任何断言。
  4. 合成四根。Q(s) = (s − β − iγ)(s − β + iγ)(s − 1 + β − iγ)(s − 1 + β + iγ)，γ = 0.7。它满足 Q(1 − s) = Q(s) 与 Q(s̄) = Q(s) 的共轭，但 β ≠ ½ 时根离开直线 σ = ½，距离 |β − ½|。这只说明“这两种对称本身推不出根在直线上”；它是模型，不涉及 ζ 的零点在哪里。
  5. 显式公式与有限截断。对 x > 1，ψ₀(x) = x − limT→∞ Σ|Im ρ|<T xρ/ρ − log 2π − ½ log(1 − x−2)，求和遍历全部非平凡零点 ρ（不论是否在直线上，按重数计），按对称的高度截断取极限；ψ₀ 在跳点取左右极限的平均。这一完整的逐点公式见 H. Davenport, Multiplicative Number Theory, 3rd ed., Ch. 17 与本书 §22.4.1；DLMF §25.16 只给出 ψ 的定义与渐近式。页面只取前十个已知的正纵坐标 γk（数值见 DLMF §25.10），把共轭成对写成 2 Re(x½+iγ/(½ + iγ))：这是有限线上零点样本，没有余项界。
  6. 阶梯。ψ₀(x) = Σn<x Λ(n)，x 恰为素数幂时再加 ½Λ(x)。Λ(n) = log p 当 n = pk，否则为 0：阶梯在 4、8、9、16 这些素数幂处也跳，不只在素数处跳。
  7. 有限截断 N。N 是级数最自然的截断，不是 Dijkstra 的最短路算法，也不是物理的芝诺效应；章中“几何划分 / 莫比乌斯外部”的说法是诠释，页面实际做的是有限的逆狄利克雷卷积。

This is a numerical illustration, not a proof. The page does finite computations only: finitely many terms, finitely many primes, ten zeros; it neither computes the analytic continuation nor gives any error certificate.

  1. Two finite approximants. SN(s) = Σn≤N n−s is a finite partial sum and EN(s) = ∏p≤N (1 − p−s)−1 a finite Euler product; the stage puts the steps n−s = n−σe−it log n head to tail and lifts them along the log n axis, and the screen shows their projection on the complex plane. For σ > 1 both converge to ζ(s) (DLMF §25.2; the book cites these two identities with frozen Lean declarations in §22.2), but at finite N they are in general not equal: the page lists their difference, calls neither of them ζ(s) and makes no universal claim about every N. For σ ≤ 1 no Euler product is drawn.
  2. A finite sum is not the analytic continuation. For σ ≤ 1 the Dirichlet series does not converge and ζ(s) is defined by analytic continuation, which the page does not compute. At σ = ½ with t at the first zero ordinate, SN is not near 0 and keeps turning as N grows.
  3. The Möbius function. μ is computed as the inverse under Dirichlet convolution: μ(1) = 1 and, for n > 1, μ(n) = −Σd|n, d<n μ(d), so that Σd|n μ(d) = [n = 1] (Dirichlet convolution and inversion: DLMF §27.5). Σ μ(n) n−s converges absolutely to 1/ζ(s) only for σ > 1 (DLMF 27.4.E5); the page lists the distance of the finite product SN·MN from 1 and asserts nothing for σ ≤ 1.
  4. The synthetic quartet. Q(s) = (s − β − iγ)(s − β + iγ)(s − 1 + β − iγ)(s − 1 + β + iγ), γ = 0.7. It satisfies Q(1 − s) = Q(s) and Q(s̄) = the conjugate of Q(s), yet for β ≠ ½ its roots sit off the line σ = ½, at distance |β − ½|. This only shows that these two symmetries by themselves do not put roots on the line; it is a model and says nothing about where the zeros of ζ are.
  5. The explicit formula and its truncation. For x > 1, ψ₀(x) = x − limT→∞ Σ|Im ρ|<T xρ/ρ − log 2π − ½ log(1 − x−2), the sum running over all nontrivial zeros ρ (on the line or not, counted with multiplicity) with the limit taken over symmetric heights; ψ₀ takes the mean of its left and right limits at a jump. This full pointwise formula is in H. Davenport, Multiplicative Number Theory, 3rd ed., Ch. 17, and in the book at §22.4.1; DLMF §25.16 gives only the definition and the asymptotics of ψ. The page takes only the first ten known positive ordinates γk (numerical values: DLMF §25.10) and writes each conjugate pair as 2 Re(x½+iγ/(½ + iγ)): a finite line-zero sample with no remainder bound.
  6. The stair. ψ₀(x) = Σn<x Λ(n), plus ½Λ(x) when x is itself a prime power. Λ(n) = log p when n = pk and 0 otherwise: the stair also steps at prime powers such as 4, 8, 9 and 16, not only at primes.
  7. The finite cutoff N. N is the most natural truncation of the series, neither Dijkstra's shortest-path algorithm nor a physical Zeno effect; the chapter's “geometric partition / Möbius exterior” wording is an interpretation, and what the page actually does is a finite inverse Dirichlet convolution.

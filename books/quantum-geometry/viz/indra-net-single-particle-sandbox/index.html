  1. Identical jewels. Thirteen jewels sit on an icosahedral net: one at the centre and twelve outside, all of one radius, lit by one lamp, each fibre the same finite-dimensional space Hx ≅ Hy ≅ Hfield. Click a jewel and it puts on the amber ring as the chief x₀; the others become its retinue. When chief and retinue swap, not one jewel of the net moves.
  2. The spherical mirror. The mirror of the chief shows the other twelve: an image sits where the normal bisects the line of sight and the direction between the jewels, with clarity cos²θ = (1 + D·d̂)/2. Images facing you are clear (cyan, → 1), images against the limb are squashed (violet, → 0); turn the view and the images slide across the mirror.
  3. Reflections of reflections. Light only the chief, Ψ(0) = δx₀, and reflect again and again: Ψ(m) = cos(KεΨ(m−1)), where Kε mixes the value of each jewel with the mean of its neighbours by ε. For values in [0, 1] this is a nonlinear contraction (Lipschitz constant sin 1), every start converges to the field equal to θ* = 0.7390851332 everywhere, and in the end the ratio of successive errors tends to sin θ* = 0.673612. Whoever is chief, the limit is the same.
  4. What the chief can read. What is read here is the linear mixing observation word, not the nonlinear reflection of the previous item: the j-th letter of x₀ is (KεjΨ)(x₀) = ⟨ej, Ψ⟩ with the effect ej = (KεT)jδx₀ (j = 0 … m). These effects span only some of the directions: at ε = 0.5 an outer chief sees at most 4 dimensions and leaves a residual of at least 9, the central one at most 2 and at least 11; the residual is what it cannot see. One jewel mirroring all is not one jewel holding the whole.
  5. The Wheeler ribbon. One closed ribbon visits the twelve outer jewels. Read the vertical as time: the slice of now, Σnow, meets it in a number of knots, +1 for a crossing upwards and −1 downwards, alternating, Σq = 0. The rail pushes the slice upwards, and the knots are born in pairs and annihilate in pairs. This closed cycle is exactly the boundary of ten faces.
  6. The cosmic zoom. As ζ runs from 0 to 1 the scale runs from the Planck length to the radius of the observable universe, Robs/ℓP ≈ 2.72 × 10⁶¹: the whole net shrinks into one outer jewel.

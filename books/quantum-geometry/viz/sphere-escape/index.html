一张清单 f 列出 N 行二进制串 f(r, ·)。沿对角线读出 f(r, r) 并逐位翻转，得到 d(r) = ¬f(r, r)：d 在第 r 位与第 r 行不同，所以无论清单怎样排，d 都不在清单里。余弦迭代 xₖ₊₁ = cos xₖ 是同一种“把自己的输出再喂给自己”的操作，它收向不动点 x* = cos x*，但任何有限步都留下非零残差；欧几里得把前 k 个素数相乘再加 1，同样逃出任何有限的素数清单。本页是数值演示，不是证明。

  1. 对角账本。第 k 步读第 k 行的对角元 f(k, k)：为 0 时用规则 D₀ 写 d(k) = 1，符号带记 +1；为 1 时用规则 D₁ 写 d(k) = 0，记 −1。符号带总和等于 |d| − tr f = N − 2·tr f，只用来记账；要紧的是每一步都留下一个见证：第 k 行在第 k 位与 d 不同。账本末行 d 随步数逐位显出。
  2. 为什么 d 不在清单里（LEAN ✓）。把行号当地址、列号当坐标，取 latent = id、twist = ¬，d 就是相对语义对角线 g。relative_semantic_diagonal_ne_listed_decode 断言 g 与目录中每一个解码 decoderCatalog(a) ∘ latent 都不同，这正是 d ≠ f(r, ·)。页面另外算出 d 与每一行的汉明距离，最小值总是至少为 1。
  3. 康托尔与拓扑逃逸。若 f 是从 {1, …, N}（或任意集合 A）到全体二进制串的满射，d 就必须等于某一行，与上一条矛盾；所以 2ᴺ 个串永远排不进 N 行（这一步推理写在这里，没有单独冻结）。第二十一章的 complete_diagonal_not_continuous 与 complete_diagonal_topological_settlement（LEAN ✓）在“目录满射”的假设下推出更强的拓扑结论：g 在旧的划分拓扑下不连续，并入 g 后观测拓扑严格加细。坐标集就是地址集本身（本页的情形）时，这个假设不可能成立，这正是康托尔的结论；只有潜坐标把地址粗分时目录才可能完备，定理的拓扑内容才显现（见第二十一章的四地址一比特手算例）。
  4. 对角矩阵。右栏画出 N × N 的 f（青色为 1，暗色为 0），底行是 d（品红）。点击任一格翻转 f(r, c) 并跳到第 r 步：改非对角格不影响 d，改对角格会同时翻转 d(r)；无论怎样改，d 都留在清单之外。转置 fᵀ 不改变对角线，所以 d 不变，但它要躲开的是一批新的行。
  5. Dottie 余弦迭代。x₀ 取 0 到 3.14 rad，第 k 步给出 xₖ = cos xₖ₋₁。在 x* ≈ 0.7390851332 处 cos x* = x*，且 |cos′(x*)| = sin x* ≈ 0.6736 < 1，所以迭代在 x* 附近收缩；cos 在 [−1, 1] 上以 sin 1 < 1 为压缩率（巴拿赫，文献结果）。账本末行给出 |cos x_N − x_N|，有限步的残差严格为正。页面在每个 xₖ 上以双精度核对 sin²xₖ + cos²xₖ = 1。
  6. 素数逃逸。第 k 步列出前 k 个素数 p₁, …, pₖ，算出 Q = p₁⋯pₖ + 1 并找出它的最小素因子 q；q 不在清单里（欧几里得，文献结果）。页面同时计算有限欧拉积 ∏ (1 − p⁻²)⁻¹，它对每个有限 k 都严格小于 ζ(2) = π²/6（巴塞尔恒等式，文献结果）；无穷乘积在 Re s > 1 上等于 ζ(s)，见第二十二章的欧拉乘积定理。
  7. 两种自指不动点。第二十四章把余弦不动点与两条已冻结的定理并列：golden_fixed_point_unique（LEAN ✓）说 φ 是 1 + 1/x = x 在正实数上的唯一解（代数的自指）；finite_contracting_updates_stabilize（LEAN ✓）说有限读数网格上的收缩更新至多 |S₀| 步就稳定。x* 是超越数（林德曼—魏尔斯特拉斯，文献结果），任何有限精度的读数都停在 x* 之外。本页不计算这两条定理的实例，只把它们列为依据。
  8. 模型边界。矩阵、对角线、汉明距离、素数与 Q 都是精确整数运算（Q 至多 9699691）；余弦迭代与欧拉积用双精度浮点。对角模式里“圆外”的画法是示意。x* 与 π 是否代数无关是开放问题。

A list f holds N binary rows f(r, ·). Reading the diagonal f(r, r) and flipping every entry gives d(r) = ¬f(r, r): d differs from row r in place r, so however the list is arranged, d is not on it. The cosine iteration xₖ₊₁ = cos xₖ is the same move of feeding an output back into itself; it approaches the fixed point x* = cos x*, yet every finite step leaves a nonzero residual. Euclid multiplies the first k primes and adds 1, and so escapes every finite list of primes. The page is a numerical illustration, not a proof.

  1. The diagonal ledger. Step k reads the diagonal entry f(k, k) of row k: a 0 fires rule D₀ and writes d(k) = 1, counted +1 on the sign tape; a 1 fires rule D₁ and writes d(k) = 0, counted −1. The tape sums to |d| − tr f = N − 2·tr f and is bookkeeping only; what matters is that every step leaves a witness: row k differs from d in place k. The last ledger row reveals d one entry per step.
  2. Why d is not on the list (LEAN ✓). Take the row numbers as addresses and the column numbers as coordinates, with latent = id and twist = ¬; then d is the relative semantic diagonal g. relative_semantic_diagonal_ne_listed_decode states that g differs from every listed decoder decoderCatalog(a) ∘ latent, which is exactly d ≠ f(r, ·). The page also computes the Hamming distance from d to every row; its minimum is always at least 1.
  3. Cantor and the topological escape. If f mapped {1, …, N} (or any set A) onto all binary strings, d would have to equal some row, contradicting the previous item; so 2ᴺ strings never fit into N rows (this step is argued here and not frozen on its own). In Chapter 21, complete_diagonal_not_continuous and complete_diagonal_topological_settlement (LEAN ✓) assume a surjective catalog and conclude more: g is discontinuous in the old partition topology, and adjoining g strictly refines the observation topology. When the coordinates are the addresses themselves, as on this page, that hypothesis cannot hold, which is Cantor's conclusion; only when the latent coordinate coarsens the addresses can the catalog be complete and the topological content appear (see the four-address, one-bit hand calculation in Chapter 21).
  4. The diagonal matrix. The right column draws the N × N list f (cyan for 1, dark for 0) with d in the bottom row (magenta). Clicking a cell flips f(r, c) and moves to step r: an off-diagonal flip leaves d alone, a diagonal flip flips d(r) with it; whatever you change, d stays off the list. The transpose fᵀ keeps the diagonal, so d is unchanged, but the rows it must avoid are new.
  5. The Dottie cosine iteration. x₀ runs from 0 to 3.14 rad and step k gives xₖ = cos xₖ₋₁. At x* ≈ 0.7390851332, cos x* = x* and |cos′(x*)| = sin x* ≈ 0.6736 < 1, so the iteration contracts near x*; on [−1, 1], cos contracts with rate sin 1 < 1 (Banach, a literature result). The last ledger row shows |cos x_N − x_N|: the residual after finitely many steps is strictly positive. The page checks sin²xₖ + cos²xₖ = 1 in double precision at every xₖ.
  6. The prime escape. Step k lists the first k primes p₁, …, pₖ, forms Q = p₁⋯pₖ + 1 and finds its least prime factor q, which is not on the list (Euclid, a literature result). The page also computes the finite Euler product ∏ (1 − p⁻²)⁻¹, which stays strictly below ζ(2) = π²/6 for every finite k (the Basel identity, a literature result); the infinite product equals ζ(s) for Re s > 1, see the Euler product theorem of Chapter 22.
  7. Two kinds of self-referential fixed point. Chapter 24 sets the cosine fixed point beside two frozen theorems: golden_fixed_point_unique (LEAN ✓) says φ is the only positive solution of 1 + 1/x = x (algebraic self-reference); finite_contracting_updates_stabilize (LEAN ✓) says contracting updates on a finite grid of readings stabilize within |S₀| steps. x* is transcendental (Lindemann–Weierstrass, a literature result), so every finite-precision reading stops short of it. The page computes no instances of these two theorems; it lists them as evidence only.
  8. Model limits. The matrix, the diagonal, the Hamming distances, the primes and Q are exact integer arithmetic (Q is at most 9699691); the cosine iteration and the Euler products are double precision. Drawing d outside a circle in the diagonal mode is schematic. Whether x* and π are algebraically independent is an open problem.

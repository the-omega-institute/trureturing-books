理论接口 · 整除图上的标量舒尔消元Theory interface · scalar Schur elimination on the divisor graph

这是数值演示，不是证明。页面在一个有限的整除图上逐个标量地做消元，每个数都由页面算出（NUM）。

  1. 图与矩阵。顶点 2…N（N ∈ [6, 24]），d 与 n 互不相等且 d | n 时连一条边。W 是邻接矩阵，L = diag(deg) − W，G = L + αI，α = 0.25，于是 G 正定。顶点 n 放在 (v₂(n), v₃(n), log(n/2v₂3v₃))：三个坐标一起唯一确定 n，没有两个顶点重合；坐标各自居中缩放。
  2. 终端与消元。终端是 4n > 3N 的顶点，两种次序都保留它们，只消去其余顶点。消去主元 p 时，对它的每一对邻居 i、j 做 Gij ← Gij − GipGpj/Gpp；原来为零的位置变成非零就是一条填充边。自然次序按标号从小到大；叶优先每步取当前度数最小的顶点，平局取标号大的。N = 6、终端 {5, 6} 时，自然次序先消 2，它的邻居 4 与 6 之间长出填充；叶优先先消 4（与 3 同为叶，取标号大的），再消 3、2，没有任何填充。页面数的运算只是这些舒尔更新：每个邻居一次除法，每对邻居一次乘法与一次减法；建 G、约化 b、终端求解、回代、残差、缓存与绘制都不计入。
  3. 舒尔补与次序无关。终端上的舒尔补 S = GTT − GTEGEE−1GET 是代数恒等式，与消元次序无关；两种次序得到同一个 S（差在舍入量级）。填充、非零元个数与运算次数却随次序而变；这只是这个例子里看到的，不是复杂度定理。
  4. 求解与残差。b = eN（最大标号）。b 沿消元同样约化，解 S xT = bT，再按相反次序回代，最后在原来的 G 上算 ‖Gx − b‖：精确消元时它在舍入量级。
  5. 丢弃。把最终 S 中 |Sij| < ε 的非对角元对称地置零，对角元不动，记下丢掉部分的弗罗贝尼乌斯范数，用它求解并回代，再算原方程的残差。DROP 卡取 N = 16、终端 13…16：最终 S 的三个耦合约为 −0.125、−0.417、−0.285，ε = 0.2 只丢掉 14 与 15 之间的那一个，丢掉的范数与原方程残差都明显不为零。N = 12（终端 10、11、12）时唯一的耦合约为 −0.542，ε ≤ 0.5 一个也丢不掉。这是近似：页面不证明丢弃后的矩阵半正定，也不给误差界。
  6. 反复平方。P = I − τL，τ = 1/(1 + 最大度数)：P 非负、行和为 1。做 d 次稠密乘法得到 P2d：所示算法的这 d 次稠密乘积共 d·n³ 次乘法与 d·n²(n − 1) 次加法；n² 是一个输出矩阵的元数，不是浏览器、缓存或工作区的总存储。页面实际一次算好并缓存 d = 0…6 的全部层，所以这些计数不是页面的总工作量，也不构成任何运行时间复杂度的断言。非负且行随机的矩阵谱半径 ≤ 1，这是标准的界，页面只核对行和与非负性，不是数值证明。平方可以让权重摊开、收缩，不会放大，所以不保证任何“共振爆发”。
  7. 文献的边界。Burnol（arXiv:math/0103058v2）定理 1.3 的对象是 Dλ，即 χ = 1(0,1) 到由 x ↦ {θ/x}（λ ≤ θ ≤ 1）张成的连续伸缩空间 Bλ 的距离，结论是 λ → 0+ 时关于 Dλ 的 liminf 下界；它不是本页这类有限整数格拉姆问题，也不是任何有限 N 的等式。arXiv:1207.6583v3 中的 4/7 只在该文所述范围内成立，不是物理的墙。页面不承诺热带（min-plus）计算百分之百精确，也不承诺 MERA 的多项式代价；与黎曼猜想的联系只是有条件的类比。

This is a numerical illustration, not a proof. The page eliminates scalar by scalar on a finite divisor graph, and every number is computed on the page (NUM).

  1. Graph and matrix. Vertices 2…N (N ∈ [6, 24]), with an edge between unequal d and n when d | n. W is the adjacency matrix, L = diag(deg) − W and G = L + αI with α = 0.25, so G is positive definite. Vertex n sits at (v₂(n), v₃(n), log(n/2v₂3v₃)): the three coordinates together determine n, so no two vertices coincide; each coordinate is centred and scaled on its own.
  2. Terminals and elimination. The terminals are the vertices with 4n > 3N; both orders keep them and eliminate only the others. Eliminating the pivot p updates every pair of its neighbours i, j by Gij ← Gij − GipGpj/Gpp; a position that was zero and becomes nonzero is a fill edge. The natural order goes up by label; leaf-first takes at each step a vertex of smallest current degree, ties going to the larger label. For N = 6 with terminals {5, 6}, the natural order eliminates 2 first and a fill grows between its neighbours 4 and 6; leaf-first eliminates 4 first (a leaf like 3, the larger label), then 3 and 2, with no fill at all. The operations the page counts are these Schur updates only: one division per neighbour, one multiplication and one subtraction per pair of neighbours; building G, reducing b, the terminal solve, back substitution, the residual, caches and painting are not counted.
  3. The Schur complement does not depend on the order. The Schur complement on the terminals, S = GTT − GTEGEE−1GET, is an algebraic identity, independent of the order of elimination: both orders give the same S (up to rounding). The fill, the number of nonzeros and the operation counts do depend on the order; that is what this example shows, not a complexity theorem.
  4. Solve and residual. b = eN (the largest label). b is reduced along the same elimination, S xT = bT is solved, the eliminated unknowns are back-substituted in reverse order, and ‖Gx − b‖ is computed with the original G: with exact elimination it is at rounding level.
  5. Dropping. The off-diagonal entries of the final S with |Sij| < ε are set to zero symmetrically, the diagonal is kept, the Frobenius norm of what was dropped is recorded, and the system is solved and back-substituted with it before the residual of the original system is computed. The DROP card takes N = 16 with terminals 13…16: the final S has three couplings, about −0.125, −0.417 and −0.285, and ε = 0.2 drops only the one between 14 and 15, so the dropped norm and the residual of the original system are both visibly nonzero. At N = 12 (terminals 10, 11, 12) the only coupling is about −0.542, and no ε ≤ 0.5 drops anything. This is an approximation: the page does not prove the dropped matrix positive semidefinite and gives no error bound.
  6. Repeated squaring. P = I − τL with τ = 1/(1 + the largest degree): P is nonnegative with row sums 1. d dense products give P2d: these d dense products of the displayed algorithm take d·n³ scalar multiplications and d·n²(n − 1) additions; n² is the number of entries of one output matrix, not the total storage of the browser, the caches or the working memory. The page actually computes and caches all levels d = 0…6 at once, so these counts are not the total work of the page and make no running-time complexity claim. A nonnegative row-stochastic matrix has spectral radius ≤ 1; this is the standard bound, and the page only checks the row sums and the signs, which is not a numerical proof. Squaring may spread and contract the weights and does not amplify them, so no “resonance explosion” is guaranteed.
  7. The scope of the literature. Theorem 1.3 of Burnol (arXiv:math/0103058v2) concerns Dλ, the distance from χ = 1(0,1) to the continuous-dilation span Bλ of x ↦ {θ/x}, λ ≤ θ ≤ 1, and proves a lower bound for a liminf involving Dλ as λ → 0+; it is not a finite integer Gram problem like this page's, nor an equality at any finite N. The 4/7 of arXiv:1207.6583v3 holds within the scope stated there and is not a physical wall. The page promises neither 100% exact tropical (min-plus) computation nor a polynomial cost for MERA; its link to the Riemann hypothesis is a conditional analogy only.

终章把“观察者”对照到 trureturing 里已经验证的黄金切投：高维的周期格点不动，一条窗口决定哪些点被看见。本页把这套构造画出来，并画出与它相伴的 ±1 接缝和回归定理。本页是数值演示，不是证明；灵魂与遗迹的说法是本书的诠释模型。

  1. 切投。黄金整数 a + bφ 有一个共轭 a + bψ，ψ = 1 − φ。把它画成平面上的点（横坐标是它本身，纵坐标是共轭），全体黄金整数是一张格点。水平的窗口 [φ − 2, φ − 1] + u 长度为 1，选出共轭落在其中的点，投到横轴上：相邻两点的间隔只有 φ 与 φ² 两种（u = 0 时两个端点都被取到，多出一个长为 1 的间隔），最小间隔不小于 1。上下挪动窗口，贴着边的点进进出出（相子翻转），离边远的点留在原地：一个点到窗口边界的距离就是它经得起的平移量。固定中间一小片之后，容许的平移只剩一小段，凡是在这一小段平移下始终留在窗口里的点（包括很远的点）都被这一小片定住（一维里的数值演示；高维的康威“帝国”是文献结果）。绿圈 β(n) 是把 n 的齐肯多夫码（贪心地取不超过余数的最大斐波那契数，码里没有相邻的两个 1）按 φ 的幂读出的黄金整数；下方条带逐个列出 n ≤ 54 的码。
  2. 卡西尼。黄金整数 φᵏ⁺¹ = Fₖ + Fₖ₊₁φ 在格点图里位于 (φᵏ⁺¹, ψᵏ⁺¹)。它与原点围成的矩形，面积是这两个坐标的乘积，也就是它的范数 Fₖ·Fₖ₊₂ − Fₖ₊₁² = (−1)ᵏ⁺¹：永远恰好是 1，一次在横轴上方，一次在下方。k 越大，矩形越扁长，面积不变。非零黄金整数的范数是非零整数，所以两条双曲线 x·x′ = ±1 之间除了原点没有别的格点。把 φᵏ⁺¹ 的两个系数记成整数点 (x, y) = (Fₖ₊₁, Fₖ)，斜率 y/x = Fₖ/Fₖ₊₁ → φ⁻¹，与 φ⁻¹ 的差是 ψᵏ⁺¹/Fₖ₊₁。
  3. 回归。圆上每一步转过黄金角。观测量取 h(x) = cos 2πx，观测窗是它的一段取值 (u₁, u₂)。页面画出前 N 步里落进窗口的步、两次进入之间目前最长的间隔与落进去的比例；这些只是有限样本的描述，不推出任何对以后或对此刻比例的不等式。定理另外断言：存在某个 n₀ 与 M，n₀ 之后每连续 M + 1 步至少落进一次，并且对每个 ε > 0，N 足够大时前 N 步的比例不低于 1/(M + 1) − ε。页面不计算 n₀ 与 M，目前最长的间隔也不是 M + 1。
  4. 接受窗（文献结果，未在本书形式化）。三维正二十面体准晶由 ℤ⁶ ⊂ ℝ⁶ = E∥³ ⊕ E⊥³ 切投得到，它的接受窗是菱形三十面体：六条五重轴各出一条棱，拼成 30 个黄金菱形面，12 个五重顶点与 20 个三重顶点。窗口里的点到边界的距离 d∂ 是它经得起的扰动大小；点推到边界上，d∂ 归零，再往外就翻转。离边界至少 d 的部分是窗口缩小 1 − d/r 倍的副本（r 是内切球半径），所以它占体积的 (1 − d/r)³。

The coda sets “the observer” beside the golden cut-and-project construction already verified in trureturing: a periodic lattice in a higher dimension stays put, and a window decides which of its points are seen. This page draws that construction together with the ±1 seam and the return theorem that go with it. The page is a numerical illustration, not a proof; what it says about souls and ruins is an interpretive model of this book.

  1. Cut and project. A golden integer a + bφ has a conjugate a + bψ, with ψ = 1 − φ. Draw it as a point of the plane (itself across, its conjugate up) and the golden integers form a lattice. The horizontal window [φ − 2, φ − 1] + u has length 1; it selects the points whose conjugate lies inside, and they are projected to the horizontal axis: neighbouring points are φ or φ² apart and nothing else (at u = 0 both ends of the window are hit and one gap of length 1 appears), and no gap is smaller than 1. Move the window up and down and the points at its edge come and go (phason flips) while points far from the edge stay: the distance of a point to the boundary of the window is the shift it can withstand. Once a small patch in the middle is fixed, only a short range of shifts remains admissible, and every point that stays in the window under all of them, however far away, is fixed by that patch (a numerical illustration in one dimension; Conway’s “empire” in higher dimensions is a literature result). The green rings β(n) are the golden integers obtained by reading the Zeckendorf code of n (greedily take the largest Fibonacci number not above what is left; no two adjacent 1s) in powers of φ; the strip below lists the codes for n ≤ 54.
  2. Cassini. The golden integer φᵏ⁺¹ = Fₖ + Fₖ₊₁φ sits at (φᵏ⁺¹, ψᵏ⁺¹) in the lattice. The rectangle it spans with the origin has as its area the product of the two coordinates, which is its norm Fₖ·Fₖ₊₂ − Fₖ₊₁² = (−1)ᵏ⁺¹: always exactly 1, above the horizontal axis and below it in turn. The larger k, the longer and flatter the rectangle, with the same area. The norm of a nonzero golden integer is a nonzero integer, so between the two hyperbolas x·x′ = ±1 there is no lattice point but the origin. Writing the two coefficients of φᵏ⁺¹ as the integer point (x, y) = (Fₖ₊₁, Fₖ), the slope is y/x = Fₖ/Fₖ₊₁ → φ⁻¹, and its difference from φ⁻¹ is ψᵏ⁺¹/Fₖ₊₁.
  3. Returns. Each step on the circle turns by the golden angle. The observable is h(x) = cos 2πx and the observation window is a range (u₁, u₂) of its values. The page draws the steps among the first N that fall into the window, the longest gap between two visits so far and the share of steps inside; these only describe a finite sample, and no inequality about later steps or about the share at this N is inferred from them. The theorem says something else: there are some n₀ and M such that after n₀ every M + 1 consecutive steps contain a visit, and for each ε > 0 the share of the first N steps is at least 1/(M + 1) − ε once N is large. The page does not compute n₀ or M, and the longest gap so far is not M + 1.
  4. The acceptance window (literature result, not formalized in this book). Icosahedral quasicrystals in three dimensions are cut and projected from ℤ⁶ ⊂ ℝ⁶ = E∥³ ⊕ E⊥³, and their acceptance window is a rhombic triacontahedron: each of the six fivefold axes supplies an edge, giving 30 golden rhombi, 12 fivefold and 20 threefold vertices. The distance d∂ of a point in the window to the boundary is the size of the disturbance it can withstand; pushed onto the boundary its d∂ is zero, and beyond it flips. The part at least d from the boundary is a copy of the window shrunk by 1 − d/r (r is the inradius), so it takes up (1 − d/r)³ of the volume.

# Appendix F Exclusion Balls, Face Polarity, and Spin-Factor Orientation Classification {#sec-appendix-f}

In [Chapter 2](ch02-born-rule.md), working inside a pre-given finite-dimensional complex matrix algebra $M_n(\mathbb{C})$, we established in the forward direction that the trace pairing between density matrices and idempotent projections satisfies the probability-measure axioms ([@thm-born-probability-skeleton]) and that least confirmations on probes peel off positive-dual spectral decompositions ([@def-confirmation-system], [@thm-positive-dual-generation]); in the converse direction, Busch's finite-dimensional effect theorem ([@thm-busch-effect-density]) and the finite-complex Gleason projection theorem for $N \ge 3$ ([@thm-gleason-finite-projection]) characterized when an additive assignment must be a trace pairing. Later, [Appendix E](appendix-e.md) audited the conditional deduction chains leading from a raw two-slot comparison interface to inner-product geometry and the quantum postulates ([@rem-appe-layer-taxonomy]), guarding against smuggling hidden geometric hypotheses behind a slogan. Here we step back one level further to pose a standalone convex-geometric reconstruction question: **If we do not presuppose a complex Hilbert space or its projection lattice, and start instead from an abstract pointed state cone $(C, u)$ in a finite-dimensional real vector space, under what explicit geometric and order-theoretic axioms on exclusion sets and binary relations does the state space exhibit face polarity, Euclidean ball charts, and oriented four-dimensional spin-factor / $2 \times 2$ Pauli matrix interfaces?**

To answer this question without conflating distinct hypotheses, this appendix arranges four Lean-verified results (`LEAN ✓`; verification boundaries in [Appendix C](appendix-c.md)) along a transparent constructive progression:

1. **Recentering a quadratic exclusion constraint** (Section F.1, [@thm-appf-recentered-ball]): showing by completing the square that the quadratic inequality $b^2 \le t(1 - t)$ relating two-level diagonal weights $t, 1 - t$ to an off-diagonal coherence amplitude $b$ is equivalent to the closed Euclidean disk of radius $1/2$ centered at $(1/2, 0)$ in $\mathbb{R}^2$;
2. **Exclusion balls and face polarity on abstract pointed state cones** (Section F.2, [@thm-appf-exclusion-face-polarity]): proving on a closed, strictly normalized, space-spanning finite-dimensional real pointed cone that once every pure state's exclusion set is explicitly assumed to carry a positive-dimensional Euclidean ball chart (`exclusionBalls`), every nonempty proper convex extreme subset of the state base is either a singleton pure state or an exposed exclusion face—and, under the additional hypothesis `pairProper`, that the minimal cone-face slice generated by any two distinct pure states is itself a third pure state's exclusion ball (`wholePairBalls`);
3. **Concrete ball charts in the qutrit Hermitian cone** (Section F.3, [@thm-appf-hermitian-exclusion-chart]): realizing these axioms concretely inside the three-level quantum state cone ($3 \times 3$ complex positive semidefinite Hermitian matrices), where every pure state's exclusion face admits an explicit `ExclusionChart` of real affine dimension $3$—namely the $2 \times 2$ qubit Bloch ball supported on the orthogonal two-dimensional subspace;
4. **Oriented four-dimensional spin-factor classification and Pauli matrix interfaces** (Section F.4, [@thm-appf-spin-factor-classification]): proving on the concrete four-dimensional carrier $E = \mathbb{R} \times \mathbb{R}^3$ that any binary relation $D$ obeying two-point Gram positivity `P2` and quadrature phase balance `PB` equals either the right-handed or left-handed spatial cross product `crossD`, simultaneously unlocking all-$n$ Gram positivity, the Jordan–Lie associator identity, and the complete $2 \times 2$ complex Pauli matrix interface.

The arrows linking Sections F.1 through F.4 indicate the order of exposition and geometric construction rather than unconditional implication across modules: abstract cones must satisfy their own explicit axioms, and the exact bridge and dimensional boundaries between the two concrete quantum realizations (the qutrit Hermitian cone and the four-dimensional spin factor) are consolidated in Section F.5 ([@rem-appf-epistemic-boundary]).

## F.1 Recentering a Quadratic Exclusion Constraint {#sec-appendix-f-1}

Why does admitting coherent superposition inflate the state space between two mutually exclusive pure states from a flat classical probability segment into a round disk or solid ball? Let us begin with the simplest two-by-two real matrix calculation (`manual derivation`). Inspect the trace-$1$ real symmetric test matrix

$$
M(t, b) = \begin{pmatrix} t & b \\ b & 1 - t \end{pmatrix}, \qquad (t, b) \in \mathbb{R}^2,
$$

where $t$ and $1 - t$ are the diagonal weights on two orthogonal basis directions and $b$ is the real off-diagonal coherence amplitude. Because $\Tr M(t, b) = t + (1 - t) = 1 > 0$, $M(t, b)$ is positive semidefinite if and only if its determinant is nonnegative:

$$
\det M(t, b) = t(1 - t) - b^2 \ge 0 \iff b^2 \le t(1 - t).
$$

Checking three landmark values of $t$ by hand immediately reveals the geometry (`manual derivation`):

- **Midpoint cross-section ($t = 1/2$)**: Equal diagonal weights yield $b^2 \le \frac{1}{2}\left(1 - \frac{1}{2}\right) = \frac{1}{4}$, i.e., $|b| \le \frac{1}{2}$, where the transverse coherence radius reaches its maximum $1/2$;
- **Pure endpoints ($t = 0$ or $t = 1$)**: Here $t(1 - t) = 0$, so nonnegativity of $b^2$ over $\mathbb{R}$ forces $b^2 \le 0 \implies b = 0$. Whenever one diagonal component is completely excluded ($t = 0$), every off-diagonal coherence coupling to it vanishes identically, pinching the locus to the isolated pure endpoints $(0, 0)$ and $(1, 0)$;
- **Exterior forbidden region ($t < 0$ or $t > 1$)**: Here $t(1 - t) < 0$, so no real $b$ can satisfy $b^2 \le t(1 - t)$.

Completing the square in the longitudinal coordinate $t$ gives $t(1 - t) = \frac{1}{4} - \left(t - \frac{1}{2}\right)^2$, converting the asymmetric product bound $b^2 \le t(1 - t)$ into the standard equation of the closed Euclidean disk in $\mathbb{R}^2$ centered at $(1/2, 0)$ with radius $1/2$. The following frozen theorem records this exact real-algebraic equivalence.

::: {.theorem #thm-appf-recentered-ball title="Recentering a Quadratic Exclusion Constraint" lean="D5/S3/Quantum/OriginExclusionSegmentRigidity.b_constraint_recenter"}
For all real numbers $t, b \in \mathbb{R}$,

$$
b^2 \le t(1 - t) \iff \left(t - \frac{1}{2}\right)^2 + b^2 \le \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^2.
$$
:::

::: proof
Over $\mathbb{R}$ (with nonzero constant denominator $2 \neq 0$), expanding the left-hand side of the recentered disk inequality minus its right-hand side yields

$$
\left(t - \frac{1}{2}\right)^2 + b^2 - \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^2 = \left(t^2 - t + \frac{1}{4}\right) + b^2 - \frac{1}{4} = b^2 - t(1 - t).
$$

Hence $\left(t - \frac{1}{2}\right)^2 + b^2 \le \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^2$ holds if and only if $b^2 - t(1 - t) \le 0$, which is equivalent to $b^2 \le t(1 - t)$. The full proof is checked by the Lean kernel.
:::

One strict epistemic boundary must be stated right away: although [@thm-appf-recentered-ball] resides in the module `OriginExclusionSegmentRigidity`, the frozen theorem itself is purely a two-variable real polynomial equivalence carrying no extra physical assumptions and no infinite-dimensional hypotheses. One must never infer from the module name alone that an origin-exclusion postulate unconditionally reconstructs all of quantum theory. In an arbitrary convex state space, excluding a pure state need not leave behind a quadratic disk or Euclidean ball; turning ball-shaped exclusion slices into a structural theorem about the face lattice requires stating every finite-dimensional cone definition and ball-chart axiom explicitly.

## F.2 Pointed State Cones, Exclusion Balls, and Face Polarity {#sec-appendix-f-2}

Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional real normed vector space (`[NormedAddCommGroup V] [NormedSpace ℝ V] [FiniteDimensional ℝ V]`), let $C : \operatorname{PointedCone}\;\mathbb{R}\;V$ be a pointed convex cone over $\mathbb{R}$ in $V$, and let $u : V \to_{\mathbb{R}} \mathbb{R}$ be a real linear normalization functional. Before stating the face-polarity theorem, we unpack every convex-geometric object appearing in its signature:

1. **Normalized state base and pure states** (`base` and `pure`): The normalized cross-section of $C$ and its set of real extreme points are

   $$
   \operatorname{base}(C, u) := \{x \in V \mid x \in C \land u(x) = 1\}, \qquad \operatorname{pure}(C, u) := \operatorname{extremePoints}_{\mathbb{R}}\bigl(\operatorname{base}(C, u)\bigr).
   $$

   Quantifying over `p : pure C u` ranges over the subtype of real extreme points of $\operatorname{base}(C, u)$, coerced back to ambient vectors $(p : V)$ when needed.
2. **Proper effects and confirming functionals** (`proper` and `confirms`): A real linear functional $f : V \to_{\mathbb{R}} \mathbb{R}$ is **proper** on $(C, u)$ (`proper C u f`) if $0 \le f(x) \le u(x)$ for every $x \in C$, and **confirms** $p \in V$ (`confirms C u p f`) if `proper C u f` holds together with $f(p) = 1$.
3. **Exclusion set** (`exclusion`): For $p \in V$, the exclusion set consists of all normalized states annihilated by *some* proper functional confirming $p$:

   $$
   \operatorname{exclusion}(C, u, p) := \bigl\{x \in V \;\big|\; x \in \operatorname{base}(C, u) \land \exists f : V \to_{\mathbb{R}} \mathbb{R},\; \operatorname{confirms}(C, u, p, f) \land f(x) = 0\bigr\}.
   $$

   Notice the existential quantifier $\exists f$: a priori, `exclusion C u p` is a **union** of zero-slices across all functionals confirming $p$, not assumed in advance to be convex or cut out by a single functional.
4. **Exclusion ball charts and the exclusion-ball axiom** (`ExclusionChart` and `exclusionBalls`): For $p \in V$, an `ExclusionChart C u p` packages a positive natural dimension $\mathrm{dim} \in \mathbb{N}$ ($0 < \mathrm{dim}$) and an injective real affine map $\mathrm{param} : \operatorname{EuclideanSpace}\;\mathbb{R}\;(\operatorname{Fin}\;\mathrm{dim}) \to^{\mathrm{aff}}_{\mathbb{R}} V$ mapping the closed unit Euclidean ball $\overline{B}_2(0, 1)$ (`Metric.closedBall 0 1`) onto the exclusion set:

   $$
   \mathrm{param}\bigl(\overline{B}_2(0, 1)\bigr) = \operatorname{exclusion}(C, u, p).
   $$

   The predicate `exclusionBalls C u` asserts `Nonempty (ExclusionChart C u p)` for every pure state `p : pure C u`.
5. **Pair-generated cone faces and whole-pair ball charts** (`pairBase`, `pairProper`, `BallChart`, and `wholePairBalls`): For $p, q \in V$, `pairBase C u p q` is the slice of $\operatorname{base}(C, u)$ lying inside every cone face $F : C.\mathrm{Face}$ that contains both $p$ and $q$:

   $$
   \operatorname{pairBase}(C, u, p, q) := \bigl\{x \in V \;\big|\; x \in \operatorname{base}(C, u) \land \forall F : C.\mathrm{Face},\; p \in F \to q \in F \to x \in F\bigr\}.
   $$

   The condition `pairProper C u` states that $\operatorname{pairBase}(C, u, p, q) \neq \operatorname{base}(C, u)$ whenever $p \neq q$ in $\operatorname{pure}(C, u)$. Parallel to `ExclusionChart`, a `BallChart C u p q` consists of a positive dimension $\mathrm{dim} > 0$ and an injective real affine map sending $\overline{B}_2(0, 1) \subset \operatorname{EuclideanSpace}\;\mathbb{R}\;(\operatorname{Fin}\;\mathrm{dim})$ onto $\operatorname{pairBase}(C, u, p, q)$, and `wholePairBalls C u` asserts `Nonempty (BallChart C u p q)` for all distinct pure states $p \neq q$.

Before stating the abstract theorem, a concrete three-level quantum hand check in $\mathbb{C}^3$ (`manual derivation`) clarifies the crucial distinction among an exclusion face, a classical two-state convex segment, and the cone-face closure `pairBase`:

- Fix the pure state $p = \lvert 0\rangle\langle 0\rvert$. Every state orthogonal to $\lvert 0\rangle$ is supported on $\operatorname{span}\{\lvert 1\rangle, \lvert 2\rangle\}$, so the exclusion face of $p$ is the entire $2 \times 2$ density-matrix Bloch ball on $\operatorname{span}\{\lvert 1\rangle, \lvert 2\rangle\}$ (a solid $3$-ball, verified in Section F.3 below);
- If we form only **classical convex mixtures** $(1 - \lambda)\lvert 1\rangle\langle 1\rvert + \lambda\lvert 2\rangle\langle 2\rvert$ ($0 \le \lambda \le 1$) of two orthogonal pure states $\lvert 1\rangle\langle 1\rvert$ and $\lvert 2\rangle\langle 2\rvert$, we obtain merely the one-dimensional diagonal segment $\operatorname{diag}(0, 1 - \lambda, \lambda)$—which must never be mistaken for the full exclusion face! By contrast, if we form the **minimal positive-cone face slice** `pairBase` containing both $\lvert 1\rangle\langle 1\rvert$ and $\lvert 2\rangle\langle 2\rvert$, any cone face containing both endpoints also contains their midpoint $\frac{1}{2}(\lvert 1\rangle\langle 1\rvert + \lvert 2\rangle\langle 2\rvert)$ and hence contains every coherent pure-state projector supported in $\operatorname{span}\{\lvert 1\rangle, \lvert 2\rangle\}$. Consequently, `pairBase` expands from the $1$-dimensional segment to the entire $3$-dimensional Bloch ball, coinciding with the exclusion face of $\lvert 0\rangle\langle 0\rvert$;
- Conversely, see how non-examples violate the hypotheses (`manual derivation`): in a four-outcome classical probability simplex $\Delta_3$ (a solid tetrahedron), excluding one vertex leaves a two-dimensional triangle with three flat edges and corners rather than an affine Euclidean disk, violating `exclusionBalls`; whereas in a single qubit ($2 \times 2$ matrices), excluding $\lvert 0\rangle\langle 0\rvert$ leaves only the antipodal singleton $\{\lvert 1\rangle\langle 1\rvert\}$ of dimension $0$ (violating $0 < \mathrm{dim}$ in `ExclusionChart`), and any two distinct pure states have a full-rank midpoint, forcing `pairBase` to equal the entire Bloch ball and violating `pairProper`.

With these geometric distinctions in view, the next theorem establishes the complete face-polarity classification for closed generating pointed cones satisfying `exclusionBalls`.

::: {.theorem #thm-appf-exclusion-face-polarity title="Face Polarity for Exclusion Balls" lean="D5/S3/Quantum/Foundation/ExclusionBallFaces.exclusion_ball_faces"}
Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional real normed vector space, let $C : \operatorname{PointedCone}\;\mathbb{R}\;V$ be a real pointed cone, and let $u : V \to_{\mathbb{R}} \mathbb{R}$ be a real linear map. Assume:

1. **Closedness** (`hclosed`): $C$ is closed as a subset of $V$ (`IsClosed (C : Set V)`);
2. **Strict normalization** (`hstrict`): for every $x \in C$ with $x \neq 0$, $0 < u(x)$;
3. **Full span** (`hgen`): the real linear span of $C$ equals $V$ (`Submodule.span ℝ (C : Set V) = ⊤`);
4. **Exclusion balls** (`hB`): `exclusionBalls C u` holds.

Then the following three conclusions hold simultaneously:

1. **Exposure, extremality, and single-functional cutout of exclusion faces**: For every pure state $p \in \operatorname{pure}(C, u)$, the exclusion set $E_p := \operatorname{exclusion}(C, u, p)$ is nontrivial (contains at least two distinct points), compact, convex over $\mathbb{R}$, exposed in $\operatorname{base}(C, u)$ (`IsExposed ℝ`), extreme in $\operatorname{base}(C, u)$ (`IsExtreme ℝ`), strictly proper ($E_p \neq \operatorname{base}(C, u)$), and admits a single real linear functional $f : V \to_{\mathbb{R}} \mathbb{R}$ such that $\operatorname{confirms}(C, u, p, f)$ and

   $$
   \operatorname{exclusion}(C, u, p) = \{x \in V \mid x \in \operatorname{base}(C, u) \land f(x) = 0\}.
   $$

2. **Exhaustive dichotomy for nonempty proper extreme subsets**: For every subset $F \subseteq V$ that is nonempty, convex over $\mathbb{R}$, extreme in $\operatorname{base}(C, u)$ (`IsExtreme ℝ (base C u) F`), and strictly proper ($F \neq \operatorname{base}(C, u)$), either $F = \{p\}$ for some $p \in \operatorname{pure}(C, u)$, or $F = \operatorname{exclusion}(C, u, p)$ for some $p \in \operatorname{pure}(C, u)$.
3. **Conditional pair-face polarity and whole-pair balls**: If `pairProper C u` holds, then for every pair of distinct pure states $p, q \in \operatorname{pure}(C, u)$ ($p \neq q$), there exists a pure state $r \in \operatorname{pure}(C, u)$ such that

   $$
   \operatorname{pairBase}(C, u, p, q) = \operatorname{exclusion}(C, u, r),
   $$

   and `wholePairBalls C u` holds.
:::

::: proof
For the first conjunct, fix $p \in \operatorname{pure}(C, u)$. Hypothesis `hB` supplies an injective real affine map $\mathrm{param}$ from the closed Euclidean unit ball $\overline{B}_2(0, 1) \subset \mathbb{R}^d$ ($d = \mathrm{dim} > 0$) onto $E_p$. Since $d \ge 1$, $\overline{B}_2(0, 1)$ is compact, convex, and contains distinct points (such as $0$ and a unit basis vector), so continuous affine injectivity transfers nontriviality, compactness, and convexity directly to $E_p$; because $p \in \operatorname{base}(C, u)$ evaluates to $1 \neq 0$ under every confirming functional, $p \notin E_p$ and hence $E_p \neq \operatorname{base}(C, u)$. Combining finite dimensionality with `hclosed`, `hstrict`, and `hgen`, gluing confirming functionals across a finite spanning family of points in the compact ball (`FacetZeroGluing`) produces a single proper functional $f$ confirming $p$ that vanishes identically across all of $E_p$; nonnegativity of $f$ on $\operatorname{base}(C, u)$ then exhibits $E_p = \{x \in \operatorname{base}(C, u) \mid f(x) = 0\}$ as an exposed (and therefore extreme) subset of $\operatorname{base}(C, u)$.

For the second conjunct, let $F \subsetneq \operatorname{base}(C, u)$ be a nonempty proper convex extreme subset. Supporting-hyperplane separation and maximum attainment on the compact base yield a proper functional vanishing on $F$ and attaining $1$ at some pure state $r \in \operatorname{pure}(C, u)$, placing $F \subseteq E_r$. Pulling $F$ back along $r$'s injective affine chart gives a nonempty convex extreme subset $\mathrm{param}^{-1}(F)$ of the Euclidean unit ball $\overline{B}_2(0, 1)$. By strict convexity of the Euclidean norm, every nonempty proper extreme subset of $\overline{B}_2(0, 1)$ is a boundary singleton! Hence $\mathrm{param}^{-1}(F)$ is either a singleton (pushing forward to $F = \{p\}$ with $p \in \operatorname{pure}(C, u)$ by extremality in $\operatorname{base}(C, u)$) or the entire closed unit ball (pushing forward to $F = E_r$).

For the third conjunct, under `pairProper C u`, any two distinct pure states $p \neq q$ generate a convex extreme subset $\operatorname{pairBase}(C, u, p, q) \subsetneq \operatorname{base}(C, u)$ containing at least two distinct elements $p$ and $q$. The singleton branch of the second conjunct is ruled out, so $\operatorname{pairBase}(C, u, p, q) = \operatorname{exclusion}(C, u, r)$ for some $r \in \operatorname{pure}(C, u)$, and reusing $r$'s `ExclusionChart` immediately yields a `BallChart` for $(p, q)$, proving `wholePairBalls C u`. The full proof is checked by the Lean kernel.
:::

Note how indispensable the finite-dimensionality and ball-chart hypotheses are in [@thm-appf-exclusion-face-polarity]: compactness of the base and attainment of supporting-functional maxima rely on `FiniteDimensional ℝ V`, `hclosed`, and `hstrict`, and cannot be extrapolated to infinite dimension; the absence of intermediate flat faces in the second conjunct comes directly from strict boundary convexity of the Euclidean unit ball postulated by `exclusionBalls`; and the pair-ball conclusion in the third conjunct explicitly requires `pairProper C u`.

## F.3 Pure-State Exclusion Charts in the Qutrit Hermitian Cone {#sec-appendix-f-3}

How is the abstract `exclusionBalls` postulate realized inside concrete matrix mechanics? We now pass to a three-level complex quantum system (qutrit) and consider the real vector space of $3 \times 3$ complex Hermitian matrices

$$
\operatorname{HermitianMat}(\operatorname{Fin} 3, \mathbb{C}) := \operatorname{selfAdjoint}\bigl(M_3(\mathbb{C})\bigr),
$$

which has real vector-space dimension $3^2 = 9$. In `HermitianExclusionKernel`, the pointed cone `cone` is defined as the positive semidefinite cone `PointedCone.positive ℝ (HermitianMat (Fin 3) ℂ)`, and the linear normalization map `unit` is the real-valued trace `HermitianMat.trace` (projecting the self-adjoint matrix trace into $\mathbb{R}$ via `IsMaximalSelfAdjoint.selfadjMap`). Accordingly, $\operatorname{base}(\mathrm{cone}, \mathrm{unit})$ is the set of $3 \times 3$ complex density matrices of trace $1$ (real affine dimension $9 - 1 = 8$), and $\operatorname{pure}(\mathrm{cone}, \mathrm{unit})$ consists of the rank-one pure-state projectors $\lvert\psi\rangle\langle\psi\rvert$ ($\norm{\psi}_2 = 1$).

Let us first work out the computational pure state $p = \lvert 0\rangle\langle 0\rvert = \operatorname{diag}(1, 0, 0)$ explicitly by hand (`manual derivation`). The functional $f_0(X) = \Tr(p X) = X_{00}$ satisfies $0 \le X_{00} \le X_{00} + X_{11} + X_{22} = 1$ on $\operatorname{base}(\mathrm{cone}, \mathrm{unit})$ and $f_0(p) = 1$, so $f_0$ confirms $p$. Whenever $X \in \operatorname{base}(\mathrm{cone}, \mathrm{unit})$ satisfies the exclusion condition $X_{00} = 0$, nonnegativity of the $2 \times 2$ principal minors formed by row/column $0$ and $j \in \{1, 2\}$ forces

$$
\det\begin{pmatrix} X_{00} & X_{0j} \\ X_{j0} & X_{jj} \end{pmatrix} = X_{00} X_{jj} - |X_{0j}|^2 = -|X_{0j}|^2 \ge 0 \implies X_{01} = X_{10} = X_{02} = X_{20} = 0.
$$

Thus the entire first row and first column collapse to zero, confining the exclusion face of $\lvert 0\rangle\langle 0\rvert$ to the lower-right $2 \times 2$ block on $\operatorname{span}\{\lvert 1\rangle, \lvert 2\rangle\}$. Parametrizing trace-$1$ Hermitian blocks in real Bloch coordinates $(x, y, z) \in \mathbb{R}^3$ via

$$
X(x, y, z) = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & \frac{1 + z}{2} & \frac{x - i y}{2} \\ 0 & \frac{x + i y}{2} & \frac{1 - z}{2} \end{pmatrix},
$$

we obtain $\Tr X(x, y, z) = 0 + \frac{1 + z}{2} + \frac{1 - z}{2} = 1$, while the lower-right $2 \times 2$ block has trace $1 > 0$ and determinant

$$
\frac{1 + z}{2}\cdot\frac{1 - z}{2} - \frac{x - i y}{2}\cdot\frac{x + i y}{2} = \frac{1 - (x^2 + y^2 + z^2)}{4}.
$$

Hence $X(x, y, z)$ is positive semidefinite if and only if $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 \le 1$! The map $(x, y, z) \mapsto X(x, y, z)$ is manifestly real-affine and injective, carrying the closed unit $3$-ball $\overline{B}_2(0, 1) \subset \mathbb{R}^3$ bijectively onto the exclusion face of $\lvert 0\rangle\langle 0\rvert$. Transporting this construction across orthonormal frames extends it to every pure state of the qutrit cone.

::: {.theorem #thm-appf-hermitian-exclusion-chart title="Pure-State Exclusion Charts in the Qutrit Hermitian Cone" lean="D5/S3/Quantum/Foundation/HermitianExclusionBall.all_pure_charts"}
Let $\mathrm{cone} : \operatorname{PointedCone}\;\mathbb{R}\;\bigl(\operatorname{HermitianMat}(\operatorname{Fin} 3, \mathbb{C})\bigr)$ be the positive semidefinite cone of $3 \times 3$ complex Hermitian matrices, and let $\mathrm{unit} : \operatorname{HermitianMat}(\operatorname{Fin} 3, \mathbb{C}) \to_{\mathbb{R}} \mathbb{R}$ be the real-valued trace `HermitianMat.trace`. For every pure state $p \in \operatorname{pure}(\mathrm{cone}, \mathrm{unit})$, there exists an exclusion chart $a : \operatorname{ExclusionChart}\;\mathrm{cone}\;\mathrm{unit}\;(p : \operatorname{HermitianMat}(\operatorname{Fin} 3, \mathbb{C}))$ of affine dimension

$$
a.\mathrm{dim} = 3.
$$
:::

::: proof
Every extreme point $p \in \operatorname{pure}(\mathrm{cone}, \mathrm{unit})$ of the $3 \times 3$ trace-$1$ positive base corresponds to a rank-one orthogonal projector onto a unit vector in $\mathbb{C}^3$, and its exclusion locus consists precisely of the trace-$1$ positive semidefinite operators supported on the two-dimensional complex orthogonal complement (`HermitianExclusionKernel`). Selecting an orthonormal frame for that two-dimensional orthogonal complement (`SectionROGFrame`) identifies the compressed exclusion slice isometrically with trace-$1$ elements of $2 \times 2$ complex Hermitian matrices. Embedding $r \in \operatorname{EuclideanSpace}\;\mathbb{R}\;(\operatorname{Fin} 3)$ via the qubit Bloch affine map $r \mapsto \frac{1}{2}\operatorname{scalarB}(1, r) = \frac{1}{2}(I_2 + r_0\sigma_X + r_1\sigma_Y + r_2\sigma_Z)$ yields an injective real affine parametrization whose positive-semidefinite locus is characterized by $\norm{r}_2 \le 1$, mapping $\overline{B}_2(0, 1)$ onto $\operatorname{exclusion}(\mathrm{cone}, \mathrm{unit}, p)$ and furnishing an `ExclusionChart` with $a.\mathrm{dim} = 3$. The full proof is checked by the Lean kernel.
:::

One dimensional distinction in [@thm-appf-hermitian-exclusion-chart] deserves special emphasis: the chart dimension $a.\mathrm{dim} = 3$ is the **real affine dimension of the embedded $2 \times 2$ qubit Bloch ball** ($2^2 - 1 = 3$), **not** the complex Hilbert-space dimension $3$ of $\mathbb{C}^3$! Indeed, the ambient qutrit state base has real affine dimension $3^2 - 1 = 8$ and is *not* a Euclidean $8$-ball (its pure-state manifold is four-dimensional $\mathbb{C}\mathrm{P}^2$, bounded by three-dimensional spherical exclusion facets). Likewise, the theorem is typed specifically at `Fin 3` and does not assert that pure-state exclusion faces of $N \times N$ Hermitian cones for $N \ge 4$ remain Euclidean balls—at $N = 4$, excluding one pure state leaves behind the eight-dimensional qutrit state base itself, which is no longer affinely equivalent to a Euclidean ball.

## F.4 Oriented Spin-Factor Classification and Pauli Matrix Interfaces {#sec-appendix-f-4}

When any three-dimensional exclusion-ball chart of Section F.3 is coned over nonnegative trace weights, it spans a four-dimensional circular real cone—the classic **four-dimensional spin-factor cone** of Jordan algebra theory and relativistic spacetime geometry. Yet a round cone together with its symmetric inner-product pairing (the Jordan product) records only unoriented overlaps; recovering the full noncommutative $2 \times 2$ complex Pauli algebra requires characterizing the complementary antisymmetric directional relation $D$. In this section we unpack the exact definitions and oriented classification theorem on this four-dimensional carrier.

Let us inspect the concrete definitions from `OriginalDContract`, `OriginalSpinConsumers`, and `OriginalClassification`:

1. **Four-dimensional carrier, Jordan product, positive cone, and norm** (`Vec`, `E`, `unit`, `jordan`, `cone`, `originalNorm`):
   Let $\mathrm{Vec} := \operatorname{Fin} 3 \to \mathbb{R}$ carry the standard dot product $\operatorname{dot}(x, y) := \sum_{i=0}^2 x_i y_i$ and Euclidean length $\operatorname{vnorm}(x) := \sqrt{\operatorname{dot}(x, x)}$. The four-dimensional carrier and order unit are

   $$
   E := \mathbb{R} \times \mathrm{Vec}, \qquad \mathrm{unit} := (1, 0) \in E.
   $$

   Writing $a = (a_1, a_2), b = (b_1, b_2) \in E$ ($a_1 \in \mathbb{R}$ scalar part, $a_2 \in \mathrm{Vec}$ spatial vector part), the symmetric Jordan product, positive cone predicate, and $\ell^1 \oplus \ell^2$ norm are defined by

   $$
   \begin{aligned}
   \operatorname{jordan}(a, b) &:= \bigl(a_1 b_1 + \operatorname{dot}(a_2, b_2),\; a_1 b_2 + b_1 a_2\bigr), \\
   \operatorname{cone}(a) &:\Longleftrightarrow \operatorname{vnorm}(a_2) \le a_1, \qquad \operatorname{originalNorm}(a) := |a_1| + \operatorname{vnorm}(a_2).
   \end{aligned}
   $$

   Note carefully that **the positive-cone condition `cone a` requires only $\operatorname{vnorm}(a_2) \le a_1$ (implying $a_1 \ge 0$) and does not automatically impose unit-trace normalization $a_1 = 1$**. Two-variable $\varepsilon$-$\delta$ continuity `OriginalContinuous D` is defined with respect to `originalNorm`.
2. **Positive evaluations and oriented spatial cross products** (`Evaluation`, `evaluate`, `Orientation`, `crossD`):
   For scalar mass $m \in \mathbb{R}$ and vector $v \in \mathrm{Vec}$, the real linear functional $\operatorname{evaluationLinear}(m, v) : E \to_{\mathbb{R}} \mathbb{R}$ acts via $\operatorname{evaluate}(m, v, a) := m a_1 + \operatorname{dot}(v, a_2)$; a general real linear map $\alpha : E \to_{\mathbb{R}} \mathbb{R}$ satisfies `PositiveEvaluation α` iff $\forall a \in E,\; \operatorname{cone}(a) \to 0 \le \alpha(a)$; and the structure `Evaluation` bundles $(m, v)$ together with the positivity witness $\operatorname{vnorm}(v) \le m$. Binary operations have type $\mathrm{Relation} := E \to E \to E$. The two-valued inductive type `Orientation` has constructors `.plus` and `.minus` (mapped by `orientationBool` to `true` and `false`, respectively). With standard 3D cross product $\operatorname{cross}(x, y) := (x_1 y_2 - x_2 y_1,\; x_2 y_0 - x_0 y_2,\; x_0 y_1 - x_1 y_0)$, the two oriented cross-product relations are

   $$
   \operatorname{crossD}(\varepsilon, a, b) := \begin{cases} \bigl(0,\; \operatorname{cross}(a_2, b_2)\bigr), & \varepsilon = \mathrm{.plus}, \\ \bigl(0,\; -\operatorname{cross}(a_2, b_2)\bigr), & \varepsilon = \mathrm{.minus}. \end{cases}
   $$

3. **Two-point Gram positivity `P2` and quadrature phase balance `PB`**:
   Given $D : \mathrm{Relation}$, $\alpha : \mathrm{Evaluation}$, and $a, b, c \in E$, define the complex two-slot readout

   $$
   \operatorname{readout}(D, \alpha, a, b) := \operatorname{evaluate}\bigl(\alpha, \operatorname{jordan}(a, b)\bigr) - i\,\operatorname{evaluate}\bigl(\alpha, D(a, b)\bigr) \in \mathbb{C},
   $$

   and assemble the $2 \times 2$ complex matrix $\operatorname{gram2}(D, \alpha, a, b) := \begin{pmatrix} \operatorname{readout}(D, \alpha, a, a) & \operatorname{readout}(D, \alpha, a, b) \\ \operatorname{readout}(D, \alpha, b, a) & \operatorname{readout}(D, \alpha, b, b) \end{pmatrix}$.
   - **Axiom `P2 D`**: For every $\alpha : \mathrm{Evaluation}$ and all $a, b \in E$, $\operatorname{gram2}(D, \alpha, a, b)$ is positive semidefinite (`PosSemidef`);
   - **Axiom `PB D`**: Setting $\operatorname{squareReadout}(D, \alpha, b, c) := \operatorname{evaluate}(\alpha, \operatorname{jordan}(b, b)) + \operatorname{evaluate}(\alpha, \operatorname{jordan}(c, c)) + 2\operatorname{evaluate}(\alpha, D(b, c))$ and defining the phase-mixed pair $\operatorname{phaseR}(D, a, b, c) := b + D(a, b) - \operatorname{jordan}(a, c)$ and $\operatorname{phaseS}(D, a, b, c) := c + \operatorname{jordan}(a, b) + D(a, c)$, `PB D` requires that for every $\alpha : \mathrm{Evaluation}$ and all $a, b, c \in E$, reversing the sign of generator $a$ leaves `squareReadout` invariant:

     $$
     \operatorname{squareReadout}\bigl(D, \alpha, \operatorname{phaseR}(D, a, b, c), \operatorname{phaseS}(D, a, b, c)\bigr) = \operatorname{squareReadout}\bigl(D, \alpha, b - D(a, b) + \operatorname{jordan}(a, c),\; c - \operatorname{jordan}(a, b) - D(a, c)\bigr).
     $$

4. **Algebraic `Consequences D` and $2 \times 2$ Pauli `MatrixInterfaces`**:
   With $\mathrm{Mat} := M_2(\mathbb{C})$ and standard Pauli matrices $\sigma_X = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ (`qubitX`), $\sigma_Z = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$ (`qubitZ`), and $\sigma_Y = i\sigma_X\sigma_Z = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -i \\ i & 0 \end{pmatrix}$, define the observable map $\phi(a) := a_1 I_2 + a_{2,0}\sigma_X + a_{2,1}\sigma_Y + a_{2,2}\sigma_Z$, the unnormalized state matrix $\operatorname{density}(m, v) := \frac{1}{2}\phi(m, v) = \frac{1}{2}(m I_2 + v_0\sigma_X + v_1\sigma_Y + v_2\sigma_Z)$, and the oriented matrix multiplication $\operatorname{product}(\mathrm{opposite}, A, B)$ ($BA$ when $\mathrm{opposite} = \mathrm{true}$, and $AB$ when $\mathrm{opposite} = \mathrm{false}$).
   - **`Consequences D`** packages 14 properties: `P2 D` and `PB D`; left and right annihilation of `unit` multiples $D(r\cdot\mathrm{unit}, b) = D(a, r\cdot\mathrm{unit}) = 0$ and `unit`-translation invariance; real bilinearity (left/right additivity and real scalar homogeneity); submultiplicativity $\operatorname{originalNorm}(D(a, b)) \le \operatorname{originalNorm}(a)\operatorname{originalNorm}(b)$, the bilinear difference bound, and `OriginalContinuous D`; positive semidefiniteness of the $n \times n$ matrix $\operatorname{gram}(D, m, v, a)_{jk} = \operatorname{evaluate}(m, v, \operatorname{jordan}(a_j, a_k)) - i\,\operatorname{evaluate}(m, v, D(a_j, a_k))$ for every $n \in \mathbb{N}$, $\operatorname{vnorm}(v) \le m$, and $a : \operatorname{Fin} n \to E$; and the Jordan–Lie reassociation identity

     $$
     \operatorname{jordan}\bigl(\operatorname{jordan}(a, b), c\bigr) - \operatorname{jordan}\bigl(a, \operatorname{jordan}(b, c)\bigr) = D\bigl(D(a, b), c\bigr) - D\bigl(a, D(b, c)\bigr).
     $$

   - **`MatrixInterfaces opposite D`** packages 9 matrix characterizations: (i) $\operatorname{density}(m, v)$ is positive semidefinite $\iff \operatorname{vnorm}(v) \le m$; (ii) $\Tr(\operatorname{density}(m, v)) = (m : \mathbb{C})$; (iii) $\operatorname{PositiveEvaluation}(\operatorname{evaluationLinear}(m, v)) \iff \operatorname{vnorm}(v) \le m$; (iv) every linear functional satisfying `PositiveEvaluation` is represented by $\operatorname{evaluate}(m, v, \cdot)$ for some $(m, v)$ with $\operatorname{vnorm}(v) \le m$; (v) Born-trace pairing $\Tr(\operatorname{density}(m, v)\,\phi(a)) = (\operatorname{evaluate}(m, v, a) : \mathbb{C})$; (vi) $\phi(a)$ is positive semidefinite $\iff \operatorname{cone}(a)$; (vii) two-sided operator interval positivity $(r I_2 - \phi(a) \succeq 0 \land r I_2 + \phi(a) \succeq 0) \iff \operatorname{originalNorm}(a) \le r$; (viii) surjectivity onto positive semidefinite $2 \times 2$ matrices $\rho = \operatorname{density}(m, v)$ with $\operatorname{vnorm}(v) \le m$; and (ix) associative product decomposition

     $$
     \operatorname{product}\bigl(\mathrm{opposite}, \phi(a), \phi(b)\bigr) = \phi\bigl(\operatorname{jordan}(a, b)\bigr) - i\,\phi\bigl(D(a, b)\bigr).
     $$

A quick hand calculation on the spatial basis vectors shows how the orientation switch `orientationBool` interlocks with the minus sign in front of $i\,\phi(D(a, b))$ (`manual derivation`). Take $e_1 = \bigl(0, (1, 0, 0)\bigr)$ and $e_2 = \bigl(0, (0, 1, 0)\bigr)$ in $E = \mathbb{R} \times \mathbb{R}^3$, so $\phi(e_1) = \sigma_X$, $\phi(e_2) = \sigma_Y$, and $\operatorname{jordan}(e_1, e_2) = (0, 0)$:

- For $\varepsilon = \mathrm{.plus}$, $\operatorname{crossD}(\mathrm{.plus}, e_1, e_2) = \bigl(0, (0, 0, 1)\bigr) = +e_3$. Because the product formula subtracts $i\,\phi(D(e_1, e_2)) = i\sigma_Z$, the right-hand side equals $-i\sigma_Z$, matching the **opposite matrix product** $\operatorname{product}(\mathrm{true}, \sigma_X, \sigma_Y) = \sigma_Y \sigma_X = -i\sigma_Z$ selected by `orientationBool .plus = true`;
- For $\varepsilon = \mathrm{.minus}$, $\operatorname{crossD}(\mathrm{.minus}, e_1, e_2) = \bigl(0, (0, 0, -1)\bigr) = -e_3$, turning $-i\,\phi(-e_3)$ into $+i\sigma_Z$, which matches the **standard matrix product** $\operatorname{product}(\mathrm{false}, \sigma_X, \sigma_Y) = \sigma_X \sigma_Y = +i\sigma_Z$ selected by `orientationBool .minus = false`!
- Setting normalized mass $m = 1$ and north-pole Bloch vector $r = (0, 0, 1)$ ($\operatorname{vnorm}(r) = 1 \le 1$) gives $\operatorname{density}(1, r) = \frac{1}{2}(I_2 + \sigma_Z) = \operatorname{diag}(1, 0)$ of trace $1$.

The following frozen theorem proves that `P2` and `PB` alone rigidly classify every admissible binary relation $D$ on $E$ into one of these two chiralities and establish all consequences and Pauli matrix interfaces simultaneously.

::: {.theorem #thm-appf-spin-factor-classification title="Oriented Spin-Factor Classification and Matrix Interfaces" lean="D5/S3/Quantum/SpinFactor/OriginalClassification.complete_consequences"}
Let $D : \mathrm{Relation}$ (that is, $D : E \to E \to E$ on $E = \mathbb{R} \times (\operatorname{Fin} 3 \to \mathbb{R})$) satisfy two-point Gram positivity `P2 D` and quadrature phase balance `PB D`. Then there exists an orientation $\varepsilon : \mathrm{Orientation}$ such that:

1. $\varepsilon = \mathrm{.plus}$ or $\varepsilon = \mathrm{.minus}$;
2. for all $a, b \in E$,

   $$
   D(a, b) = \operatorname{crossD}(\varepsilon, a, b);
   $$

3. both `Consequences D` and `MatrixInterfaces (orientationBool ε) D` hold.
:::

The proof proceeds across three linked stages. First, on purely spatial inputs (`OriginalPureRigidity.global_pure`), varying the positive evaluation $\alpha$ ($\operatorname{vnorm}(v) \le m$) inside `P2 D` forces the diagonal entries of $\operatorname{gram2}(D, \alpha, a, b)$ to be real and nonnegative, enforces Hermitian conjugate symmetry (hence antisymmetry of $D$), kills the scalar component of $D$ as well as pairings with `unit`, and bounds the spatial norm of $D$ via the $2 \times 2$ determinant inequality; applying the phase-balance identity `PB D` on orthonormal spatial frames then supplies exact quadratic norm conservation, eliminating contraction factors and locking $D$ on spatial vectors to the Euclidean cross product up to a single global handedness $\varepsilon \in \{\mathrm{.plus}, \mathrm{.minus}\}$.

Second, full-domain extension (`OriginalResidual.all_inputs_of_pure`) uses annihilation and translation invariance along scalar multiples of `unit` to lift the spatial formula from $(0, a_2), (0, b_2)$ to arbitrary four-vectors $a, b \in E$, establishing $D(a, b) = \operatorname{crossD}(\varepsilon, a, b)$ pointwise on $E$.

Third, consumer transfer (`OriginalSpinConsumers.same_D_consequences`) transports `Consequences` and `MatrixInterfaces (orientationBool ε)` from the explicit verification on $\operatorname{crossD}(\varepsilon, \cdot, \cdot)$, where Pauli expansion $\phi : E \to M_2(\mathbb{C})$, eigenvalue bounds $m \pm \operatorname{vnorm}(v) \ge 0$, $n \times n$ Gram factorizations through the positive semidefinite square root of $\operatorname{density}(m, v)$, and Pauli multiplication identities discharge all 14 algebraic clauses and 9 matrix equivalences.

# Reducing Matrices to XOR: Three-Bit Blades and the XOR–Pauli Geometric Algebra Engine

> *"Quantum = two particles = the measuring particle …"*

Across the twenty-four chapters of Parts I through VI, we climbed the dimensional ladder — from point to line, line to plane, plane to sphere, coupled spheres, causal cones, and the sphere beyond the sphere — restoring quantum mechanics from mystical particle folklore to orthogonal projection, oriented bivector area, and spinor geometry on $S^2$. With this chapter we enter **Part VII (*Construction and Verification*)**: here we turn our geometric architecture into two concrete artifacts that any reader can run and audit line by line — **a zero-dependency Python 3 geometric algebra and rotor compilation engine** (Chapters 25 and 26), and **a direct source-level audit of the frozen Lean 4 proofs** (Chapters 27 and 28).

Start with an elementary computational question. When we simulate single-qubit Pauli operators $X, Y, Z$ or the three-dimensional Euclidean Clifford algebra $\mathcal{Cl}_{3,0}(\mathbb{R})$ on a classical computer, do we actually need to allocate $2\times 2$ complex matrices and execute eight complex multiplications and four additions per product?

Not at all! Once we strip away matrix redundancy, the entire algebraic engine reduces to bitwise logic:

1. In two-dimensional complex Hilbert space, the noncommutative structure of the qubit algebra is generated by the Weyl pair `qubitX` and `qubitZ` obeying three laws: **involutory squares** ($X^2 = I, Z^2 = I$), **self-adjointness** ($X^* = X, Z^* = Z$), and **anticommutation** ($ZX = -XZ$);
2. In the eight-dimensional real Clifford algebra $\mathcal{Cl}_{3,0}(\mathbb{R})$, the eight basis blades $\{1, e_1, e_2, e_1 e_2, e_3, e_1 e_3, e_2 e_3, e_1 e_2 e_3\}$ correspond bijectively to **3-bit integer masks** $m \in \{000_2, \dots, 111_2\}$: the metric rule $e_i^2 = +1$ is literally **bitwise XOR** (`left ^ right`), while anticommutation $e_j e_i = -e_i e_j$ ($i < j$) is simply the **inversion parity sign** counting bit crossings!

This chapter establishes four frozen Lean 4 theorems — the qubit Weyl star identities, the no-character theorem, the absence of common Pauli eigenvectors, and the traceless Bloch operator formula — adds a fifth, the composition, adjoint and conjugation laws of Weyl displacement words, as the kernel-checked basis of the "XOR plus sign" multiplication of Pauli words, and then builds the complete runnable zero-dependency Python 3 `GeometricState` engine.

## The Weyl Pair, Anticommutation, and the No-Character Theorem {#sec-weyl-pair-no-character}

In the Lean 4 module `D5/S3/Quantum/FiniteDimensional.lean`, the single-qubit operator algebra is formalized as the $2\times 2$ complex matrix algebra $\mathrm{QubitMatrix} = M_2(\mathbb{C})$ (`Matrix (Fin 2) (Fin 2) ℂ`), generated by the standard bit-flip and phase-flip matrices:

$$
\operatorname{qubitX} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad
\operatorname{qubitZ} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}.
$$

Why do `qubitX` and `qubitZ` alone lock down the entire noncommutative structure of a single qubit? Our first frozen theorem packages their five fundamental $\ast$-algebra identities — anticommutation, self-adjointness of both generators, and unit squares of both generators — into a single parameter-free conjunction.

::: {.theorem #thm-qubit-weyl-star title="Anticommutation, Self-Adjointness, and Unit Squares of the Qubit Weyl Pair" lean="D5/S3/Quantum/FiniteDimensional.qubit_weyl_star"}
In the single-qubit complex matrix algebra $\mathrm{QubitMatrix} = M_2(\mathbb{C})$, the Pauli generators $\operatorname{qubitX}$ and $\operatorname{qubitZ}$ simultaneously satisfy all five identities:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{qubitZ} \cdot \operatorname{qubitX} &= -(\operatorname{qubitX} \cdot \operatorname{qubitZ}), \\
\operatorname{star}(\operatorname{qubitX}) &= \operatorname{qubitX}, \qquad
\operatorname{star}(\operatorname{qubitZ}) = \operatorname{qubitZ}, \\
\operatorname{qubitX}^2 &= 1, \qquad\qquad\;\;
\operatorname{qubitZ}^2 = 1.
\end{aligned}
$$
:::

::: proof
In `D5/S3/Quantum/FiniteDimensional.lean:24`, the proof splits the five conjuncts via four `constructor` steps, applies matrix extensionality `ext i j` and finite case enumeration `fin_cases i <;> fin_cases j` over `Fin 2`, and discharges each entry by `simp [qubitX, qubitZ, pow_two, Matrix.mul_apply, Fin.sum_univ_two]`. Explicitly,

$$
\operatorname{qubitZ}\cdot\operatorname{qubitX} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix} = -\begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix} = -(\operatorname{qubitX}\cdot\operatorname{qubitZ}),
$$

while both real symmetric matrices equal their conjugate transposes and square to $I_2$. The full proof is checked by the Lean kernel.
:::

::: {.figure src="ch25-anticommute"}
**Swap the two mirrors and the arrow lands at the opposite end.** In the real plane of state vectors, $\operatorname{qubitX}$ is the mirror along the slanted cyan dashed line and $\operatorname{qubitZ}$ the mirror along the horizontal one. The amber $u$ reflected in $X$ and then $Z$ (the dashed jumps) ends at $ZXu$; reflected in $Z$ and then $X$ (the solid jumps) it ends at $XZu$. The two dark arrows make one diameter: $ZX = -XZ$ ([@thm-qubit-weyl-star]).
:::

Directly from [@thm-qubit-weyl-star], a foundational obstruction follows: **why can no classical valuation assign simultaneous complex numbers to every qubit observable while preserving algebra multiplication and the identity?**

In algebraic terms, such a valuation would be a unital $\mathbb{C}$-algebra homomorphism (a **character**) $\chi : \mathrm{QubitMatrix} \to_{\mathrm{alg}[\mathbb{C}]} \mathbb{C}$. Using only the anticommutation and unit-square conjuncts of [@thm-qubit-weyl-star], the next frozen lemma proves that the type of unital algebra homomorphisms from $\mathrm{QubitMatrix}$ to $\mathbb{C}$ is empty (`IsEmpty`).

::: {.lemma #lemma-qubit-no-character title="No Unital Complex-Algebra Character Exists on the Qubit Matrix Algebra" lean="D5/S3/Quantum/FiniteDimensional.qubit_matrix_algebra_has_no_character"}
The type of unital $\mathbb{C}$-algebra homomorphisms from $\mathrm{QubitMatrix} = M_2(\mathbb{C})$ to $\mathbb{C}$ is empty:

$$
\operatorname{IsEmpty}\bigl(\mathrm{QubitMatrix} \to_{\mathrm{alg}[\mathbb{C}]} \mathbb{C}\bigr).
$$
:::

::: proof
Suppose $\chi : \mathrm{QubitMatrix} \to_{\mathrm{alg}[\mathbb{C}]} \mathbb{C}$ is a unital $\mathbb{C}$-algebra homomorphism. Applying $\chi$ to the anticommutation identity $\operatorname{qubitZ}\cdot\operatorname{qubitX} = -(\operatorname{qubitX}\cdot\operatorname{qubitZ})$ from [@thm-qubit-weyl-star] yields $\chi(\operatorname{qubitZ})\chi(\operatorname{qubitX}) = -\chi(\operatorname{qubitX})\chi(\operatorname{qubitZ})$. Because multiplication in the target field $\mathbb{C}$ is commutative (`mul_comm`), we have

$$
\chi(\operatorname{qubitX})\chi(\operatorname{qubitZ}) = \chi(\operatorname{qubitZ})\chi(\operatorname{qubitX}) = -\chi(\operatorname{qubitX})\chi(\operatorname{qubitZ}),
$$

so multiplying by $\frac{1}{2}$ (`linear_combination (1 / 2 : ℂ) * hSelfNegating`) forces $\chi(\operatorname{qubitX})\chi(\operatorname{qubitZ}) = 0$. Since $\mathbb{C}$ has no zero divisors (`mul_eq_zero.mp`), either $\chi(\operatorname{qubitX}) = 0$ or $\chi(\operatorname{qubitZ}) = 0$. Yet applying $\chi$ to $\operatorname{qubitX}^2 = 1$ and $\operatorname{qubitZ}^2 = 1$ gives $\chi(\operatorname{qubitX})^2 = 1$ and $\chi(\operatorname{qubitZ})^2 = 1$; either zero case reduces to $0 = 1$, a contradiction. The full proof is checked by the Lean kernel.
:::

## No Common Eigenvector and the Traceless Bloch Operator Core {#sec-no-common-eigenvector-bloch-core}

While [@lemma-qubit-no-character] rules out multiplicative characters on the full matrix algebra, one might ask a weaker state-level question: **does there exist a nonzero state vector $\psi \in \mathrm{QubitState} = (\operatorname{Fin} 2 \to \mathbb{C})$ that is simultaneously an eigenvector of both $\operatorname{qubitX}$ and $\operatorname{qubitZ}$?**

In `D5/S3/Quantum/QubitWitnesses.lean`, the next frozen proposition proves that any vector $\psi : \operatorname{Fin} 2 \to \mathbb{C}$ satisfying both $\operatorname{qubitX}\psi = x\psi$ and $\operatorname{qubitZ}\psi = z\psi$ must vanish identically ($\psi = 0$).

::: {.proposition #prop-pauli-no-common-eigenvector title="Pauli X and Z Observables Have No Nonzero Common Eigenvector" lean="D5/S3/Quantum/QubitWitnesses.pauli_observables_have_no_common_eigenvector"}
Let $\psi : \mathrm{QubitState} = (\operatorname{Fin} 2 \to \mathbb{C})$ be a two-component complex vector and let $x, z \in \mathbb{C}$ be complex scalars. If

$$
\operatorname{mulVec}(\operatorname{qubitX}, \psi) = x \bullet \psi \qquad\text{and}\qquad \operatorname{mulVec}(\operatorname{qubitZ}, \psi) = z \bullet \psi,
$$

then

$$
\psi = 0.
$$
:::

::: proof
In `D5/S3/Quantum/QubitWitnesses.lean:18`, evaluating `hX` and `hZ` at indices $0$ and $1$ (`congrFun hX 0`, `congrFun hX 1`, `congrFun hZ 0`, `congrFun hZ 1`) yields four scalar equations:

$$
\psi_1 = x\psi_0, \qquad \psi_0 = x\psi_1, \qquad \psi_0 = z\psi_0, \qquad -\psi_1 = z\psi_1.
$$

First we prove $\psi_0 = 0$ by contradiction (`by_contra hPsi0`): if $\psi_0 \neq 0$, then $\psi_0 = x\psi_1$ forces $\psi_1 \neq 0$; meanwhile $(z - 1)\psi_0 = z\psi_0 - \psi_0 = 0$ with $\psi_0 \neq 0$ implies $z = 1$. Substituting $z = 1$ into $-\psi_1 = z\psi_1$ gives $-\psi_1 = \psi_1$, so multiplying by $-\frac{1}{2}$ (`linear_combination (-1 / 2 : ℂ) * hNeg`) yields $\psi_1 = 0$, contradicting $\psi_1 \neq 0$. Thus $\psi_0 = 0$. Substituting $\psi_0 = 0$ into $\psi_1 = x\psi_0$ gives $\psi_1 = 0$, and function extensionality `funext i` with `fin_cases i` concludes $\psi = 0$. The full proof is checked by the Lean kernel.
:::

::: {.figure src="ch25-no-common-line"}
**The eigenlines of $X$ and $Z$ sit $45^\circ$ apart and meet only at the origin.** Drawn in the real plane of state vectors (every eigenvector is a complex multiple of a vector on one of these lines): the solid cyan lines are the two eigenlines of $X$ ($+1$ at $45^\circ$, $-1$ at $135^\circ$), the dashed cyan lines those of $Z$ ($+1$ horizontal, $-1$ vertical). No line belongs to both, and the only common point is the green $\psi = 0$ ([@prop-pauli-no-common-eigenvector]).

How, then, does a three-dimensional real vector $a = (a_0, a_1, a_2) \in \mathbb{R}^3$ (`EuclideanSpace ℝ (Fin 3)`) embed into $\mathrm{QubitMatrix}$? In `D5/S3/Quantum/Measurement/Bloch/QubitBloch.lean`, the traceless Bloch operator `B a` is defined by

$$
B(a) = (a_0 : \mathbb{C})\bullet \sigma_X + (a_1 : \mathbb{C})\bullet \sigma_Y + (a_2 : \mathbb{C})\bullet \sigma_Z
$$

(alongside the shifted operator $\operatorname{scalarB}(t, a) = (t : \mathbb{C})\bullet I_2 + B(a)$). The fourth frozen result in this chapter gives the exact $2\times 2$ complex matrix representation of $B(a)$.

::: {.corollary #cor-bloch-operator-core title="Explicit 2×2 Matrix Formula for the Traceless Bloch Operator" lean="D5/S3/Quantum/Measurement/Bloch/QubitBloch.B_formula_core"}
For every vector $a \in \operatorname{EuclideanSpace}\,\mathbb{R}\,(\operatorname{Fin} 3)$, the traceless Bloch operator $B(a)$ equals the explicit $2\times 2$ complex matrix

$$
B(a) = \begin{pmatrix} (a_2 : \mathbb{C}) & (a_0 : \mathbb{C}) - i\,(a_1 : \mathbb{C}) \\ (a_0 : \mathbb{C}) + i\,(a_1 : \mathbb{C}) & -(a_2 : \mathbb{C}) \end{pmatrix}.
$$
:::

::: proof
In `D5/S3/Quantum/Measurement/Bloch/QubitBloch.lean:31`, the proof applies `ext i j` and `fin_cases i <;> fin_cases j`, unfolds `B`, `pauliMatrix` (where $\sigma_X = \operatorname{qubitX}$, $\sigma_Z = \operatorname{qubitZ}$, and $\sigma_Y = i\operatorname{qubitX}\operatorname{qubitZ}$), `qubitX`, and `qubitZ`, and closes all four matrix entries by `simp` and `ring`. The full proof is checked by the Lean kernel.
:::

## Three-Bit Blade Masks, Inversion Parity, and Two-Bit Symplectic Pauli Words {#sec-three-bit-blade-masks-and-symplectic-pauli}

Inspecting [@thm-qubit-weyl-star] and [@cor-bloch-operator-core] together reveals why a real 3-vector $a = (a_0, a_1, a_2) \in \mathbb{R}^3$ requires $2\times 2$ complex matrices when written in standard quantum notation: the single-qubit Pauli algebra is the complex matrix representation of the **three-dimensional real Euclidean Clifford algebra $\mathcal{Cl}_{3,0}(\mathbb{R})$**!

In $\mathcal{Cl}_{3,0}(\mathbb{R})$, three mutually orthogonal unit vectors $e_1, e_2, e_3$ obey

$$
e_i^2 = +1 \quad (i \in \{1, 2, 3\}), \qquad e_i e_j = -e_j e_i \quad (i \neq j).
$$

Instead of storing $2\times 2$ matrices, we represent the $2^3 = 8$ basis blades of $\mathcal{Cl}_{3,0}(\mathbb{R})$ by a **3-bit integer blade mask** $m = b_2 b_1 b_0 \in \{0, 1, \dots, 7\}$, where bit $0$ (`1 << 0 = 1`) indicates the presence of $e_1$, bit $1$ (`1 << 1 = 2`) indicates $e_2$, and bit $2$ (`1 << 2 = 4`) indicates $e_3$:

::: {.figure src="ch25-mask-cube"}
**The eight blades are the corners of a cube, and multiplying by a blade walks one edge along each direction it lights up.** Right is $e_1$ (bit $0$), into the page $e_2$, up $e_3$; each corner carries its blade and mask. The two amber arrows leave $e_{12}$ ($011_2$), walk one edge along $e_2$ and one along $e_3$, and stop at $e_{13}$ ($101_2$): multiplying by $e_{23}$ ($110_2$) lands on $011_2 \oplus 110_2 = 101_2$. The sign of this product is $+$; the figure draws no signs, which come from the inversion count below.

With this 3-bit encoding, the geometric product of any two basis blades $E_{\mathrm{left}}$ and $E_{\mathrm{right}}$ reduces to two bitwise operations (Panel I of Figure 25):

1. **Target blade mask via bitwise XOR**: Whenever a basis vector $e_i$ appears in both $\mathrm{left}$ and $\mathrm{right}$, $e_i^2 = +1$ cancels that bit; basis vectors appearing in only one operand survive. Thus the product blade has mask
   $$
   \mathrm{mask} = \mathrm{left} \oplus \mathrm{right} = \texttt{left \^{} right}.
   $$
2. **Sign factor via bit-inversion parity**: Sorting the concatenated product $E_{\mathrm{left}} E_{\mathrm{right}}$ into canonical order $e_1 e_2 e_3$ requires moving each active bit $i \in \{0, 1, 2\}$ of $\mathrm{left}$ past every active bit $j < i$ of $\mathrm{right}$ (captured by the low-bit mask `(1 << i) - 1`). Each crossing flips the sign via $e_i e_j = -e_j e_i$ ($j < i$), giving `_blade_sign(left, right)`:
   $$
   \operatorname{inv}(\mathrm{left}, \mathrm{right}) = \sum_{i=0}^{2} \bigl((\mathrm{left} \gg i)\,\&\,1\bigr)\cdot \operatorname{popcount}\bigl(\mathrm{right}\,\&\,(2^i - 1)\bigr), \qquad
   \operatorname{sign}(\mathrm{left}, \mathrm{right}) = (-1)^{\operatorname{inv}(\mathrm{left}, \mathrm{right})}.
   $$

**One swap, one sign: e₂ · e₁ = −e₁₂.** In either order the masks give 001₂ ⊕ 010₂ = 011₂. In e₁ · e₂ the two factors are already in order, no swap is needed, and that cell of the table is cyan; in e₂ · e₁ the right e₁ has to move past the left e₂, the two magenta lines in the right panel cross once, the sign becomes −, and this cell is magenta.

Notice how both conventions in the code fit together with 100% consistency:

- **3-bit $\mathcal{Cl}_{3,0}(\mathbb{R})$ basis blades (`basis(0..2)` at masks `1, 2, 4`) and pseudoscalar $I_3$ (`mask = 7`)**: The three orthogonal spatial axes $e_1, e_2, e_3$ occupy bits $0, 1, 2$ (`masks 1, 2, 4`). The oriented volume pseudoscalar $I_3 = e_1 e_2 e_3$ (`mask = 7`) commutes with every blade and satisfies $I_3^2 = -1$. Under the complex representation $e_1 \mapsto X, e_2 \mapsto Y, e_3 \mapsto Z, I_3 \mapsto i I_2$, the bivector $e_1 e_2$ equals $I_3 e_3 \leftrightarrow iZ$ (and similarly $e_2 e_3 = I_3 e_1 \leftrightarrow iX$, $e_3 e_1 = -e_1 e_3 = I_3 e_2 \leftrightarrow iY$).

::: {.viz src="blade-multiplication" preset="pseudo" height="500"}
**The square of the pseudoscalar: I₃ · I₃ = −1.** All three bits cancel (three cyan crosses in the ⊕ row); the magenta crossings in the right panel number 0 + 1 + 2 = 3, an odd count, so the result is −1. The violet row and column of I₃ agree cell by cell: I₃ commutes with every blade.
:::

- **2-bit symplectic Pauli words (`pauli(x, z, phase)` at masks `0..3`)**: To model a binary Weyl pair $X_{\mathrm{sym}}^x Z_{\mathrm{sym}}^z$ directly via two anticommuting involutions, `GeometricState.pauli(x, z, phase)` places the $x \in \{0, 1\}$ bit on bit $0$ ($e_1$, mask `1`) and the $z \in \{0, 1\}$ bit on bit $1$ ($e_2$, mask `2`), forming `mask = (1 if x else 0) | (2 if z else 0)`. When both bits are active ($x = 1, z = 1$), the mask is `011₂ = 3` ($e_1 e_2$), and XOR multiplication automatically satisfies
  $$
  \operatorname{pauli}(1, 0) \cdot \operatorname{pauli}(0, 1) = e_1 e_2 = -\operatorname{pauli}(0, 1) \cdot \operatorname{pauli}(1, 0).
  $$

Finally, the **reversion** anti-involution $\widetilde{M}$ (`reverse()`) reverses the order of vector factors in each $k$-blade, multiplying grade $k = \operatorname{popcount}(m)$ by $(-1)^{k(k-1)/2}$ ($+1$ for grades $0, 1$ and $-1$ for grades $2, 3$).

::: {.figure src="ch25-reversion"}
**Reversing the factors takes $k(k-1)/2$ swaps, and every swap flips the sign.** Each panel has the factors of a grade-$k$ blade on top and the reversed order at the bottom; each swap of neighbours is two crossing magenta strands. Grades $0$ and $1$ need no swap and carry a cyan $+$; grade $2$ needs $1$ swap and grade $3$ needs $3$, and both carry a magenta $-$, as in the “Reverse” column of the table.
:::

So far, the rule "XOR the indices, then multiply by a sign" has been obtained only by counting swaps against the Clifford axioms. For words built from one pair of generators it has a kernel-checked general form, and not only for two levels: replace "negate" by "multiply by an $M$-th root of unity" and the same rule holds for any $M$-level system. The module `D5/S3/Quantum/Algebra/WeylDisplacement` at the pinned commit, with its two follow-up modules, freezes it.

::: {.definition #def-weyl-displacement-word title="Window Root, Clock Matrix, Shift Matrix, and Weyl Displacement Words" lean="D5/S3/Observer/WindowRegister.windowRoot D5/S3/Observer/WindowRegister.clockMatrix D5/S3/Observer/WindowRegister.shiftMatrix D5/S3/Quantum/Algebra/WeylDisplacement.displacement"}
Let $M$ be a positive integer (`[NeZero M]`), with indices in $\mathbb{Z}_M$ (`ZMod M`); $a.\mathrm{val} \in \{0, \dots, M-1\}$ denotes the standard representative of $a \in \mathbb{Z}_M$.

1. The **window root** [`windowRoot`](lean:D5/S3/Observer/WindowRegister.windowRoot) $M$ is $\omega = e^{2\pi i/M}$;
2. The **clock matrix** [`clockMatrix`](lean:D5/S3/Observer/WindowRegister.clockMatrix) $M$ is the diagonal complex matrix indexed by $\mathbb{Z}_M$ whose $i$-th diagonal entry is $\omega^{\,i.\mathrm{val}}$, written $Z$;
3. The **shift matrix** [`shiftMatrix`](lean:D5/S3/Observer/WindowRegister.shiftMatrix) $M$ is the circulant matrix `Matrix.circulant (fun i => if i = 1 then 1 else 0)`, whose $(i, j)$ entry is $1$ when $i - j = 1$ and $0$ otherwise, written $X$;
4. The **displacement word** [`displacement`](lean:D5/S3/Quantum/Algebra/WeylDisplacement.displacement) $M\ a\ b$ is $X^{a.\mathrm{val}}\,Z^{\,b.\mathrm{val}}$, written $D(a, b)$.
:::

::: {.theorem #thm-weyl-displacement-laws title="Composition, Adjoint, Exchange, and Conjugation Laws for Weyl Displacement Words" lean="D5/S3/Quantum/Algebra/WeylDisplacement.displacement_mul D5/S3/Quantum/Algebra/WeylDisplacementAdjoint.displacement_adjoint D5/S3/Quantum/Algebra/WeylDisplacement.displacement_comm D5/S3/Quantum/Algebra/WeylDisplacementConjugation.displacement_conjugation D5/S3/Quantum/Algebra/WeylDisplacement.displacement_two_anticommute"}
Let $M$ be a positive integer, in the notation of [@def-weyl-displacement-word]. For all $a, b, c, d \in \mathbb{Z}_M$ (products and differences in the exponents are computed in $\mathbb{Z}_M$ and then replaced by their standard representatives):

1. **Composition law**: $D(a, b)\,D(c, d) = \omega^{\,bc}\,D(a + c,\ b + d)$;
2. **Adjoint law**: $D(a, b)^{\dagger} = \omega^{\,ab}\,D(-a,\ -b)$;
3. **Exchange law**: $\omega^{\,da}\,D(a, b)\,D(c, d) = \omega^{\,bc}\,D(c, d)\,D(a, b)$;
4. **Conjugation law**: $D(a, b)\,D(c, d)\,D(a, b)^{\dagger} = \omega^{\,bc - ad}\,D(c, d)$;
5. **The sign really occurs**: for $M = 2$, $D(0, 1)\,D(1, 0) = -\,D(1, 0)\,D(0, 1)$.
:::

::: proof
The key step is pushing the clock past the shift: $Z X = \omega\,X Z$, hence $Z^{b} X^{c} = \omega^{\,bc} X^{c} Z^{b}$; moreover $X^M = Z^M = 1$, so exponents matter only modulo $M$. Claim 1 follows by reordering $X^a Z^b X^c Z^d$ accordingly. Claim 2: $(X^a Z^b)^{\dagger} = Z^{-b} X^{-a} = D(0, -b)\,D(-a, 0)$, reordered by Claim 1 with phase $\omega^{(-b)(-a)}$. Claim 3 compares Claim 1 applied in the two orders. Claim 4 multiplies Claim 1 by Claim 2; the three phase exponents add up to $bc + ab + (b + d)(-a) = bc - ad$. Claim 5 is Claim 1 with $\omega = -1$. The full proof is checked by the Lean kernel.
:::

The exponent $bc - ad$ of Claim 4 is the **symplectic pairing** of the two index pairs $(a, b)$ and $(c, d)$: it changes sign when the pairs are swapped and vanishes when a pair is paired with itself. Claim 3 yields a commutation criterion (a manual derivation, not formalized in this book; the Lean module itself states no such consequence): $D(c, d)\,D(a, b)$ is invertible, so $D(a, b)$ and $D(c, d)$ commute if and only if $\omega^{\,bc - ad} = 1$, that is, $bc - ad \equiv 0 \pmod M$.

::: {.figure src="ch25-weyl-clock"}
**For $M = 3$ one swap is not a change of sign but a turn by $120^\circ$.** The left panel shows the three basis vectors $\lvert 0\rangle, \lvert 1\rangle, \lvert 2\rangle$ of a three-level system: the cyan arrows are the shift $X$, which pushes each one to the next round the ring, and the violet $1, \omega, \omega^2$ are the phases the clock $Z$ gives them. The right panel is the unit circle of phases: by the composition law $ZX = \omega\,XZ$, and the violet arc turns $120^\circ$ from $XZ$ to $ZX$; the dashed arc is the same arc for $M = 2$, a turn of $180^\circ$ to $-1$, which is a change of sign ([@thm-weyl-displacement-laws]).
:::

The multiplication law for several $M$-level systems side by side is already cited in [Chapter 14](ch14-cloning-codes-and-braids.md), [@lemma-pauli-word-phase]: its cross-phase exponent $\sum_n a.z_n \cdot b.x_n$ is the exponent $bc$ of Claim 1 summed over the systems.

::: {.example #ex-pauli-words-xor-sign-table title="M = 2: the Multiplication Table of the Four Pauli Words, Checked Entry by Entry Against XOR Plus Sign"}
Take $M = 2$. Then $\omega = -1$, and by [@def-weyl-displacement-word]:

$$
X = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad
Z = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad
D(1, 1) = XZ = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix} = -iY .
$$

The four words are $D(0,0) = I$, $D(1,0) = X$, $D(0,1) = Z$ and $D(1,1) = XZ$. Addition in $\mathbb{Z}_2$ is XOR, so the composition law reads

$$
D(a, b)\,D(c, d) = (-1)^{bc}\,D(a \oplus c,\ b \oplus d) .
$$

**Multiplication table** (rows: left factor; columns: right factor; every entry obtained from the formula above and cross-checked by multiplying $2 \times 2$ matrices):

| left \ right | $I$ | $X$ | $Z$ | $XZ$ |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| $I$ | $I$ | $X$ | $Z$ | $XZ$ |
| $X$ | $X$ | $I$ | $XZ$ | $Z$ |
| $Z$ | $Z$ | $-XZ$ | $I$ | $-X$ |
| $XZ$ | $XZ$ | $-Z$ | $X$ | $-I$ |

Minus signs occur exactly in the four cells where the left factor contains $Z$ ($b = 1$) and the right factor contains $X$ ($c = 1$): this is the exponent $bc$. The bottom-right entry $(XZ)^2 = -I$ agrees with $(-iY)^2 = -I$.

**Comparison with the bit rule of this section.** With $x$ in bit $0$ and $z$ in bit $1$, the inversion-count formula of this section has a single term for two-bit masks:

$$
\operatorname{inv}(\mathrm{left}, \mathrm{right}) = z_{\mathrm{left}}\cdot x_{\mathrm{right}} ,
$$

which is the exponent $bc$ of the composition law ($b = z_{\mathrm{left}}$, $c = x_{\mathrm{right}}$); the XOR of the output mask corresponds to adding indices. The engine's two-bit rule agrees entry by entry with the multiplication of the matrix words $X^a Z^b$.

**Commutation table.** By the symplectic pairing $bc - ad \bmod 2$: $X$ and $Z$ give $0\cdot 0 - 1\cdot 1 \equiv 1$, so they anticommute; $X$ and $XZ$ give $0\cdot 1 - 1\cdot 1 \equiv 1$, anticommute; $Z$ and $XZ$ give $1\cdot 1 - 0\cdot 1 \equiv 1$, anticommute; every word paired with itself gives $0$. The three nontrivial words anticommute pairwise, in agreement with the opposite signs at mirrored positions of the multiplication table.

**Three levels for comparison.** For $M = 3$, $\omega = e^{2\pi i/3}$ and the composition law gives $Z X = \omega\,X Z$: one exchange is no longer a sign flip but a turn through $120^\circ$; two words commute if and only if $bc - ad \equiv 0 \pmod 3$.
:::

::: {.viz src="blade-multiplication" preset="pauli" height="500"}
**The multiplication table of the four Pauli words.** Two bits only: bit 0 is X, bit 1 is Z. The four magenta cells are exactly those whose left factor contains Z and right factor contains X; the chosen Z · X takes one swap and gives −XZ. This is the exponent bc of the composition law [@thm-weyl-displacement-laws] for M = 2.
:::

## Zero-Dependency Python 3 Geometric Algebra Engine and Self-Test {#sec-geometric-state-python-engine}

The eight blades first combine with real coefficients: the table gives a faithful complex Pauli matrix representation of the real algebra $\mathcal{Cl}_{3,0}(\mathbb{R})$, with its literature context in [@rem-clifford-stabilizer-literature]. The code stores coefficients as `complex`, allowing an independent complex coefficient for every blade. The `1j` in `pauli(x, z, phase)` is an external coefficient phase, kept separate from the real algebra's volume blade $I_3$; the table no longer gives a faithful representation of this enlarged complex-coefficient container. `reverse()` reverses blade factors and extends linearly in the coefficients, without conjugating them. For real coefficients, the scalar part read by `norm_squared()` is the nonnegative sum of the squared blade coefficients. The next chapter uses real coefficients on even blades and imposes the unit condition on its rotors, so reversion supplies the inverse and the sandwich acts as a three-dimensional rotation.

Here is the complete, runnable, zero-dependency Python 3 implementation of `GeometricState` (from the companion verification script `part7_engine.py`):

```python
from __future__ import annotations

from dataclasses import dataclass
from typing import Dict, Mapping


def _blade_sign(left: int, right: int) -> int:
    """Sign of e_left * e_right in Cl(3,0), with e_i^2 = 1."""
    inversions = 0
    for i in range(3):
        if (left >> i) & 1:
            inversions += (right & ((1 << i) - 1)).bit_count()
    return -1 if inversions % 2 else 1


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class GeometricState:
    """Sparse multivector in the eight-dimensional Cl(3,0) basis."""

    terms: Mapping[int, complex]

    def __post_init__(self) -> None:
        clean = {int(k): complex(v) for k, v in self.terms.items() if abs(v) > 1e-14}
        if any(k < 0 or k > 7 for k in clean):
            raise ValueError("a Cl(3,0) blade mask must use three bits")
        object.__setattr__(self, "terms", clean)

    @staticmethod
    def zero() -> "GeometricState":
        return GeometricState({})

    @staticmethod
    def scalar(value: complex) -> "GeometricState":
        return GeometricState({0: value})

    @staticmethod
    def basis(index: int) -> "GeometricState":
        if index not in (0, 1, 2):
            raise ValueError("basis index must be 0, 1, or 2")
        return GeometricState({1 << index: 1})

    @staticmethod
    def pauli(x: int, z: int, phase: int = 0) -> "GeometricState":
        """Binary Pauli word, encoded by the X/Z symplectic bits."""
        if x not in (0, 1) or z not in (0, 1):
            raise ValueError("Pauli bits must be 0 or 1")
        mask = (1 if x else 0) | (2 if z else 0)
        return GeometricState({mask: (1j) ** phase})

    def __add__(self, other: "GeometricState") -> "GeometricState":
        out: Dict[int, complex] = dict(self.terms)
        for mask, value in other.terms.items():
            out[mask] = out.get(mask, 0j) + value
        return GeometricState(out)

    def __neg__(self) -> "GeometricState":
        return GeometricState({m: -v for m, v in self.terms.items()})

    def __sub__(self, other: "GeometricState") -> "GeometricState":
        return self + (-other)

    def __rmul__(self, scalar: complex) -> "GeometricState":
        return GeometricState({m: scalar * v for m, v in self.terms.items()})

    def __mul__(self, other: "GeometricState") -> "GeometricState":
        out: Dict[int, complex] = {}
        for left, a in self.terms.items():
            for right, b in other.terms.items():
                mask = left ^ right
                value = a * b * _blade_sign(left, right)
                out[mask] = out.get(mask, 0j) + value
        return GeometricState(out)

    def reverse(self) -> "GeometricState":
        out = {}
        for mask, value in self.terms.items():
            grade = mask.bit_count()
            out[mask] = value * (-1 if grade * (grade - 1) // 2 % 2 else 1)
        return GeometricState(out)

    def scalar_part(self) -> complex:
        return self.terms.get(0, 0j)

    def norm_squared(self) -> float:
        value = (self * self.reverse()).scalar_part()
        return float(value.real)

    def close(self, other: "GeometricState", tol: float = 1e-10) -> bool:
        return all(abs((self - other).terms.get(m, 0j)) <= tol
                   for m in set(self.terms) | set(other.terms))
```

Let us trace the bitwise arithmetic and Bloch-operator correspondence on concrete inputs.

::: {.example #ex-xor-pauli-table title="Tracing Three-Bit XOR Products, Pseudoscalar Square, and Bloch Vector Square"}
1. **Bitwise trace of `_blade_sign`**:
   - For $e_1 \cdot e_2$: $\mathrm{left} = 001_2 = 1$, $\mathrm{right} = 010_2 = 2$, and `1 ^ 2 = 3` ($e_1 e_2$). In `_blade_sign(1, 2)`, only $i = 0$ is active in $\mathrm{left}$, and `(2 & 0).bit_count() == 0`, so $\operatorname{sign}(1, 2) = (-1)^0 = +1$ and $e_1 e_2 = +E_3$.
   - For $e_2 \cdot e_1$: $\mathrm{left} = 010_2 = 2$, $\mathrm{right} = 001_2 = 1$, and `2 ^ 1 = 3`. In `_blade_sign(2, 1)`, $i = 1$ is active in $\mathrm{left}$, and `(1 & 1).bit_count() == 1`, so $\operatorname{sign}(2, 1) = (-1)^1 = -1$ and $e_2 e_1 = -E_3 = -e_1 e_2$.
2. **Square of the volume pseudoscalar $I_3 = e_1 e_2 e_3$**: With $\mathrm{left} = \mathrm{right} = 111_2 = 7$, the XOR mask is `7 ^ 7 = 0` (the scalar $1$). Across $i = 0, 1, 2$, `_blade_sign(7, 7)` counts $0 + 1 + 2 = 3$ inversions, yielding $I_3^2 = (-1)^3 \cdot 1 = -1$.
3. **Square of a Bloch vector $v = a_0 e_1 + a_1 e_2 + a_2 e_3$**: Take $a = (3, 4, 12) \in \mathbb{R}^3$ and form $v = 3e_1 + 4e_2 + 12e_3$ (`terms = {1: 3, 2: 4, 4: 12}`). In $v \cdot v$, all six off-diagonal products $a_i a_j (e_i e_j + e_j e_i)$ cancel by opposite inversion parity, leaving only the scalar mask `0`:
   $$
   v^2 = (3^2 + 4^2 + 12^2)\cdot 1 = (9 + 16 + 144)\cdot 1 = 169 = 13^2,
   $$
   matching the square of the $2\times 2$ complex matrix from [@cor-bloch-operator-core]:
   $$
   B(3, 4, 12)^2 = \begin{pmatrix} 12 & 3 - 4i \\ 3 + 4i & -12 \end{pmatrix}^2 = \begin{pmatrix} 144 + 25 & 0 \\ 0 & 25 + 144 \end{pmatrix} = 169\,I_2.
   $$
:::

::: {.remark #rem-clifford-stabilizer-literature title="Clifford Algebra Classification and Binary Symplectic Stabilizer Encoding (Literature Result, Not Formalized in This Book)"}
**Literature result, not formalized in this book.** Two bodies of classical literature surround the algebraic structures of this chapter:

1. **Isomorphism $\mathcal{Cl}_{3,0}(\mathbb{R}) \cong M_2(\mathbb{C})$ and Clifford algebra classification** (P. Lounesto, *Clifford Algebras and Spinors*, 2nd Ed., Cambridge University Press, 2001, Ch. 3 & Ch. 16; H. B. Lawson & M.-L. Michelsohn, *Spin Geometry*, Princeton University Press, 1989, Ch. I): As an eight-dimensional real associative algebra, $\mathcal{Cl}_{3,0}(\mathbb{R})$ is isomorphic to $M_2(\mathbb{C})$ with center $\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{1, I_3\} \cong \mathbb{C}$, while its four-dimensional even subalgebra $\mathcal{Cl}^+_{3,0}(\mathbb{R})$ is isomorphic to the quaternion division ring $\mathbb{H}$.
2. **Binary symplectic Pauli representation and the Gottesman–Knill theorem** (D. Gottesman, *Stabilizer Codes and Quantum Error Correction*, PhD thesis, Caltech, 1997, arXiv:quant-ph/9705052; S. Aaronson & D. Gottesman, *Phys. Rev. A* **70**, 052328, 2004, DOI `10.1103/PhysRevA.70.052328`): Modulo central phases, the $n$-qubit Pauli group is isomorphic to the $2n$-dimensional binary symplectic space $\mathbb{F}_2^{2n}$, where group multiplication is bitwise XOR and commutation is governed by the binary symplectic form. For a single $M$-level system, the composition phase and the conjugation phase of displacement words ($M = 2$ being the one-qubit Pauli words) are verified by [@thm-weyl-displacement-laws], and the multiplication of multi-mode Pauli words with its cross phase by [@lemma-pauli-word-phase] in [Chapter 14](ch14-cloning-codes-and-braids.md); the global statement "isomorphic to a symplectic vector space modulo phases", the stabilizer tableau, the action of Clifford gates on symplectic vectors and the Gottesman–Knill simulation theorem remain literature results.
3. **Exact scope of the frozen theorems**: The five Lean-verified results in this chapter are the composition, adjoint, exchange and conjugation laws of Weyl displacement words ([@thm-weyl-displacement-laws]), the qubit Weyl star identities ([@thm-qubit-weyl-star]), the no-character theorem on $\mathrm{QubitMatrix}$ ([@lemma-qubit-no-character]), the absence of a nonzero common eigenvector of `qubitX` and `qubitZ` ([@prop-pauli-no-common-eigenvector]), and the explicit $2\times 2$ matrix formula for the traceless Bloch operator $B(a)$ ([@cor-bloch-operator-core]). The Python 3 `GeometricState` engine provides an executable computational model of $\mathcal{Cl}_{3,0}(\mathbb{R})$ and does not claim a Lean formalization of the universal Clifford isomorphism or the $n$-qubit Gottesman–Knill complexity theorem.
:::

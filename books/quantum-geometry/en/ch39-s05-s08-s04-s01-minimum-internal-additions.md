# 35.6.8.4.1 The minimum number of internal additions

Fix $d,r\ge 1$ and put $q=3^d$. The points are $\mathbb F_3^d\times\mathbb Z/(3r)\mathbb Z$, with one row for each $x\in\mathbb F_3^d$. Keep all the mixed lifted triples, whose first coordinates are distinct and whose sum in the product group is zero. New triples may lie within a row. Every triple must have three distinct points, and any two distinct points may occur together at most once. [@thm-joint-circuit-internal-dimension]

::: {.theorem #thm-joint-circuit-minimum-internal-lower-bound title="A lower bound for gaining one potential dimension" lean="D5/S3/Combinatorics/Matroids/RankThreeMinimumInternalAdditions.internal_additions_lower_bound"}
Under these conditions, if the normalized potential space grows to dimension $d+1$, at least $qr^2$ internal triples must be added.
:::

::: proof
The dimension criterion requires every uncovered pair within a row to have the same residue modulo three. Thus two points in the same row with different residues must occur together in an added triple. Each row has six ordered pairs of distinct residue classes, with $r$ points per class. Across all rows this gives $6qr^2$ ordered point pairs.

For every such pair, choose a containing triple and record the ordered slots of the two points. The triple and slots recover the original pair, so the assignment is injective. Each added triple supplies only six ordered pairs of distinct slots, giving the lower bound $qr^2$. The full proof is checked by the Lean kernel. [@thm-joint-circuit-internal-dimension]
:::

::: {.theorem #thm-joint-circuit-minimum-internal-attained title="A transversal design on the residue classes attains the bound" lean="D5/S3/Combinatorics/Matroids/RankThreeMinimumInternalAdditions.exists_internal_additions_attaining"}
Exactly $qr^2$ internal triples can be added to the mixed family above so that each triple in the combined family has three distinct points, every pair of distinct points occurs at most once, and the normalized potential space has dimension exactly $d+1$.
:::

::: proof
In each row, divide the cyclic coordinates into their three residue classes modulo three. Each class has $r$ points, which can be labelled by $\mathbb Z/r\mathbb Z$. For every pair of labels $a,b$, take the points labelled $a$ in class $0$, $b$ in class $1$, and $-a-b$ in class $2$. Any two points from different classes determine the third label. Hence every different-residue pair within a row occurs exactly once, while same-residue pairs lie in none of these triples. Each new triple has residue sum $0+1+2=0$, and each uncovered pair in a row has residue difference zero. Both residue conditions hold, so the potential dimension is $d+1$.

Each row contributes $r^2$ new triples, and none shares a pair with a mixed triple. The labels need only give a bijection from each residue class to the set $\mathbb Z/r\mathbb Z$, so the construction also applies when $9\mid 3r$. For example, when $d=1$ and $r=2$, each row has classes $\{0,3\}$, $\{1,4\}$, and $\{2,5\}$. Label each class by $0,1$ in the order shown. This gives the four triples $\{0,1,2\}$, $\{0,4,5\}$, $\{3,1,5\}$, and $\{3,4,2\}$ in each row, for twelve additions across the three rows. The full proof is checked by the Lean kernel. [@thm-joint-circuit-internal-dimension]
:::

## 35.6.8.4.1.1 All extensions at the minimum

The construction above uses a cyclic rule for the last label. The exact count also tells us how much freedom is left. In each row and each residue class, fix a labelling by $\{0,\ldots,r-1\}$, independently of the labellings in the other classes and rows.

::: {.theorem #thm-joint-circuit-minimum-internal-latin title="A minimum family is generated by rowwise Latin squares" lean="D5/S3/Combinatorics/Matroids/RankThreeOptimalInternalLatin.exists_latin_of_optimal_family"}
Keep the mixed family fixed. If the added family has $3^d r^2$ triples and the normalized potential dimension is $d+1$, there is an order-$r$ Latin square $L_x$ for each row $x$. The unordered triples in row $x$ are exactly those formed from label $a$ in residue class $0$, label $b$ in class $1$, and label $L_x(a,b)$ in class $2$, for all $a,b$.
:::

::: proof
At the minimum, the injection from the lower bound is a bijection. Every ordered pair of distinct positions of an added triple is used by a same-row point pair with different residues. The three points in each triple therefore have residues $0,1,2$ in some order. Each pair in different residue classes has a unique third point. Define $L_x(a,b)$ using the pair in classes $0,1$. The unique completions for pairs in classes $0,2$ and $1,2$ make every row and every column of $L_x$ a permutation of the labels. The full proof is checked by the Lean kernel.
:::

::: {.theorem #thm-joint-circuit-minimum-internal-latin-unique title="The unordered family determines the squares" lean="D5/S3/Combinatorics/Matroids/RankThreeOptimalInternalLatin.latin_assignment_injective"}
For the fixed labellings, two assignments of tables give the same unordered triple family only if their entries agree in every row and cell.
:::

::: proof
The point residues distinguish the three roles in a triple after its order is forgotten. For a fixed row and cell $(a,b)$, the points in classes $0,1$ recover $a,b$, and the point in class $2$ recovers the entry. The full proof is checked by the Lean kernel.
:::

::: {.theorem #thm-joint-circuit-minimum-internal-latin-converse title="Any assignment of Latin squares gives a minimum family" lean="D5/S3/Combinatorics/Matroids/RankThreeOptimalInternalLatin.optimal_family_of_latin"}
Choose an order-$r$ Latin square independently in every row. Adding its cell triples to the fixed mixed family gives $3^d r^2$ distinct internal triples. The combined family remains pair-unique and its normalized potential space has dimension $d+1$.
:::

::: proof
The permutation laws give unique completion for each pair of points with different residues in a row. The internal triples never repeat a point and cover no same-residue pair. A mixed triple covers only pairs from different rows, so the new triples preserve pair uniqueness. Every internal residue sum is zero, and every uncovered same-row pair has residue difference zero. The dimension criterion then gives $d+1$. There are $r^2$ cells per row. The full proof is checked by the Lean kernel. [@thm-joint-circuit-internal-dimension]
:::

## 35.6.8.4.1.2 All extensions with the extra dimension

A minimum extension already covers every pair of different residues in a row. To add more triples while retaining the extra dimension, the dimension criterion allows additions inside individual residue classes. Keep the mixed family and the independent labellings fixed. A partial Steiner triple system is a family of three-element sets in which any two distinct labels occur together at most once.

::: {.theorem #thm-joint-circuit-steiner-decomposition title="The Latin squares and partial Steiner systems of an extension" lean="D5/S3/Combinatorics/Matroids/RankThreeInternalSteinerDecomposition.forward_decomposition"}
Suppose the added triples have three distinct points within one row and the combined family is pair-unique. If the normalized potential space has dimension $d+1$, the unordered added family consists of the cell triples of one Latin square per row and the triples of a partial Steiner system in each actual residue class of each row.
:::

::: proof
The residue sum of every internal triple is zero, so its residues are all equal or all distinct. Every same-row pair with different residues needs a triple. The pairs in classes $0,1$ count each different-residue triple once, giving exactly $3^d r^2$. Deleting the same-residue triples retains all different-residue pairs and the extra dimension. Thus the minimum-family classification applies to the retained triples. The remaining triples inherit pair uniqueness within each residue class. The full proof is checked by the Lean kernel.
:::

::: {.theorem #thm-joint-circuit-steiner-realization title="The square and packing choices are independent" lean="D5/S3/Combinatorics/Matroids/RankThreeInternalSteinerDecomposition.realize_decomposition"}
Any rowwise assignment of order-$r$ Latin squares and any assignment of partial Steiner systems on the $r$ labels of each residue class gives a pair-unique extension of the mixed family with normalized potential dimension $d+1$.
:::

::: proof
Mixed triples use pairs from different rows. Latin triples use pairs of different residues in one row. The partial systems use pairs within a single residue class. These pair types are disjoint. The added same-residue triples have residue sum zero and can only remove uncovered pairs, so the condition for the extra dimension survives. The full proof is checked by the Lean kernel.
:::

::: {.theorem #thm-joint-circuit-steiner-unique title="The unordered family determines all the choices" lean="D5/S3/Combinatorics/Matroids/RankThreeInternalSteinerDecomposition.decomposition_unique"}
For the fixed labellings, the unordered extension uniquely determines the square in each row and the partial Steiner system in each row and residue class.
:::

::: proof
The residue pattern identifies which triples come from the squares. Each remaining triple identifies its row and residue class, and the labels in that class recover the three-element set. The full proof is checked by the Lean kernel.
:::

For example, take $d=1$ and $r=3$. Each residue class contains exactly three points. Its unique triple can be added or left out, independently in each of the nine classes across the three rows. Thus the Latin squares supply $27$ triples, and any number from zero through nine can be added to them while keeping the normalized potential space two-dimensional.

## 35.6.8.4.1.3 Attainable numbers of internal triples

In the preceding example, any number of same-residue triples can be removed while retaining the dimension. The same deletion property holds for arbitrary residue classes. Write $D(r,3,2)$ for the largest number of blocks in a partial Steiner triple system on $r$ labels.

::: {.theorem #thm-joint-circuit-internal-size-spectrum title="The full interval of attainable sizes" lean="D5/S3/Combinatorics/Matroids/RankThreeInternalSizeSpectrum.exact_size_spectrum"}
Keep the mixed triples, pair uniqueness and normalization specified above. The possible numbers of added same-row triples that retain potential dimension $d+1$ are exactly the integers in
$$
3^d r^2 \le N \le 3^d r^2+3^{d+1}D(r,3,2).
$$
:::

::: proof
Each row contributes $r^2$ Latin-square triples. Those triples occupy distinct rows or distinct cells and have three different residues. Each remaining triple belongs to a partial Steiner system in a single residue class. There are $3^{d+1}$ such classes, each contributing at most $D(r,3,2)$ triples. This gives the two endpoints.

To realize any integer between them, place a largest packing in each residue class. Choose any $N-3^d r^2$ triples from their union. Deleting triples preserves pair uniqueness within each class. These choices and the rowwise Latin squares give an extension by [@thm-joint-circuit-steiner-realization]. The full proof is checked by the Lean kernel.
:::

## 35.6.8.4.1.4 Sizes of saturated extensions

Keep the mixed triples fixed and require pair uniqueness and normalized potential dimension $d+1$. An extension is **saturated** if one more same-row triple cannot be added under these conditions. Any proper compatible enlargement permits adding one of its new triples first, so saturation is equivalent to inclusion maximality. [@thm-joint-circuit-steiner-insert]

::: {.theorem #thm-joint-circuit-steiner-insert title="Adding one triple from a larger extension" lean="D5/S3/Combinatorics/Matroids/RankThreeInternalSizeSpectrum.internal_extension_insert"}
If compatible families of same-row triples satisfy $G\subseteq H$, adjoining any triple of $H$ to $G$ gives a compatible family.
:::

::: proof
Each Latin-square triple of $G$ remains a Latin-square triple in $H$. Its points of residues $0$ and $1$ determine the cell, and its point of residue $2$ determines the entry. Thus the squares agree. If the chosen triple is new, it belongs to a single residue class. Adjoining it leaves that class's packing inside the corresponding packing of $H$, preserving pair uniqueness and the extra dimension.
:::

::: {.theorem #thm-joint-circuit-steiner-saturated title="Saturation is maximality within each residue class" lean="D5/S3/Combinatorics/Matroids/RankThreeInternalSizeSpectrum.saturated_internal_extension_iff"}
Under the actual residue labellings, an extension is saturated exactly when every partial Steiner triple system in its decomposition is inclusion-maximal.
:::

::: proof
A larger packing in one class gives a proper global enlargement by changing that class alone. Conversely, a proper global enlargement fixes the Latin squares and therefore enlarges the packing in at least one residue class.
:::

::: {.theorem #thm-joint-circuit-steiner-six-saturated-sizes title="Saturated sizes with six labels have step two" lean="D5/S3/Combinatorics/Matroids/RankThreeInternalSizeSpectrum.saturated_six_size_spectrum"}
For $r=6$, the attainable sizes of saturated extensions are exactly
$$
36\cdot 3^d+2\cdot 3^{d+1}+2k,\qquad 0\le k\le 3^{d+1},\quad k\in\mathbb Z.
$$
:::

::: proof
The two local maximal sizes also follow from the classical packing spectrum surveyed by Colbourn. [Sections 2.3.1–2.3.3](https://ajc.maths.uq.edu.au/pdf/4/ocr-ajc-v4-p143.pdf)

On six labels, each label lies in at most two triples. Each incident triple uses two of the label's five available partners. Counting incidences gives at most four blocks. An empty or one-block packing can be extended. In a three-block packing, no two blocks can be disjoint, since a third would then repeat a pair of one of them. The three pairwise intersections are distinct singletons. Each block also has one point belonging only to it. All three pairs among these private points are unused, so the three points form an addable fourth block.

Both maximal sizes occur. Writing $abc=\{a,b,c\}$, the two-block example $012,345$ leaves a complete bipartite graph of unused pairs and hence no available triangle. The example $012,034,135,245$ attains the four-block upper bound. Each of the $3^{d+1}$ actual residue classes can independently take either size. Choosing four blocks in exactly $k$ classes and adding the $36$ Latin-square blocks per row gives the formula. For $d=1$, the saturated sizes are precisely $126,128,\ldots,144$.
:::

### The gap below a complete packing

When the $r$ labels support a complete Steiner triple system, the two largest saturated sizes again differ by two. A complete packing uses every unordered pair, so each class contains $r(r-1)/6$ blocks. To use two fewer blocks, choose three points that form no block and replace the blocks covering their three pairs.

::: {.theorem #thm-joint-circuit-steiner-saturated-top-gap title="The two largest saturated sizes from a complete Steiner system" lean="D5/S3/Combinatorics/Matroids/RankThreeSaturatedTopGap.saturated_top_gap"}
Keep the cross-row triples, pair uniqueness, and normalization of this section. Let $d>0$ and $r\ge7$, and assume that the $r$ labels admit a Steiner triple system covering every unordered pair. Write
$$
N_{\max}=3^d r^2+3^{d+1}\frac{r(r-1)}{6}.
$$

Every saturated extension has at most $N_{\max}$ added within-row triples. Both $N_{\max}$ and $N_{\max}-2$ occur, and $N_{\max}-1$ is impossible.
:::

::: proof
A complete packing in every residue class attains the upper bound. To lose two blocks, change one class. Choose $a,b,c$ that do not form a block and write $abx,bcy,caz$ for the blocks covering $ab,bc,ca$, respectively. Pair uniqueness makes $x,y,z$ distinct and places them outside $\{a,b,c\}$. Remove the three blocks and insert $abc$. The uncovered pairs form exactly the cycle $a,x,b,y,c,z,a$. This cycle has no triangle, so no block can be added.

Every block through a point uses two of its partners. The complete packing covers all $r-1$ partners at each point, so $r$ is odd and every vertex has even degree in the uncovered graph of any partial packing. One missing block would leave three edges. The degree sum is six and each nonisolated vertex has degree at least two, so there are at most three nonisolated vertices. The three different edges therefore form a triangle that can be added. By the classwise saturation criterion, the global loss in block count is the sum of the local losses, and every positive term is at least two. The full proof is checked by the Lean kernel.
:::

For example, the Fano plane on seven labels has seven blocks. With $d=1$, the Latin squares give $147$ blocks and the nine residue classes contribute up to $63$. The two largest saturated sizes are $210$ and $208$. For the local maximal-packing spectrum, see [Colbourn, Section 2.3.3](https://ajc.maths.uq.edu.au/pdf/4/ocr-ajc-v4-p143.pdf).

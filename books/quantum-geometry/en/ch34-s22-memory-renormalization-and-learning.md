# 34.22 Memory Renormalization and Predictive Distillation: Octave Coarse-Graining, Sufficient Statistics, and Landauer Dissipation {#sec-ch34-memory-renormalization-and-learning}

In [§34.13](ch34-s13-point-volume-octave.md#sec-ch34-point-volume-octave-and-particle-field), Pascal's `1—3—3—1` exterior-algebra palindrome together with idempotent record pinching proposed the cross-scale octave helix whereby a saturated three-dimensional standing-wave volume resets at the next conceptual level into a single-degree-of-freedom point ($\Lambda^3_\ell \equiv \Lambda^0_{\ell+1}$, [@thm-ch34-point-volume-octave-and-particle-field]). In [§34.18](ch34-s18-gram-window-and-consensus.md#sec-ch34-gram-window-and-consensus), we saw conversely that a register-bounded embedded observer (of finite window order $M$) must continually release its oldest historical anchors and pay Landauer erasure heat into the surrounding bath to keep its local Gram window $\mathcal{W}_M$ from freezing in an over-constrained Zeno–Laplace deadlock. Placing those two sections side by side, however, exposes a decisive mechanistic gap at the heart of learning and cognition: **sliding first-in-first-out truncation (FIFO forgetting) reopens the immediate feasibility corridor only by indiscriminately amputating long-range cross-step regularities encoded in earlier anchors, whereas a purely static algebraic identification $\Lambda^3_\ell \equiv \Lambda^0_{\ell+1}$ that leaves the fate of internal micro-arc degrees of freedom unspecified violates the hard ceiling of a finite physical register.**

The bridge uniting these two regimes is **memory renormalization and predictive distillation** by a bounded observer (`MODEL`): confronted with an overflowing finite Gram window $\mathcal{W}_M$, the observer neither memorizes raw microscopic trajectories verbatim as an ever-growing lookup table nor blindly drops the oldest time slice; instead, it folds a closed block of lower-level historical microstates into a single **predictive sufficient-statistic concept anchor** $q_\star(h) \in \Lambda^0_{\ell+1}$ retained inside the window, while tracing out into the thermal bath—and paying the minimal Landauer heat bill for—only the statistically redundant fiber remainder $r_\star(h)$ relative to future observable targets.

## 34.22.1 Two-Stage Fiber Decomposition, Conditional Landauer Dissipation, and the Invariant Unobservable-Kernel Criterion {#sec-ch34-22-1}

To audit the exact ledger of "forming a concept" versus "physically forgetting," we must first separate **reversible logical recoordinatization** from **irreversible physical bath erasure** at both the operator and thermodynamic levels. Let $H$ denote the raw microscopic history space accumulated over a finite historical block (equipped with prior probability mass function $\mu$), let $q : H \to Q$ be a coarse-grained concept readout, and let $Y : H \to A$ denote the future target variable relevant to upcoming interactions.

Algebraically, any concept readout $q$ partitions the raw history space $H$ across its image coordinates $c \in Q$ into pairwise disjoint preimage fibers $q^{-1}(c)$. Choosing local intra-fiber coordinates $r(h)$ along each fiber decomposes a single step of concept distillation into two physically distinct stages (`manual derivation` + `MODEL`):

1. **Stage I: Bijective fiber recoordinatization $h \mapsto \bigl(q(h),\, r(h)\bigr)$ (reversible relabeling, zero heat dissipation $\Delta Q_{\mathrm{relabel}} = 0$)**.
   So long as the intra-fiber residual coordinate $r(h)$ is retained intact, the map $h \leftrightarrow \bigl(q(h), r(h)\bigr)$ is a strict dependent bijection (`concept_fiber_decomposition` supplies the type-theoretic fiber equivalence $X \simeq \Sigma b : B,\, \operatorname{ConceptFiber}(q_C, b)$), while embedding discrete states as computational basis vectors in a Hilbert register $\mathcal{H}_H \cong \bigoplus_{c \in Q}\mathcal{H}_{q^{-1}(c)}$ is a separate finite-dimensional `MODEL` + `manual derivation` realization, factoring directly as a unitary basis change $U_{\mathrm{split}}$ on $\mathcal{H}_Q \otimes \mathcal{H}_R$ when all fibers have equal cardinality $\lvert q^{-1}(c)\rvert = \dim\mathcal{H}_R$ (as in the uniform three-bit parity benchmark below, or after isometric ancilla padding for unequal fibers). Because bijective recoding and unitary rotation (or isometric embedding) are strictly reversible, total system entropy is conserved ($H(Q, R) = H(H)$, $\Delta S_{\mathrm{sys}} = 0$): zero predictive information about $Y$ is lost and zero heat is dissipated into the environment ($\Delta Q_{\mathrm{relabel}} = 0$), at the price that zero physical register bits have yet been freed and the Gram window remains full.
2. **Stage II: Physical bath erasure of the redundant fiber register $(q, r) \mapsto (q, 0_R)$ (slot liberation, conditional-entropy heat $\Delta Q_{\mathrm{bath}} \ge k_B T \ln 2 \cdot H(H \mid Q)$)**.
   To actually liberate register capacity for fresh incoming observations, the observer couples the intra-fiber subregister $R$ to an external thermal reservoir at temperature $T$ (inverse temperature $\beta = (k_B T)^{-1}$), irreversibly resetting $R$ to a standard blank state $0_R$ while retaining only the coarse concept coordinate $q(h) \in Q$. Because $q$ is a deterministic function of $H$, joint entropy satisfies $H(H, Q) = H(H)$, so erasing the fiber register $R$ changes the observer's memory entropy (in $\mathrm{nats}$) by the exact negative conditional Shannon entropy:

   $$
   \Delta S_{\mathrm{sys}} \;=\; S(Q) - S(H) \;=\; -S(H \mid Q) \;=\; -(\ln 2)\,H(H \mid Q) \;\le\; 0,
   $$

   where $H(H \mid Q) := \sum_{c \in Q} \mathbb{P}(Q = c)\,H(H \mid Q = c)$ (in $\mathrm{bits}$) measures the average intra-fiber microscopic redundancy folded inside concept $Q$. Substituting this entropy drop into the exact reservoir-plus-unitary balance law—noting that achieving the conditional-entropy bound below when resetting $R$ utilizes retained $Q$ as side information under degenerate memory levels (`MODEL` + `manual derivation`)—yields the minimal physical heat bill required to erase the redundant fibers:

   $$
   \Delta Q_{\mathrm{bath}} \;\ge\; k_B T \ln 2 \cdot H(H \mid Q).
   $$

Yet not every capacity-respecting coarse-graining $q : H \to Q$ qualifies as a genuine predictive concept: when does fiber erasure leave future predictions unharmed? Suppose system state representations evolve inside a finite-dimensional inner-product space $E$ under a single-step linear transition operator $T : E \to E$, while an instantaneous visible readout map $C : E \to W$ (spanned by a family of visible effect vectors) extracts observable expectations at each future step $k \ge 0$. Two initial history representations $x, x' \in E$ generate identical observable expectation trajectories across **all future steps**, $(C T^k x)_{k \ge 0} = (C T^k x')_{k \ge 0}$, if and only if their difference $x - x'$ lies in the multi-step **unobservable kernel**:

$$
K \;:=\; \bigcap_{k \ge 0} \ker(C T^k).
$$

This subspace $K$ is automatically $T$-invariant ($v \in K \implies \forall k \ge 0,\; C T^k(T v) = C T^{k+1} v = 0 \implies T v \in K$), allowing the quotient map $\pi_K : E \to E/K$ to induce a well-defined, zero-loss coarse-grained dynamics $\overline{T}(x + K) := T x + K$ on the reduced concept space $E/K$. Conversely, if an observer mistakes a **non-$T$-invariant** one-step blind subspace for an erasable fiber, hidden differences inside that subspace rotate into the visible readout sector one step later and trigger irreversible predictive failure. The mathematical spine of bounded memory distillation and concept renormalization in this section rests on six Lean kernel theorem declarations, paired with explicit multi-step kernel and counterexample boundaries:

1. **Concept preimage fiber decomposition ([`concept_fiber_decomposition`](lean:D5/S3/ConceptDynamics/ConceptFiberDecomposition.concept_fiber_decomposition))**: For arbitrary object type $X$, coordinate type $B$, and concept readout $q_C : \operatorname{Concept}(X, B)$, writing $\operatorname{ConceptFiber}(q_C, b) := \Sigma'(x : X),\; q_C(x) = b$ for the dependent preimage fiber over $b \in B$, there is a nonempty type equivalence $\operatorname{Nonempty}\bigl(X \simeq \Sigma b : B,\; \operatorname{ConceptFiber}(q_C, b)\bigr)$. This supplies the exact type-theoretic foundation for Stage-I zero-heat bijective recoordinatization $h \leftrightarrow \bigl(q(h), r(h)\bigr)$.
2. **Monotonicity of conditional logical impurity under factorized refinement ([`refinement_impurity_monotone`](lean:D5/S3/ConceptDynamics/Information/RefinementImpurityMonotonicity.refinement_impurity_monotone))**: For any probability mass function $\mu : \operatorname{PMF} X$, coarse concept $\mathrm{coarse} : \operatorname{Concept}(X, C)$, refined concept $\mathrm{refined} : \operatorname{Concept}(X, D)$, and target concept $\mathrm{target} : \operatorname{Concept}(X, A)$, whenever $\operatorname{Refines}(\mathrm{coarse}, \mathrm{refined})$ holds ($\mathrm{coarse} = \mathrm{factor} \circ \mathrm{refined}$ for some $\mathrm{factor} : D \to C$), conditional logical (Gini) impurity relative to $\mathrm{target}$ never decreases under coarsening (equivalently, never increases under refinement):

   $$
   \operatorname{conditionalLogicalImpurity}(\mu, \mathrm{refined}, \mathrm{target}) \;\le\; \operatorname{conditionalLogicalImpurity}(\mu, \mathrm{coarse}, \mathrm{target}).
   $$

   Arbitrary coarse-graining therefore risks inflating target impurity; distinct from `refinement_impurity_monotone`'s inequality, strict concavity of Gini impurity implies that equality holds with zero excess Gini impurity loss if and only if within every coarse fiber all positive-probability merged subfibers share identical posterior distributions over $\mathrm{target}$ (the predictive sufficient-statistic condition, which also follows from the exact identity below).

   **Quadratic scoring and its scope.** Gneiting–Raftery’s [Example 1](https://sites.stat.washington.edu/raftery/Research/PDF/Gneiting2007jasa.pdf#page=5) (DOI: `10.1198/016214506000001437`) identifies the multicategory quadratic-score divergence as squared Euclidean distance between posterior probability vectors. Bröcker’s [§3, Eq. (15), and Appendix C](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0806.0813v2#page=7) (journal DOI: `10.1002/qj.456`) give an expected-divergence decomposition under forecast sufficiency; §2 explicitly assumes a finite target set. These results explain the connection between no additional quadratic prediction loss and posterior agreement for finite targets, while keeping reliability, sufficiency, and determinism distinct. Steinwart and Ziegel’s [Theorem 1.1, Eq. (4)](https://arxiv.org/html/1712.05279#S1.Thmthm1) (journal DOI `10.1016/j.acha.2019.11.005`) expresses expected score regret for a measurable positive definite kernel as half the squared distance between kernel mean embeddings when $\sqrt{k(x,x)}$ is integrable under both distributions. On a countable discrete target set, the kernel that is 1 for equal targets and 0 otherwise embeds a distribution as its vector of probabilities. The positive-mass support of a PMF has a countable target image, so this kernel can be used on that image. The calculation below gives the corresponding identity for probability mass functions.

   **The exact cost of coarsening ([`conditional_gini_exact_defect`](lean:D5/S3/ConceptDynamics/Information/ConditionalGiniDefect.conditional_gini_exact_defect)).** Let $r:X	o D$ be the refined readout, $f:D	o C$ the coarsening map, and $t:X	o A$ the target. For a single probability mass function on $X$, write $M(d)$ for the mass of refined fiber $d$ and $P_d(a)$ and $Q_c(a)$ for the refined and coarse target posteriors. Each posterior coordinate is the joint mass divided by the corresponding fiber mass, with value $0$ on a zero-mass fiber. If $I(s)$ is the conditional logical impurity of readout $s$, then
   $$
   I(f\circ r)=I(r)+\sum_d\sum_a M(d)\,\operatorname{ofReal}\!\left[
      \bigl(\operatorname{toReal}P_d(a)-\operatorname{toReal}Q_{f(d)}(a)\bigr)^2
   \right].
   $$

   For each target value, its posterior coordinate gives the conditional expectation of its indicator on positive-mass fibers. The Pythagorean identity for the two nested conditional expectations gives the increase in squared loss for that coordinate. When summed over all target values, the conditional squared losses of the indicators recover the original pair-disagreement impurity. Within a positive-mass fiber with posterior coordinates $q_a$, this sum is $\sum_a q_a(1-q_a)=1-\sum_a q_a^2$, the probability that two independent draws from that posterior have different targets. Grouping by refined fiber gives the weighted sum on the right.

   The outer arithmetic uses extended nonnegative reals. Each posterior is converted to a real before subtraction and squaring. Zero-mass fibers contribute zero. For the loss to vanish, the posterior of every positive-mass refined fiber must equal that of its coarse fiber. The coarse posterior is the mass-weighted average of those refined posteriors, so this is equivalent to agreement among all positive-mass refined fibers within each coarse fiber. The result applies to probability mass functions on arbitrary types and to deterministic readouts.

3. **Channel data-processing inequality for support-aware quantum relative entropy ([`extended_quantum_relative_entropy_channel_dpi`](lean:D5/S3/Quantum/Divergence/CanonicalChannelDPI.extended_quantum_relative_entropy_channel_dpi))**: For nonempty finite decidable index types $a, b$, any completely positive trace-preserving quantum channel $\Phi : \operatorname{QuantumChannel}(a, b)$, and density states $\rho, \sigma : \operatorname{DensityState} a$, extended quantum relative entropy obeys $D_{\mathrm{ext}}\bigl(\Phi(\rho) \,\|\, \Phi(\sigma)\bigr) \le D_{\mathrm{ext}}(\rho \,\|\, \sigma)$. Applying `extended_quantum_relative_entropy_channel_dpi` to classical–quantum states ($\rho_{YH}$ against $\rho_Y \otimes \rho_H$) under a target-preserving channel $(\mathrm{id}_Y \otimes \mathcal{E}_M)$ yields predictive mutual information monotonicity $I(Y; \mathcal{E}_M(H)) \le I(Y; H)$ (`manual derivation` application): once Stage-II physical erasure discards a fiber component carrying target correlations, no local algorithm or post-processing channel can ever regenerate the lost predictive information.
4. **Exact Landauer identity from reservoir and unitary balances ([`landauer_identity_from_balances`](lean:D5/S3/DivergenceSupport/LandauerIdentity.landauer_identity_from_balances))**: For real scalars $\beta, Q, \Delta S_{\mathrm{sys}}, \Delta S_{\mathrm{res}}, I_{\mathrm{mut}}, D_{\mathrm{res}} \in \mathbb{R}$, combining the reservoir heat–entropy balance $\beta Q = \Delta S_{\mathrm{res}} + D_{\mathrm{res}}$ with the bipartite unitary entropy balance $I_{\mathrm{mut}} = \Delta S_{\mathrm{sys}} + \Delta S_{\mathrm{res}}$ yields the exact identity

   $$
   \beta Q \;=\; -\Delta S_{\mathrm{sys}} + I_{\mathrm{mut}} + D_{\mathrm{res}}.
   $$

   Pairing non-negativity of residual system–bath mutual information ($I_{\mathrm{mut}} \ge 0$) and reservoir relative entropy ($D_{\mathrm{res}} \ge 0$) with Stage-II fiber-erasure entropy drop $\Delta S_{\mathrm{sys}} = -(\ln 2)\,H(H \mid Q)$ (`manual derivation`) at positive temperature $\beta = (k_B T)^{-1} > 0$ immediately gives $\Delta Q_{\mathrm{bath}} \ge k_B T \ln 2 \cdot H(H \mid Q)$, with equality if and only if post-erasure memory–bath correlation vanishes ($I_{\mathrm{mut}} = 0$) and the bath undergoes zero nonequilibrium lag ($D_{\mathrm{res}} = 0$).
5. **Retrospective zero loss and non-anticipating failure of full-table copying ([`lookup_copy_zero_loss_and_nonanticipating_failure`](lean:D5/S3/ConceptDynamics/DefinitionEscapeAdjudication/RetrospectiveLookupFailure.lookup_copy_zero_loss_and_nonanticipating_failure))**: Over any finite decidable domain $Z$, if $\mathrm{commitment} : \operatorname{CopyCommitment} Z$ incorporates full-table dependence ($\operatorname{IncorporatesTableCopy}(\mathrm{commitment})$, i.e., $Z \subseteq \mathrm{evidenceDependencies}$), then $\operatorname{tableCopy}(\mathrm{comparison})$ achieves zero retrospective loss $\operatorname{retrospectiveLoss}(\mathrm{comparison}, \operatorname{tableCopy}(\mathrm{comparison})) = 0$, yet fails non-anticipation at every point ($\forall z \in Z,\; \neg\operatorname{NonAnticipating}(\mathrm{commitment}, z)$), and satisfies the exact **negated universal implication**:

   $$
   \neg\Bigl(\operatorname{retrospectiveLoss}\bigl(\mathrm{comparison}, \operatorname{tableCopy}(\mathrm{comparison})\bigr) = 0 \;\longrightarrow\; \forall R : (Z \to \mathrm{Answer}) \to \mathbb{N},\; \operatorname{PositiveProspectiveGain}\bigl(R, \operatorname{tableCopy}(\mathrm{comparison})\bigr)\Bigr).
   $$

   Faithfulness to the quantifier prefix is essential: the theorem refutes the universal claim that zero retrospective loss automatically guarantees strictly positive gain $0 < R(\operatorname{tableCopy})$ across **every** prospective reward functional $R$ (witnessed in the kernel proof by the identically zero functional $R_0 = (\_ \mapsto 0)$), rather than asserting that every prospective functional vanishes on $\operatorname{tableCopy}$. Epistemically, smuggling the full comparison answer table into a commitment ([§38.4](ch38-s04-lookup-degradation.md#sec-ch38-pnp-lookup-degradation)) violates non-anticipating causal discipline everywhere, and zero retrospective lookup loss never by itself confers legitimate prospective generalization.
6. **Orthogonal-residual characterization of finite future expectation words and a 2-bit non-invariant countercheck ([`future_word_orthogonal_residual`](lean:D5/S3/Quantum/Algebra/FutureWordOrthogonalResidual.future_word_orthogonal_residual) + `manual derivation`)**: In any finite-dimensional inner-product space $E$ over $\mathbb{K}$, for a visible effect family $\mathrm{effect} : \operatorname{Fin}(m+1) \to E$ and state representation $X : \mathrm{State} \to E$, two states $\rho, \sigma$ share the same finite future expectation word $\operatorname{futureExpectationWord}(\mathrm{effect}, X, \rho) = \operatorname{futureExpectationWord}(\mathrm{effect}, X, \sigma)$ if and only if $X(\rho) - X(\sigma) \in (\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{K}}\operatorname{range}(\mathrm{effect}))^\perp$, and if and only if their orthogonal projections onto the visible effect span coincide. Passing from `future_word_orthogonal_residual` (indexed by finite $\operatorname{Fin}(m+1)$) to the all-time Heisenberg orbit $\{(T^*)^k \mathrm{effect}_j\}_{k \ge 0}$ uses finite-dimensional span stabilization in $\dim E < \infty$ (so a finite horizon spans the entire orbit), identifying their common orthogonal residual with the $T$-invariant unobservable kernel $K = \bigcap_{k \ge 0}\ker(C T^k)$ (Kalman observability decomposition, `manual derivation` + `Literature`).
   **Two-bit non-$T$-invariant countercheck (`manual derivation`)**: Embed two-bit states $(x_1, x_2) \in \{0, 1\}^2$ into $E = \mathbb{R}^2$ under instantaneous readout $C(x_1, x_2) = x_1$ and one-step shift dynamics $T(x_1, x_2) = (x_2, 0)$. At $k = 0$, the instantaneous blind subspace is $N_0 = \ker C = \{(0, x_2)\} = \operatorname{span}\{\mathbf{e}_2\}$; yet $T(0, 1) = (1, 0) \notin N_0$, so $N_0$ **is not $T$-invariant**, and the true multi-step unobservable kernel collapses to $K = \ker C \cap \ker(C T) = \operatorname{span}\{\mathbf{e}_2\} \cap \operatorname{span}\{\mathbf{e}_1\} = \{(0, 0)\}$! Erasing $N_0$ at $k = 0$ by folding $(0, 0)$ and $(0, 1)$ into one symbol incurs zero immediate error at $k = 0$, but suffers an immediate prediction discrepancy $C T(0, 0) = 0 \neq 1 = C T(0, 1)$ at $k = 1$.

**The sharp total variation bound for a finite target set.** [Lean total variation bound](lean:D5/S3/ConceptDynamics/Information/ConditionalGiniTV.weighted_posterior_totalVariation_sq_le_gini_defect) For a PMF, refined readout $r$, and deterministic coarsening $f$, write $M(d)$ for refined fiber mass and $P_d$, $Q_{f(d)}$ for the fine and coarse target posteriors. A posterior coordinate is the joint mass divided by the corresponding fiber mass, with value zero for a zero-mass fiber. Let $I(s)$ denote the conditional logical impurity for readout $s$ and the same target, and let $\Delta=I(f\circ r)-I(r)$ in real coordinates. If the target takes values in a set of size $n\ge 2$, write $T=\sum_d M(d)\operatorname{TV}(P_d,Q_{f(d)})$, with $\operatorname{TV}(u,v)=\tfrac12\sum_a|u(a)-v(a)|$. Then

$$
T^2\le \frac{\lfloor n^2/4\rfloor}{n}\,\Delta.
$$

In a positive-mass refined fiber, the posterior difference $z=P_d-Q_{f(d)}$ has coordinate sum zero. If $S$ is the set of positive coordinates and $j=|S|$, then $\operatorname{TV}=\sum_{a\in S}z(a)$. Subtracting $j/n$ from the indicator of $S$ preserves this inner product and gives the indicator squared norm $j(n-j)/n$. Cauchy–Schwarz bounds the TV of that fiber. Applying the weighted version across fibers and using the exact impurity increase gives the displayed bound. Fibers of zero mass have zero weight.

The coefficient is attained for every $n$. Let $k=\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ and split the target set into blocks of sizes $k$ and $n-k$, assigning mass $1/2$ uniformly within each block. The refined readout reports the block, and the coarse readout merges the blocks. Each refined posterior has TV $1/2$ from the coarse posterior, and $\Delta=n/[4k(n-k)]$. For even $n$ growing without bound, $\Delta=1/n$ tends to zero while $T$ stays at $1/2$. A uniform upper bound vanishing with $\Delta$ requires a bound on the target-set size or additional structure. [Lean attaining construction](lean:D5/S3/ConceptDynamics/Information/ConditionalGiniTV.conditional_gini_tv_attained)

Here $T$ is also the TV between the original joint law $(d,a)\mapsto M(d)P_d(a)$ and the substituted joint law $(d,a)\mapsto M(d)Q_{f(d)}(a)$. Grouping the half sum of absolute differences by refined fiber gives this interpretation. For a fixed probability law and coarsening, this average posterior TV is also the greatest change in the probability of a joint event. Gibbs and Su give the half-$\ell^1$ normalization on [p. 7 of the preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0209021#page=7).

**One recoding law shared by all fibers.** Suppose a finite random choice of target recoding $h$ separates each pair of distinct target values with the same probability $q$. For a fixed readout $s$, the expected impurity of $h\circ t$ is $qI(s)$. In the ordered-pair formula, a same-target pair still contributes zero. Each distinct-target pair contributes its mass times $q$, and the readout fiber mass is independent of $h$. Averaging over $h$ before summing the pair masses gives the equality, including zero-mass fibers. All the impurities lie in $[0,1]$. Apply the equality to both readouts with the same recoding law and subtract to obtain the expected Gini increase $q\Delta$.

**A binary event of prescribed size.** Suppose the target alphabet $A$ has $n\ge2$ values, and fix an integer $1\le m<n$. Let $\Delta_S$ be the Gini increase after replacing the target with $x\mapsto\mathbf1_{\{t(x)\in S\}}$. If $S$ is uniform over the $m$-element subsets of $A$, a distinct pair of target values is separated by $2\binom{n-2}{m-1}$ such subsets. Choose which of the two values belongs to $S$, then choose $m-1$ from the remaining $n-2$. It follows that

$$
\mathbb E_{|S|=m}\Delta_S
 =\frac{2\binom{n-2}{m-1}}{\binom{n}{m}}\Delta
 =\frac{2m(n-m)}{n(n-1)}\Delta.
$$

At least one term in the finite average reaches or exceeds its mean, so one event of size $m$ retains this fraction of the defect. The coefficient is sharp for every such $n,m$. Take the uniform law on $X=A$, let the fine readout and target be the identity, and let the coarse readout be constant. Each fine fiber is a singleton, giving zero fine impurity for both targets. In the coarse fiber, two independent draws give $n^2$ equally likely ordered pairs. The original target separates $n(n-1)$ pairs, and the binary target separates the $2m(n-m)$ pairs that cross the boundary of $S$. Thus $\Delta=(n-1)/n>0$ and every size-$m$ event gives $\Delta_S=2m(n-m)/n^2$. The guarantee concerns a single event chosen for a given law and coarsening and then used in every fiber. For fixed $m$, the guaranteed fraction tends to zero as $n$ grows.

**What do fixed-size binary events preserve?** [Lean weighted-cut variance formula](lean:D5/S3/Combinatorics/Graph/Incidence/FixedSizeCutVariance.fixed_size_cut_variance) Given a readout $s$, sample a fiber by its mass, then make two independent draws within that fiber. Write $Q_s(a,b)$ for the resulting law of the ordered target pair. For each unordered edge $\{a,b\}$ of distinct target values, set
$w_{ab}=2\bigl(Q_{f\circ r}(a,b)-Q_r(a,b)\bigr)$. Exchangeability of the two draws lets us group ordered pairs into unordered edges, giving $\Delta=\sum_{a<b}w_{ab}$. The same grouping gives $\Delta_S=C_w(S)$, where $C_w(S)$ sums the weights of edges with exactly one endpoint in $S$. The weights may be signed.

Suppose $n\ge4$ and $2\le m\le n-2$. Let $\bar w$ be the mean over all unordered edges. Put
$u_a=\sum_{b\ne a}(w_{ab}-\bar w)/(n-2)$ and
$z_{ab}=w_{ab}-\bar w-u_a-u_b$. The residual weights incident to each vertex sum to zero. For a uniform size-$m$ set $S$,

$$
\operatorname{Var}(C_w(S))
=\frac{m(n-m)(n-2m)^2}{n(n-1)(n-2)}
   \sum_{a<b}(u_a+u_b)^2
+\frac{4m(m-1)(n-m)(n-m-1)}{n(n-1)(n-2)(n-3)}
   \sum_{a<b}z_{ab}^2.
$$

The probability that both edges cross the cut depends on whether they coincide, share a vertex, or are disjoint. Counting those cases gives a quadratic form on edge weights in terms of vertex incidence. Once the mean is removed, the degree contribution and the zero-degree residual are orthogonal, giving the displayed sum. If $2m\ne n$, both coefficients are positive, so all events give the same increase exactly when the edge weights are constant. If $2m=n$, the degree term vanishes and a zero residual suffices. When edge weights arise by adding the two endpoint contributions $u_a$ and $u_b$, a balanced cut sees only their total sum. These recovery statements concern the edge weights. Distinct probability laws may give the same weights.

**Which second moments can come from one posterior mixture?** For signal weights $\lambda_s$ and posterior probability vectors $v_s$, the prior and moment satisfy
$p=\sum_s\lambda_s v_s$ and $M=\sum_s\lambda_s v_s v_s^{\mathsf T}$. Hence $M$ is entrywise nonnegative, its row sums equal $p$, and $M-pp^{\mathsf T}$ is positive semidefinite. The centered matrix is the covariance of the posterior vectors around $p$. A finite family of posteriors with this barycenter is realized by giving state $(s,a)$ mass $\lambda_s v_s(a)$ and using $s$ as the readout and $a$ as the target. This is the finite posterior splitting construction used by Kamenica and Gentzkow. [Proposition 1 and Appendix A](https://web.stanford.edu/~gentzkow/research/BayesianPersuasion.pdf#page=6)

These matrix conditions also admit moments that no finite posterior mixture realizes. Take five targets with prior mass $1/5$ each. Let $W$ be the adjacency matrix of the five-cycle and set $M=(5I+3W)/55$. Every row sums to $1/5$. Let $J$ be the all-ones matrix and $H=W+2I-(4/5)J$. Counting two-step walks on the cycle gives $W^2=I+J-W$ and $WJ=JW=2J$, so $M-pp^{\mathsf T}=H^{\mathsf T}H/55$.

Every nonedge entry of $M$ is zero, and each posterior contributes a nonnegative amount to that entry. Thus the support of every positive-weight posterior in a finite mixture realizing $M$ would be a clique. The five-cycle is triangle-free, so the support would be a single vertex or the endpoints of one edge. On such a support, the sum of squared coordinates is at least twice the sum of products across cycle edges. Averaging would force $\operatorname{tr}M\ge2\sum_{\{a,b\}\text{ an edge}}M_{ab}$. The two sides are $25/55$ and $30/55$, which rules out any finite posterior mixture. This example connects the fixed-prior posterior constraint to the classical distinction between completely positive and doubly nonnegative matrices of order five. [Burer, Anstreicher, and Dür](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.laa.2009.05.021) · [Lean refutation](lean:D5/S3/ConceptDynamics/ObservationOrder/PosteriorMomentObstruction.result)

**When does a complex two-sparse decomposition yield a posterior mixture?** ([Lean reconstruction theorem](lean:D5/S3/Quantum/Measurement/FactorWidth/NonnegativeFactorWidthTwo.nonnegative_two_sparse_gram)) Let $X$ be an entrywise nonnegative real $n\times n$ matrix. If its complexification is a finite sum $\sum_t w_t w_t^*$ with at most two nonzero coordinates in each $w_t$, there are at most $n+\binom{n}{2}$ nonnegative real vectors $b_s$, each with at most two nonzero coordinates, such that $X=\sum_s b_s b_s^{\mathsf T}$.

Fix a pair of distinct coordinates $\{i,j\}$. Only complex vectors with exactly that support contribute to $X_{ij}$. Within this group, let $A$ and $B$ be the sums of squared moduli at the two endpoints and let $c=X_{ij}\ge 0$ be the sum of the cross terms. Cauchy–Schwarz gives $c^2\le AB$. If $A>0$, give the new factor coordinates $\sqrt{A}$ and $c/\sqrt{A}$. Their product is $c$, and their squares are $A$ and $c^2/A\le B$. If $A=0$, then $c=0$ and the pair can use a zero vector. The groups are disjoint, so the factors at each coordinate use at most the original diagonal entry. One singleton factor at each coordinate supplies the difference. This construction preserves every entry of $X$ and gives an upper bound on the number of factors. Shaked-Monderer reviews the classical comparison-matrix criterion and the triangle-free case in Section 4. [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2009.12290v2#page=8)

Johnston, Moein, and Plosker use the same support grouping for signed real factors in Proposition 2. [Source](https://njohnston.ca/factor_width_rank.pdf#page=5)

If the entries of $X$ sum to $1$, let $a_s=\sum_i b_s(i)$. For every $a_s>0$, take posterior $v_s=b_s/a_s$ and signal weight $\lambda_s=a_s^2$. Discard zero factors. Summing the matrix equality gives $\sum_s\lambda_s=1$, and summing each row gives $\sum_s\lambda_s v_s(i)=\sum_j X_{ij}$. The same matrix is therefore realized by a finite posterior mixture with prior equal to its row sums, and each posterior has support on at most two targets. The sufficient condition requires the complex two-sparse decomposition.

**The five-target counterexample has factor width exactly three.** ([Lean factor-width theorem](lean:D5/S3/Quantum/Measurement/FactorWidth/FiveCyclePosteriorFactorWidth.cycle_moment_width_exactly_three)) Keep $M=(5I+3W)/55$. Sum the outer products of all five cyclic shifts of $(1,2,1,0,0)$, and do the same for $(2,2,0,-1,0)$. The first sum has entries $6,4,1$ on the diagonal, edges and nonedges, respectively. The second has entries $9,4,-4$. Multiply the sums by $12/1100$ and $3/1100$, then add $I/1100$. The nonedge terms cancel, each edge entry is $60/1100=3/55$, and each diagonal entry is $100/1100=5/55$. This gives a real Gram decomposition with at most three nonzero coordinates per vector. The negative coordinate in the second family permits cancellation on nonedges.

For the outer product of a complex vector with at most two nonzero coordinates, the diagonal sum minus twice the sum of the real parts of the five edge entries is nonnegative. If the support consists of the endpoints of an edge, this difference is the squared modulus of the difference of the two coordinates. Otherwise the edge terms vanish and only squared moduli remain. Summing over the factors gives the required matrix inequality. For $M$ the difference is $25/55-30/55=-1/11$, so the complex factor width is at least three. The construction attains three. The same matrix still fails the posterior realizability test above.

**The nearest realizable posterior moment at a fixed prior.** ([Lean distance theorem](lean:D5/S3/ConceptDynamics/ObservationOrder/PosteriorMomentDistance.projected_moment_unique)) Keep the uniform prior and let $M(t)=(1/5-2t)I+tW$ and $\delta=20t-1$. $$
\frac1{10}-\frac{\delta}{15},\qquad \frac1{20},\qquad \frac{\delta}{30},
$$

respectively. The squared Frobenius distance sums squared differences over all $25$ ordered entries. Its minimum is $\delta^2/24$, which equals $1/2904$ at the original example $t=3/55$. Competitors may have positive nonedge entries.

The five-cycle has clique number two. For any probability vector $x$, the Motzkin–Straus theorem gives $\sum_{i\bmod 5}x_i x_{i+1}\le 1/4$. Averaging over posterior outer products yields $\langle N,X\rangle_F\ge-1/5$, where $N=(4/5)J-2W$ and $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_F$ is the sum of entrywise products. Direct summation gives $\langle N,P(t)\rangle_F=-1/5$, $\|N\|_F^2=24$ and $P(t)-M(t)=(\delta/24)N$. Expanding the squared distance gives

$$
\|X-M(t)\|_F^2=\|X-P(t)\|_F^2+\frac{\delta^2}{24}
+\frac{\delta}{12}\bigl(\langle N,X\rangle_F+1/5\bigr).
$$

The final term is nonnegative. Equality at the lower bound forces $\|X-P(t)\|_F^2=0$, so $X=P(t)$. [Motzkin–Straus, §1, Theorem 1](https://doi.org/10.4153/CJM-1965-053-6)

Set $w=8\delta/3$. Divide mass $1-w$ equally among the five cyclic shifts of $(1/2,1/2,0,0,0)$, and divide mass $w$ equally among the five shifts of $(1/4,1/2,1/4,0,0)$. Each orbit separately has the uniform barycenter. Their average outer products have entries $(1/10,1/20,0)$ and $(3/40,1/20,1/80)$ on the diagonal, edges and nonedges, respectively. Their mixture therefore realizes $P(t)$. The stated interval guarantees $0\le w\le 1$ and includes endpoints with one orbit of zero mass. The interval describes the scope of this construction.

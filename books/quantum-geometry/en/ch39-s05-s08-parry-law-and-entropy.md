# 35.6.8 Parry Stationary Law, Escape-Pair Counting, and the Fractal Horizon Entropy Problem {#sec-ch39-p2e-entropy}

Before discussing entropy, distinguish three levels: finite-state Markov laws, measurable coordinate reconstruction of complete paths on integer time, and entropy comparison over all invariant probability measures. Lean verifies the path law, the metric entropy upper bound and its attainment, and the topological entropy formula. The maximizing law is unique, as the theorem and its derivation below show. Fractal entanglement scaling remains an open problem.

On the Markov-chain side, for each integer order $k \ge 2$, let $p = \operatorname{parryParameter}(k) = \beta_k^{-1}$ be the reciprocal Perron parameter. On the signed run-length state space $\operatorname{State}(k) = \mathrm{Bool} \times \operatorname{Fin} k$ recording an absolute sign bit together with the current run length of `1`s, define the right-eigenvector suffix weights $w_j = \operatorname{suffixWeight}(k, p, j) = \sum_{a=0}^{k-j-1} p^{a+1}$, normalizer $Z_k = \sum_{j=0}^{k-1} p^j w_j$, sign involution $\operatorname{flip}(b, j) = (!b, j)$, and reset transition kernel $P_k = \operatorname{kernel}(k, p)$: from $s = (b, j)$, the chain flips sign and resets to $(!b, 0)$ with probability $p / w_j$, advances to $(b, j + 1)$ with probability $p\, w_{j+1} / w_j$ whenever $j + 1 < k$, and assigns probability $0$ to all other transitions. Define the signed stationary distribution

$$
\pi_k(b, j) = \operatorname{parryLaw}\bigl(k, (b, j)\bigr) = \frac{p^j w_j}{2 Z_k}.
$$

Row-stochasticity, normalization, stationarity, and sign-flip fairness of this signed reset chain are all verified by the Lean kernel:

::: {.theorem #thm-ch39-p2e-parry-escape-laws title="Parry Stochastic Kernel, Stationary Distribution, and Sign-Flip Symmetry on the Signed K-Bonacci Reset Chain" lean="D5/S3/TotalVariation/ParryResetLaw.parry_stationary_law"}
For every natural order $k \ge 2$, set $p = \operatorname{parryParameter}(k) = \beta_k^{-1}$. On $\operatorname{State}(k) = \mathrm{Bool} \times \operatorname{Fin} k$:

1. **Reciprocal root range and unit power sum**: $1/2 < p \le \varphi^{-1}$ and $\sum_{a=1}^k p^a = 1$;
2. **Suffix-weight and normalizer bounds**: $p \le w_j \le 1$ for every $j \in \operatorname{Fin} k$, and $1 \le Z_k$;
3. **Nonnegative row-stochastic kernel**: $P_k(s, t) \ge 0$ for all $s, t \in \operatorname{State}(k)$, and $\sum_t P_k(s, t) = 1$ for every $s$;
4. **Stationarity and sign-flip symmetry**: $\pi_k(s) \ge 0$ for all $s$, $\sum_s \pi_k(s) = 1$, $\sum_s \pi_k(s) P_k(s, t) = \pi_k(t)$ for every target state $t \in \operatorname{State}(k)$, and $\pi_k(\operatorname{flip}(s)) = \pi_k(s)$ for every $s$.
:::

::: proof
Because $\beta_k$ increases strictly from $\beta_2 = \varphi$ toward $2$ on $k \ge 2$, its reciprocal $p = \beta_k^{-1}$ lies in $(1/2, \varphi^{-1}]$, and multiplying $\beta_k^k = \sum_{i=0}^{k-1} \beta_k^i$ by $\beta_k^{-k}$ gives $\sum_{a=1}^k p^a = 1$. Every partial sum $w_j = \sum_{a=1}^{k-j} p^a$ therefore satisfies $p \le w_j \le 1$, terminal value $w_{k-1} = p$, and backward recurrence $w_j = p + p w_{j+1}$ for $j + 1 < k$, making all entries of $P_k$ nonnegative with row sums $(p + p w_{j+1})/w_j = 1$ (and $p/p = 1$ at $j = k - 1$). Weighting by the left/right product $p^j w_j / (2 Z_k)$, total mass flowing into $(b, 0)$ equals $\sum_j \frac{p^j w_j}{2 Z_k}\cdot\frac{p}{w_j} = \frac{p}{2 Z_k}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1} p^j = \frac{w_0}{2 Z_k} = \pi_k(b, 0)$, mass flowing into $(b, j+1)$ equals $\frac{p^j w_j}{2 Z_k}\cdot\frac{p w_{j+1}}{w_j} = \pi_k(b, j+1)$, and $\operatorname{flip}$ complements only the sign bit on which $\pi_k$ does not depend. The full proof is checked by the Lean kernel.
:::

A finite state records the current sign and run length. Reconstructing an entire orbit requires relation bits at every integer time and one absolute sign at the origin. Write $\mathrm{false}=0$, $\mathrm{true}=1$, $\neg$ for Boolean complement, and $\oplus$ for XOR. The actual bilateral path spaces are

$$
\begin{aligned}
X_k&=\operatorname{RelationPath}(k)\\
&=\{r:\mathbb Z\to\mathrm{Bool}\mid
  \forall u\in\mathbb Z,\ \exists i\in\{0,\ldots,k-1\},\ r_{u+i}=\mathrm{false}\},\\
Y_k&=\operatorname{ResetPath}(k)\\
&=\{x:\mathbb Z\to\operatorname{State}(k)\mid
  \forall t\in\mathbb Z,\ P_k(x_t,x_{t+1})>0\}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Here $k\ge2$, $p=\operatorname{parryParameter}(k)$, and $P_k=\operatorname{kernel}(k,p)$ are as above. Equip $\mathrm{Bool}$ and $\operatorname{Fin}k$ with their discrete measurable structures, the function spaces with the product $\sigma$-algebras generated by coordinate cylinders, $X_k$ and $Y_k$ with the inherited subspace structures, and $\mathrm{Bool}\times X_k$ with the product structure. For a state $s=(b,j)$, write $a(s)=b$ and $j(s)=j$, viewing $j\in\operatorname{Fin}k$ as a natural number in subscripts. Given the origin sign $b$ and $r\in X_k$, the source definition `signAt` uses finite XOR in both time directions:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{signAt}(b,r,n)
 &=b\oplus\bigoplus_{i=0}^{n-1}\neg r_i &&(n\in\mathbb N),\\
\operatorname{signAt}(b,r,-n)
 &=b\oplus\bigoplus_{i=0}^{n-1}\neg r_{-i-1} &&(n\in\mathbb N,\ n\ge1).
\end{aligned}
$$

The empty XOR is $\mathrm{false}$. Thus the origin sign is $b$, and $a_{t+1}=a_t\oplus\neg r_t$ holds at every $t\in\mathbb Z$: a true relation bit preserves the sign, and a false bit flips it.

::: {.theorem #thm-ch39-p2e-native-bilateral-reset-coding title="Measurable Reconstruction of Bilateral Reset Paths and the Anchored Skew Shift" lean="D5/S3/TotalVariation/ParryBilateralLaw.bilateral_reset_coding"}
For every natural number $k\ge2$, there exists a measurable equivalence $e:\mathrm{Bool}\times X_k\simeq_{\mathrm m}Y_k$, a bijection for which both directions are measurable, satisfying all four clauses:

1. For every $b\in\mathrm{Bool}$, $r\in X_k$, and $t\in\mathbb Z$, $a(e(b,r)_t)=\operatorname{signAt}(b,r,t)$.
2. For every $z=(b,r)\in\mathrm{Bool}\times X_k$ and $t\in\mathbb Z$, put $j=j(e(z)_t)$. Then $0\le j<k$, and

   $$
   r_{t-j-1}=\mathrm{false},\qquad
   \forall i\in\mathbb N,\ i<j\ \Longrightarrow\ r_{t-i-1}=\mathrm{true}.
   $$

3. For every $x\in Y_k$, the inverse is exactly

   $$
   \begin{aligned}
   e^{-1}(x)&=(a(x_0),\operatorname{readRelation}(x)),\\
   \operatorname{readRelation}(x)_t
     &=\neg\bigl(a(x_t)\oplus a(x_{t+1})\bigr)\quad(t\in\mathbb Z).
   \end{aligned}
   $$

   In particular, the relation path read from $x$ belongs to $X_k$.
4. Set $(\operatorname{relationShift}r)_t=r_{t+1}$. For every $(b,r)\in\mathrm{Bool}\times X_k$ and $t\in\mathbb Z$, the exact anchored skew-shift identity is

   $$
   e\bigl(b\oplus\neg r_0,\operatorname{relationShift}r\bigr)_t
     =e(b,r)_{t+1}.
   $$
:::

::: proof
For every integer $t$, the preceding $k$ bits $r_{t-k},\ldots,r_{t-1}$ contain a false bit. Choose the least natural number $j$ with $r_{t-j-1}=\mathrm{false}$. Then $j<k$ and all closer bits are true, uniquely characterizing the suffix. Advancing time resets the suffix to zero at a false bit and increments it at a true bit. Pair this suffix with the sign obtained by finite XOR in both directions to define $e(b,r)_t$. Since $p>0$ and every $w_j>0$, a positive entry $P_k(s,s')$ corresponds exactly to one of two edges: a sign flip with suffix reset, or an unchanged sign with suffix increment, always below $k$. The constructed path therefore belongs to $Y_k$.

Conversely, every positive-kernel edge of $x\in Y_k$ follows these rules. Tracing backward from suffix $j$ at time $t$, the preceding $j$ edges must be increments with unchanged sign, and the edge before them must be a sign-flipping reset. This gives the nearest-zero characterization in clause 2. For any integer block origin $u$, applying it at $t=u+k$ locates a false relation bit in $u,\ldots,u+k-1$, so `readRelation` belongs to $X_k$. The origin sign and XOR of adjacent signs recover the relation bits. The sign recurrence is unique in both time directions, and the nearest zero uniquely fixes the suffix. Hence clause 3 is a two-sided inverse.

At each fixed time, the suffix reads at most $k$ preceding bits, the sign reads the anchor and finitely many relation bits, and each inverse relation coordinate reads two adjacent states. These coordinate maps are measurable for the stated product and subspace structures. Finally, moving the origin to time $1$ changes the anchor to $b\oplus\neg r_0$. The shifted sign recurrence and nearest-zero characterization are unchanged; their uniqueness gives clause 4. The full proof is checked by the Lean kernel.
:::

This supplies measurable coordinates for every supported bilateral path at every integer origin. Its time domain includes negative times, and its target is precisely $Y_k$. The skew shift updates the anchor: fixing $b=\mathrm{false}$ selects a lift of each relation path, but does not yield a fixed sign page commuting with shift. The coding theorem alone specifies no probability measure on paths; it supplies neither existence of an invariant probability law nor a joint law with a fair independent anchor. The separate law theorem below chooses a coding and measures together, supplying these two probability conclusions. A generating-partition interface for full Kolmogorov–Sinai entropy, entropy comparison over all invariant probability measures, and uniqueness in the maximal-entropy equality case still require separate arguments.

Coordinates describe how to read a path; a probability law must also assign mass to every finite window. Retain $X_k,Y_k,p,w_j,Z_k,\pi_k,P_k$ from above. In the following theorem, abbreviate $S=\operatorname{State}(k)=\mathrm{Bool}\times\operatorname{Fin}k$ and $Z=Z_k=\operatorname{normalizer}(k)$. Let $u=\operatorname{fairAnchor}$ be the fair probability measure on the two signs:

$$
u(\{\mathrm{false}\})=u(\{\mathrm{true}\})=\frac12.
$$

For $j,h\in\operatorname{Fin}k$, write $\bar j=j.\mathrm{val}$ and $\bar h=h.\mathrm{val}$. Define the unsigned suffix mass and transition kernel by

$$
\begin{aligned}
q_j&=\operatorname{suffixLaw}(k,j)=\frac{p^{\bar j}w_j}{Z},\\
Q_{jh}&=\operatorname{suffixKernel}(k,j,h)\\
&=\begin{cases}
p/w_j,&\bar h=0,\\
pw_h/w_j,&\bar h\ne0\ \text{and}\ \bar h=\bar j+1,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\end{aligned}
$$

These indices always belong to the actual type $\operatorname{Fin}k$. When $\bar j=k-1$, there is no available increment state. For a measurable coding satisfying the four properties above, $e:\mathrm{Bool}\times X_k\simeq_{\mathrm m}Y_k$, put $F_e(b,r)=(e(b,r)).\mathrm{val}\in S^{\mathbb Z}$ and define

$$
J_t^e(r)=j\bigl(F_e(\mathrm{false},r)_t\bigr)\in\operatorname{Fin}k.
$$

Thus $J_t^e$ reads the true-bit run immediately before time $t$. The theorem below makes this suffix a function of the entire relation path; choosing $\mathrm{false}$ is only a convention for writing the suffix event. Also write $(\operatorname{shiftPath}x)_t=x_{t+1}$. In the formulas, $\operatorname{ofReal}:\mathbb R\to[0,\infty]$ denotes Lean's `ENNReal.ofReal`: it embeds nonnegative real numbers with their original values and sends negative numbers to $0$.

::: {.theorem #thm-ch39-p2e-native-bilateral-parry-law title="The Actual Bilateral Parry Source, Independent Fair Anchor, and Exact Cylinder Laws" lean="D5/S3/TotalVariation/ParryBilateralLaw.bilateral_parry_law"}
For every natural number $k\ge2$, there exist a single measurable equivalence $e:\mathrm{Bool}\times X_k\simeq_{\mathrm m}Y_k$, a probability measure $\mu$ on $X_k$, and a probability measure $\nu$ on the full integer-indexed state path space $S^{\mathbb Z}$, satisfying all the following clauses together:

1. This $e$ satisfies all four coding properties of [@thm-ch39-p2e-native-bilateral-reset-coding]. Explicitly, for every $b\in\mathrm{Bool}$, $r\in X_k$, and $t\in\mathbb Z$,

   $$
   a(F_e(b,r)_t)=\operatorname{signAt}(b,r,t).
   $$

   Put $j_t=j(F_e(b,r)_t)$. Then $j_t\in\operatorname{Fin}k$, and

   $$
   \begin{aligned}
   r_{t-j_t.\mathrm{val}-1}&=\mathrm{false},\\
   r_{t-i-1}&=\mathrm{true}\quad(i\in\mathbb N,\ i<j_t.\mathrm{val}).
   \end{aligned}
   $$

   For every $x\in Y_k$,

   $$
   \begin{aligned}
   e^{-1}(x)&=(a(x_0),\operatorname{readRelation}(x)),\\
   \operatorname{readRelation}(x)_t
   &=\neg\bigl(a(x_t)\oplus a(x_{t+1})\bigr)\quad(t\in\mathbb Z).
   \end{aligned}
   $$

   For all $b,r$ and every $t\in\mathbb Z$,

   $$
   F_e\bigl(b\oplus\neg r_0,\operatorname{relationShift}r\bigr)_t
   =F_e(b,r)_{t+1}.
   $$

2. The two measures are stationary under their respective shifts:

   $$
   \begin{aligned}
   (\operatorname{relationShift})_*\mu&=\mu,\\
   (\operatorname{shiftPath})_*\nu&=\nu.
   \end{aligned}
   $$

3. The coding sends the product of the fair anchor and relation source exactly to the signed path law:

   $$
   (F_e)_*(u\otimes\mu)=\nu.
   $$

   In particular, the origin sign has law $u$ and is independent of the entire relation path.
4. For every $\ell\in\mathbb Z$, $m\in\mathbb N$, and word $w:\operatorname{Fin}(m+1)\to S$,

   $$
   \begin{aligned}
   &\nu\{x\in S^{\mathbb Z}:\ \forall i\in\operatorname{Fin}(m+1),\ x_{\ell+i.\mathrm{val}}=w_i\}\\
   &\quad=\operatorname{ofReal}\!\left(
   \pi_k(w_0)\prod_{i\in\operatorname{Fin}m}
   P_k\bigl(w_{i.\mathrm{castSucc}},w_{i.\mathrm{succ}}\bigr)\right).
   \end{aligned}
   $$

5. For every $\ell\in\mathbb Z$, $m\in\mathbb N$, and word $v:\operatorname{Fin}(m+1)\to\operatorname{Fin}k$,

   $$
   \begin{aligned}
   &\mu\{r\in X_k:\ \forall i\in\operatorname{Fin}(m+1),\ J^e_{\ell+i.\mathrm{val}}(r)=v_i\}\\
   &\quad=\operatorname{ofReal}(q_{v_0})
   \prod_{i\in\operatorname{Fin}m}
   \operatorname{ofReal}\!\left(Q_{v_{i.\mathrm{castSucc}},v_{i.\mathrm{succ}}}\right).
   \end{aligned}
   $$

Here $i.\mathrm{castSucc},i.\mathrm{succ}\in\operatorname{Fin}(m+1)$ have natural values $i.\mathrm{val}$ and $i.\mathrm{val}+1$, respectively. Clauses 4 and 5 do not require positive transition masses. When $m=0$, the product is empty and equals $1$.
:::

::: proof
First form the finite signed Markov word masses from $\pi_k$ and $P_k$. Unit row sums make the masses consistent when the last coordinate is removed; stationarity of $\pi_k$ makes them consistent when the first is removed. Apply the existing inverse-limit probability extension theorem to centered windows $[-n,n]$, then identify coherent windows with paths on the integers to obtain $\nu$. Cropping a sufficiently large centered window gives clause 4 at every integer origin. Two-point cylinders containing a zero-kernel edge have zero mass. Since there are only countably many integer edges, $\nu$ lies in $Y_k$ almost surely.

Use the measurable inverse coding to pull this supported path law back to a probability measure $\rho$ on $\mathrm{Bool}\times X_k$, and let $\mu$ be its relation-path marginal. The finite signed word masses are invariant under complementing every sign. Under this transformation, the inverse coding complements only the origin sign and preserves every relation bit. Uniqueness of the extension with these prescribed finite-dimensional distributions transfers that symmetry to the path law and to $\rho$. Thus, for every measurable $A\subseteq X_k$ and every $b\in\mathrm{Bool}$,

$$
\rho(\{b\}\times A)=\frac12\mu(A).
$$

The two anchor slices give $\rho=u\otimes\mu$ and hence clause 3. This independence holds for every measurable event of the entire relation path.

The nearest-zero characterization makes the suffix independent of the anchor sign. Summing finite signed word masses over the signs gives initial mass $q_j$ and transition masses $Q_{jh}$, yielding clause 5. The finite-dimensional laws at every integer origin also establish shift invariance of $\nu$. Apply the coding's skew-shift identity and project to the relation coordinate to obtain shift invariance of $\mu$. The full proof is checked by the Lean kernel.
:::

The existential quantifier chooses $e,\mu,\nu$ together; the statement does not take an arbitrary preselected coding as input. The measure $\nu$ is defined on all of $S^{\mathbb Z}$, while the product pushforward identity concentrates it on the positive-kernel paths $Y_k$. The measure $\mu$ is defined on the actual bilateral $1^k$-free relation space $X_k$, and its suffix events use the actual second coordinate of $e(\mathrm{false},r)$. Fairness and independence therefore describe a joint probability law; a fair sign marginal alone cannot replace it.

The cylinder formulas retain their distinct source forms: the signed formula applies $\operatorname{ofReal}$ once to the entire real product, while the unsigned formula applies it to each factor before multiplication. All entries of $\pi_k,P_k,q,Q$ here are nonnegative, so ordinary real probabilities are the initial mass times the successive transition probabilities. At $m=0$ the formulas give the one-coordinate masses $\pi_k(w_0)$ and $q_{v_0}$. Any word containing a zero transition, including words forbidden by the corresponding reset rules, has mass $0$. Every integer $\ell$ is covered, including negative times.

Uniqueness of an extension with prescribed finite-dimensional distributions determines the path law for those distributions. We can now ask how probability correlations between two observation windows decay as the windows move apart along the time axis. The next result places the stationary joint law, mixing, and the obstruction to sign selection under a single set of existential quantifiers. The application in [@rem-ch39-p2e-abramov-literature] separately derives Kolmogorov–Sinai entropy comparison against all invariant competitors and uniqueness of the maximizer. The topological entropy formula for the same paths appears in [@thm-ch39-p2e-parry-topological-entropy].

Retain $X_k,Y_k,S=\operatorname{State}(k)$, $\pi_k$, $F_e$, and the fair anchor measure $u=\operatorname{fairAnchor}$ from above. Throughout this passage, $P_k=\operatorname{kernel}(k,\operatorname{parryParameter}(k))$ is the transition matrix on **signed states**; $Q$ continues to denote the **unsigned suffix** kernel in the preceding law. For a finite word $w:\operatorname{Fin}(m+1)\to S$, write

$$
\begin{aligned}
H_m(w)&=\prod_{i=0}^{m-1}P_k(w_i,w_{i+1}),\\
L_m(w)&=\pi_k(w_0)H_m(w),\\
C_{\ell,m}(w)&=\{x\in S^{\mathbb Z}:\ \forall i\in\operatorname{Fin}(m+1),\ x_{\ell+i.\mathrm{val}}=w_i\}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Empty products equal $1$. For a finite set $A$ of such words, put $E_{\ell,m}(A)=\bigcup_{w\in A}C_{\ell,m}(w)$; this represents any event on that finite window. For the measures chosen by the existential statement below, write $\nu_{\mathbb R}(D)=(\nu(D)).\operatorname{toReal}$ and $\mu_{\mathbb R}(D)=(\mu(D)).\operatorname{toReal}$, Lean's `Measure.real`. The two original cylinder laws retain their respective $\operatorname{ofReal}$ forms. The joint formula, finite-block estimate, and mixing limit for $\nu$ below use real probabilities. Also put

$$
\begin{aligned}
(\sigma x)_t&=x_{t+1},\qquad \tau=\operatorname{relationShift},\\
\rho&=u\otimes\mu,\\
T(b,r)&=(b\oplus\neg r_0,\tau r),\\
\Delta(h)&=\mu_{\mathbb R}\{r\in X_k:\ (h(\tau r)\oplus h(r))\oplus\neg r_0=\mathrm{true}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Both $\sigma^{-n}B$ and $T^{-n}B$ denote preimages under the corresponding $n$-fold iterate.

::: {.theorem #thm-ch39-p2e-native-bilateral-parry-mixing title="Mixing of the Actual Bilateral Parry Source and an Unattained Zero Selector-Defect Infimum" lean="D5/S3/TotalVariation/ParryBilateralMixing.bilateral_parry_mixing"}
For every natural number $k\ge2$, there exist a measurable equivalence $e:\mathrm{Bool}\times X_k\simeq_{\mathrm m}Y_k$, a probability measure $\mu$ on $X_k$, and a probability measure $\nu$ on all of $S^{\mathbb Z}$, satisfying all conclusions of [@thm-ch39-p2e-native-bilateral-parry-law] and all clauses below together. Precisely, the nine retained properties are the four coding properties in that theorem's clause 1 (sign reconstruction, the nearest-zero suffix, the inverse map, and the anchored skew shift), the two stationarity identities in clause 2, the independent fair-anchor product pushforward identity in clause 3, and the signed cylinder and unsigned suffix cylinder laws in clauses 4 and 5. Their original quantifiers and measure-valued forms are retained in full.

1. For every $\ell\in\mathbb Z$, $a,b,g\in\mathbb N$, and arbitrary words $w:\operatorname{Fin}(a+1)\to S$ and $v:\operatorname{Fin}(b+1)\to S$,

   $$
   \begin{aligned}
   &\nu_{\mathbb R}\bigl(C_{\ell,a}(w)\cap C_{\ell+a+g,b}(v)\bigr)\\
   &\quad=L_a(w)(P_k^g)(w_a,v_0)H_b(v).
   \end{aligned}
   $$

   Here $w_a=w_{\operatorname{Fin.last}(a)}$. The words need not be admissible or have positive mass.
2. For every $\ell\in\mathbb Z$, $a,b,g\in\mathbb N$, and arbitrary finite word sets $A\subseteq S^{\operatorname{Fin}(a+1)}$ and $B\subseteq S^{\operatorname{Fin}(b+1)}$,

   $$
   \begin{aligned}
   \Bigl|&\nu_{\mathbb R}\bigl(E_{\ell,a}(A)\cap E_{\ell+a+g,b}(B)\bigr)\\
   &-\nu_{\mathbb R}\bigl(E_{\ell,a}(A)\bigr)\nu_{\mathbb R}\bigl(E_{0,b}(B)\bigr)\Bigr|\\
   &\le\left(\frac34\right)^{\lfloor g/3\rfloor}.
   \end{aligned}
   $$

   The exponent $\lfloor g/3\rfloor$ is the quotient from natural-number division.
3. For every pair of **fixed** measurable sets $A,B\subseteq S^{\mathbb Z}$, the real probabilities satisfy

   $$
   \lim_{n\to\infty}\nu_{\mathbb R}(A\cap\sigma^{-n}B)
   =\nu_{\mathbb R}(A)\nu_{\mathbb R}(B),\qquad n\in\mathbb N.
   $$

4. On the fair anchored extension $(\mathrm{Bool}\times X_k,\rho,T)$, for every pair of fixed measurable sets $A,B$,

   $$
   \lim_{n\to\infty}\rho(A\cap T^{-n}B)=\rho(A)\rho(B),\qquad n\in\mathbb N.
   $$

   This limit is taken in $[0,\infty]$, as in the source statement. The transformation $T$ is ergodic with respect to $\rho$.
5. For **every measurable map** $h:X_k\to\mathrm{Bool}$, the equivariance relation

   $$
   h(\tau r)=h(r)\oplus\neg r_0
   $$

   cannot hold for $\mu$-almost every $r$, and $\Delta(h)>0$.
6. These real defects satisfy

   $$
   \inf\{\Delta(h):\ h:X_k\to\mathrm{Bool},\ h\text{ measurable}\}=0.
   $$

   No measurable $h$ attains this infimum.
:::

::: proof
Obtain $e,\mu,\nu$ and their nine properties together from the preceding law. For two finite words, sum over all intermediate states between the first word's final state $w_a$ and the second word's initial state $v_0$. This gives the matrix entry $(P_k^g)(w_a,v_0)$. The path weights on either side yield clause 1 without dividing by cylinder masses. At $g=0$, handle the shared endpoint separately using the identity matrix. The map from an endpoint state to the entire future word is a nonnegative probability channel with unit row sums. Total-variation data processing, the existing endpoint mixing estimate, and summation over word sets give clause 2.

Finite-coordinate cylinders approximate every measurable event in symmetric-difference measure. Apply the decay estimate to the two fixed approximating windows, then use shift preservation to control the approximation error at every time. This yields the fixed-event limit in clause 3. The coding $F_e$ is a measurable embedding into the supported paths, with $(F_e)_*\rho=\nu$ and $F_e\circ T=\sigma\circ F_e$, so the mixing limit transfers to the fair anchored extension. Applying that limit to an invariant event equates its probability with its square and gives ergodicity.

If a measurable $h$ satisfied the stated equivariance relation almost everywhere, its graph $G_h=\{(b,r):b=h(r)\}$ would be invariant under $T$ modulo null sets. The fair independent anchor gives $\rho(G_h)=1/2$, contradicting ergodicity. If $\Delta(h)=0$, the Boolean identity would force equivariance almost everywhere. Thus every measurable selector has strictly positive defect.

Conversely, a rule depending on finitely many strictly preceding coordinates defines a measurable map on $X_k$. The exact cylinder law identifies its actual defect with the existing finite stationary defect. The established finite-window construction supplies defect upper bounds tending to zero, using polynomial-geometric decay when $0<p<1$. Every defect is nonnegative, and constant rules make the set of values nonempty, so its infimum is zero. Strict positivity for each selector shows that the infimum is not attained. The full proof is checked by the Lean kernel.
:::

Here $g$ counts steps from the first window's endpoint to the second window's starting point. At $g=1$ the blocks are adjacent; at $g=0$ they share time $\ell+a$ rather than forming disjoint windows. Since $P_k^0=I$, nonzero joint mass then requires $w_a=v_0$, and a matching endpoint is counted only once. Forbidden words with zero transitions remain within the formula's quantifiers and have mass zero. No additional assumption is needed for $a=0$, $b=0$, or negative integer origins.

The finite-block estimate writes the second marginal at origin $0$, exactly as the source does. Stationarity equates it with the marginal at $\ell+a+g$. This estimate controls events on the specified finite blocks. Clauses 3 and 4 instead fix any two measurable events before sending the time separation to infinity; they supply no common exponential rate for arbitrary full-path events that change with the separation. The waiting time obtained by finite-window approximation may depend on the events and the desired accuracy.

A selector $h$ here may read the entire bilateral relation path. Strictly positive defect is not restricted to finite-window rules: each measurable selector violates the sign-update relation with positive probability in the comparison of two consecutive times. The zero infimum means that changing the rule can make this probability arbitrarily small, while no single measurable rule makes it zero. A measurable coding with an evolving anchor can therefore coexist with the absence of an almost-everywhere equivariant measurable sign selector.

The existential quantifier chooses one triple $e,\mu,\nu$ carrying all conclusions together. It does not take an arbitrary preselected triple as input or assert uniqueness of an invariant probability measure. This citation now supplies mixing and the selector obstruction. Full shift entropy, maximal-entropy comparison and uniqueness, Abramov's formula, a spectral gap, a computable sampling algorithm, and physical realization remain beyond its conclusions. The boundary-collision probabilities, finite escape-pair proportions, and various entropies below retain their separate definitions.

Alongside this stationary reset law stand two independent formal quantitative bounds governing boundary collisions and observational blind spots: first, the boundary cylinder inequality [`parry_boundary_cylinder_bound`](lean:D5/S3/TotalVariation/ParryBoundaryCylinderBound.parry_boundary_cylinder_bound), which proves for any finite collection $C$ of $n \ge 1$ positive run-length pairs (with each `1`-run $< k$ and encoded length at most $R$) that the left- and right-boundary selected-prefix stationary masses are bounded above by $\operatorname{stationaryWordMass}(10)\cdot(1 - S + M)$ and $\operatorname{stationaryWordMass}(01)\cdot(1 - S + M)$ respectively (an exact probability inequality, not a Hausdorff-dimension or entropy formula); second, continuing the ledger of [Chapter 36](ch38-observer-memory-pointer-and-ontological-qram.md) §36.6, the finite-arena information-escape criterion [`lowersEscape_iff_uniqueCaptureCount_pos`](lean:D5/S3/ConceptDynamics/InformationEscape/ExactRate.lowersEscape_iff_uniqueCaptureCount_pos), which proves on a nondegenerate finite arena of cardinality $q \ge 2$ that the set $E(S)$ of ordered distinct state pairs indistinguishable under catalog $S$ defines a normalized rational escape rate $e(S) = |E(S)|/[q(q-1)]$, and that observation $i$ strictly lowers escape rate if and only if its unique-capture count satisfies $|U_i| > 0$ (with exact drop $|U_i|/[q(q-1)]$). Rational ordered-pair escape rate $e(S)$, von Neumann page entropy $S(\sigma_b)$, and infinite-shift ergodic entropy are distinct mathematical quantities and are never conflated here.

::: {.theorem #thm-ch39-p2e-parry-ks-entropy title="The Parry Law Attains the Bilateral Shift Entropy Bound" lean="D5/S3/TotalVariation/ParryKSEntropy.bilateral_parry_entropy"}
Fix $K\ge2$, set $p=\operatorname{parryParameter}(K)$, and let $T$ be the left shift of the admissible bilateral relation paths $X_K$. There exist a bilateral coding $e$ and an invariant probability $\mu$ on $X_K$ with the stationary suffix cylinder formula of [@thm-ch39-p2e-native-bilateral-parry-law] at every integer origin. With that law,

$$
h_{\mathrm{KS}}(\mu,T)=-\log p,\qquad
h_{\mathrm{KS}}(\rho,T)\le-\log p
\quad\text{for every invariant probability }\rho\text{ on }X_K.
$$
:::

::: proof
For any realized suffix word with $n$ transitions, the logarithm of its Parry mass is $n\log p$ plus terms depending only on its first and last suffixes. Those terms are uniformly bounded on the finite alphabet. Gibbs' inequality bounds each competitor's word entropy by the cross entropy against these reference masses. For the Parry law itself, the same endpoint estimate also bounds word entropy from below. The endpoint terms vanish after division by the word length and passage to the limit.

Suffix partitions at successively earlier starting points have the same entropy rate by stationarity. Their forward translates generate all Boolean coordinates, since the suffix after a bit is zero precisely when that bit is false. This gives the full Kolmogorov–Sinai entropy. The full proof is checked by the Lean kernel.
:::

::: {.theorem #thm-ch39-p2e-parry-ks-maximizer title="The Bilateral Parry Law Is the Unique Entropy Maximizer" lean="D5/S3/TotalVariation/ParryKSMaximizer.bilateral_parry_maximizer"}
Let $K\ge2$ and take a coding $e$ with the nearest-zero suffix property and a probability $\mu$ with the stationary suffix cylinders of [@thm-ch39-p2e-native-bilateral-parry-law] at every integer origin. Set $p=\operatorname{parryParameter}(K)$. For every probability $\rho$ preserved by the left shift $T$ on the admissible unsigned paths,

$$
h_{\mathrm{KS}}(\rho,T)\le -\log p,\qquad
h_{\mathrm{KS}}(\rho,T)=-\log p\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\rho=\mu.
$$
:::

::: proof
At equality the joint law of each finite history and its next suffix agrees with the history marginal followed by the Parry kernel. Stationarity determines the initial suffix marginal because each positive suffix has a unique possible predecessor. These marginal and transition formulas determine all suffix cylinders. A relation bit is false exactly when the next suffix is zero, so equality on those cylinders identifies the entire bilateral relation law. The full proof is checked by the Lean kernel.
:::

::: {.remark #rem-ch39-p2e-abramov-literature title="Maximal-Entropy Application of the Actual Bilateral Parry Law and Separate Literature Boundaries"}
**The application and its objects.** Fix any integer order $K\ge2$ (the preceding $k=K$), and put $p=\operatorname{parryParameter}(K)$, $X_K=\operatorname{RelationPath}(K)$, and $T=\operatorname{relationShift}$, so $(Tr)_t=r_{t+1}$. Take the joint witness $e,\mu,\nu$ from [@thm-ch39-p2e-native-bilateral-parry-law]; throughout, $\mu$ is that same unsigned probability law. For every Borel probability measure $\rho$ on $X_K$ with $T_*\rho=\rho$, the application below gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
h_{\mathrm{KS}}(\rho,T)&\le-\log p,\\
h_{\mathrm{KS}}(\mu,T)&=-\log p,\\
h_{\mathrm{KS}}(\rho,T)=-\log p
&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\rho=\mu,\\
\rho\ne\mu&\quad\Longrightarrow\quad h_{\mathrm{KS}}(\rho,T)<-\log p.
\end{aligned}
$$

Logarithms are natural, and the unit is nats per original step. Here $h_{\mathrm{KS}}$ is the supremum of entropy rates over all finite measurable partitions, initially regarded as an extended real number; the conclusion also proves that it is finite in this system. Competitors range over all invariant Borel probabilities, with no additional Markov, ergodicity, generation, entropy-limit, or uniqueness assumption. The signed extension $\nu$ helps construct $\mu$, while the entropy comparison takes place on the unsigned space $X_K$. The owning exposition is the [bilateral Parry law discussion](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/blob/dev/Blueprint/D5/S3/TotalVariation/ParryBilateralLaw.md). The entropy bound and attainment are formalized in [@thm-ch39-p2e-parry-ks-entropy]. The equality criterion is formalized in [@thm-ch39-p2e-parry-ks-maximizer] and derived below.

**From the actual suffix to a two-sided generator.** Set $J_t(r)=J_t^e(r)$, the second coordinate of $e(\mathrm{false},r)$ at integer time $t$. The nearest-zero characterization makes $J_t$ exactly the length of the true run immediately preceding $t$, independently of the sign anchor. On every admissible path,

$$
\begin{aligned}
J_{t+1}(r)&=\begin{cases}
0,&r_t=\mathrm{false},\\
J_t(r)+1,&r_t=\mathrm{true},
\end{cases}\\
r_t=\mathrm{false}&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad J_{t+1}(r)=0.
\end{aligned}
$$

The true branch automatically has $J_t+1<K$. For every $c\in\mathbb Z$, translation $(\theta_c r)_t=r_{t+c}$ preserves $X_K$ and satisfies $J_t(\theta_c r)=J_{t+c}(r)$. These are pathwise identities, including on paths of probability zero.

Take the finite measurable partition $\mathcal P_j=\{r:J_0(r)=j\}$, $j\in\operatorname{Fin}K$. The measurable inverse of $T=\theta_1$ is $\theta_{-1}$. The space $X_K$ is a closed subset of the bilateral product of discrete Boolean spaces, with its Borel structure, so it is standard Borel. Every suffix cell is measurable. Conversely, the displayed identity recovers every relation-coordinate event from a translated zero-suffix cell. Thus **all integer translates** of $\mathcal P$ generate the entire Borel $\sigma$-algebra. The two-sided finite-generator theorem for invertible probability-preserving systems therefore gives

$$
h_{\mathrm{KS}}(\rho,T)=h_\rho(T,\mathcal P).
$$

The two-sided generator and integer-iterate suppliers are Marcel Morgenstern's [generator theorem](https://github.com/marcmorningstar/lean4-ergodic-theory/blob/040fec2e0ddf4fcba49af9f76c866d295308dd7f/ErgodicTheory/Entropy/GeneratorTheoremTwoSided.lean) and [integer iterates](https://github.com/marcmorningstar/lean4-ergodic-theory/blob/040fec2e0ddf4fcba49af9f76c866d295308dd7f/ErgodicTheory/Krieger/ZIterate.lean); attribution and licensing are recorded in the [entropy supplier note](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/blob/dev/Library/Entropy/morgenstern2026ks.md). These are library sources, cited here without presenting them as newly frozen theorems of this book. A condition using only nonnegative iterates cannot replace this bilateral step.

**Finite histories and the entropy rate.** For $n\ge0$ and $w\in(\operatorname{Fin}K)^n$, write

$$
\begin{aligned}
A_{\ell,n}(w)&=\{r:\ J_{\ell+i}(r)=w_i\ (0\le i<n)\},\\
m_n(w)&=\rho(A_{0,n}(w)),\\
H_n&=-\sum_w m_n(w)\log m_n(w),\\
c_n&=H_\rho(J_n\mid J_0,\ldots,J_{n-1}).
\end{aligned}
$$

The empty history is the one-cell partition, so $H_0=0$ and $c_0=H_\rho(J_0)$. Zero-mass terms use $0\log0=0$. The finite partition chain rule gives $H_{n+1}=H_n+c_n$. Conditioning on more information cannot increase conditional entropy; stationarity of this same joint law then gives $c_{n+1}\le c_n$, without assuming that the competitor is Markov. Stationarity also makes $H_n$ subadditive, and the Fekete entropy-rate limit gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
h_\rho(T,\mathcal P)&=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{H_N}{N},\\
H_N&\le H_m+(N-m)c_m\quad(N\ge m),\\
h_\rho(T,\mathcal P)&\le c_m\quad(m\ge0).
\end{aligned}
$$

The second line bounds each summand in $H_N=H_m+\sum_{i=m}^{N-1}c_i$; the third follows by taking the limit. This connects the entropy rate to every finite history and derives existence of the limit rather than assuming it. The finite chain rule, conditioning, and limit suppliers appear in the library's [partition](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/blob/dev/D5/S3/Entropy/KolmogorovSinai/Partition.lean), [conditioning](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/blob/dev/D5/S3/Entropy/KolmogorovSinai/GeneratorConditioning.lean), and [entropy-rate](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/blob/dev/D5/S3/Entropy/KolmogorovSinai/EntropyRate.lean) modules. Their partition conditional-entropy identities are distinct from the first-return formula stated separately below.

**A KL defect for the same joint distribution.** Keep the original law's $Q=\operatorname{suffixKernel}(K)$ and $q_j=\operatorname{suffixLaw}(K,j)$, and set $R_j=w_j=\operatorname{suffixWeight}(K,p,j)$. The bounds and normalization in [@thm-ch39-p2e-parry-escape-laws] give $p>1/2$, $0<R_j\le1$, $R_0=1$, and nonnegative stochastic rows of $Q$. Exactly on the allowed edges $i\to j$, namely $j=0$ or the available $j=i+1$, we have $Q(i,j)>0$ and

$$
\log Q(i,j)=\log p+\log R_j-\log R_i.
$$

For any history length $n\ge1$, define two arrays on the finite index set $(w,j)$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
a(w,j)&=\rho\bigl(A_{0,n}(w)\cap\{J_n=j\}\bigr),\\
b(w,j)&=m_n(w)Q(w_{n-1},j).
\end{aligned}
$$

Both arrays are nonnegative and sum to $1$. They satisfy $b(w,j)=0\Rightarrow a(w,j)=0$: a zero-mass history has only zero-mass extensions, and a zero kernel entry makes the forbidden extension event itself empty by the suffix recurrence. Absolute continuity therefore covers all histories, including zero-mass histories and forbidden transitions, under every competitor.

The actual joint array has row masses $m_n(w)$ and next-suffix column masses $\rho(J_n=j)$; its last-history-suffix marginal is $\rho(J_{n-1}=i)$. Stationarity equates the last two marginals. Expand logarithms only where $a(w,j)>0$, defining zero terms to contribute zero. Then

$$
\begin{aligned}
D(a\Vert b)
&=\sum_{a(w,j)>0}a(w,j)\log\frac{a(w,j)}{b(w,j)}\\
&=-c_n-\sum_{a(w,j)>0}a(w,j)\log Q(w_{n-1},j)\\
&=-c_n-\log p.
\end{aligned}
$$

The two expected $\log R$ terms cancel exactly because those marginals agree. [Finite Gibbs nonnegativity](lean:D5/S3/Divergence/GrandmotherTheorem.kl_divergence_nonneg) gives $D(a\Vert b)\ge0$, so for every $n\ge1$,

$$
h_{\mathrm{KS}}(\rho,T)\le c_n\le-\log p.
$$

This uses the actual history/next-suffix joint distribution throughout, with no substitution by separately chosen marginals.

**Equality forces the original kernel after every history.** If $h_{\mathrm{KS}}(\rho,T)=-\log p$, the preceding inequalities force $c_n=-\log p$ and hence $D(a\Vert b)=0$ at every history length. [The Gibbs equality criterion](lean:D5/S3/Divergence/GibbsEquality.kl_divergence_eq_zero_iff) gives $a=b$ pointwise. Since $T$ is invertible and preserves $\rho$, every $\theta_\ell$ preserves $\rho$. Translation therefore gives, for all $\ell\in\mathbb Z$, $n\ge1$, words $w$, and destination suffixes $j$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\rho\bigl(A_{\ell,n}(w)\cap\{J_{\ell+n}=j\}\bigr)\\
&\qquad=\rho(A_{\ell,n}(w))Q(w_{n-1},j).
\end{aligned}
$$

Dividing by a positive history mass identifies the conditional next-suffix distribution given that entire finite history as $Q(w_{n-1},\cdot)$. The undivided identity also retains zero histories. No infinite-past conditional-expectation formula is needed or asserted.

**The original $\mu$ attains the bound.** Clause 5 of the original law directly gives, for every $\ell$, $n\ge1$, and word $w$,

$$
\mu(A_{\ell,n}(w))=q_{w_0}\prod_{i=0}^{n-2}Q(w_i,w_{i+1}).
$$

At $n=1$ the product is empty; words with zero transitions remain in the formula. Extending the word by one entry gives the preceding undivided transition identity, with the same initial mass $q$ and without constructing another candidate law. Thus for $\rho=\mu$, every KL defect at $n\ge1$ vanishes and $c_n=-\log p$. Monotonicity also gives $c_0\ge-\log p$, so the chain rule yields $H_N\ge N(-\log p)$. The established Fekete limit and universal upper bound now give $h_{\mathrm{KS}}(\mu,T)=-\log p$.

**Rigidity from stationary marginals to the full law.** Continue to suppose that $\rho$ attains the bound. The length-one transition identity and stationarity imply that $u_j=\rho(J_0=j)$ satisfies $uQ=u$; the original law likewise has $qQ=q$. Uniqueness of this initial marginal need not be assumed. Every row has $Q(i,0)=p/R_i\ge1/2$, so put

$$
\begin{aligned}
d_j&=\tfrac12\mathbf1_{\{j=0\}},\\
U(i,j)&=2\bigl(Q(i,j)-d_j\bigr).
\end{aligned}
$$

The kernel $U$ is nonnegative and stochastic. For any normalized stationary probability rows $u,v$, the common reset mass cancels, and [total-variation contraction under a stochastic channel](lean:D5/S3/TotalVariation/DataProcessing.total_variation_channel_le) gives

$$
\operatorname{TV}(u,v)
=\tfrac12\operatorname{TV}(uU,vU)
\le\tfrac12\operatorname{TV}(u,v).
$$

[Nonnegativity](lean:D5/S3/TotalVariation/Metric.total_variation_nonneg) and [the zero criterion](lean:D5/S3/TotalVariation/Metric.total_variation_eq_zero_iff) imply $u=v$, hence $u=q$. Starting from this common initial mass, induction on word length using the undivided transition identity gives agreement of $\rho$ and $\mu$ on all finite suffix cylinders at every integer origin. The cylinder for a length-zero window is the whole space and has mass $1$ under both laws.

The identity $r_t=\mathrm{false}\Longleftrightarrow J_{t+1}=0$ makes each finite relation-bit window a measurable function of a finite suffix window. Equality of suffix cylinders therefore gives equality of all cylinders of the actual Boolean relation paths. [Bilateral Boolean cylinder uniqueness](lean:D5/S3/Observer/ProbabilisticClosure/TrajectoryLaws/TwelveStateGhost.visible_eq_of_cylinders) identifies the pushforward measures on $\mathrm{Bool}^{\mathbb Z}$; the measurable subtype embedding of $X_K$ then gives $\rho=\mu$. Conversely, equality with $\mu$ gives the attainment already proved. This establishes the equality criterion. In the extended-real order, the universal upper bound together with exclusion of equality gives the strict inequality for each $\rho\ne\mu$. This strictness is pointwise in the measure and supplies no common positive entropy gap for all distinct measures.

**The general Parry theorem.** The application above gives the metric entropy bound, attainment, and uniqueness for this bilateral system. The following references address the general finite-type theorem. M. Pollicott et al.'s survey *The mathematical research of William Parry FRS* ([institutional PDF](https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/id/eprint/682/1/WRAP_pollicott_Parry_research.pdf), §2.3, pp. 323–324; DOI: [10.1017/S0143385708000102](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0143385708000102)) describes the unique intrinsic Markov measure of maximal entropy under a **primitive finite adjacency matrix, that is, irreducibility and aperiodicity**. Aperiodicity alone does not replace irreducibility. This section used that survey; for Parry's 1964 original paper (*Trans. Amer. Math. Soc.* **112**, 55–66; DOI: [10.1090/S0002-9947-1964-0161372-1](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9947-1964-0161372-1)), only bibliographic information was checked, and the original text was not read.

**Abramov's first-return formula: a separate literature result, not formalized in this book.** L. M. Abramov (*Dokl. Akad. Nauk SSSR* **128**, 1959, 647–650) proved that in a probability-preserving ergodic system $(X,\mathcal B,\eta,S)$, for a measurable return set $A\in\mathcal B$ with $\eta(A)>0$, the first-return time $\tau_A(x)=\min\{n\ge1:S^nx\in A\}$ is finite almost everywhere on $A$. With induced map $S_A(x)=S^{\tau_A(x)}x$ and normalized conditional measure $\eta_A(B)=\eta(B\cap A)/\eta(A)$,

$$
h_{\eta_A}(S_A)=\frac{h_\eta(S)}{\eta(A)}.
$$

Both sides must use the same logarithm base. The left side is entropy per return, while $h_\eta(S)$ on the right is entropy per original step. The maximal-entropy application above does not prove this first-return formula or supply an entropy formula for a physical realization, irreversible readout, or finite $p$-adic remainder tree. Applying the literature result to such objects still requires the actual probability-preserving system, return set, and correspondence to be established separately.

::: {.theorem #thm-ch39-p2e-parry-topological-entropy title="Topological Entropy of the Bilateral Parry Shift" lean="D5/S3/TotalVariation/ParryTopologicalEntropy.bilateral_entropy"}
Let $K\ge2$ and equip $\mathrm{Bool}^{\mathbb Z}$ with the product uniformity. Let $X_K$ consist of paths whose every length-$K$ window contains a zero. Write $T$ for the one-step left shift and $\beta_K$ for the order-$K$ d-bonacci Perron root. Use Mathlib's cover entropy on $X_K$. Then

$$
h_{\mathrm{top}}(T,X_K)=\log\beta_K.
$$
:::

::: proof
Appending zeros extends each admissible finite word to a one-sided path. Let $a_n$ count the words of length $n$. The word counts and the powers $\beta_K^n$ obey the same $K$-term recurrence. The initial values give $\beta_K^n\le a_n\le 2^K\beta_K^n$. The limit of $n^{-1}\log a_n$ gives the one-sided topological entropy $\log\beta_K$.

Reading the entire forward tail from any nonpositive integer origin maps the bilateral space onto the same one-sided subshift and intertwines the shifts. Moving the origin to the left includes any prescribed finite set of coordinates. Thus every entourage of the bilateral product uniformity can be controlled by one forward-tail reading, and the two cover entropies agree. The full proof is checked by the Lean kernel.
:::

Complementing bits identifies this model with the finite $S$-gap shift for $S=\{0,\ldots,K-1\}$. The entropy formula then also follows from Ahmadi Dastjerdi and Jangjoo’s entropy function. [Computations on Sofic S-gap Shifts](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12346-013-0096-2)

Dividing by $\log 2$ gives $\log_2\beta_K$ bits per step. Numerical evaluation gives approximately $0.69424$, $0.87915$, and $0.94678$ bits per step at $K=2,3,4$. As $\beta_K\nearrow2$, the value increases to one bit per step.

In the bilateral Parry system above, take the return section to consist of paths that have just read a zero. Every path encounters another zero within $K$ steps. The conditional measure on this section reconstructs the original measure.

::: {.theorem #thm-ch39-p2e-native-parry-first-return title="First Return and Occupation Measure of the Bilateral Parry Source" lean="D5/S3/TotalVariation/ParryFirstReturn.actual_first_return_system"}
Let $K\ge2$. On $X_K$ let $T$ be the left shift and let $J_t$ be the suffix length at time $t$ under the bilateral coding above. Take a coding and probability $\mu$ that satisfy the preceding-zero condition, shift invariance and the suffix-cylinder formula of [@thm-ch39-p2e-native-bilateral-parry-law]. Set $A=\{r:r_{-1}=\mathrm{false}\}$. Then $A=\{J_0=0\}$ and $\mu(A)=1/\operatorname{normalizer}(K)>0$, where $\operatorname{normalizer}(K)=\sum_{j=0}^{K-1}p^jR_j$.

For each $r\in X_K$, let $\tau(r)$ be the least positive integer for which $T^{\tau(r)}r\in A$. It satisfies $1\le\tau(r)\le K$. The map $T_A(r)=T^{\tau(r)}r$ preserves the probability $\mu_A=\mu|_A/\mu(A)$, represented as a measure on $X_K$ supported on $A$. The original probability satisfies the measure identity

$$
\mu=\mu(A)\sum_{i=0}^{K-1}(T^i)_*
  \bigl(\mu_A|_{\{r:\,i<\tau(r)\}}\bigr).
$$
:::

::: proof
The variable $J_0$ counts the consecutive true bits immediately before position $0$, so $J_0=0$ exactly when $r_{-1}=\mathrm{false}$. The one-coordinate suffix cylinder gives the probability of this event. A zero occurs within the next $K$ steps from every position. Partition $A$ by the first-return time. On each part the return map is a fixed iterate of the left shift. To invert it, search the image path backward from index $-2$ and shift the first zero found there to index $-1$. This determines a unique inverse branch, so the images are disjoint and cover $A$. Shift invariance then transfers to the conditional probability.

At level $i$, take the paths in $A$ that have taken $i$ steps without yet returning. Shifting them by $i$ gives exactly the paths with $J_0=i$. Shifting back by $i$ recovers the starting point in $A$, and the preceding-zero condition excludes an intervening return. Thus the mass of every measurable event splits by its value of $J_0$. Multiplying the conditional level contributions by $\mu(A)$ and adding them recovers the original event mass. The full proof is checked by the Lean kernel.
:::

Consider any probability distribution on the same duration alphabet. Dividing its entropy per return by the mean number of original steps puts the entropy in units of nats per step.

::: {.remark #rem-ch39-p2e-parry-duration-entropy-rate title="The Parry Distribution Uniquely Maximizes Entropy per Original Step"}
Let $K\ge2$, $p=\beta_K^{-1}$, and $q_d=p^d$ for $1\le d\le K$. For any probability vector $a=(a_1,\ldots,a_K)$, write $H(a)=-\sum_d a_d\log a_d$ and $\ell(a)=\sum_d d\,a_d$. With natural logarithms and zero contributions at zero probabilities,

$$
D(a\Vert q)=-H(a)-\ell(a)\log p,
\qquad
\frac{H(a)}{\ell(a)}\le-\log p.
$$

Equality holds exactly when $a=q$.

Expand each $a_d\log(a_d/q_d)$ using $\log q_d=d\log p$. At zero probability the contributions vanish directly. The mean duration $\ell(a)$ is at least one. Apply the [finite Gibbs inequality](lean:D5/S3/Divergence/GrandmotherTheorem.kl_divergence_nonneg) and divide by this mean to get the upper bound. The [Gibbs equality criterion](lean:D5/S3/Divergence/GibbsEquality.kl_divergence_eq_zero_iff) gives uniqueness.
:::

::: {.theorem #thm-ch39-p2e-parry-raw-window title="Raw Bit Windows on the Parry Zero Section" lean="D5/S3/TotalVariation/ParryFirstReturn.zero_section_raw_window"}
With the coding and measure of [@thm-ch39-p2e-native-parry-first-return], write $p=\beta_K^{-1}$ and $h_j=\sum_{\ell=1}^{K-j}p^\ell$. Let $v_0,\ldots,v_m\in\{0,\ldots,K-1\}$ start at $v_0=0$ and satisfy $v_{i+1}\in\{0,v_i+1\}$ at every step. Set $w_i=\mathbf{1}_{v_{i+1}\ne 0}$ for $0\le i<m$, with $0$ and $1$ denoting the false and true relation bits. Then

$$
\mu_A\{r:(r_0,\ldots,r_{m-1})=w\}=p^m h_{v_m}.
$$

For $m=0$ the event is the whole path space and $h_0=1$.
:::

::: proof
On $A$, the suffix at position zero is zero. Each successive raw bit either resets the suffix to zero or increments it by one. Starting with $v_0=0$, induction identifies the raw window event on $A$ with the single suffix cylinder $(v_0,\ldots,v_m)$. Dividing its mass by $\mu(A)$ removes the initial suffix mass. The ratios $h_{v_{i+1}}/h_{v_i}$ from the allowed transition probabilities telescope to $h_{v_m}$. Thus an unfinished run at the end of a window retains a suffix weight. The full proof is checked by the Lean kernel.
:::

::: {.theorem #thm-ch39-p2e-parry-zero-marginals title="The Zero-Section Law under the Original Shift" lean="D5/S3/TotalVariation/ParrySectionMarginals.section_zero_marginals"}
Use the native bilateral Parry law and its conditional probability $\mu_A$ from [@thm-ch39-p2e-native-parry-first-return]. Write $p=\beta_K^{-1}$. Then

$$
\mu_A\{r:r_0=0\}=p,\qquad
\mu_A\{r:r_1=0\}=2p^2.
$$

The one-step left shift $T$ does not preserve $\mu_A$.
:::

::: proof
The windows $0$, $00$, and $10$ all end at suffix zero. By [@thm-ch39-p2e-parry-raw-window], their conditional masses are $p$, $p^2$, and $p^2$. The windows $00$ and $10$ disjointly exhaust the event $r_1=0$, which is the $T$-preimage of $r_0=0$. Since $p>1/2$, the preimage has strictly larger mass. The full proof is checked by the Lean kernel.
:::

A return orbit records how many steps it takes to read each successive zero. On the zero section, the joint probability of these durations can be computed directly from a suffix cylinder of the original path.

::: {.theorem #thm-ch39-p2e-parry-return-durations title="Joint Distribution of Return Durations in the Bilateral Parry Source" lean="D5/S3/TotalVariation/ParryFirstReturn.return_time_cylinder_probability"}
Use the same $K$, coding and probability $\mu$, zero section $A$, first-return time $\tau$, return map $T_A$, and conditional probability $\mu_A$ as in [@thm-ch39-p2e-native-parry-first-return]. Set $p=\beta_K^{-1}$. For every finite sequence of durations $d_0,\ldots,d_{m-1}\in\{1,\ldots,K\}$,

$$
\mu_A\bigl(\{r\in A:\tau(T_A^i r)=d_i\ \text{for every }0\le i<m\}\bigr)
=\prod_{i=0}^{m-1}p^{d_i}.
$$

For $m=0$ the event is the entire section and the empty product is $1$.
:::

::: proof
A return of duration $d$ traces the suffix sequence $0,1,\ldots,d-1,0$. Its final zero also starts the next return. Joining successive blocks at their common zeros identifies the duration event with one suffix cylinder on the original path. Each allowed transition contributes a factor $p$ times the ratio of the destination and origin weights. The ratios telescope because the initial and final suffixes are both zero. Conditioning divides out the initial cylinder mass $\mu(A)$ and leaves one factor $p^d$ per return. The full proof is checked by the Lean kernel.
:::

::: {.theorem #thm-ch39-p2e-iid-return-prefix title="Raw Windows under Independent Return Durations" lean="D5/S3/TotalVariation/ParryReturnPrefix.iid_return_prefix"}
For $K\in\mathbb{N}$, let $X_K$ be the space of bilateral bit paths with a zero in every block of $K$ consecutive positions. Let $\eta$ be a measure on $X_K$ and let $q_d\in[0,\infty]$ for $1\le d\le K$. Write $[w]_0$ for the cylinder fixing the finite word $w$ from coordinate $0$ onward. All other coordinates are free subject to admissibility, including coordinate $-1$. Assume that for every finite list of durations $d_i\in\{1,\ldots,K\}$, the cylinder of the concatenated blocks $1^{d_i-1}0$ has mass $\prod_{i<m}q_{d_i}$. This includes the empty list and its cylinder $X_K$, with empty product $1$. For any such list and $j\in\mathbb{N}$, the extended window has mass

$$
\eta\bigl([1^{d_0-1}0\cdots 1^{d_{m-1}-1}0\,1^j]_0\bigr)
=\left(\prod_{i<m}q_{d_i}\right)\left(\sum_{d>j}q_d\right).
$$

The last sum runs over $1\le d\le K$.
:::

::: proof
The complete durations fix the number of raw steps already read. At the next return origin, the first $j$ bits are true exactly when the next duration is strictly greater than $j$. Partition the window by this next duration. The complete-duration cylinders are disjoint and measurable, and their masses factor by hypothesis. Summing over the next durations gives the formula. The full proof is checked by the Lean kernel.
:::

The duration distribution also determines the entropy of the induced system. One step here is one return and may span several steps of the original shift.

::: {.theorem #thm-ch39-p2e-parry-induced-entropy title="Kolmogorov–Sinai Entropy of the Induced Parry Return Map" lean="D5/S3/TotalVariation/ParryInducedEntropy.actual_return_entropy"}
Under the hypotheses of [@thm-ch39-p2e-native-parry-first-return], let $T_A$ be the actual first-return map on the same zero section, with normalized conditional probability $\mu_A$. Put $p=\beta_K^{-1}$. With natural logarithms,

$$
h_{\mu_A}(T_A)
=-\sum_{d=1}^{K}p^d\log(p^d)
=\frac{-\log p}{\mu(A)}.
$$

The entropy is in nats per return.
:::

::: proof
By [@thm-ch39-p2e-parry-return-durations], the joint entropy of the first $n$ durations is $n$ times the entropy of one duration. The duration partition therefore gives a lower bound on the Kolmogorov–Sinai entropy.

For the upper bound, observe the relation bits within radius $m$ at each of the first $n$ return points. A duration $d$ determines the block $1^{d-1}0$. The $m$ bits before the starting point and the first $n+m$ durations determine all these windows. The initial bits contribute at most $m\log 2$ to the entropy bound. Dividing by $n$ and taking the limit bounds the rate for each fixed window by the entropy of one duration. As the radius increases, the windows generate the measurable structure of the full bilateral path. The same upper bound then applies to the supremum over all finite measurable partitions.

Finally, expand the stationary suffix weights. The term $p^d$ appears exactly $d$ times in the normalizing sum, so $\sum_{d=1}^K d\,p^d=\operatorname{normalizer}(K)=1/\mu(A)$. Substitution of $\log(p^d)=d\log p$ gives the second equality. The full proof is checked by the Lean kernel.
:::

**Literature model and boundary conventions.** A concrete physical object to investigate is a nearest-neighbor hopping chain with zero on-site potential. Following Kolehmainen–Lado–Moustaj, *One-dimensional quasicrystals with tensor-network finite-state automata* ([arXiv:2609.06040v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.06040v1), §III, Eqs. (2)–(3), and Appendix B.2), take the substitution $\sigma(A)=AB$, $\sigma(B)=AC$, $\sigma(C)=A$ and its infinite fixed word starting from $A$, $W=\lim_{j\to\infty}\sigma^j(A)$, indexed from $W_0$. Fix $0<\rho<1$ and use $t_C=1$ as the energy unit, setting $t_A=\rho^2$, $t_B=\rho$, so $t_A/t_B=t_B/t_C=\rho$. For every integer $N\ge2$, define on the single-particle site space $\mathbb{C}^N$, with basis $\{\lvert i\rangle:0\le i<N\}$,

$$
h_N=\sum_{i=0}^{N-2}t_{W_i}\bigl(\lvert i\rangle\langle i+1\rvert+\lvert i+1\rangle\langle i\rvert\bigr).
$$

We use $W$'s first $N-1$ letters to label bonds; sites $0$ and $N-1$ are not joined. The paper's digit tensors encode this single-particle site index. Cutting binary or Tribonacci address indices does not specify a real-space many-body bipartition. This also limits the analogy with the direct-encoder entanglement ranks in §35.6.7: it does not identify a uniform superposition of legal words with a Slater ground state of this hopping chain.

**Spectral distance for the specified even open prefixes.** Set

$$
\begin{aligned}
L_j&=\lvert\sigma^j(A)\rvert,\qquad N_j=2\lfloor L_j/2\rfloor,\\
h_{\rho,j}&=h_{N_j}\quad(j\ge1).
\end{aligned}
$$

On these deterministic open prefixes with an even number of sites, define

$$
\delta_{\rho,j}:=\min\{\lvert\lambda\rvert:\lambda\in\operatorname{Spec}(h_{\rho,j})\}.
$$

The standalone statement of [@thm-ch39-p2e-tribonacci-open-chain-zero-gap] gives the full-sequence limit for each fixed $\rho\in(0,1)$. The stronger [@ex-ch39-p2e-tribonacci-prefix-rate] retains the quantitative paper derivation: $\delta_{\rho,j}\le73728/N_j$ for every $j\ge4$ and $0<\rho<1$, yielding convergence uniform in $\rho$. The complete quantitative application remains a paper proof, without a separate Lean verification badge or a claim to resolve a new external conjecture. Finite invertibility, membership of zero in an infinite-volume spectrum, finite-resolution density of states, or criticality in the sense of Anderson localization cannot directly replace the limit proof for these specified boundaries and prefix sequence. **Ordered Slater amplitudes and the literal prefix partial trace.** The following finite-dimensional application is independent of the hopping parameter. It allows any natural number $N$, $0\le k\le N$, and complex matrix $V\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times k}$, requiring only $V^\dagger V=I_k$. Keep the original sites in their natural order $0,\ldots,N-1$, and label the orthonormal Boolean occupation basis $\lvert S\rangle$ by its occupied subsets $S$. For $|S|=k$, let $V_S$ retain those rows in increasing site order, with the column order unchanged. Define the same actual many-body vector by

$$
\langle S\mid\Psi_V\rangle=
\begin{cases}
\det(V_S),&\lvert S\rvert=k,\\
0,&\lvert S\rvert\ne k.
\end{cases}
$$

The empty determinant is $1$, so $k=0$ gives the vacuum. The occupation basis is already normalized; no additional factor $1/\sqrt{k!}$ multiplies the amplitudes. Applying the [rectangular Cauchy–Binet identity](lean:D5/S3/Quantum/Schatten/CompoundMatrices.cauchyBinet) to $V^\dagger,V$ in natural site order gives

$$
\sum_{|S|=k}\overline{\det(V_S)}\det(V_S)
=\det(V^\dagger V)=1.
$$

Normalization thus follows from column orthonormality. Dereziński, [*Clifford algebras and fermions*](https://www.fuw.edu.pl/~derezins/clifford.pdf), §7.1–§7.2, Eqs. (7.51)–(7.54), provides the literature background for the CAR, ordered occupation basis, and normalized Slater determinant. To use this vector as a ground state of the hopping chain above, the occupied modes, filling, and many-body Hamiltonian still need to be specified and energy minimization verified.

Specify $0\le\ell\le N$ and the contiguous prefix $I=\{0,\ldots,\ell-1\}$. Let $a,a'\in\{0,1\}^{\ell}$ and $b\in\{0,1\}^{N-\ell}$; $ab$ concatenates the prefix and tail in the original site order. Define

$$
\begin{aligned}
\psi_V(a,b)&:=\langle ab\mid\Psi_V\rangle,\\
M_V((a,b),(a',b'))&:=\psi_V(a,b)\overline{\psi_V(a',b')},\\
R_V(a,a')&:=\sum_{b\in\{0,1\}^{N-\ell}}
\psi_V(a,b)\overline{\psi_V(a',b)}.
\end{aligned}
$$

This is the ordinary partial trace $R_V=\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathrm{tail}}M_V$ in the ordered site tensor product, retaining all $2^\ell$ prefix occupation strings. The rank-one matrix $M_V$ is positive semidefinite, so [positivity under partial trace](lean:D5/S3/Quantum/Information/PartialTraceMutualInformation.partialTraceRight_posSemidef) gives $R_V\ge0$. Reindexing its diagonal sum by the concatenation bijection and using the normalization above gives $\Tr R_V=1$. Below, $\rho_I$ is the density state whose matrix is this $R_V$.

**Unitary correspondence in the original basis and the complete spectrum.** Write $X=V_I$ for the first $\ell$ rows and $Y$ for the remaining $N-\ell$ rows, and set $A=XX^\dagger$. The full-space projection $P=VV^\dagger$ and $I_N-P$ are both positive semidefinite; compression gives $0\le A\le I_\ell$. Choose an orthonormal eigenvector matrix $Q$ for $A$ and retain all $\ell$ eigenvalues $\nu_i$. Then

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q^\dagger Q&=QQ^\dagger=I_\ell,\\
Q^\dagger A Q&=\operatorname{diag}(\nu_1,\ldots,\nu_\ell),
\qquad 0\le\nu_i\le1,\\
\widetilde V&=(Q^\dagger\oplus I_{N-\ell})V.
\end{aligned}
$$

The prefix rows of $\widetilde V$ are $\widetilde X=Q^\dagger X$, its tail remains $Y$, and $\widetilde V^\dagger\widetilde V=I_k$. Define $\Gamma_m(B)$ on all $m$-site occupation strings: when the occupied subsets of its two arguments have equal size, its entry is the minor of $B$ with both rows and columns in natural increasing order; otherwise it is zero. The vacuum entry is the empty determinant $1$. Applying Cauchy–Binet in each particle-number sector gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Gamma_m(BC)&=\Gamma_m(B)\Gamma_m(C),\\
\Gamma_m(B^\dagger)&=\Gamma_m(B)^\dagger,\qquad
\Gamma_m(I_m)=I_{2^m}.
\end{aligned}
$$

A single-particle unitary therefore lifts to a unitary on the entire Boolean occupation space. Natural order is used throughout. If one instead uses a compound matrix with a fixed classical order, the permutation sign for each of its left and right occupied subsets must be included; the order difference cannot simply be ignored.

Every prefix site precedes every tail site, so the block minors introduce no additional exchange sign, and

$$
\Gamma_N(B\oplus I_{N-\ell})(ab,a'b')
=\Gamma_\ell(B)(a,a')\,\delta_{bb'}.
$$

Apply this identity to the same determinant vector, then use the [local conjugation and partial-trace identity](lean:D5/S3/Quantum/Dynamics/KickedIsingNegativityRefutation.trace_local_right) with the identity as its tail matrix. This gives the relation between the actual marginals

$$
\begin{aligned}
G&=\Gamma_\ell(Q^\dagger),\qquad G^\dagger G=GG^\dagger=I_{2^\ell},\\
R_{\widetilde V}&=G R_V G^\dagger.
\end{aligned}
$$

The transformation law follows from the amplitudes and partial trace, without assuming a correlation transformation law or Gaussianity.

The matrix $R_{\widetilde V}$ itself must still be computed. For equal-size occupied prefix subsets $S,T$, put $r=|S|=|T|$, and let $\widetilde X_S,\widetilde X_T$ retain their increasing rows. Stack these rows above $Y$, assigning polynomial weight $t$ to each prefix row and weight $1$ to each tail row. The coefficient of $t^r$ in Cauchy–Binet selects exactly the minors containing every specified prefix row. Thus

$$
R_{\widetilde V}(S,T)
=[t^r]\det\bigl(Y^\dagger Y+t\widetilde X_T^\dagger\widetilde X_S\bigr).
$$

Here $[t^r]$ extracts a coefficient. Let $E_{T,S}$ send the $q$th increasing coordinate of $S$ to the $q$th increasing coordinate of $T$, with all other entries zero. Using $Y^\dagger Y=I_k-\widetilde X^\dagger\widetilde X$ and $\det(I+BC)=\det(I+CB)$, the determinant above equals

$$
\det\bigl(I_\ell-D+tE_{T,S}D\bigr),
\qquad D=\operatorname{diag}(\nu_1,\ldots,\nu_\ell).
$$

Block the matrix by $T$ and its complement. Its coefficient of $t^r$ is the principal minor of $E_{T,S}D$ on $T$, multiplied by $\prod_{i\notin T}(1-\nu_i)$. If $S=T$, that minor is $\prod_{i\in S}\nu_i$; if $S\ne T$, a coordinate in $T\setminus S$ supplies a zero column, so the coefficient vanishes. If $|S|\ne|T|$, the same tail subset cannot make both total particle counts equal $k$, and every summand of the original partial trace is already zero. This computes every entry:

$$
\begin{aligned}
b_i(0)&=1-\nu_i,\qquad b_i(1)=\nu_i,\\
\lambda_a&=\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}b_i(a_i),\qquad a\in\{0,1\}^{\ell},\\
R_{\widetilde V}&=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_a),\\
U&=G^\dagger,\qquad
R_V=U\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_a)U^\dagger.
\end{aligned}
$$

The last line returns to the same $R_V$ in the original site basis. The [spectral permutation theorem for unitary similarity of Hermitian matrices](lean:D5/S3/Quantum/Schatten/MatrixSimilarity.IsHermitian.eigenvalues_eq_of_unitary_similarity_diagonal) therefore identifies all its eigenvalues with $\lambda_a$, indexed by a permutation of the Boolean occupation strings. The multiset has exactly $2^\ell$ entries, retaining repeated values, zero eigenvalues, and impossible occupation sectors with their multiplicities. A completely empty or filled mode still contributes the weight pair $1,0$; selecting the two factors explicitly avoids $0^0$. For $\ell=0$ the empty product is $1$, the sole density eigenvalue.

The $k\times k$ occupied-mode Gram matrix $X^\dagger X$ and $A$ share only their nonzero spectrum; their dimensions can differ. The complete spatial spectrum requires all $\ell$ spatial modes: retain the multiplicities of the nonzero compressed eigenvalues and pad with zeros to $\ell$ entries. The particle-mode count $k$ cannot replace the spatial-mode count. The derivation also covers $N=0$, the vacuum $k=0$, full filling $k=N$, and the full prefix $\ell=N$. The full prefix is still pure, with one density eigenvalue $1$ and all others zero.

**Entropy of the actual density state and correspondence with the literature.** For this $\rho_I$ constructed by partial trace, the [spectral entropy identity](lean:D5/S3/Quantum/Information/PartialTraceMutualInformation.entropy_eq_spectral) gives its von Neumann entropy. Extend $x\log x$ continuously to zero at the origin. Then

$$
\begin{aligned}
S(\rho_I)&=-\Tr(\rho_I\log\rho_I)
=-\sum_{a\in\{0,1\}^{\ell}}\lambda_a\log\lambda_a\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}h(\nu_i),\\
h(x)&=-x\log x-(1-x)\log(1-x).
\end{aligned}
$$

The density matrix used here is $R_V$ in the original basis, rather than a separately chosen realization of the spectrum. The product weights satisfy $\sum_a\lambda_a=\prod_i((1-\nu_i)+\nu_i)=1$. Split the sum by one Boolean coordinate and use $-(xy)\log(xy)=y(-x\log x)+x(-y\log y)$; iterating gives the binary-entropy sum. This identity and the zero convention retain singular density states, so $h(0)=h(1)=0$. Logarithms are natural; entropy in bits requires division by $\log2$. This is an application derived from the original finite-matrix results, with no separate full Slater theorem or independent verification badge.

Peschel, [*Calculation of reduced density matrices from correlation functions*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/cond-mat/0212631v1), Eqs. (8)–(11), gives the mode description of the reduced free-fermion density matrix. Klich, [*Lower entropy bounds and particle number fluctuations in a Fermi sea*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/0406068v2), Eq. (4), Eq. (7) and its subsequent factors $\operatorname{diag}(1-d_i,d_i)$, and Eq. (11), supplies the corresponding projected-mode decomposition, product spectrum, and entropy formula. Here $d_i=\nu_i$, and $\ell$ counts sites in the spatial region. Klich's Eq. (7) uses an appropriate fermionic tensor product; the prefix derivation above additionally makes the correspondence with the literal tensor partial trace in natural site order explicit.

**Two-point functions of the same Slater state.** Keep the actual vector above, write $\psi_V(x)=\langle x\mid\Psi_V\rangle$ and $\rho_V=\lvert\Psi_V\rangle\langle\Psi_V\rvert$, and use the original site order's [`JordanWigner.fullC`](lean:D5/S3/Quantum/SpinChains/SupersymmetricFermion/JordanWigner.fullC) as $c_i$. Its [occupation-basis entry](lean:D5/S3/Quantum/SpinChains/SupersymmetricFermion/HardCoreCompression.fullC_entry) is nonzero exactly when the input occupies $i$ and the output empties that site, with coefficient $(-1)^{n_S(i)}$, where $n_S(i)=|\{a\in S:a<i\}|$. These original operators and determinant amplitudes directly give

$$
\begin{aligned}
K_{ij}&=\Tr\bigl((\rho_V c_j^\dagger)c_i\bigr)=P_{ij},\\
C_{ij}&=\Tr\bigl((\rho_V c_i^\dagger)c_j\bigr)
=P_{ji}=\overline{P_{ij}},\\
C&=P^{\mathsf T}.
\end{aligned}
$$

To track the exchange signs, let $E_{j,i}$ have its sole nonzero entry $1$ in row $j$, column $i$. Over the complex polynomial ring, set

$$
\begin{aligned}
W(X)&=(I_N+XE_{j,i})V,\\
W(X)_{j,b}&=V_{j,b}+XV_{i,b}.
\end{aligned}
$$

All other rows stay fixed. Define $\psi_{W(X)}(x)$ by the same increasing-row minor rule, without requiring column orthonormality of $W(X)$. For every occupation word,

$$
[X]\psi_{W(X)}(x)
=\bigl(c_j^\dagger c_i\Psi_V\bigr)(x).
$$

Here $[X]$ extracts the linear coefficient. Words of the wrong particle number give zero on both sides because the operator preserves particle number. Now suppose $|S(x)|=k$. If $j\notin S(x)$, the selected minor contains no changing row, and the creation action also vanishes at that output. If $j\in S(x)$, put $R=S(x)\setminus\{j\}$. When $i\in R$, the replacement produces a repeated row, so both the determinant and annihilation action vanish. Otherwise, returning the replacement row to increasing order gives, on both sides,

$$
(-1)^{n_R(j)+n_R(i)}\det V_{R\cup\{i\}}.
$$

The two counts record the row exchanges for removing $j$ and inserting $i$, respectively; this includes $i=j$. The original suppliers for this sign correspondence are the private `fullC_sparse` and `fullC_create_sparse` in `D5/S3/Quantum/Fermionic/PeriodicSshExcessPseudoEntropyRefutation.lean`, and the private `sorted_position_count` and `sorted_removeNth` in `D5/S3/Quantum/FermionicCorrelationalBound/PhysicalContractionRdmBridge.lean`, accessible from the [pinned source root](trureturing:). The first pair gives the sparse actions above. The second identifies a row's position in increasing order with the number of smaller elements and preserves increasing order after removal. Its statements use natural-number indices; sending finite site indices to their natural-number values preserves order and gives the row permutation signs used here. These are helpers in the original source, without separate public declaration links.

Now apply the same [Cauchy–Binet identity](lean:D5/S3/Quantum/Schatten/CompoundMatrices.cauchyBinet) to $V^\dagger,W(X)$, this time over $\mathbb C[X]$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
F(X)&:=\det\bigl(V^\dagger W(X)\bigr)\\
&=\sum_{x\in\{0,1\}^N}
\overline{\psi_V(x)}\,\psi_{W(X)}(x)\\
&=\det\bigl(I_k+XV^\dagger E_{j,i}V\bigr).
\end{aligned}
$$

The last line uses only $V^\dagger V=I_k$. In the determinant expansion, linear terms select exactly one diagonal position, so $[X]\det(I+XB)=\Tr B$. Extracting the coefficient on the sum side and expanding the trace of the rank-one density matrix gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
[X]F(X)
&=\sum_x\overline{\psi_V(x)}
\bigl(c_j^\dagger c_i\Psi_V\bigr)(x)\\
&=\Tr\bigl((\rho_V c_j^\dagger)c_i\bigr),\\
[X]F(X)
&=\Tr\bigl(V^\dagger E_{j,i}V\bigr)\\
&=\Tr\bigl(E_{j,i}VV^\dagger\bigr)=P_{ij}.
\end{aligned}
$$

This proves $K_{ij}=P_{ij}$ for the same actual state. Exchanging $i,j$ and using $P^\dagger=P$ gives the formula for $C$. No covariance, Gaussianity, or normalization is assumed, and no other density matrix replaces $\rho_V$. Complex phases matter: for $N=2,k=1$ and $V=(3/5,4\mathrm{i}/5)^{\mathsf T}$, $K_{01}=-12\mathrm{i}/25$ whereas $C_{01}=12\mathrm{i}/25$. These finite sums also cover the vacuum ($K=0$), full filling ($K=I_N$), equal sites, and both orders of distinct sites. For $N=0$ there are no site indices $i,j$ to choose. This is a standard application of the original results, without a separate full endpoint theorem or independent verification badge.

Dereziński's [§7.1–§7.2, Eqs. (7.51)–(7.54)](https://www.fuw.edu.pl/~derezins/clifford.pdf) gives the occupation-basis and Slater conventions. His Theorem 8.1, particularly $\omega_\pi(d\Gamma(h))=\Tr(\pi h)$ in Eq. (8.103), gives the corresponding one-body expectation formula. Peschel's Eqs. (7), (9)–(10) use $C$; the transpose in his Eq. (12) cannot be omitted for complex modes. Peschel also notes that, for a non-Slater state, the free-fermion density matrix reconstructed from the same two-point function generally fails to reproduce other expectation values. Correlation agreement therefore does not determine a general density matrix. More general gauge-invariant Gaussian density matrices have the same spectral recipe, but require a separate Gaussianity hypothesis; their scope differs from the derivation starting with the specified determinant amplitudes. When $0$ or $1$ occurs, Peschel's matrix-logarithm formula (12) cannot be applied directly. Arbitrary nonprefix site subsets also involve Jordan–Wigner strings and a choice of fermionic subsystem, whose correspondence requires a separate argument. **A calculation for disjoint sites.** On four sites ordered $0,1,2,3$, take the orthonormal orbitals $u=(e_0+e_2)/\sqrt{2}$ and $v=(e_1+e_3)/\sqrt{2}$ and keep $I=\{0,2\}$. The two-row determinants in increasing site order give

$$
\lvert\Psi\rangle
=\tfrac12\bigl(\lvert1100\rangle+\lvert1001\rangle
-\lvert0110\rangle+\lvert0011\rangle\bigr).
$$

For the ordinary tensor partial trace, group the amplitudes by the occupation string on $(1,3)$. When those sites have string $10$, the retained amplitude vector is $(\lvert10\rangle-\lvert01\rangle)/2$. For string $01$, it is $(\lvert10\rangle+\lvert01\rangle)/2$. The two vectors are orthogonal and each has squared norm $1/2$, so the reduced density matrix has spectrum $(1/2,1/2,0,0)$ and entropy $\log 2$.

The one-particle compression $P_I=u_Iu_I^\dagger$ of the same state has eigenvalues $1,0$. The fermionic mode product formula gives spectrum $(1,0,0,0)$ and zero entropy. Replacing the second orbital with $e_3$ gives exactly the same $P_I$, while the ordinary partial trace now has only the component with discarded string $01$, leaving a pure state on the retained sites. Thus $P_I$ alone cannot determine the ordinary tensor partial-trace spectrum for this disjoint cut. For occupied sites $1,2$, the row order puts the component of $v$ first and the component of $u$ second, giving the negative determinant. For the role of the Jordan–Wigner string between the two blocks in the reduced state, see Fagotti and Calabrese, [*Entanglement entropy of two disjoint blocks in XY chains*, §2, Eqs. (11)–(19)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1003.1110).

**When does the product spectrum hold for every state?** On a finite ordered set of sites, let $I$ be the retained side and $J$ the side traced out. Require the ordinary tensor partial trace to have the full fermionic mode product spectrum above for every particle number $0\le k\le N$ and every Slater state with that particle number. This holds exactly when $I$ or $J$ is an interval in the original site order, with the empty set counted as an interval. Equivalently, membership switches sides at most twice as we read the sites in order. With at least three switches, taking one site from each of the first four runs gives an increasing $I,J,I,J$ or $J,I,J,I$.

Write $a\subseteq I$ and $b\subseteq J$ for the occupied sites on the two sides. The amplitudes are supported on $|a|+|b|=k$. Starting with the occupied determinant rows in the original site order, move the rows for $a$ ahead of the rows for $b$, preserving the order within each group. Each $j\in b$ to the left of $i\in a$ crosses $i$ exactly once, so the two amplitudes differ by $(-1)^{c(a,b)}$, where

$$
c(a,b)=\bigl|\{(i,j)\in a\times b\mid j<i\}\bigr|.
$$

The grouped amplitudes are precisely the ordered-prefix amplitudes in the site order that lists all of $I$ before $J$. In that order the wedge product of occupation basis vectors puts the retained vectors first and the traced vectors second, so the coefficients in the fermionic tensor product are exactly the grouped determinants. This reorders only the rows of the orbital matrix and gives the same $P_I=V_I V_I^\dagger$ in the original order within $I$. Taking the partial trace of these grouped coefficients gives the spectrum in the fermionic mode product formula above.

If $I$ is a nonempty interval, let $b_{\mathrm{left}}$ consist of the sites of $b$ to its left. Each of those sites crosses every site in $a$, giving $c(a,b)=(k-|b|)|b_{\mathrm{left}}|$. The sign depends only on $b$ and cancels with its conjugate in the trace over $b$. If $J$ is a nonempty interval, let $a_{\mathrm{right}}$ consist of the sites of $a$ to its right. Then $c(a,b)=(k-|a|)|a_{\mathrm{right}}|$. The sign depends only on $a$, so the retained density matrices in the two orderings are related by conjugation with a diagonal unitary. Both cases preserve every eigenvalue with its multiplicity. An empty side gives $c(a,b)=0$.

If four alternating sites exist, put the two orbitals from the example above on those four sites and leave every other site empty. Reversing which side starts the alternation exchanges the retained and traced sides. The two reductions of the four-site pure state have the same nonzero eigenvalues by the Schmidt decomposition. In either orientation the ordinary partial trace has exactly two nonzero eigenvalues, both $1/2$. Meanwhile $P_I$ is the rank-one projection onto the orbital supported entirely on the retained side, so the product spectrum has just one nonzero eigenvalue, $1$. Extra empty sites only add zeros. This gives a two-particle counterexample for every cut containing four alternating sites.

**The criterion at a given filling.** For a given particle number $k$, the product formula holds for every Slater state at every cut if $k\le 1$ or $N-k\le 1$. For $2\le k\le N-2$, it holds for every Slater state at a cut exactly when $I$ or $J$ is an interval. [Fixed-filling criterion](lean:D5/S3/Quantum/Fermionic/OrderedSlaterFixedFilling.fixed_filling_spectral_criteria)

Write the holes as $a^{\mathrm h}=I\setminus a$ and $b^{\mathrm h}=J\setminus b$. Partition $I\times J$ according to whether each endpoint is occupied or empty, and count the pairs with $j<i$. This gives

$$
c(a,b)+c(I,J)=c(a,J)+c(I,b)+c(a^{\mathrm h},b^{\mathrm h}).
$$

The reordering sign of an occupation string and that of the corresponding hole string therefore differ by the separate factors $(-1)^{c(a,J)}$ and $(-1)^{c(I,b)+c(I,J)}$. The first gives diagonal unitary conjugation on the retained side, and the second cancels in the trace over $b$. This accounts for the two filling limits. When $k\le1$, every nonzero amplitude has $c(a,b)=0$. When $N-k\le1$, at least one side has no holes, so the hole term $c(a^{\mathrm h},b^{\mathrm h})$ vanishes.

For $2\le k\le N-2$, choose $k-2$ sites outside the four alternating sites and call their set $T$. In increasing site order, append the corresponding unit vectors to the two original orbitals. The appended orbitals are mutually orthogonal and have support disjoint from the original orbitals. If an occupation set omits a site of $T$, the corresponding appended column in its determinant is zero. If the occupation set contains $T$, its two remaining sites must come from the original four, since any other row is zero.

Write such an occupation set as $a\cup b\cup T$, where $a$ and $b$ are the occupied subsets of the four original sites on sides $I$ and $J$. Move the rows of $T$ to the end in increasing order. They form an identity block on the appended columns, leaving the determinant for the four-site amplitude. The row move crosses each pair $s\in T$, $i\in a\cup b$ with $s<i$ once. With $m_T(i)=|\{s\in T\mid s<i\}|$, the sign is

$$
(-1)^{\sum_{i\in a}m_T(i)}\,(-1)^{\sum_{j\in b}m_T(j)}.
$$

Let $D_I,D_J$ be the diagonal sign matrices given by these two factors on the respective occupation bases of the four-site example. Let $E_I$ send the retained basis vector $\lvert a\rangle$ to $\lvert a\cup(T\cap I)\rangle$, and define $E_J$ on the traced side in the same way. The remaining sites stay empty. Both maps send distinct basis vectors to distinct basis vectors and are therefore isometries. Writing $M_0$ and $M_T$ for the four-site and extended amplitude matrices gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
M_T&=E_I D_I M_0 D_J E_J^\dagger,\\
M_T M_T^\dagger&=E_I D_I M_0M_0^\dagger D_I^\dagger E_I^\dagger.
\end{aligned}
$$

The ordinary reduced state has the four-site spectrum on the image of $E_I$ and is zero on its orthogonal complement. Its full spectrum therefore still has exactly two nonzero eigenvalues, $1/2,1/2$. The compression $P_I$ adds the coordinate projections for the sites in $T\cap I$ to the original rank-one projection. These projections have mutually orthogonal ranges, so $P_I$ is a projection of rank $1+|T\cap I|$ and all its eigenvalues are $0$ or $1$. The fermionic product spectrum therefore has just one nonzero eigenvalue, $1$.

**When does the one-particle compression determine the reduced spectrum?** Fix the cut and the particle number $k$. Whenever the product-spectrum criterion above holds, $P_I$ determines the full spectrum of the ordinary tensor partial trace. For intermediate fillings at a cut containing four alternating sites, use the four-site construction with the same fixed occupied set $T$. Replace the orbital supported entirely on $J$ with the unit vector at either site in its support. The two orbital matrices agree column by column on $I$, so they give the same $P_I$. After the replacement, every nonzero amplitude has the same occupation string on $J$, consisting of $T\cap J$ and the site of the new orbital. Grouping by the occupation string on $J$ leaves a single amplitude vector, so the reduced state is pure.

Write $D=2^{|I|}$. The two reduced states, before and after the replacement, have characteristic polynomials $x^{D-2}(x-1/2)^2$ and $x^{D-1}(x-1)$, respectively, and entropies $\log 2$ and $0$. The two states give [a counterexample with the same compression and different spectra](lean:D5/S3/Quantum/Fermionic/OrderedSlaterFixedFilling.alternating_fixed_filling_compression_twins). When the filling and the cut are known, the product-spectrum criterion above is also the exact criterion for $P_I$ to determine the reduced spectrum for every Slater state.

**The alternation matrix for several two-site orbitals.** Take $a$ orbitals supported entirely in $I$ and $b$ supported entirely in $J$, each with amplitude $1/\sqrt{2}$ at its two sites. All pairs of sites are disjoint. Order the retained columns first. Use $x\in\mathbb F_2^a$ and $y\in\mathbb F_2^b$ to encode a choice of the earlier or later endpoint of each pair. Every nonzero determinant selects exactly one site from each pair, so the amplitude matrix vanishes on all other occupations. Let $A_{ij}\in\mathbb F_2$ indicate whether retained pair $i$ and discarded pair $j$ have alternating endpoints.

On these occupations, the row permutation contributes its inversion sign. Inversions within either side depend only on that side's choices. For two pairs across the cut, the sum modulo $2$ of the inversion indicators over the four endpoint choices equals the parity of the number of discarded endpoints strictly between the retained endpoints. Thus the mixed coefficient of $x_i,y_j$ is precisely $A_{ij}$. Absorbing the signs that depend on one side alone gives the amplitude matrix

$$
M_{xy}=2^{-(a+b)/2}(-1)^{x^\mathsf T A y}.
$$

Summing over $y$ gives $(MM^\dagger)_{xx'}=2^{-a}$ when $A^\mathsf T x=A^\mathsf T x'$. Otherwise, pair each $y$ with the result of flipping a coordinate that reverses the sign, so the sum vanishes. Put $r=\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F_2} A$. Each nonempty fiber of $A^\mathsf T$ has $2^{a-r}$ elements. The marginal therefore has $2^r$ constant blocks, each contributing one eigenvalue $2^{-r}$. Including the zero space on all other occupations gives the full characteristic polynomial

$$
x^{\,2^{|I|}-2^r}(x-2^{-r})^{2^r}.
$$

[Equal-weight characteristic polynomial](lean:D5/S3/Quantum/Fermionic/SlaterPairCutRank.orderedCut_pair_charpoly)

The same binary-rank calculation appears for bipartitions of graph states. Here the matrix entries come from the site order in the Slater determinants. [Hein, Eisert and Briegel, Proposition 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0307130)

Replace each discarded orbital by the coordinate vector at its earlier endpoint. The retained compression stays the same. The discarded side now has one occupation string, so the ordinary marginal is pure and the entropy difference is $r\log 2$. For example, concatenate $m$ four-site blocks ordered $I,J,I,J$. Endpoints from distinct blocks do not alternate, so $A$ is the $m$-dimensional identity. This gives two $2m$-particle Slater states with equal retained compression at the same cut and ordinary marginal entropies $m\log 2$ and zero.

**The entropy bound for unequal endpoint weights and when it is attained (written proof).** Keep the cut and disjoint pairs above, and allow arbitrary complex amplitudes on the two endpoints of each normalized orbital. An orbital with zero amplitude at one endpoint must occupy the other. Fix all such endpoints and delete the corresponding rows and columns of the alternation matrix. Write $A$ for the resulting matrix and $a,b$ for the remaining pair counts on each side. Let $p_i,q_j$ be the probabilities of selecting the later endpoint, given by the squared moduli of the corresponding amplitudes. Put $r=\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F_2}A$. The ordinary marginal has rank $2^r$ and entropy at most $r\log 2$. Equality holds exactly when the rows with $p_i=1/2$ span the row space of $A$ and the columns with $q_j=1/2$ span its column space, both over $\mathbb F_2$.

Each determinant with nonzero amplitude still has a single term, the product of the selected amplitudes times the inversion sign. An inversion involving a fixed endpoint depends on choices on at most one side. The mixed coefficients for the remaining cross-cut inversions still follow from the four endpoint choices above. Absorb the complex phases of the endpoint amplitudes and the signs that depend on one side into the basis vectors. Let $\mu,\nu$ be the product Bernoulli laws of the endpoint strings $x\in\mathbb F_2^a$ and $y\in\mathbb F_2^b$, with parameters $p_i,q_j$, respectively. On the corresponding occupation strings the amplitude matrix is

$$
M_{xy}=\sqrt{\mu(x)}\,(-1)^{x^\mathsf T A y}\sqrt{\nu(y)} .
$$

The fixed occupations are included when embedding these basis vectors in the original space. Distinct endpoint strings give distinct occupation strings, and all other strings have zero amplitude.

Set $U=\operatorname{im} A^\mathsf T$ and $V=\operatorname{im} A$. Group endpoint strings by $u=A^\mathsf T x$ and $v=Ay$ on the two sides. The probabilities of the groups are

$$
P(u)=\sum_{A^\mathsf T x=u}\mu(x),\qquad
Q(v)=\sum_{Ay=v}\nu(y).
$$

Every group has positive mass. For each $u,v$, set $H_{uv}=(-1)^{x^\mathsf T A y}$ using any representatives of the groups. Adding a vector in $\ker A^\mathsf T$ to $x$, or a vector in $\ker A$ to $y$, leaves the exponent the same. The normalized vector for $u$ has components $\sqrt{\mu(x)/P(u)}$ within its group and zero elsewhere. The groups have disjoint supports, so these vectors are orthonormal. The vectors for $v$ are defined in the same way, with components $\sqrt{\nu(y)/Q(v)}$ on their groups. Let $E_I,E_J$ have these normalized vectors as columns. Then

$$
M=E_I K E_J^\dagger,\qquad K_{uv}=\sqrt{P(u)}\,H_{uv}\sqrt{Q(v)} .
$$

The nonzero spectrum of the original marginal is therefore the nonzero spectrum of $KK^\dagger$. Put $d=2^r$, the number of indices on each side. If $u\ne u'$, choose a coordinate $j$ in which $u+u'$ is nonzero. Pairing $v$ with $v+Ae_j$ reverses the sign of each term in $\sum_{v\in V}H_{uv}H_{u'v}$, so the sum is zero. When $u=u'$ the sum is $d$. Thus $HH^\dagger=dI$. The positive masses give invertible diagonal factors on either side of $H$ in $K$, so the rank is $d$.

The $d$ positive eigenvalues $\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_d$ sum to $1$. Strict concavity of $-t\log t$ gives the entropy bound $\log d$, with equality exactly when each $\lambda_\ell=1/d$. At equality, $KK^\dagger=I/d$, and invertibility of the square matrix $K$ gives $K^\dagger K=I/d$ as well. The two diagonals are $P(u)$ and $Q(v)$, respectively, since $|H_{uv}|=1$ and each group of masses sums to $1$. Both distributions must therefore be uniform. Conversely, when both sets of masses are $1/d$, we have $K=H/d$ and $KK^\dagger=I/d$.

Write $a_i\in U$ for row $i$ of $A$. The variable $u=\sum_i x_i a_i$ has law $P$. For any linear functional $\ell:U\to\mathbb F_2$, independence of the endpoint choices gives

$$
\mathbb E_P[(-1)^{\ell(u)}]
=\prod_{i:\,\ell(a_i)=1}(1-2p_i).
$$

The $d$ sign functions form an orthogonal basis of the functions on $U$. For orthogonality, sum a nontrivial sign function by pairing each point with its translate by a vector at which the functional is $1$. Thus $P$ is uniform exactly when every nonzero $\ell$ has zero expectation. The product vanishes precisely when some row with $\ell(a_i)=1$ has $p_i=1/2$. If the balanced rows span $U$, every nonzero $\ell$ takes value $1$ on some such row. If they span a proper subspace, extend a basis of that subspace to a basis of $U$ to obtain a nonzero functional vanishing on every balanced row. Applying the same argument to $Q$ and the columns of $A$ gives the condition on the other side. When $r=0$, each group index has a single possible value, the marginal is pure, and both spanning conditions hold automatically. This includes the case where either side has no remaining orbitals.

The [uniformity criterion for binary endpoint laws](lean:D5/S3/Combinatorics/Probability/BinaryEndpointLaw.mass_eq_uniform_iff_span) applies to both $P$ and $Q$.

For example, take retained pairs $(0,4)$ and $(1,3)$ and a discarded pair $(2,5)$. Both rows of $A$ are $(1)$. If either one of the retained orbitals is balanced and the discarded orbital is balanced, the entropy is $\log 2$. The other retained orbital may have any positive endpoint probabilities.

**Purity of the unequal-weight marginal (written derivation).** Use the matrix $A$ and endpoint probabilities after fixing deterministic endpoints as above. Let $\rho_I$ be the retained marginal. Draw $x,x'$ independently from $\mu$ and set $z=x+x'$ over $\mathbb F_2$. The bits $z_i$ are independent and each equals $1$ with probability $2p_i(1-p_i)$. Averaging over these bits gives the purity.

$$
\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_I^2)
=\mathbb E_z\!\left[\prod_{j:\,(A^\mathsf{T}z)_j=1}(1-2q_j)^2\right].
$$

On the supported occupation basis, $(MM^\dagger)_{xx'}$ is $\sqrt{\mu(x)\mu(x')}$ times $\mathbb E_{y\sim\nu}[(-1)^{z^\mathsf{T}Ay}]$. Independence factors the expectation over $y_j$. Each coordinate with $(A^\mathsf{T}z)_j=1$ contributes $1-2q_j$, and every other coordinate contributes $1$. The trace of the square of a Hermitian matrix is the sum of the squared moduli of its entries. Summing over $x,x'$ gives the formula. Conjugating by the diagonal unitary of retained phases and inserting zero rows and columns for the other occupations preserve the trace. The empty product equals $1$, so the purity is $1$ when $A$ is zero.

The [purity formula for the ordinary occupation marginal](lean:D5/S3/Quantum/Fermionic/WeightedSlaterPairCut.orderedMarginal_purity) allows arbitrary complex endpoint amplitudes, including zeros. When their squared moduli are normalized endpoint probabilities, the retained mass autocorrelations give the distribution of $z$.

When one retained pair alternates with one discarded pair, $A=(1)$. Adding the terms for $z=0$ and $z=1$ gives purity $1-8p(1-p)q(1-q)$. For instance, $p=q=1/4$ gives $23/32$, while $p=q=1/2$ gives $1/2$.

**Purity excess and the two endpoint laws (written derivation).** Return to the two image spaces of size $d=2^r$ with laws $P,Q$. Write $\Delta_P=\sum_u(P(u)-1/d)^2$ and define $\Delta_Q$ in the same way. Let $m=\min_u P(u)$. Then

$$
\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_I^2)-\frac1d
\ \ge\ \Delta_P+(2dm-d^2m^2)\Delta_Q .
$$

The bound also holds with the two sides exchanged and the coefficient calculated from $\min_v Q(v)$. Equality holds in the displayed bound when $P$ exceeds $m$ at at most one site. For a single alternating pair on each side with $p=q=1/4$, the two deviations are both $1/8$. The right side is $7/32$, exactly $23/32-1/2$.

Set $C=H\operatorname{diag}(Q)H^\mathsf T$. This real symmetric matrix has $C_{uu}=1$. Since $H^\mathsf T H=dI$ and $H_{uv}^2=1$, each row has squared norm $d\sum_v Q(v)^2$. Removing the diagonal contribution leaves $d\Delta_Q$ in every row and column. The marginal purity is $\sum_{u,u'}P(u)P(u')C_{uu'}^2$. On the off-diagonal terms, write $P(u)=m+R(u)$. The constant and linear terms contribute $(dm^2+2m\sum_u R(u))d\Delta_Q$. Using $\sum_u R(u)=1-dm$ and subtracting the uniform-spectrum purity $1/d$ gives the displayed right side plus the nonnegative remainder $\sum_{u\ne u'}R(u)R(u')C_{uu'}^2$. This remainder vanishes when the surplus is concentrated at one site. Transposing the amplitude matrix exchanges the sides and preserves the nonzero spectrum, so the same calculation controls the other law.

**Does balancing one orbital lower purity?** Define $A$ using all the pairs, including any orbital that originally occupies a fixed endpoint. Keep the discarded probabilities $q_j$ and the other retained orbitals fixed. Making retained orbital $i$ balanced when $p_i\ne1/2$ weakly decreases purity. Let $L=\{j:0<q_j<1\}$. The decrease is strict exactly when row $i$ restricted to $L$ lies outside the $\mathbb F_2$-span of the other balanced retained rows restricted to $L$.

Take two independent discarded endpoint strings and let $w$ be their binary difference. Write $b_k=(1-2p_k)^2$. The two marginals have the same nonzero spectrum, so the preceding purity formula can be applied on the discarded side. A fixed endpoint on that side has difference bit zero. A fixed endpoint on the other side contributes a squared factor of $1$. Thus the formula uses the full $A$. Making orbital $i$ balanced sets $b_i$ to zero, and the purity before balancing minus the purity afterward is

$$
b_i\sum_{w:\,(Aw)_i=1}\Pr(w)
  \prod_{\substack{k\ne i\\(Aw)_k=1}} b_k .
$$

Every term is nonnegative. The support of $w$ consists of all binary strings that vanish off $L$. The decrease is strictly positive precisely when one of these strings has dot product $1$ with row $i$ and dot product zero with every other balanced row. Such a string cannot exist if the restricted row is in their span. Otherwise append the restricted row to a basis of that span and extend to a basis of $\mathbb F_2^L$. The functional taking value $1$ on the appended row and zero on the other basis vectors gives the required string under the dot product.

**A lower bound on the purity decrease (written derivation).** If the decrease is strict, form a matrix $C$ from row $i$ and the other balanced rows, all restricted to $L$. Choose any index set $J$ whose columns form a basis of the column space of $C$. The purity before balancing minus the purity afterward is at least

$$
b_i\left(\prod_{\substack{k\ne i\\p_k\ne 1/2}} b_k\right)
    \left(\prod_{j\in J} 2q_j(1-q_j)\right).
$$

The first product ranges over the other biased orbitals. Each $b_k$ is at most $1$, so the product in the purity difference is at least this product whenever $Cw=e$, where $e$ takes value $1$ in row $i$ and zero in every other balanced row. Strict decrease guarantees that $e$ belongs to the column space of $C$. Fix the difference bits outside $J$ and subtract their contribution from $e$. The residual is still in the column space, so the basis columns uniquely determine the required bits in $J$. At each $j\in J$, the probability of a $1$ is $2q_j(1-q_j)\le 1/2$, which also lower bounds the probability of either prescribed bit. Multiply these bounds using independence and average over the bits outside $J$. This gives the claimed purity bound. When one retained pair alternates with one discarded pair, $C=(1)$ and the constraint asks for the sole discarded bit to be $1$, so equality holds.

The [purity-drop lower bound](lean:D5/S3/Quantum/Fermionic/WeightedSlaterPurityDropBound.orderedMarginal_purity_drop_lower_bound) formalizes this inequality for the ordinary marginal.

**When the bound is attained (written derivation).** Under strict decrease, write $a_k$ for retained row $k$ restricted to $L$, and let $S$ be the span of the other balanced restricted rows. Write $e_j$ for the row with a $1$ in coordinate $j$ and zeros elsewhere. For a given column basis $J$, the bound is attained exactly when both conditions hold.

- For every retained orbital with $k\ne i$, $0<p_k<1$ and $p_k\ne 1/2$, the row $a_k$ belongs to the affine coset $a_i+S$.
- For every basis column $j\in J$ with $q_j\ne 1/2$, the coordinate row $e_j$ belongs to $a_i+S$.

The [per-basis equality criterion](lean:D5/S3/Quantum/Fermionic/OrderedSlaterPurityDropEquality.orderedMarginal_purity_drop_eq_iff) formalizes the equivalence between attainment and these two conditions.

Every assignment of the bits in $L\setminus J$ has positive probability and extends uniquely to a solution of $Cw=e$ on $L$. At each solution, every basis-bit probability and every other biased retained factor is at least the positive lower bound used in the proof. Equality in the sum requires equality in every factor at every solution. A retained orbital with $0<p_k<1$ and $p_k\ne 1/2$ has $0<b_k<1$, so its factor reaches the lower bound exactly when $a_k\cdot w=1$. For a basis column with $q_j\ne 1/2$, bit $1$ is strictly less probable than bit zero, so the required bit must be $e_j\cdot w=1$. A deterministic retained endpoint has $b_k=1$. A balanced discarded orbital gives probability $1/2$ to each difference bit.

Choose a solution $w_0$ of $Cw=e$. The full solution set is $w_0+\ker C$. A row $r$ has dot product $1$ with every solution exactly when it annihilates $\ker C$ and $r\cdot w_0=1$. The annihilator is the row space $S+\mathbb F_2 a_i$. Rows of $S$ give zero on $w_0$, and $a_i$ gives $1$, so the required rows form precisely $a_i+S$.

**Does any column basis attain the bound? (Written derivation.)** Continue to assume a strict decrease and suppose the retained-row condition above holds. Let $Q=\{j\in L:q_j=1/2\}$, and let $C_Q$ consist of the columns for those balanced discarded orbitals. Let $K$ contain the indices $j\in L\setminus Q$ for which deleting column $j$ from $C$ removes $e$ from the span of the remaining columns. Some column basis attains the bound exactly when

$$
\operatorname{rank} C_Q + |K| = \operatorname{rank} C.
$$

In that case, take any column basis of $C_Q$ and add every column indexed by $K$. If the retained-row condition fails, every column basis gives a lower bound strictly below the actual decrease.

The remaining columns span $e$ after deletion of column $j$ exactly when $Cw=e$ has a solution with $w_j=0$. Thus $K$ consists precisely of the unbalanced indices whose difference bits are $1$ in every solution. For $j\in K$, the other columns cannot span column $j$, since that would let us delete it without changing the column space. Each of these columns therefore belongs to every column basis, and together they are linearly independent modulo the column space of $C_Q$. The equality criterion permits only columns in $Q\cup K$. Their span has dimension $\operatorname{rank}C_Q+|K|$ and fills the entire column space exactly when this dimension equals the rank of $C$.

Take retained pairs $(0,2)$ and $(4,5)$ and discarded pair $(1,3)$, each with later-endpoint probability $1/4$. Balancing $(0,2)$ decreases purity by $3/32$, with a lower bound of $3/128$. The second retained pair has zero restricted row, so the first condition fails. Next take retained pair $(2,5)$ and discarded pairs $(0,3)$ and $(1,4)$, again with probabilities $1/4$. Now $C=(1\ \ 1)$ and either singleton column basis gives a bound of $3/32$. Only unequal difference bits contribute, giving a drop of $15/128$. Both coordinate rows lie outside the coset, so the second condition fails.

For example, suppose both retained pairs alternate with the sole discarded pair and its endpoint probability satisfies $0<q<1$. Both restricted rows are $(1)$. When the first retained orbital is already balanced, balancing the second leaves purity unchanged. When all three later-endpoint probabilities start at $1/4$, balancing just the second retained orbital lowers purity from $83/128$ to $5/8$.

Choose any subset of the initially biased retained orbitals, including the empty set, and balance the selected orbitals one at a time. The number of strict purity decreases equals the dimension gain of the span of the restricted rows. Let $S_0$ be the span of the initially balanced orbitals' restricted rows, and let $S_1$ be the span after adjoining all the selected rows. Each step adjoins a single row. If the row lies in the current span, both dimension and purity stay the same. If it lies outside, the dimension increases by one and purity strictly decreases. Thus every ordering gives $\dim S_1-\dim S_0$ strict decreases. Purity is nonincreasing at every step, so the final purity is strictly below its initial value exactly when $\dim S_1>\dim S_0$.

For this literal word, ordinary letter-count balance and a sum weighted by position parity are different observables. Retain the letter encoding $A=0$, $B=1$, $C=2$ and take integer weights

$$
\begin{aligned}
w(A)&=2,\qquad w(B)=1,\qquad w(C)=0,\\
F(v)&=\sum_{r=0}^{|v|-1}(-1)^r w(v_r),\\
W_{[s,s+m)}&=W_sW_{s+1}\cdots W_{s+m-1}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Here $s,m\in\mathbb N$, positions start at zero, and the empty word $\varepsilon$ has $F(\varepsilon)=0$. Each interval starts with a positive sign, independently of the parity of its global start $s$. The preceding hoppings are exactly $t_X=\rho^{w(X)}$, so finite products satisfy

$$
\prod_{r=0}^{m-1}t_{W_{s+r}}^{\,(-1)^r}
=\rho^{F(W_{[s,s+m)})}.
$$

For the fixed actual word $W$ and integer weights $w$ above,

\neg\left(
\begin{aligned}
&\exists K\in\mathbb R,\quad 0<K\ \land\\
&\forall s,m\in\mathbb N,\quad
\left|F(W_{[s,s+m)})\right|
\le K\log(m+2)
\end{aligned}
\right).
$$

In this inequality, the integer charge and natural-number length are viewed as real numbers, and $\log$ is the natural logarithm. The quantifiers include $m=0$, with no lower length cutoff or restriction on the start.
:::

::: proof
Set $\tau=\sigma^4$ and extend it to finite words by concatenation. Four literal substitution steps give

$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau(A)&=ABACABAABACAB,&|\tau(A)|&=13,\\
\tau(B)&=ABACABAABAC,&|\tau(B)|&=11,\\
\tau(C)&=ABACABA,&|\tau(C)|&=7.
\end{aligned}
$$

Every $\tau^n(X)$ has odd length: at $n=0$ its length is one, and the next level concatenates $13$, $11$, or $7$ preceding-level blocks of odd length. This proves the assertion simultaneously for all letters by induction. For arbitrary finite words, concatenation satisfies

$$
F(uv)=F(u)+(-1)^{|u|}F(v).
$$

Thus, when expanding $\tau^{n+1}(X)$, each preceding-level block reverses the sign of the next block. Write $a_n=F(\tau^n(A))$, $b_n=F(\tau^n(B))$, and $c_n=F(\tau^n(C))$. Alternating block signs in the three literal images above gives the recurrence, valid for every $n\in\mathbb N$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
(a_0,b_0,c_0)&=(2,1,0),\\
a_{n+1}&=a_n,\\
b_{n+1}&=2a_n-b_n,\\
c_{n+1}&=4a_n-2b_n-c_n.
\end{aligned}
$$

The recurrence $a_{n+1}=a_n$ and its initial value give $a_n=2$, so the recurrence for $b$ becomes $b_{n+1}-2=-(b_n-2)$. Set $d_n=(-1)^n(c_n-2)$; the recurrence for $c$ becomes $d_{n+1}=d_n-2$, with $d_0=-2$. The solution at every level is therefore

$$
\begin{aligned}
a_n&=2,\\
b_n&=2-(-1)^n,\\
c_n&=2-(2n+2)(-1)^n.
\end{aligned}
$$

The charge of a whole $A$ image remains $2$, but this does not control its internal prefixes. Define the explicit finite-word family

$$
U_0=\varepsilon,\qquad
U_{n+1}=\tau^{n+1}(C)U_n,\qquad m_n=|U_n|.
$$

The new block is prepended. The literal identity $\tau(A)=\tau(C)\,A\,BACAB$ gives

$$
\tau^{n+1}(A)=
\tau^{n+1}(C)\,\tau^n(A)\,\tau^n(BACAB).
$$

The empty word $U_0$ is a prefix of $A$. If $\tau^n(A)=U_nR_n$, the identity becomes

$$
\tau^{n+1}(A)=
U_{n+1}R_n\,\tau^n(BACAB).
$$

Inductively, $U_n$ is a literal prefix of $\tau^n(A)=\sigma^{4n}(A)$, hence

$$
W_{[0,m_n)}=U_n.
$$

This uses the original infinite word starting from $A$, with no additional occurrence assumption, cyclic closure, or position shift. All three fourth images are nonempty, so $m_n>0$ for $n\ge1$. Simultaneous induction over all letters uses at most thirteen preceding-level images at each step and gives $|\tau^n(X)|\le13^n$. Prefix containment then yields

$$
0<m_n\le|\tau^n(A)|\le13^n\qquad(n\ge1).
$$

Let $q_n=F(U_n)$. The prepended block $\tau^{n+1}(C)$ has odd length, so

$$
q_0=0,\qquad q_{n+1}=c_{n+1}-q_n.
$$

To solve this accumulation explicitly, set $r_n=(-1)^n(q_n-1)$. Substitution of the formula for $c_{n+1}$ gives $r_0=-1$ and $r_{n+1}=r_n-(2n+4)$. Summation yields

$$
\begin{aligned}
q_n&=1-(-1)^n(n^2+3n+1)\\
&=\begin{cases}
-n(n+3),&n\text{ even},\\
(n+1)(n+2),&n\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}
\end{aligned}
$$

Consequently $|q_n|\ge n^2$, including $n=0$. For $n\ge1$, we have $13^n\ge1$ and $3\le3^n$, so the length bound gives $m_n+2\le13^n+2\le3\cdot13^n\le39^n$, and therefore

$$
\log(m_n+2)\le n\log39.
$$

Now suppose the uniform bound negated in the theorem holds. Take its constant $K>0$ and choose a natural number $n>K\log39$. Since $K\log39>0$, we have $n\ge1$. On an actual prefix starting at zero,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\left|F(W_{[0,m_n)})\right|
&=|q_n|\ge n^2\\
&>Kn\log39
\ge K\log(m_n+2),
\end{aligned}
$$

contradicting the uniform bound at $s=0$, $m=m_n$. This contradiction covers every positive real $K$. The explicit family starting at zero is a construction within the proof, with no extra hypothesis and no extrapolation from finite samples. The full proof is checked by the Lean kernel.
:::

The counterexample family shows how charge can accumulate across levels in certain actual prefixes. Controlling intervals of every length at every start also requires control of all prefixes inside each level's blocks. A reusable estimate, independent of the Tribonacci alphabet, supplies this step: at each level, at most one child block remains unfinished, while the charges of the complete children contribute a sum over levels.

The prefix envelope controls every truncated prefix of a general substitution word, and the uniform squared-logarithmic bound controls every interval of the actual Tribonacci word. The later open-chain convergence theorem gives convergence to zero of the minimum absolute eigenvalue along the full sequence of specified even open prefixes for each fixed $0<\rho<1$. The quantitative bound and parameter-uniform convergence in [@ex-ch39-p2e-tribonacci-prefix-rate] remain a paper application and are not supplied by this convergence theorem.

::: {.lemma #lem-ch39-p2e-substitution-prefix-envelope title="A Level Prefix Envelope for Substitution Words" lean="D5/S1/Words/SubstitutionPrefixEnvelope.prefix_envelope"}
Let $\mathcal A$ be any alphabet, let $w:\mathcal A\to\mathbb Z$ be an integer weight, and let $f$ send each letter to a finite word, extended to finite words by concatenation. Take $f^0(a)$ to be the one-letter word $a$, and set

$$
Q(v)=\sum_{r=0}^{|v|-1}(-1)^r w(v_r).
$$

Suppose $M\in\mathbb Z$, $B\in\mathbb N$, and $H:\mathbb N\to\mathbb Z$ satisfy

$$
\begin{aligned}
&0\le M,\qquad 0\le H(i)
&&\text{for every }i\in\mathbb N,\\
&|w(a)|\le M,\qquad |f(a)|\le B
&&\text{for every }a\in\mathcal A,\\
&|Q(f^i(a))|\le H(i)
&&\text{for every }i\in\mathbb N,\ a\in\mathcal A.
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, for every $j,t\in\mathbb N$ and $a\in\mathcal A$,

$$
\left|Q\bigl(\operatorname{take}_t(f^j(a))\bigr)\right|
\le P(j):=M+B\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}H(i).
$$

Here $\operatorname{take}_t$ takes the first $\min(t,|f^j(a)|)$ letters, and the sum is empty at $j=0$. The conclusion includes empty prefixes, whole images, and indices beyond the word's end. It requires neither a finite alphabet, nonempty images, nor any prescribed parity of image lengths.
:::

::: {.proof #pf-ch39-p2e-substitution-prefix-envelope}
This proof follows the statement and argument of [the source declaration `prefix_envelope`](lean:D5/S1/Words/SubstitutionPrefixEnvelope.prefix_envelope).

The alternating charge with a local positive first sign satisfies $Q(uv)=Q(u)+(-1)^{|u|}Q(v)$, hence $|Q(uv)|\le|Q(u)|+|Q(v)|$. A level-zero image is a single letter, so every prefix has absolute charge at most $M$. Write $f^{j+1}(a)$ as the concatenation of at most $B$ level-$j$ children specified by $f(a)$. Each prefix consists of complete children followed by at most one child's prefix. The complete part contributes at most $BH(j)$, and induction bounds the unfinished part by $P(j)$, giving $P(j)+BH(j)=P(j+1)$. An index beyond the total length leaves only complete blocks; empty images add no charge. This induction uses absolute values while retaining the exact concatenation phase.

The full proof is checked by the Lean kernel.
:::

For the literal Tribonacci word, the complete-block charge formulas give a linear $H(j)$, so this estimate yields a quadratic $P(j)$. To obtain a bound independent of the start, we must also place each actual interval inside blocks at a suitable level.

::: {.theorem #thm-ch39-p2e-tribonacci-uniform-log-squared title="A Uniform Squared-Logarithmic Interval Bound for the Actual Tribonacci Word" lean="D5/S1/Words/TribonacciUniformLogSquared.uniform_log_squared"}
Take $\sigma(A)=AB$, $\sigma(B)=AC$, and $\sigma(C)=A$, and let $W=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sigma^n(A)$ be the infinite word starting from $A$, indexed from zero. Assign integer weights $w(A)=2$, $w(B)=1$, and $w(C)=0$, and define the charge with a positive first sign within each interval:

$$
F(W_{[s,s+m)})=\sum_{r=0}^{m-1}(-1)^r w(W_{s+r}).
$$

There exists a real constant $C>0$, independent of both start and length, such that

$$
\forall s,m\in\mathbb N,\qquad
\left|F(W_{[s,s+m)})\right|
\le C\bigl(\log(m+2)\bigr)^2.
$$

The integer charge and natural-number length are viewed as real numbers in this inequality, and $\log$ is the natural logarithm. The quantifiers include $m=0$, when the charge is zero. The first sign remains positive even when $s$ is odd.
:::

::: {.proof #pf-ch39-p2e-tribonacci-uniform-log-squared}
This proof follows the statement and argument of [the source declaration `uniform_log_squared`](lean:D5/S1/Words/TribonacciUniformLogSquared.uniform_log_squared).

**From complete blocks to prefixes.** Retain $\tau=\sigma^4$, whose three image lengths are $13,11,7$. The complete-block charges computed above are $2$, $2-(-1)^j$, and $2-(2j+2)(-1)^j$. Thus, for every letter $X$ and level $j$,

$$
|F(\tau^j(X))|\le H(j):=2j+4.
$$

Apply [@lem-ch39-p2e-substitution-prefix-envelope] with $M=2$, $B=13$, and this $H$. Every prefix of every level-$j$ image has the common envelope

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(j)&=2+13\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}(2i+4)\\
&=2+13j^2+39j.
\end{aligned}
$$

This controls every truncation position; the constant charge $2$ of a complete $A$ block cannot replace it.

**From prefixes to local intervals.** Let $v$ be a contiguous subword of $T=\tau^j(X)\tau^j(Y)$. Each prefix of $T$ is the concatenation of at most one prefix from each block, so its absolute charge is at most $2P(j)$. If $R$ is the prefix preceding $v$, then both $R$ and $Rv$ are prefixes of $T$, and

$$
F(Rv)-F(R)=(-1)^{|R|}F(v).
$$

Taking absolute values gives

$$
|F(v)|\le4P(j)\le156(j+1)^2\qquad(j\in\mathbb N).
$$

This two-block estimate requires no odd-length hypothesis and assumes no occurrence of these blocks in the infinite word. An interval contained in a single block also qualifies, since it is a contiguous subword of $\tau^j(X)\tau^j(X)$.

**Containment in the actual word.** Each $\tau$ image has length at least $7$. Starting with length one at level zero, simultaneous induction over all letters concatenates at least seven preceding-level images at every step, giving

$$
|\tau^j(X)|\ge7^j\qquad\text{for every }j\in\mathbb N,\ X\in\{A,B,C\}.
$$

Now fix any $s$ and $m>0$. Take $k=\lfloor\log_7 m\rfloor$ and $j=k+1$, so $7^k\le m<7^j$. Choose $N\ge j$ large enough that $s+m\le|\tau^N(A)|$. These $A$ images are actual prefixes of $W$, so $W_{[s,s+m)}$ is exactly the word obtained by dropping the first $s$ letters of $\tau^N(A)$ and taking the next $m$. The concatenation identity for substitution iterates gives

$$
\tau^N(A)=\tau^j\bigl(\tau^{N-j}(A)\bigr).
$$

The right side is a concatenation of level-$j$ blocks in their actual order, each strictly longer than $m$. Starting in the block containing its first letter, an interval of length $m$ either stays there or enters the immediately following block. In the latter case its remaining length is less than $m$, so it cannot cross the entire second block. If the starting block is already the last one, the total-length condition ensures that the interval ends inside it. Thus the whole actual interval lies in one block or two adjacent blocks, and the bound $156(j+1)^2$ applies. The higher level $N$ merely accommodates the start $s$; the level $j$ determining the bound depends only on $m$.

**From level to length.** Since $2^k\le7^k\le m$, monotonicity of the logarithm gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
k\log2&\le\log(m+2),\\
\log2&\le\log(m+2),\\
(j+1)\log2=(k+2)\log2&\le3\log(m+2).
\end{aligned}
$$

Both sides are nonnegative and $\log2>0$. Squaring and inserting the interval estimate yields

$$
\begin{aligned}
|F(W_{[s,s+m)})|
&\le156(j+1)^2\\
&\le\frac{1404}{(\log2)^2}\bigl(\log(m+2)\bigr)^2.
\end{aligned}
$$

An empty interval has zero charge and satisfies the same inequality directly. Hence $C=1404/(\log2)^2>0$ is one sufficient witness for the existential statement; no optimization of this constant is asserted.

The full proof is checked by the Lean kernel.
:::

::: {.remark #rem-ch39-p2e-tribonacci-refutation-boundaries title="Alternating Discrepancy, Spectral Literature, and Physical Scope"}
**Literature background and scope, not a formalized physical conclusion of this book.** The square in the prefix estimate $|q_n|\ge n^2$ is a square of the substitution level $n$, but the existing length estimates also determine the worst-case order. In this remark, write $Q(s,m)=F(W_{[s,s+m)})$, with charges and lengths viewed as real numbers in the following inequalities. For $n\ge1$, we already have $0<\log(m_n+2)\le n\log39$. Both sides are nonnegative and $\log39>0$, so squaring and applying [@thm-ch39-p2e-tribonacci-uniform-log-squared] gives, with one $C>0$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{(\log(m_n+2))^2}{(\log39)^2}
&\le n^2\le|q_n|=|Q(0,m_n)|\\
&\le C(\log(m_n+2))^2.
\end{aligned}
$$

The recursion gives $m_{n+1}=|\sigma^{4(n+1)}(C)|+m_n>m_n$, since every letter image is nonempty. These actual prefix lengths therefore increase strictly and are unbounded, giving matching squared-logarithmic bounds along this sequence. At $n=0$, we have $m_0=q_0=0$, outside the scope of this positive lower bound.

To distinguish a length cutoff from an exact length, define in this remark

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(M)&=\max_{\substack{m\in\mathbb N\\0\le m\le M}}|Q(0,m)|,\\
R(M)&=\sup_{\substack{s\in\mathbb N\\m\in\mathbb N,\ m\le M}}|Q(s,m)|.
\end{aligned}
$$

Here $M\in\mathbb N$; in $R(M)$, the start $s$ ranges over all natural numbers, without a restriction by $M$. The maximum defining $P(M)$ is over a nonempty finite set, and the uniform upper bound makes the nonempty set defining $R(M)$ bounded above. For every positive natural $M$, the same constant $C>0$ satisfies

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{(\log(M+2))^2}{4(\log39)^2}
&\le P(M)\le R(M)\\
&\le C(\log(M+2))^2.
\end{aligned}
$$

The lower bound follows directly from the existing prefix estimates. For $M\ge13$, take $n=\lfloor\log_{13}M\rfloor\ge1$. Then $13^n\le M<13^{n+1}$ and $m_n\le13^n\le M$, so $P(M)\ge|Q(0,m_n)|\ge n^2$. Also, $M+2\le3M$ gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\log(M+2)&\le\log3+\log M\\
&\le\log3+(n+1)\log13\\
&\le(n+1)\log39\le2n\log39.
\end{aligned}
$$

Squaring and dividing by the positive number $4(\log39)^2$ proves the required lower bound. For $1\le M\le12$, use the actual one-letter prefix $W_{[0,1)}=A$, whose charge is $Q(0,1)=2$, so $P(M)\ge2$. Meanwhile, $\log(M+2)\le\log14<\log39$, so the required lower bound's left side is less than $1/4$. For the upper bound, for every $m\le M$ and arbitrary $s\in\mathbb N$, apply the uniform estimate and $0<\log(m+2)\le\log(M+2)$, then take the maximum or supremum. Taking $s=0$ gives $P(M)\le R(M)$. Thus the prefix running maximum and the running supremum over all starts both have squared-logarithmic order: the uniform upper bound has sharp worst-case order. No constant is optimized here. At $M=0$, we have $P(0)=R(0)=0$, so the displayed positive lower bound fails.

Statements at every exact length require a separate distinction. Complete $A$ images supply another family of actual prefixes: put $\ell_n=|\sigma^{4n}(A)|$. The preceding identity $a_n=2$ and actual prefix containment give

$$
Q(0,\ell_n)=2,\qquad \ell_n\ge4n+1\qquad(n\in\mathbb N).
$$

The length bound follows from $\sigma^{k+1}(A)=\sigma^k(A)\sigma^k(B)$ for $k\ge0$: every $B$ image is nonempty, so the length starts at one and increases by at least one per step. Hence $\ell_n\to\infty$, while $|Q(0,\ell_n)|/(\log(\ell_n+2))^2\to0$. There are no $c>0$ and $m_0\in\mathbb N$ such that every $m\ge m_0$ satisfies $|Q(0,m)|\ge c(\log(m+2))^2$. Requiring this lower bound simultaneously at all starts also fails, since it includes start zero. If instead the exact length is fixed before taking the supremum over starts, write $E(m)=\sup_{s\in\mathbb N}|Q(s,m)|$. Whether there exist $c>0$ and $m_0\in\mathbb N$ such that every $m\ge m_0$ satisfies $E(m)\ge c(\log(m+2))^2$ is not determined by this argument: constant charge at one start does not control other starts, and a running maximum may select a shorter length. Optimal constants are likewise undetermined here. The bond-word prefix length $m_n$ cannot be identified directly with the specified even site count $N_j$.

::: {.remark #rem-ch39-p2e-tridiagonal-localization title="Localizing the Lower Norm of a Tridiagonal Operator with Both Boundary Residuals"}
Let $A$ be a bounded complex-linear operator on $H=\ell^2(\mathbb Z;\mathbb C)$. Assume only that its matrix entries $A_{ij}=(Ae_j)_i$ vanish for $|i-j|>1$, where $e_j$ is the coordinate unit vector at $j$. Even an input on a single site $j$ can produce output on all three sites $j-1,j,j+1$. For each integer $n\ge1$ and $k\in\mathbb Z$, let $E_{n,k}$ be the subspace of vectors supported on $k+1,\ldots,k+n$. The restriction $A|_{E_{n,k}}$ still takes values in $H$. Its $(n+2)\times n$ matrix retains rows $k$ and $k+n+1$ in the output norm. Taking the $n\times n$ principal submatrix would discard both rows.

Read the three coefficient sequences directly from $A$ as $a_i=A_{i,i-1}$, $b_i=A_{ii}$ and $c_i=A_{i,i+1}$. All three are bounded because $|A_{ij}|\le\|Ae_j\|\le\|A\|$. Write $M_-=\sup_i|a_i|$ and $M_+=\sup_i|c_i|$, with both suprema over $\mathbb Z$. Let $(Ux)_i=x_{i-1}$ and let $M_f$ denote coordinatewise multiplication by $f$. The bilateral shift $U$ preserves the norm (`updateLinearIsometryEquiv`), and the multiplication operator has norm equal to the supremum norm of its coefficients (`diagonalOperator_norm`). The three terms agree with $A$ on every $e_j$. By `lp.ext_continuousLinearMap` in the pinned Mathlib, the identity extends to all of $H$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= M_a U+M_b+M_c U^{-1},\\
(Ax)_i &= a_i x_{i-1}+b_i x_i+c_i x_{i+1}.
\end{aligned}
$$

For a bounded operator $B$ with nonzero domain, write $\nu(B)=\inf_{\|x\|=1}\|Bx\|$ for its lower norm. Each window contains the unit vector $e_{k+1}$. For a given window length $n$, the lower norms satisfy

$$
\begin{aligned}
\inf_{k\in\mathbb Z}\nu(A|_{E_{n,k}})&\le \nu(A)+\delta_n,\\
\delta_n&=2(M_-+M_+)\sin\frac{\pi}{2(n+1)}.
\end{aligned}
$$

For fixed $A$, the error bound is $O(1/n)$. The sine factor measures the cost of shifting the window weights by one site. Put $\theta=\pi/(n+1)$ and take $w_j=\sin(j\theta)$ for $1\le j\le n$, with $w_j=0$ elsewhere. Multiply the sine recurrence $w_{j-1}+w_{j+1}=2\cos(\theta)w_j$ (`sine_reference_eigenvector`) by $w_j$ and sum over the window. Writing $S=\|w\|^2>0$ and expanding the squared differences gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
T=\|Uw-w\|^2 &= \sum_{j=0}^{n}(w_{j+1}-w_j)^2\\
&=2(1-\cos\theta)S=4\sin^2(\theta/2)S .
\end{aligned}
$$

For $n=1$ the weights rise from $0$ to $1$ and fall back to $0$, so each end contributes one squared difference. For any $x\in H$, form the windowed vectors $(D_kx)_i=w_{i-k}x_i$. At fixed $i$, summing over $k$ gives a total squared mass of $S|x_i|^2$. Hence $\sum_k\|D_kx\|^2=S\|x\|^2$, even for $x$ of infinite support. In $AD_kx-D_kAx$ the diagonal terms cancel. By the shift-difference formula in [@thm-observer-read-update-commutator] and its bounded-operator factorization `readout_update_commutator_factorization`, the two off-diagonals contribute backward and forward differences of the weights. Both differences have norm $\sqrt T$. Applying the triangle inequality to the entire family of outputs in $\ell^2(\mathbb Z;H)$ gives

$$
\left(\sum_k\|AD_kx\|^2\right)^{1/2}
\le \sqrt S\,\|Ax\|+(M_-+M_+)\sqrt T\,\|x\|.
$$

Given any $\eta>0$, choose a unit vector $x$ with $\|Ax\|<\nu(A)+\eta/2$ and put $q=\|Ax\|+\delta_n+\eta/2$. If $\|AD_kx\|\ge q\|D_kx\|$ held for every window, the left side would be at least $q\sqrt S$, while the right side equals $(q-\eta/2)\sqrt S$. Thus some integer $k$ satisfies the strict reverse inequality and $D_kx\ne0$. Normalizing $D_kx$ gives a specific $u\in E_{n,k}$ with $\|u\|=1$ and $\|Au\|<\nu(A)+\delta_n+\eta$. Taking the infimum over positions and letting $\eta$ tend to zero gives the stated bound. The argument applies when an infimum is unattained, when $A$ is nonselfadjoint, and when either or both off-diagonals vanish identically.

This scalar complex Hilbert-space application uses the localization estimate of Chandler-Wilde, Chonchaiya and Lindner (§5.1, Proposition 5.1 and Corollary 5.2, pp. 765–766, with the sine window on p. 767). Spectral inclusion and optimality of the error constant each require a separate argument. [*On spectral inclusion sets and computing the spectra and pseudospectra of bounded linear operators*, *J. Spectr. Theory* **14**, 719–804](https://doi.org/10.4171/JST/514)

A gap estimate for finite Tribonacci open chains via this route would still require proving that zero belongs to the spectrum of the corresponding whole-line model, transplanting the actual word interval together with one neighboring site on each side into the specified finite open prefixes, and checking length ratios and small scales. The proof of [@thm-ch39-p2e-tribonacci-open-chain-zero-gap] works directly with finite matrices. Relating physical states and spatial cuts to the Rauzy boundary and establishing the entanglement-entropy scaling in [@prob-ch39-p2e-fractal-horizon] remain separate problems.
:::

A second finite-matrix route uses an odd repeated block. An odd shift exchanges even and odd bonds, allowing two strings of hopping ratios from forward substitution to be paired. The following bound expresses that cancellation in entries of the actual inverse matrix.

::: {.theorem #thm-ch39-p2e-odd-repeat-inverse-mass title="Inverse-Mass Bound for an Odd Repeated Block" lean="D5/S3/Quantum/Dynamics/OddSquareGreenMass.odd_square_inverse_paired_mass"}
Let $q\in\mathbb{N}$ and $t:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}$ satisfy $0<t(i)\le1$ for every $i\in\mathbb{N}$, and assume

$$
t(i+2q+1)=t(i)\qquad(0\le i<2q).
$$

Set $n=2q+1$. Index the lower bidiagonal matrix $L\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}$ from zero, with entries

$$
\begin{aligned}
L_{i,i}&=t(2i) &&(0\le i<n),\\
L_{i+1,i}&=-t(2i+1) &&(0\le i<n-1).
\end{aligned}
$$

All other entries vanish. Write $G=L^{-1}$ and, for $0\le l<q$ and $0\le d<q-l$, define the paired mass

$$
\begin{aligned}
P_{l,d}&=G_{l+d+1,l}\\
&\quad+G_{q+l+d+1,q+l+1}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Every displayed matrix index lies in $0,\ldots,2q$, and

$$
q(q+1)\le\sum_{l=0}^{q-1}\sum_{d=0}^{q-l-1}P_{l,d}.
$$

For $q=0$, both sums are empty and have value zero.
:::

::: proof
The positive diagonal lets forward substitution produce the actual inverse. Odd repetition makes the product of the two positive entries in each pair exactly $1/[t(2l)t(2(l+d+1))]\ge1$, so their sum is at least $2$. The triangular index domain has $q(q+1)/2$ pairs, giving the bound. The full proof is checked by the Lean kernel.
:::

If $m=2q+1$, $s$ is even, $W_{s+i+m}=W_{s+i}$ for $0\le i<m-1$, and $s+2m\le N_j$, setting $t(i)=t_{W_{s+i}}$ matches this matrix model and a $2m$-site window inside the prefix. The following theorem constructs the required windows from the substitution word itself and compares their lengths uniformly with the prefix length.

::: {.theorem #thm-ch39-p2e-aligned-tribonacci-windows title="Uniform Odd Repetition Windows in Actual Tribonacci Prefixes" lean="D5/S1/Words/TribonacciAlignedWindows.aligned_windows"}
For the actual word $W$ above, the substitution lengths $L_j=|\sigma^j(A)|$, and the even prefix lengths $N_j=2\lfloor L_j/2\rfloor$, there are natural constants $K>0$ and $J\ge1$ such that every natural $j\ge J$ admits natural numbers $m,s$ satisfying

$$
\begin{aligned}
m&\ge3,\qquad m\text{ is odd},\quad s\text{ is even},\\
N_j&\le Km,\qquad s+2m\le N_j,\\
W_{s+i+m}&=W_{s+i}\qquad(0\le i<m-1).
\end{aligned}
$$

The last line holds for every natural $i$ in the displayed range; the constants $K,J$ do not vary with $j$.
:::

::: proof
The formal proof takes $K=128$ and $J=4$. The two copies of $ABA$ in the fourth iterate supply the repeated blocks. Four substitution steps preserve oddness of all three letter-image lengths, giving an odd block length and an even starting position. A nonempty tail keeps the whole window inside the prefix after even rounding, and seven length-growth steps give the factor $128$. The example below spells out this construction; the full proof is checked by the Lean kernel.
:::

::: {.example #ex-ch39-p2e-aligned-tribonacci-windows title="An Explicit Odd Repetition Window"}
For each $j\ge4$, set $r=\lfloor(j-4)/4\rfloor$ and $k=4r$, so $k+4\le j\le k+7$. Split the fourth iterate into four pieces:

$$
\sigma^4(A)=ABAC\,\vert\,ABA\,\vert\,ABA\,\vert\,CAB.
$$

Write $a=\sigma^k(A)$, $b=\sigma^k(B)$, and $c=\sigma^k(C)$; the vertical bars indicate concatenation, not additional letters. Applying $k$ further substitutions gives

$$
\sigma^{k+4}(A)=(abac)\,(aba)\,(aba)\,(cab).
$$

Take $s=|abac|$ and $m=|aba|$. At every level divisible by four, the lengths of $a,b,c$ are all odd: at level zero each length is $1$, and the fourth images of the three letters contain $13,11,7$ letters, respectively, so four substitution steps preserve this oddness. Thus $s$ is even, $m\ge3$ is odd, and the two copies of $aba$ supply the required equality. The nonempty tail $cab$ ensures that rounding the prefix length down to an even number preserves the entire repeated window. Each substitution at most doubles length, giving

$$
\begin{aligned}
N_j&\le L_j\le L_{k+7}\le128L_k\le128m,\\
s+2m&\le N_j,\\
W_{s+i+m}&=W_{s+i}\qquad(0\le i<m-1).
\end{aligned}
$$

Here $s,m$ are nonnegative integers, and the constant $128$ is independent of $j$ and the hopping parameter $\rho$. This gives explicit choices of the constants and windows in [@thm-ch39-p2e-aligned-tribonacci-windows].
:::

::: {.example #ex-ch39-p2e-full-inverse-euclidean title="From Local Mass to a Euclidean Estimate for the Full Inverse"}
Take a positive hopping sequence $t$ bounded above by $1$, and let $G=L(t,n)^{-1}$ be the full inverse of the bidiagonal block above. Suppose integers $q\ge1$ and $a\ge0$ satisfy $a+2q+1\le n$ and

$$
t\bigl(2a+i+(2q+1)\bigr)=t(2a+i)\qquad(0\le i<2q).
$$

Set $x=(1,\ldots,1)\in\mathbb R^n$ and $v=x/\sqrt n$. With Euclidean norms throughout,

$$
\|v\|_2=1,\qquad q(q+1)\le 2n\|Gv\|_2.
$$

This inverse retains all exterior sites on both sides of the window. The forward-substitution inverse formula shows that shifting both indices of the local inverse by $a$ gives exactly the corresponding entry of $G$. All entries of $G$ are nonnegative, so each of the two triangular regions in [@thm-ch39-p2e-odd-repeat-inverse-mass] embeds into the full matrix, giving

$$
q(q+1)\le2\sum_{i,j=0}^{n-1}G_{ij}.
$$

The factor $2$ lets us bound the two regions separately without an additional disjointness argument. Meanwhile, Cauchy–Schwarz gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{i,j=0}^{n-1}G_{ij}&=\langle x,Gx\rangle,\\
&\le\|x\|_2\|Gx\|_2,\\
&=n\|Gv\|_2.
\end{aligned}
$$

Combining the inequalities proves the estimate. For the preceding window, the parameter correspondence is $m=2q+1$, $s=2a$, $N_j=2n$. This is an application of the existing inverse-mass result, the forward-substitution formula and Euclidean inequalities. It introduces no new formal declaration, and this example carries no separate Lean verification badge.
:::

::: {.example #ex-ch39-p2e-open-chain-residual title="From the Full Inverse to a Spectral Residual Bound for a Finite Open Chain"}
For an integer $n\ge1$, take a real hopping sequence $t:\mathbb N\to\mathbb R$ with $t_k>0$ for every $k\in\mathbb N$. On $2n$ sites indexed by natural numbers starting at zero, define the actual open-chain matrix $H\in\mathbb R^{2n\times2n}$ by

$$
H_{ij}=\begin{cases}
t_i,&i+1=j,\\
t_j,&j+1=i,\\
0,&\text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\qquad 0\le i,j<2n.
$$

Its diagonal is zero, its last physical bond is $t_{2n-2}$, and there is no closing bond. When $n=1$, it consists of two sites joined only by $t_0$. The same entrywise formula defines a Hermitian matrix on $\mathbb C^{2n}$. A real orthonormal eigenbasis of the symmetric matrix is also a complex orthonormal eigenbasis, with the same real eigenvalues. The spectral-distance estimates below therefore apply to the complex open chain defined earlier. Let $B=L(t,n)$ be the lower bidiagonal block above, so

$$
\begin{aligned}
B_{i,i}&=t_{2i} &&(0\le i<n),\\
B_{i,i-1}&=-t_{2i-1} &&(1\le i<n),
\end{aligned}
$$

with all other entries zero. Lower triangularity gives $\det B=\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}t_{2i}>0$, so the ordinary matrix inverse $G=B^{-1}$ exists. For every Euclidean unit vector $v\in\mathbb R^n$, there are a real number $\lambda$ and a nonzero real vector $z\in\mathbb R^{2n}$ such that

$$
\begin{aligned}
Hz&=\lambda z,\\
|\lambda|\,\|Gv\|_2&\le1.
\end{aligned}
$$

To see this, reorder coordinates into even sites followed by odd sites, and write $C_{ij}=H_{2i,2j+1}$. The diagonal entries of $C$ are $t_{2i}$, its subdiagonal entries are $t_{2i-1}$, and all other entries vanish. Set $D=\operatorname{diag}((-1)^i)_{i=0}^{n-1}$. Then $D^2=I$, and entrywise calculation gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
H&=\begin{pmatrix}0&C\\C^{\mathsf T}&0\end{pmatrix},\\
DCD&=B,\qquad CD=DB.
\end{aligned}
$$

The first line denotes the reordered matrix. Construct an auxiliary vector $u$ by setting $u_{\mathrm{even}}=0$ and $u_{\mathrm{odd}}=DGv$. Since $BG=I$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
(Hu)_{\mathrm{even}}&=CDGv=DBGv=Dv,\\
(Hu)_{\mathrm{odd}}&=0,\\
\|u\|_2&=\|Gv\|_2,\qquad \|Hu\|_2=1.
\end{aligned}
$$

Here $D$ only changes coordinate signs, preserving the Euclidean norm; $BGv=v\ne0$ also ensures $u\ne0$. Since $H$ is a finite real symmetric matrix, take a real orthonormal eigenbasis $e_k$ with real eigenvalues $\lambda_k$ ($0\le k<2n$). Set $\alpha=\min_{0\le k<2n}|\lambda_k|$ and substitute $u=\sum_k c_ke_k$ into Parseval's identity to obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
\alpha^2\|u\|_2^2
&=\alpha^2\sum_{k=0}^{2n-1}c_k^2,\\
&\le\sum_{k=0}^{2n-1}\lambda_k^2c_k^2
=\|Hu\|_2^2=1.
\end{aligned}
$$

The finite nonempty set of eigenvalues contains an index $k_0$ with $|\lambda_{k_0}|=\alpha$. Choose $\lambda=\lambda_{k_0}$ and $z=e_{k_0}$; taking square roots gives the claimed inequality. The selected $z$ is an eigenvector; the auxiliary vector $u$ generally is not. This example requires no upper bound on the hoppings.

If the upper-bound and repeated-window hypotheses of [@ex-ch39-p2e-full-inverse-euclidean] also hold, use the same unit vector $v$ as in that example to obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
|\lambda|q(q+1)
&\le2n|\lambda|\,\|Gv\|_2\le2n,\\
\delta:=\min_{\mu\in\operatorname{Spec}(H)}|\mu|
&=|\lambda|\le\frac{2n}{q(q+1)}.
\end{aligned}
$$

This converts the mass estimate for the full inverse into a bound on the spectral distance to zero energy for the same finite open chain.
:::

::: {.example #ex-ch39-p2e-tribonacci-prefix-rate title="A paper spectral bound for the specified even open prefixes"}
Return to the actual word $W$. Fix $0<\rho<1$ and set $t(i)=t_{W_i}$, with $t_A=\rho^2$, $t_B=\rho$ and $t_C=1$. These hoppings satisfy $0<t(i)\le1$ at every position. For each $j\ge4$, use the explicit window in [@ex-ch39-p2e-aligned-tribonacci-windows] and write

$$
\begin{aligned}
m&=2q+1,\qquad s=2a,\qquad N_j=2n,\\
q&\ge1,\qquad a+m\le n,\qquad N_j\le128m.
\end{aligned}
$$

Here $q,a,n$ are natural numbers. Equal letters have equal hoppings, so the word identity inside the window gives

$$
t\bigl(2a+i+(2q+1)\bigr)=t(2a+i)\qquad(0\le i<2q).
$$

The preceding two examples therefore apply to this same $N_j$-site open chain: the local matrix is the block for the shifted hoppings $t(2a+\cdot)$, while the full inverse remains $L(t,n)^{-1}$ for the entire prefix. No external sites are removed and no closing bond is added. Use their common unit vector $v$ to obtain $\delta_{\rho,j}q(q+1)\le N_j$. Since $q\ge1$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
9q(q+1)-2(2q+1)^2&=(q-1)(q+2)\ge0,\\
N_j\delta_{\rho,j}
&\le\frac{N_j^2}{q(q+1)}
\le\frac{128^2(2q+1)^2}{q(q+1)}
\le73728.
\end{aligned}
$$

The constant $73728=128^2\cdot9/2$ is independent of both $j$ and $\rho$; no optimality is claimed. Every substitution iterate starts with $A$. The substitution erases no letter, and this initial letter adds at least one letter, so $L_{j+1}\ge L_j+1$ and $L_j\ge j+1$. Hence $N_j\ge L_j-1\ge j$, and $\delta_{\rho,j}\le73728/N_j\to0$. This tail conclusion needs no separate estimate for $j=1,2,3$.

The bound also gives uniform convergence in the hopping parameter. For any $\varepsilon>0$, take

$$
J=\max\left\{4,\left\lfloor\frac{73728}{\varepsilon}\right\rfloor+1\right\}.
$$

Since $N_j\ge j$, every $j\ge J$ and every $0<\rho<1$ satisfy

$$
0\le\delta_{\rho,j}\le\frac{73728}{N_j}\le\frac{73728}{j}<\varepsilon.
$$

Here $J$ is independent of $\rho$. The conclusion covers the entire open interval; it does not include the degenerate endpoint model at $\rho=0$.

This is a paper application of the displayed window construction, full-inverse mass estimate and finite-dimensional spectral conversion, with no separate Lean verification badge; the parameter correspondence and formal sources are given in the [formalization interface](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/issues/30166). This finite open-chain spectral bound neither constructs a many-body ground state nor determines entanglement across a spatial cut or the dimension of the Rauzy boundary.
:::

The example gives a paper $1/N_j$ bound and convergence to zero for the specified even open prefixes. Fractal entropy scaling still requires a separate argument involving physical states and spatial cuts; those parts of [@prob-ch39-p2e-fractal-horizon] remain open.

The preceding quantitative paper application already gives a stronger rate. A separate argument needs only the actual prefixes constructed in the proof of [@thm-ch39-p2e-tribonacci-prefix-log-refutation]: their charge is positive and unbounded at odd levels, making one entry of the full inverse sufficiently large.

::: {.theorem #thm-ch39-p2e-tribonacci-open-chain-zero-gap title="Vanishing Spectral Distance for Actual Positive Tribonacci Open Chains" lean="D5/S3/Quantum/Dynamics/TribonacciOpenPathGap.gap_tendsto_zero"}
Let $W$ be the actual fixed word starting from $A$ for $\sigma(A)=AB$, $\sigma(B)=AC$, $\sigma(C)=A$, with weights $w(A)=2$, $w(B)=1$, $w(C)=0$. For each fixed real $0<\rho<1$, set $t_i=\rho^{w(W_i)}$. For integers $j\ge1$, define

$$
\begin{aligned}
L_j&=|\sigma^j(A)|,\qquad N_j=2\lfloor L_j/2\rfloor,\\
(h_{\rho,j})_{ab}&=\begin{cases}
t_a,&a+1=b,\\
t_b,&b+1=a,\\
0,&\text{otherwise},
\end{cases}\quad 0\le a,b<N_j,\\
\delta_{\rho,j}&=\min\{|\lambda|:\lambda\in\operatorname{Spec}(h_{\rho,j})\}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The matrix acts on $\mathbb C^{N_j}$, has zero diagonal and only the nearest-neighbor physical bonds $t_0,\ldots,t_{N_j-2}$, and has no closing bond between the endpoints. All its eigenvalues are real. Then

$$
\lim_{j\to\infty}\delta_{\rho,j}=0.
$$

Equivalently, for every $\varepsilon>0$ there is an integer $J\ge1$ such that every integer $j\ge J$ satisfies $0\le\delta_{\rho,j}<\varepsilon$.
:::

::: {.proof #pf-ch39-p2e-tribonacci-open-chain-zero-gap}
This proof follows the statement and argument of [the source declaration `gap_tendsto_zero`](lean:D5/S3/Quantum/Dynamics/TribonacciOpenPathGap.gap_tendsto_zero).

First fix an actual prefix of odd length, then use that same prefix in every sufficiently long open chain. Recall $\tau=\sigma^4$, $F$, and the words from the proof of [@thm-ch39-p2e-tribonacci-prefix-log-refutation]:

$$
U_0=\varepsilon,\qquad U_{n+1}=\sigma^{4(n+1)}(C)U_n,\qquad m_n=|U_n|.
$$

That proof established the literal prefix identity $U_n=W_{[0,m_n)}$ and $F(U_n)=1-(-1)^n(n^2+3n+1)$. Each prepended block $\sigma^{4(n+1)}(C)$ has odd length, so $m_{n+1}\equiv1+m_n\pmod2$. Starting from $m_0=0$, induction gives $m_n\equiv n\pmod2$. Thus for every odd $n$ we can write

$$
m_n=2q+1,\qquad F(U_n)=(n+1)(n+2)>0.
$$

Suppose $N_j\ge m_n+1$ and put $M=\lfloor L_j/2\rfloor$, so $N_j=2M$ and $q<M$. Take the entire physical open chain $H=h_{\rho,j}$. Use the bidiagonal block $B=L(t,M)$, positive block $C$, and $D=\operatorname{diag}((-1)^r)_{r=0}^{M-1}$ from [@ex-ch39-p2e-open-chain-residual]. They satisfy

$$
\begin{aligned}
B_{r,r}&=t_{2r},\qquad B_{r,r-1}=-t_{2r-1},\\
D^2&=I,\qquad DCD=B.
\end{aligned}
$$

The second entry formula applies for $1\le r<M$. Every $t_i>0$, so $B$ has nonzero diagonal, its ordinary inverse $G=B^{-1}$ exists, and $C^{-1}=DGD$. Ordering the sites as evens $2r$ followed by odds $2r+1$ gives the full chain and its inverse as

$$
\begin{aligned}
H&=\begin{pmatrix}0&C\\C^{\mathsf T}&0\end{pmatrix},\\
H^{-1}&=\begin{pmatrix}0&(C^{-1})^{\mathsf T}\\C^{-1}&0\end{pmatrix}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Both displays use the permuted coordinates; they also establish invertibility of $H$. Forward substitution starts the first column at $G_{0,0}=1/t_0$ and gives $t_{2r}G_{r,0}=t_{2r-1}G_{r-1,0}$ for $1\le r<M$. Hence

$$
G_{q,0}=\frac{\prod_{r=0}^{q-1}t_{2r+1}}{\prod_{r=0}^{q}t_{2r}}>0.
$$

This is the preceding Green-entry formula at start zero, with empty numerator product $1$ when $q=0$. Returning to physical sites, the signs in $D$ give $(H^{-1})_{2q+1,0}=(-1)^qG_{q,0}$. Substituting the actual hoppings $t_i=\rho^{w(W_i)}$, and retaining the final even bond $t_{2q}$ in the denominator, yields

$$
\begin{aligned}
|(H^{-1})_{2q+1,0}|&=G_{q,0}\\
&=\rho^{-\sum_{i=0}^{2q}(-1)^iw(W_i)}\\
&=\rho^{-F(U_n)}=\rho^{-(n+1)(n+2)}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The physical bonds used are exactly those with indices $0,\ldots,2q$, and site $2q+1<N_j$ is present. The condition $N_j\ge|U_n|+1$ retains this last bond and its right endpoint. This is an entry of the inverse of the full $N_j$-site matrix: all outside sites remain, no inverse of a local compression replaces it, and no boundary bond is added.

The spectral step uses the induced operator norm on complex Euclidean space, $\|T\|_{2\to2}=\sup_{\|v\|_2=1}\|Tv\|_2$. Testing on the unit coordinate vector $e_0$ gives

$$
|(H^{-1})_{2q+1,0}|\le\|H^{-1}e_0\|_2\le\|H^{-1}\|_{2\to2}.
$$

This is not the maximum-entry norm. The invertible Hermitian matrix $H$ has a Hermitian inverse whose eigenvalues are the reciprocals of its own. By the finite-dimensional spectral theorem, the Euclidean operator norm of $H^{-1}$ is its largest absolute eigenvalue. Therefore some real eigenvalue $\lambda$ of the original matrix satisfies

$$
\begin{aligned}
|\lambda|&=\|H^{-1}\|_{2\to2}^{-1},\\
0\le\delta_{\rho,j}&\le|\lambda|\le\rho^{(n+1)(n+2)}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, given $\varepsilon>0$, choose a natural number $k$ with $\rho^k<\varepsilon$, which is possible since $0<\rho<1$, and fix the odd integer $n=2k+1$. Since $(n+1)(n+2)\ge k$, the bound above is below $\varepsilon$. The actual iterate lengths satisfy $L_j\ge j+1$, hence $N_j\ge L_j-1\ge j$. Thus there is $J\ge1$ such that every $j\ge J$ satisfies $N_j\ge m_n+1$ and admits the estimate from this same prefix. The level $n$ is chosen once from $\rho,\varepsilon$; every subsequent $j\ge J$ then satisfies $0\le\delta_{\rho,j}<\varepsilon$. This proves convergence along all indices, rather than just a selected subsequence.

The source declaration defines `gap` at $j=0$ to be $0$ to avoid the empty eigenvalue set of a zero-dimensional matrix. For $j\ge1$, $L_j\ge2$, and its definition is precisely the minimum absolute value of the nonempty finite real spectrum used here. This single-value convention does not affect the limit.

The full proof is checked by the Lean kernel.
:::

This standalone convergence theorem states only the full-sequence limit for each fixed positive $\rho$; its single-inverse-entry argument supplies no $1/N_j$ rate. The preceding [@ex-ch39-p2e-tribonacci-prefix-rate] already gives the stronger paper bound $73728/N_j$ and convergence uniform in the parameter, using actual repeated windows and full-inverse mass. The evidence scopes of the two arguments remain distinct; physical states, spatial cuts, and fractal entropy scaling still require further work.

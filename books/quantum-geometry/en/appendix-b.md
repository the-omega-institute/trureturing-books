## B.1 Fundamental Geometric Product and Grade Decomposition ($\mathcal{Cl}_{3,0}(\mathbb{R})$) {#sec-appendix-b-geometric-product}

Over three-dimensional Euclidean space $\mathbb{R}^3$, the Clifford algebra $\mathcal{Cl}_{3,0}(\mathbb{R})$ is generated by three orthonormal basis vectors $e_1, e_2, e_3$ subject to positive-definite squaring and orthogonal anticommutation:

$$
e_1^2 = e_2^2 = e_3^2 = +1, \qquad e_i e_j = -e_j e_i \quad (i \neq j).
$$

For any two grade-1 vectors $\mathbf{u} = \sum_{i=1}^3 u_i e_i$ and $\mathbf{v} = \sum_{j=1}^3 v_j e_j$, their **geometric product** unifies the symmetric scalar inner product (grade $0$) and the antisymmetric exterior bivector wedge product (grade $2$):

$$
\mathbf{u}\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v} + \mathbf{u}\wedge\mathbf{v},
$$

where

$$
\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v} = \frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{u}\mathbf{v} + \mathbf{v}\mathbf{u}) = \sum_{i=1}^3 u_i v_i \quad (\text{scalar measuring collinear projection}),
$$

$$
\mathbf{u}\wedge\mathbf{v} = \frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{u}\mathbf{v} - \mathbf{v}\mathbf{u}) = (u_1 v_2 - u_2 v_1)e_{12} + (u_2 v_3 - u_3 v_2)e_{23} + (u_3 v_1 - u_1 v_3)e_{31} \quad (\text{bivector measuring oriented rotation area}).
$$

The eight-dimensional algebra decomposes into four grade subspaces of dimensions $\binom{3}{0} + \binom{3}{1} + \binom{3}{2} + \binom{3}{3} = 1 + 3 + 3 + 1 = 8$:

| Grade $k$ | Subspace Name | Dimension $\binom{3}{k}$ | Canonical Basis Blades | 3-Bit Mask $m = b_0 + 2b_1 + 4b_2 \in \operatorname{Fin} 8$ | Square $B^2$ | Geometric & Physical Meaning |
| :---: | :--- | :---: | :--- | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| $0$ | Scalars | $1$ | $1$ | `000` ($0$) | $+1$ | Real inner-product projections, Born weights, and traces |
| $1$ | Vectors | $3$ | $e_1,\; e_2,\; e_3$ | `001` ($1$), `010` ($2$), `100` ($4$) | $+1$ | Bloch measurement axes and reflection normal vectors |
| $2$ | Bivectors | $3$ | $e_{12},\; e_{13},\; e_{23}$ | `011` ($3$), `101` ($5$), `110` ($6$) | $-1$ | Oriented rotation planes and $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ Lie generators |

$$
E_a E_b = (-1)^{\operatorname{inv}(a, b)}\, E_{a \oplus b}, \qquad \operatorname{inv}(a, b) = \#\bigl\{(i, j) \in \{1, 2, 3\}^2 \;\big|\; i > j,\; a_i = 1,\; b_j = 1\bigr\}.

## B.3 Pseudoscalar $I_3$, Reversion $\widetilde{A}$, Rotors, and ZYZ Euler Synthesis {#sec-appendix-b-rotors-and-zyz}

1. **Pseudoscalar $I_3 = e_1 e_2 e_3$ and Hodge Duality**:
   - Squaring law: $I_3^2 = (e_1 e_2 e_3)(e_1 e_2 e_3) = -1$;
   - Centrality: in 3D, $I_3$ commutes with all grade-1 vectors and grade-2 bivectors ($e_i I_3 = I_3 e_i$), making the center of $\mathcal{Cl}_{3,0}(\mathbb{R})$ isomorphic to $\mathbb{R} \oplus \mathbb{R} I_3 \cong \mathbb{C}$;
   - Vector–bivector duality:

     $$
     e_{23} = e_1 I_3, \qquad e_{31} = -e_{13} = e_2 I_3, \qquad e_{12} = e_3 I_3, \qquad \mathbf{u}\wedge\mathbf{v} = (\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{v})\,I_3.
     $$

2. **Reversion Anti-Involution ($\widetilde{A}$)**:
   Reversing the order of vector factors inside every blade ($\widetilde{AB} = \widetilde{B}\widetilde{A}$) acts on grade $k$ with sign $(-1)^{k(k-1)/2}$:

   $$
   \widetilde{\langle A\rangle_0} = +\langle A\rangle_0, \qquad \widetilde{\langle A\rangle_1} = +\langle A\rangle_1, \qquad \widetilde{\langle A\rangle_2} = -\langle A\rangle_2, \qquad \widetilde{\langle A\rangle_3} = -\langle A\rangle_3.
   $$

3. **3D Rotors and the Double-Sided Sandwich Action**:
   For a unit bivector $\hat{B}$ ($\hat{B}^2 = -1$) representing an oriented rotation plane, the unit rotor generating a rotation by angle $\theta$ in plane $\hat{B}$ belongs to the even subalgebra $\mathcal{Cl}_{3,0}^+(\mathbb{R}) = \operatorname{span}\{1, e_{23}, e_{31}, e_{12}\} \cong \mathbb{H}$:

   $$
   R(\theta, \hat{B}) = \exp\!\left(-\frac{\theta}{2}\hat{B}\right) = \cos\frac{\theta}{2} - \hat{B}\sin\frac{\theta}{2}, \qquad \widetilde{R}(\theta, \hat{B}) = \cos\frac{\theta}{2} + \hat{B}\sin\frac{\theta}{2}, \qquad R\widetilde{R} = \cos^2\frac{\theta}{2} + \sin^2\frac{\theta}{2} = 1.
   $$

   The double-sided sandwich map $\mathbf{v} \mapsto R\mathbf{v}\widetilde{R}$ preserves grade-1 vectors and Euclidean inner products (at $\theta = 2\pi$, $R(2\pi) = -1$ while $(-1)\mathbf{v}(-1) = +\mathbf{v}$, realizing the spinor double cover $S^3 \to \mathrm{SO}(3)$).

4. **ZYZ Euler Rotor Decomposition and Cascade Laws**:
   With elementary bivectors $B_z = e_{12}$ and $B_y = e_{31} = -e_{13}$, the elementary $Z$-axis and $Y$-axis rotors are

   $$
   R_z(\alpha) = \cos\frac{\alpha}{2} - e_{12}\sin\frac{\alpha}{2}, \qquad R_y(\beta) = \cos\frac{\beta}{2} - e_{31}\sin\frac{\beta}{2}.
   $$

   They obey the coaxial half-angle addition laws $R_z(\alpha)R_z(\gamma) = R_z(\alpha + \gamma)$ and $R_y(\alpha)R_y(\beta) = R_y(\alpha + \beta)$, inverse cancellation $R_z(\alpha)R_z(-\alpha) = 1$, and the universal three-factor ZYZ synthesis:

   $$
   R_{\mathrm{ZYZ}}(\alpha, \beta, \gamma) = R_z(\alpha)\,R_y(\beta)\,R_z(\gamma), \qquad R_{\mathrm{ZYZ}}(\alpha, 0, \gamma) = R_z(\alpha + \gamma).
   $$

## B.4 Pauli Correspondence and the 2-Bit Symplectic Phase Table {#sec-appendix-b-pauli-symplectic}

Mapping $e_1, e_2, e_3$ to the Pauli matrices $\sigma_x, \sigma_y, \sigma_z$ and the pseudoscalar $I_3 = e_1 e_2 e_3$ to $i I_2$ ($\sigma_x \sigma_y \sigma_z = i I_2$) establishes the canonical isomorphism $\mathcal{Cl}_{3,0}(\mathbb{R}) \cong M_2(\mathbb{C})$.

In stabilizer and Clifford circuit calculations, a single-qubit Pauli operator $P \in \{I, X, Z, Y\}$ is encoded by a binary pair $(x, z) \in \mathbb{F}_2^2$ via $P(x, z) = i^{xz} X^x Z^z$. $$

## B.5 Relativistic Spacetime Algebras $\mathcal{Cl}_{1,1}(\mathbb{R})$ and $\mathcal{Cl}_{1,3}(\mathbb{R})$ {#sec-appendix-b-spacetime-algebra}

In Part V (Chapters 17–18), replacing the Euclidean signature with the Minkowski metric $\eta_{\mu\nu} = \operatorname{diag}(+1, -1, -1, -1)$ yields the four-dimensional Spacetime Algebra (STA) $\mathcal{Cl}_{1,3}(\mathbb{R})$ (whose 2D lightcone slice is $\mathcal{Cl}_{1,1}(\mathbb{R})$):

1. **Dirac Generators $\gamma_\mu$ and Metric Anticommutation**:

   $$
   \gamma_0^2 = +1, \qquad \gamma_1^2 = \gamma_2^2 = \gamma_3^2 = -1, \qquad \gamma_\mu \gamma_\nu + \gamma_\nu \gamma_\mu = 2\eta_{\mu\nu}.
   $$

2. **Spacetime Split and Relative Spatial Vectors $\boldsymbol{\sigma}_k$**:
   Defining the timelike bivectors $\boldsymbol{\sigma}_k = \gamma_k \gamma_0$ ($k = 1, 2, 3$) gives

   $$
   \boldsymbol{\sigma}_k^2 = (\gamma_k \gamma_0)(\gamma_k \gamma_0) = -\gamma_k^2 \gamma_0^2 = -(-1)(+1) = +1, \qquad \boldsymbol{\sigma}_j \boldsymbol{\sigma}_k = -\boldsymbol{\sigma}_k \boldsymbol{\sigma}_j \quad (j \neq k).
   $$

   Hence the even subalgebra $\mathcal{Cl}_{1,3}^+(\mathbb{R})$ is canonically isomorphic to $\mathcal{Cl}_{3,0}(\mathbb{R})$, with 4D pseudoscalar $I_4 = \gamma_0 \gamma_1 \gamma_2 \gamma_3 = \boldsymbol{\sigma}_1 \boldsymbol{\sigma}_2 \boldsymbol{\sigma}_3$ satisfying $I_4^2 = -1$.
3. **Hyperbolic Boost Rotors and Lorentz Transformations**:
   Because $\boldsymbol{\sigma}_1 = \gamma_1 \gamma_0$ satisfies $\boldsymbol{\sigma}_1^2 = +1$, a Lorentz boost of rapidity $\eta$ along the $x^1$-axis expands in hyperbolic cosine and sine:

   $$
   L(\eta) = \exp\!\left(\frac{\eta}{2}\gamma_1\gamma_0\right) = \cosh\frac{\eta}{2} + \gamma_1\gamma_0\sinh\frac{\eta}{2}, \qquad L(\eta)\widetilde{L}(\eta) = \cosh^2\frac{\eta}{2} - \sinh^2\frac{\eta}{2} = 1.
   $$

   The sandwich action $x^\mu \gamma_\mu \mapsto L(\eta)(x^\mu \gamma_\mu)\widetilde{L}(\eta)$ preserves the Minkowski quadratic form $Q(x) = (x^0)^2 - (x^1)^2 - (x^2)^2 - (x^3)^2$ ([`bogoliubov_norm_conservation`](lean:D5/S3/Quantum/Bogoliubov/BogoliubovNormConservation.bogoliubov_norm_conservation)).
4. **Six-Dimensional Lorentz Lie Algebra Bivector Commutators**:
   Writing spatial rotation generators $J_k = \frac{1}{4}\varepsilon_{klm}\gamma_l\gamma_m$ ($J_1 = \frac{1}{2}\gamma_2\gamma_3,\; J_2 = \frac{1}{2}\gamma_3\gamma_1,\; J_3 = \frac{1}{2}\gamma_1\gamma_2$, satisfying $J_k^2 = -1/4$) and boost generators $K_k = \frac{1}{2}\gamma_k\gamma_0$ ($K_k^2 = +1/4$), the commutator brackets are

   $$
   [J_i, J_j] = \varepsilon_{ijk} J_k, \qquad [J_i, K_j] = \varepsilon_{ijk} K_k, \qquad [K_i, K_j] = -\varepsilon_{ijk} J_k.
   $$

   The third bracket $[K_i, K_j] = -\varepsilon_{ijk} J_k$ is the geometric origin of Thomas precession under non-collinear Lorentz boosts.

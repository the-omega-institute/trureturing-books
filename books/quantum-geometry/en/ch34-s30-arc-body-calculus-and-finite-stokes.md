## 34.30.1 Beyond Local Linearization: Comparative Rotor Differentials, the Chord–Sagitta Lock, and Subtraction-Free $90^\circ$ Complex-Step Differentiation {#sec-ch34-30-1}

When Leibniz (1684) and Newton (1687) erected classical calculus three centuries ago, they built it upon a foundational compromise—**local tangent-space linearization** ("replacing curves by straight lines"): flatten curved evolution near $x_0$ onto the flat tangent space $T_{x_0}\mathcal{M}$, extract first-order slope from the subtractive secant quotient $\frac{f(x_0 + \Delta x) - f(x_0)}{\Delta x}$ in the continuum limit $\Delta x \to 0$, and discard every higher-order bending term. Differential geometry demands a sharp distinction here: the blowup on the circle at $\theta = \frac{\pi}{2}$ is a pure coordinate-chart projection artifact where $(x \mapsto y(x))$ hits the observer's $90^\circ$ orthogonal blind zone (dissolving immediately under arc-length parameterization), whereas the singularity of the semicubical parabola $y^2 = x^3$ at the origin is a genuine intrinsic cusp where unit tangent direction reverses discontinuously. ## 34.30.2 The Path-Ordered Rotor Product Integral and the Four-Regime Roughness Phase Transition of $\Sigma_{\mathrm{sag}}(\epsilon, \alpha)$ {#sec-ch34-30-2}

## 34.30.3 Zero-Error Finite-Mesh Stokes Exactness on `1—3—3—1`, a Three-Line Gauss–Bonnet Proof, and Non-Abelian Surface Whiskering {#sec-ch34-30-3}

Conventional numerical analysis teaches a deeply ingrained habit of thought: *"Continuum calculus is exact, whereas partitioning a curved surface into a finite mesh reduces Stokes's theorem $\int_{\partial\Sigma}\omega = \int_\Sigma d\omega$ to an $\mathcal{O}(h^2)$ approximation that becomes exact only in the infinite-refinement limit $h \to 0, M \to \infty$."*

Viewed inside the **`1—3—3—1` Clifford–cochain complex** of [§34.8](ch34-s08-clifford-monads.md#sec-ch34-clifford-monad-tiling) and [§34.13](ch34-s13-point-volume-octave.md#sec-ch34-point-volume-octave-and-particle-field), the truth is the exact opposite: **the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus and the generalized Stokes theorem are not analytic approximations granted by $\epsilon \to 0$, but pure combinatorial topological identities of cellular gluing—once differential forms are formulated natively as cochains on minimal circular-arc bodies, finite-mesh Stokes holds with identically zero discretization error at any coarse halting aperture (even on an $M = 4$ octant mesh of an entire hemisphere!)**

Working across Pascal's palindromic grade ladder $\binom{3}{0} : \binom{3}{1} : \binom{3}{2} : \binom{3}{3} = 1 : 3 : 3 : 1$ on a cellular complex $\mathcal{K}$ (Hirani 2003; Desbrun et al. 2005 Discrete Exterior Calculus, literature result), let us cleanly separate **unordered Abelian cancellation** from **basepoint-anchored non-Abelian surface holonomy**:

### 1. Abelian Cochains and Spherical Excess: Unordered Shared-Dart Cancellation, Zero-Error Stokes, and Three-Line Gauss–Bonnet

Tile a compact oriented surface patch $\Sigma$ seamlessly with $M \ge 1$ minimal circular-arc bodies (spherical triangles bounded by great-circle arcs) $\{\Delta\mathcal{T}_k\}_{k=1}^M$, and assign to each oriented dart $e_{AB}$ an Abelian-group-valued $1$-cochain circulation $\Phi_\epsilon(e_{AB}) \in A$ obeying orientation reversal $\Phi_\epsilon(e_{BA}) = -\Phi_\epsilon(e_{AB})$. Defining the $2$-cochain curvature flux on each cell by $(d_\epsilon\Phi_\epsilon)(\Delta\mathcal{T}_k) := \sum_{e \subset \partial\Delta\mathcal{T}_k} \Phi_\epsilon(e)$ (whose single-cell oriented spherical-triangle phase excess in projective pure-state geometry is locked by the three-point Bargmann Bloch trace theorem [`triple_bargmann_bloch`](lean:D5/S3/Quantum/Information/GroupInvariantPsi/C129QubitBargmann.triple_bargmann_bloch); cf. [§34.6](ch34-s06-berry-sectors-and-path-integrals.md#sec-ch34-berry-sector-volume-and-path-integral)) and summing across all $M$ cells, every shared interior arc $e_{\mathrm{int}}$ is traversed once as $e_{AB}$ and once as its opposite dart $e_{BA}$ by the two incident faces (mirroring the opposite-dart boundary operator `R.boundary` in Clause 4 of [@thm-ch34-arc-rotor-calculus-and-finite-stokes]):

$$
\Phi_\epsilon(e_{AB}) + \Phi_\epsilon(e_{BA}) = 0 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \sum_{k=1}^{M} (d_\epsilon\Phi_\epsilon)(\Delta\mathcal{T}_k) \equiv \sum_{e \subset \partial\Sigma} \Phi_\epsilon(e)!
$$

No $\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^2)$ truncation remainder exists on either side, and summation order is completely immaterial! Ascending one grade further to a tetrahedral 3-cell $\Delta^3 = [v_0, v_1, v_2, v_3]$, each of its six edges appears twice with opposite orientations across the four boundary faces, locking in **exact finite-aperture nilpotency** (Clause 5 `tetra_defects_incidence_repair_and_exactness` of [@thm-ch34-arc-rotor-calculus-and-finite-stokes]):

$$
(d_\epsilon^2 \omega)(\Delta^3) = \omega(\partial^2\Delta^3) \equiv 0 \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad \partial^2\Delta^3 = \emptyset.
$$

Better still, apply this coordinate-free gluing to any compact oriented triangulated surface $\Sigma$ tiled by $F = M$ constant-curvature spherical triangles $\Delta\mathcal{T}_k$ bounded by great-circle geodesic arcs (edge geodesic curvature $\kappa_g \equiv 0$), having $E = E_{\mathrm{int}} + E_\partial$ edges and $V = V_{\mathrm{int}} + V_\partial$ vertices (with $V_\partial = E_\partial$ around closed boundary loops). What takes pages of Christoffel symbols in classical differential geometry—the **Gauss–Bonnet theorem**—collapses on a finite circular-arc mesh into a **limit-free, three-line combinatorial identity** (manual derivation; cf. Regge 1961):

* **Line 1 (Half-edge incidence double counting)**: Across $F$ triangles there are $3F$ half-edges, covering every interior edge twice and every boundary edge once, so $3F = 2E_{\mathrm{int}} + E_\partial$;
* **Line 2 (Vertex regrouping of Girard spherical excess)**: Summing Girard's excess $\Omega(\Delta\mathcal{T}_k) = (\alpha_{k,1} + \alpha_{k,2} + \alpha_{k,3}) - \pi$ over all $F$ faces and regrouping angles at interior vertices ($2\pi$ per $v \in V_{\mathrm{int}}$) and boundary vertices ($\pi - \theta_{\mathrm{ext}}(v)$ per $v \in V_\partial$, where $\theta_{\mathrm{ext}}(v)$ is the discrete exterior turning angle) gives

  $$
  \sum_{k=1}^{F} \Omega(\Delta\mathcal{T}_k) = 2\pi V_{\mathrm{int}} + \sum_{v \in V_\partial}\bigl(\pi - \theta_{\mathrm{ext}}(v)\bigr) - \pi F = \bigl(2V_{\mathrm{int}} + V_\partial - F\bigr)\pi - \sum_{v \in V_\partial}\theta_{\mathrm{ext}}(v);
  $$

* **Line 3 (Substitution into $\chi(\Sigma) = V - E + F$)**: Substituting $V_{\mathrm{int}} = V - E_\partial$ and $-F = 2F - 3F = 2F - (2E_{\mathrm{int}} + E_\partial)$ reduces the integer prefactor identically to $2(V - E_\partial) + E_\partial + 2F - 2E_{\mathrm{int}} - E_\partial = 2(V - E + F) = 2\chi(\Sigma)$, yielding the **exact finite-mesh Gauss–Bonnet identity**:

  $$
  \sum_{k=1}^{F} \Omega(\Delta\mathcal{T}_k) + \sum_{v \in V_\partial} \theta_{\mathrm{ext}}(v) \equiv 2\pi\chi(\Sigma)!
  $$

## 30.3.1 Beyond Local Linearization: Comparative Rotor Differentials, the Chord–Sagitta Lock, and Subtraction-Free $90^\circ$ Complex-Step Differentiation {#sec-ch34-30-1}

When Leibniz (1684) and Newton (1687) erected classical calculus three centuries ago, they built it upon a foundational compromise—**local tangent-space linearization** ("replacing curves by straight lines"): flatten curved evolution near $x_0$ onto the flat tangent space $T_{x_0}\mathcal{M}$, extract first-order slope from the subtractive secant quotient $\frac{f(x_0 + \Delta x) - f(x_0)}{\Delta x}$ in the continuum limit $\Delta x \to 0$, and discard every higher-order bending term. Audited through background-free comparison geometry, this flat tangent blade harbors four structural pathologies:

1. **Berkeley's "ghosts of departed quantities" and continuum-limit baggage**: To form an instantaneous rate of change, the difference quotient divides by $\Delta x$ under the premise $\Delta x \neq 0$, only to set $\Delta x = 0$ moments later to erase the remainder—prompting George Berkeley in *The Analyst* (1734) to famously indict infinitesimals as *"the ghosts of departed quantities."* While nineteenth-century Cauchy–Weierstrass $\varepsilon\text{-}\delta$ filters repaired the logical breach within real analysis, they saddled every elementary derivative with infinite topological limit machinery that runs directly counter to embedded observers operating above a finite self-referential halting aperture $\epsilon \ge \varepsilon_{\mathrm{self}} > 0$.
2. **Coordinate-chart vertical-tangent blowup versus genuine intrinsic cusps**: At the equatorial point $(1, 0)$ of the smooth unit circle $x^2 + y^2 = 1$, the curve itself is perfectly analytic with constant intrinsic curvature $\kappa \equiv 1$; yet merely because its tangent turns perpendicular to the horizontal reference axis at $\theta = \frac{\pi}{2}$, the Cartesian graph slope $dy/dx = \tan\theta$ explodes to $\pm\infty$! Differential geometry demands a sharp distinction here: the blowup on the circle at $\theta = \frac{\pi}{2}$ is a pure coordinate-chart projection artifact where $(x \mapsto y(x))$ hits the observer's $90^\circ$ orthogonal blind zone (dissolving immediately under arc-length parameterization), whereas the singularity of the semicubical parabola $y^2 = x^3$ at the origin is a genuine intrinsic cusp where unit tangent direction reverses discontinuously. Classical slope notation conflates chart blind spots with intrinsic manifold singularities.
3. **Amputation of the second-order sagitta, erasing curvature and quadratic variation alike**: In [§29.9](ch34-s08-clifford-monads.md#sec-ch34-clifford-monad-tiling), we examined the first-order nilpotent ring $D_1 = \{d \mid d^2 = 0\}$ of Lawvere (1967) and Kock (1981) Synthetic Differential Geometry (SDG), alongside the dual numbers $\mathbb{R}[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)$ of Wengert's (1964) forward-mode automatic differentiation. While the nilpotency axiom $d^2 = 0$ dispenses with limits, it algebraically executes the second-order bending sagitta—leaving no room either for Girard's spherical excess $\Omega = \mathcal{O}(\epsilon^2)$ (for the right-angled minimal trihedron with legs $\epsilon$, $\Omega = 2\arctan\bigl(\tan^2\frac{\epsilon}{2}\bigr) = \frac{1}{2}\epsilon^2 + \frac{1}{12}\epsilon^4 + \cdots$; for a general triangle the second-order coefficient depends on its shape) or for the nonvanishing Brownian/Feynman quadratic variation $(dW_t)^2 = dt \neq 0$!
4. **Catastrophic subtractive cancellation in finite-precision arithmetic**: As step size $\epsilon$ shrinks toward machine precision, $f(x_0 + \epsilon)$ and $f(x_0)$ agree across nearly all leading mantissa bits; subtracting them along the real axis annihilates significant digits and amplifies rounding noise by $\epsilon^{-1}$, destroying numerical derivatives altogether.

## 30.3.2 The Path-Ordered Rotor Product Integral and the Four-Regime Roughness Phase Transition of $\Sigma_{\mathrm{sag}}(\epsilon, \alpha)$ {#sec-ch34-30-2}

By contrast, Volterra's (1887) **path-ordered rotor product integral** $\mathcal{I}_\epsilon[\Gamma] = \hat{\mathcal{D}}_\epsilon(s_N)\cdots\hat{\mathcal{D}}_\epsilon(s_1)$ in Clause 2 of [@def-ch34-arc-body-calculus-primitives] enjoys two structural guarantees:

* **Zero norm drift and exact Lie-manifold preservation at every finite $\epsilon > 0$**: Because each factor $\hat{\mathcal{D}}_\epsilon(s_k) = \exp(\hat{\mathbf{B}}(s_k)\kappa(s_k)\epsilon)$ is an exact group element, their ordered product $\mathcal{I}_\epsilon[\Gamma]$ remains on the unit sphere or compact Lie group with **identically zero norm drift** at any step size $\epsilon > 0$ and step count $N$!

For split non-commuting generators $A + B$, continuum convergence is machine-certified by [`trotter_product`](lean:D5/S3/Quantum/Analysis/TrotterProduct.trotter_product) and the non-asymptotic remainder bound [`exp_tail_le`](lean:D5/S3/Observer/Linear/ExactTaylorObservationRemainder.exp_tail_le) in Clause 3 of [@thm-ch34-arc-rotor-calculus-and-finite-stokes].

Standard textbooks present Newton–Leibniz differentials $df = f'(x)dx$ and Itô's (1944) extra second-order drift $df = f'(X_t)dX_t + \frac{1}{2}\sigma_0^2 f''(X_t)dt$ as separate worlds. Partition $[0, T]$ into $N = T/\epsilon$ uniform steps along a trajectory of **local Hölder roughness exponent $\alpha \in (0, 1]$**, so that typical per-step angular increments scale as $\lvert\Delta\theta_k\rvert = \sigma_0 \epsilon^\alpha$ ($\sigma_0 > 0$). $$
\Sigma_{\mathrm{sag}}(\epsilon, \alpha) := \sum_{k=1}^{N} \bigl(1 - \cos\Delta\theta_k\bigr) = \sum_{k=1}^{N} 2\sin^2\!\left(\frac{\Delta\theta_k}{2}\right) = \frac{1}{2}\sigma_0^2 T\,\epsilon^{2\alpha - 1} - \frac{1}{24}\sigma_0^4 T\,\epsilon^{4\alpha - 1} + \mathcal{O}\!\left(\epsilon^{6\alpha - 1}\right)!

   **The famous $\frac{1}{2}\sigma_0^2 f''(X_t)\,dt$ term in Itô's lemma is no mysterious stochastic patch—for angular Brownian motion on a circle with observables $\cos$ and $\sin$, it is literally the macroscopic cumulative circular-arc sagitta $\Sigma_{\mathrm{sag}}$ that classical tangent linearization threw away, surviving unattenuated (in expectation) at the critical exponent $\alpha = \frac{1}{2}$!** Better still, circular-arc geometry resolves the celebrated **Itô (left-endpoint) versus Stratonovich–Feynman (circular-arc midpoint)** dichotomy in one picture:

   * Stepping off each left endpoint $t_{k-1}$ along a **flat tangent line** (non-anticipating sampling preserving adapted martingale structure) drops off the curved manifold by the exact per-step sagitta $v_k \approx \frac{1}{2}(\Delta X_k)^2$, forcing **Itô calculus** to add the missing curvature sagitta $\frac{1}{2}\sigma_0^2 f''(X_t)\,dt$ back by hand;
   * Evaluating along the **symmetric circular-arc midpoint** $\frac{X_{t_{k-1}} + X_{t_k}}{2}$ absorbs rising and falling half-arc sagittas symmetrically inside the rotor step, restoring the coordinate-free chain rule $df(X_t) = f'(X_t) \circ dX_t$—yielding manifold-covariant **Stratonovich (1966) integration** (ordinary integrals along smooth approximating paths converge to the Stratonovich integral: E. Wong & M. Zakai, *Ann. Math. Statist.* **36**, 1560–1564, 1965, `Literature`) and explaining why Feynman path integrals coupled to a magnetic vector potential $\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{x})$ must use **midpoint action discretization** $\mathbf{A}\!\bigl(\frac{\mathbf{x}_k + \mathbf{x}_{k-1}}{2}\bigr)\cdot(\mathbf{x}_k - \mathbf{x}_{k-1})$ to preserve electromagnetic $U(1)$ gauge invariance! *(Note: as audited in [§29.12](ch34-s11-quantum-arcs-and-macro-integrals.md#sec-ch34-quantum-arc-and-macro-integral) [@rem-ch34-feynman-ito-trotter-regge-literature], imaginary-time Euclidean diffusion enjoys a countably additive positive Wiener measure via the Feynman–Kac formula Kac 1949, whereas real-time Lorentzian Feynman amplitudes rely on oscillatory Fresnel cancellation and admit no bounded-variation complex measure on path space Cameron 1960; both short-time kernels nevertheless share the exact $\alpha = \frac{1}{2}$ critical scaling and midpoint sagitta algebra.)*

4. **Regime IV: $\alpha < \frac{1}{2}$ Super-Rough Divergent Phase (Lyons Iterated-Area Signatures versus Finite Halting $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{self}} > 0$)**:
   Once roughness crosses below $\alpha < \frac{1}{2}$ (e.g., turbulent cascades or anti-persistent fractional Brownian motion at $H = \frac{1}{3}$), $2\alpha - 1 < 0$ sends second-order cumulative sagitta diverging to $\Sigma_{\mathrm{sag}}(\epsilon, \alpha) \propto \epsilon^{2\alpha - 1} \to +\infty$ as $\epsilon \to 0^+$ (with quartic remainders $\epsilon^{4\alpha - 1}$ diverging as well once $\alpha < \frac{1}{4}$; at $\alpha = \frac{1}{4}$ the quartic term is the constant $-\frac{1}{24}\sigma_0^4 T$)! Terry Lyons's (1998, literature result) **Rough Paths Theory** proves that well-posed continuum integration of $\alpha$-Hölder paths requires lifting increments into the truncated tensor algebra of degree $p = \lfloor 1/\alpha\rfloor$—specifically supplying for $\frac{1}{3} < \alpha \le \frac{1}{2}$ the second-level iterated integral $\mathbb{X}_{s,t}^{(2)} = \int_s^t (X_u - X_s)\otimes dX_u$, whose antisymmetric part is the Lévy area bivector, readable as the oriented area spanned by each minimal circular-arc body—whereas for any physical observer operating above a nonzero halting aperture $\epsilon \ge \varepsilon_{\mathrm{self}} > 0$, finite step resolution provides an intrinsic ultraviolet floor.

**The Path-Ordered Rotor Tube and the Sagitta Phase Transition: At $\alpha = \frac{1}{2}$ the Sagitta Sum Locks onto $0.5$ (`roughness-ito-phase-transition`).** Trace the violet path-ordered rotor tube of $N = 1/\epsilon$ unit-modulus steps of angle $\sigma_0\epsilon^\alpha$ across the unit sphere while dragging the amber handle for roughness exponent $\alpha$: as $\alpha$ drops from the smooth phase $\alpha = 1$ to the critical Brownian/Feynman threshold $\alpha = \frac{1}{2}$, the magenta cumulative sagitta bar $\Sigma_{\mathrm{sag}}$ rises from $0.0005$ and locks onto $0.5$ (Itô's $\frac{1}{2}\sigma_0^2 T$ drift), depressing the magenta Itô latitude ring $\mathbb{E}[z] = (1 - v_\epsilon)^N$ in lockstep (green `LEAN ✓` badges anchor `exp_tail_le` and `trotter_product`).

The expansion above gives only the leading terms. The frozen library has a non-asymptotic version: two-sided bounds that hold for every finite $N$, and the three limit regimes that follow from them.

::: {.lemma #lem-ch34-accumulated-sagitta-bounds title="Two-Sided Bounds on the Accumulated Sagitta at Every Finite Step Count" lean="D5/S3/Analytic/Asymptotics/AccumulatedSagittaPhases.accumulated_sagitta_bounds"}
Let $\sigma,T,\alpha$ be real numbers and $N$ a natural number. Write $\theta_N=\sigma\,(T/N)^{\alpha}$ for the step angle and $\Sigma_N=N\,(1-\cos\theta_N)$ for the accumulated sagitta. Then

$$
N\left(\frac{\theta_N^2}{2}-\frac{\theta_N^4}{24}\right)\ \le\ \Sigma_N\ \le\ N\,\frac{\theta_N^2}{2}.
$$

Division and real powers follow Lean's total-function conventions here: $T/N=0$ when $N=0$, and real powers of a negative base are those defined in Mathlib. The inequality carries no sign or size condition on $\sigma,T,\alpha$.
:::

::: proof
Both sides come from the pointwise cosine bounds $\frac{x^2}{2}-\frac{x^4}{24}\le1-\cos x\le\frac{x^2}{2}$, valid for every real $x$, taken at $x=\theta_N$ and multiplied by $N\ge0$. The upper bound is $\cos x\ge1-\frac{x^2}{2}$. For the lower bound, write $1-\cos x=2\sin^2\frac x2$ and use $\lvert y-\sin y\rvert\le\frac{\lvert y\rvert^3}{6}$ at $y=\frac x2$: expanding $\sin^2y=\bigl(y-(y-\sin y)\bigr)^2$ and discarding the square of the difference gives $\sin^2y\ge y^2-\frac{y^4}{3}$. The full proof is checked by the Lean kernel.
:::

::: {.theorem #thm-ch34-accumulated-sagitta-phases title="The Three Limit Regimes of the Accumulated Sagitta: $\alpha$ Above, At and Below $\frac{1}{2}$" lean="D5/S3/Analytic/Asymptotics/AccumulatedSagittaPhases.accumulated_sagitta_phases"}
Let $\sigma,T,\alpha$ be real numbers with $T>0$, and let $\theta_N$ and $\Sigma_N$ be as in [@lem-ch34-accumulated-sagitta-bounds]. As $N\to\infty$:

1. if $\alpha>\frac12$, then $\Sigma_N\to0$;
2. if $\alpha=\frac12$, then $\Sigma_N\to\frac{\sigma^2T}{2}$;
3. if $\sigma\neq0$ and $0<\alpha<\frac12$, then $\Sigma_N\to+\infty$.
:::

::: proof
For positive $N$ one has $N\theta_N^2=c\,N^{1-2\alpha}$ and $\theta_N^2=c\,N^{-2\alpha}$ with $c=\sigma^2T^{2\alpha}$. When $\alpha>\frac12$, the upper bound of [@lem-ch34-accumulated-sagitta-bounds] tends to $0$ while $\Sigma_N\ge0$. When $\alpha=\frac12$, the upper bound is the constant $\frac{\sigma^2T}{2}$ and the lower bound is $\frac{\sigma^2T}{2}-\frac{(\sigma^2T)^2}{24N}$, and the two squeeze the limit. When $0<\alpha<\frac12$, $\theta_N^2\to0$, so from some step on it is at most $6$, and from then on the lower bound is at least $\frac c4N^{1-2\alpha}$; $\sigma\neq0$ makes $c>0$, hence the divergence. The full proof is checked by the Lean kernel.
:::

With $\epsilon=T/N$ and $\sigma=\sigma_0$, $\Sigma_N$ is the $\Sigma_{\mathrm{sag}}(\epsilon,\alpha)$ above: Regimes I and II correspond to item 1, Regime III to item 2, and Regime IV to item 3. The theorem does not cover $\alpha\le0$: for $\alpha=0$ and $\sigma=2\pi$ every step is a full turn and $\Sigma_N\equiv0$, so the condition $\alpha>0$ in item 3 cannot be dropped (manual derivation). Nor does the theorem concern paths with unequal steps or random signs; the statements above about the Itô correction and rough-path theory keep their own labels as literature results.

::: {.remark #rem-ch34-heap-recurrence-and-sagitta-sums title="The Heap Recurrence, the Exact One-Step Split, and Sagitta Sums over Arbitrary Partitions (Manual Derivation and Literature)"}
**The heap recurrence.** For a sequence in $S^3(1)$ the following are equivalent: $x_{n+1} = [x_n, x_{n-1}, x_n]$; $x_n = g^n x_0$ ($g = x_1\bar{x}_0$); the linear form $x_{n+1} = 2c\,x_n - x_{n-1}$ ($c = \langle x_0, x_1\rangle$). Then $x_n = U_{n-1}(c)\,x_1 - U_{n-2}(c)\,x_0$ ($U_k$ the Chebyshev polynomials of the second kind), i.e. the samples at integer times of the constant-speed great-circle geodesic, and $x_{n+1} - 2x_n + x_{n-1} = -2v\,x_n$ ($v = 1 - c$): the discrete acceleration is normal, of size twice the sagitta. These are classical in substance (O. Loos, *Symmetric Spaces I*, Benjamin, 1969; J. C. Mason & D. C. Handscomb, *Chebyshev Polynomials*, 2003, `Literature`). For a $C^4$ curve $x(t)$ the residual of the recurrence is

$$
x(t + h) - \bigl[x(t), x(t - h), x(t)\bigr] \;=\; h^2\,\nabla_t\dot{x}(t) - \frac{h^3}{2}\,\frac{d}{dt}\lvert\dot{x}\rvert^2(t)\,x(t) + \mathcal{O}(h^4),
$$

with $\nabla_t\dot{x} = \ddot{x} + \lvert\dot{x}\rvert^2 x$ the covariant acceleration; the residual vanishes for all small $h$ if and only if $x$ is a constant-speed geodesic (`manual derivation`).

**The exact one-step split (`manual derivation`).** For $x, y \in S^3(1)$,

$$
y - x \;=\; \operatorname{Im}(y\bar{x})\,x \;-\; v(x, y)\,x, \qquad \operatorname{Im}(y\bar{x})\,x = P_x(y - x), \qquad \lvert\operatorname{Im}(y\bar{x})\rvert = \sin d(x, y),
$$

where $P_x$ is the orthogonal projection onto $T_x S^3$ and $v(x, y) = 1 - \cos d(x, y) = \frac{1}{2}\lVert y - x\rVert^2$: every step is exactly a tangential rotation plus a radial sagitta.

**Sagitta sums over arbitrary partitions (the deterministic part is a reformulation of H. Föllmer's pathwise quadratic variation, *Séminaire de Probabilités XV*, 1981; `Literature` + `manual derivation`).** For a continuous path on $[0, T]$ and a partition $\pi$, with $d_k = d(x_k, x_{k+1})$ and $v_k = v(x_k, x_{k+1})$, one has $\sum_k v_k \le \frac{1}{2}\max_k d_k\sum_k d_k$, so the sagitta sum of a rectifiable path tends to $0$ as the mesh is refined; if the path has quadratic variation $[x]$ along a sequence of partitions, then $\sum_k v_k f(x_k) \to \frac{1}{2}\int_0^T f(x_t)\,d[x]_t$ for continuous $f$; and for Brownian motion on $S^3$ (generator $\frac{1}{2}\Delta$) and any sequence of deterministic partitions with mesh tending to zero, in probability,

$$
\sum_k v_k \;\to\; \frac{3}{2}\,T, \qquad \sum_k v_k\,x_k \;\to\; \frac{3}{2}\int_0^T x_t\,dt,
$$

the latter being the Itô drift; for the fluctuations, along dyadic partitions $2^{n/2}\bigl(\sum_k v_k - \frac{3}{2}T\bigr)$ converges in law to $N\bigl(0, \frac{3}{2}T^2\bigr)$ (J. Jacod & P. Protter, *Discretization of Processes*, Springer, 2012, `Literature`). This extends the equal-step case of [@thm-ch34-accumulated-sagitta-phases] to arbitrary partitions and general paths; the constant $\frac{1}{2}\sigma_0^2 T$ on the circle above is the one-dimensional case $d[x]_t = \sigma_0^2\,dt$.
:::

Recast the Cayley ratio $w=(s-1)/s$ of [§22.4](ch22-s04-critical-line-and-rh.md#sec-ch22-4) in the language of this section: it is a rotor. For a point $\rho=\tfrac12+i\gamma$ on the critical line ($\gamma>0$), $u_\rho:=1-\tfrac1\rho$ lies on the unit circle; its chord $\Delta_\rho=u_\rho-1=-\tfrac1\rho$ has length $1/\lvert\rho\rvert$, its sagitta is $v_\rho=\operatorname{Re}\tfrac1\rho=\tfrac{2}{1+4\gamma^2}$, and the two obey the exact chord–sagitta identity of §30.3.1, $\lvert\Delta_\rho\rvert^2=2v_\rho$ (manual derivation). Sum $1-u_\rho^{\,n}$ over all nontrivial zeros of $\zeta$, counted with multiplicity and whether or not they lie on the critical line, and the resulting number has a name in analytic number theory: the Li coefficient (literature result; see [@rem-ch34-li-criterion-sagitta-literature] below). For a zero on the critical line the real part of the term, $1-\operatorname{Re}u_\rho^{\,n}$, is exactly the sagitta of the $n$-th power of the rotor $u_\rho$; whether every zero lies on the line is the open [@conj-riemann-hypothesis]. The pinned repository freezes the Li coefficients in their derivative definition, together with the following facts.

::: {.proposition #prop-ch34-li-sagitta-frozen title="Chord–Sagitta Identities for Cayley Nodes, the Derivative-Defined Li Coefficients, and Their Two Relations to `RiemannHypothesis`" lean="D5/S3/Weil/FiniteSpectralCayleyIdentity.cayleyNode_normSq D5/S3/Weil/FiniteSpectralCayleyIdentity.cayleyNode_one_sub_normSq D5/S3/Weil/FiniteSpectralCayleyIdentity.finiteLiCoefficient_eq_diagonalHilbertSchmidtDefect D5/S3/Zeros/Endpoints/CanonicalLiLocalExpansion.canonical_li_one D5/S3/Weil/ZeroData/ActualReciprocalSquareBounds.actual_complex_pairWeight_hasSum D5/S3/Weil/Probability/CanonicalLiNonnegativeConverse.canonical_li_nonnegative_implies_rh D5/S3/Weil/Probability/CanonicalLiDiskEquivalence.rh_iff_canonical_li_disk_summable D5/S3/Weil/ZeroData/UnconditionalCanonicalZeroData.zetaZeroData_representation_iff"}
Let $\xi$ be the $\operatorname{xiReading}$ of [@def-xi-reading], let `RiemannHypothesis` be Mathlib's proposition (spelled out in item 2 of [@thm-cayley-critical-line-criterion]), and let $\gamma_{\mathrm{EM}}$ be the Euler–Mascheroni constant.

1. **Cayley nodes.** For real $\gamma$ put $x(\gamma)=\dfrac{1}{4\gamma^2+1}$ and $c(\gamma)=\bigl(1-2x(\gamma)\bigr)+2\sqrt{x(\gamma)\bigl(1-x(\gamma)\bigr)}\;i$. Then $\lvert c(\gamma)\rvert^2=1$ and $\lvert1-c(\gamma)\rvert^2=4x(\gamma)$; moreover, for every finite index set $J$, every $\gamma:J\to\mathbb{R}$ and every natural number $n$,

   $$
   \sum_{j\in J}2\bigl(1-\operatorname{Re}c(\gamma_j)^n\bigr)=\sum_{j\in J}\bigl\lvert1-c(\gamma_j)^n\bigr\rvert^2 .
   $$

2. **Li coefficients.** Put $\lambda_0=0$ and, for each natural number $n$, $\lambda_{n+1}=\operatorname{Re}\left[\dfrac{1}{n!}\,\left.\dfrac{d^{\,n+1}}{ds^{\,n+1}}\bigl(s^n\operatorname{Log}\xi(s)\bigr)\right|_{s=1}\right]$, where $\operatorname{Log}$ is the principal branch of the complex logarithm. Then $\lambda_1=1+\dfrac{\gamma_{\mathrm{EM}}}{2}-\ln\bigl(2\sqrt\pi\bigr)$.
3. **The same number on the zero side.** Let $\rho_0,\rho_1,\dots$ be the enumeration of the nontrivial zeros provided unconditionally by the pinned repository (its order is fixed by the axiom of choice; the zeros are pairwise distinct, and each zeta zero in the critical strip occurs exactly once), and let $m_k$ be the analytic multiplicity of $\rho_k$. Then the series $\sum_k\dfrac{m_k}{\rho_k(1-\rho_k)}$ converges to the same sum in every order of summation (Lean's `HasSum`), with sum $2+\gamma_{\mathrm{EM}}-2\ln\bigl(2\sqrt\pi\bigr)$.
4. **Nonnegativity implies `RiemannHypothesis`.** If $\lambda_n\ge0$ for every natural number $n\ge1$, then `RiemannHypothesis` holds.
5. **Disk-summability criterion.** `RiemannHypothesis` holds if and only if, for every real $0\le r<1$, the series $\sum_{n\ge0}\lvert\lambda_{n+1}\rvert\,r^n$ converges.
:::

::: proof
Item 1 adds the squares of the real and imaginary parts of $c(\gamma)$, using $\bigl(\sqrt{x(1-x)}\bigr)^2=x(1-x)$ for $0<x\le1$; the summed identity expands $\lvert1-w\rvert^2=1-2\operatorname{Re}w+\lvert w\rvert^2$ term by term and inserts $\lvert c^n\rvert=1$. Item 2: for $n=0$ the definition reduces to the real part of $\xi'(1)/\xi(1)$, whose closed form is a frozen result of the same repository and uses Mathlib's asymptotics of zeta near $s=1$. Item 3 evaluates the partial-fraction expansion of $\xi'/\xi$ over the zeros at $s=1$, then uses the reflection $\xi'/\xi(0)=-\xi'/\xi(1)$ and the closed form of item 2. Item 4: $\lambda_{n+1}$ is the Taylor coefficient at $z=0$ of $(1-z)^{-2}\,(\xi'/\xi)\bigl(\tfrac{1}{1-z}\bigr)$; a power series with nonnegative coefficients has a singularity at its radius of convergence on the positive real axis, whereas this function is analytic at every point of the real segment $0\le z<1$, because the zeros of $\xi$ lie in the critical strip and $\tfrac{1}{1-z}\ge1$ is not one of them; hence the radius is at least $1$ and the sum $L(z)$ of the series is analytic in the unit disk; $\varphi(z)=\xi\bigl(\tfrac1{1-z}\bigr)$ satisfies $\varphi'=L\varphi$ near the origin, and by analytic continuation throughout the disk; if $\varphi$ vanished at a point of the disk, all its derivatives would vanish there and $\varphi$ would be identically zero, contradicting $\varphi(0)=\tfrac12$; so $\xi\bigl(\tfrac1{1-z}\bigr)$ has no zero in the unit disk, that is, $\xi$ has no zero with $\operatorname{Re}s>\tfrac12$, and the right-half-strip reduction yields `RiemannHypothesis`. The forward direction of item 5 derives analyticity of that function in the unit disk from `RiemannHypothesis`; the converse repeats the last step of item 4. The full proof is checked by the Lean kernel.
:::

The node $c(\gamma)$ of item 1 is the zero rotor: for $\gamma\ge0$, $c(\gamma)=u_\rho=1-\tfrac{1}{1/2+i\gamma}$; for $\gamma<0$, $c(\gamma)=c(-\gamma)=\overline{u_\rho}$ (the square root in the definition is nonnegative); in both cases $x(\gamma)=\tfrac12v_\rho$ (manual derivation). So the summed identity of item 1 says that twice the total sagitta of the $n$-th powers equals the total squared chord of the $n$-th powers. It is the chord–sagitta identity applied to $u^n$; it holds for any family of **real** $\gamma_j$ and says nothing about where the zeros of $\zeta$ lie. The sum in item 3 is exactly twice the closed form of item 2, hence $\lambda_1=\sum_k m_k\cdot\tfrac{1}{2\rho_k(1-\rho_k)}$ (manual derivation). Item 4 gives a sufficient condition for `RiemannHypothesis` and item 5 an equivalent form of it; neither premise (all $\lambda_n\ge0$; summability for every $r<1$) has been proved, and the Riemann hypothesis ([@conj-riemann-hypothesis]) remains open.

::: {.remark #rem-ch34-li-criterion-sagitta-literature title="Li's Criterion Read Through the Sagitta (Literature result, not formalized in this book)"}
**Literature result, not formalized in this book.** By the Hadamard product, the $\lambda_n$ of item 2 of [@prop-ch34-li-sagitta-frozen] equals $\sum_\rho\bigl[1-(1-\tfrac1\rho)^n\bigr]$, summed over all nontrivial zeros with multiplicity, truncated symmetrically at $\lvert\operatorname{Im}\rho\rvert\le H$ and then letting $H\to\infty$ (X.-J. Li, *J. Number Theory* **65**, 325–333, 1997). The functional equation pairs $\rho$ with $1-\rho$, and their rotors are reciprocal: $u_{1-\rho}=u_\rho^{-1}$. Put $v_\rho:=1-\tfrac12\bigl(u_\rho+u_\rho^{-1}\bigr)=\dfrac{1}{2\rho(1-\rho)}$; on the critical line this is the $\operatorname{Re}\tfrac1\rho$ used above, and off the line it is a complex number. Such a pair contributes $2\bigl[1-T_n(1-v_\rho)\bigr]$, with $T_n$ the Chebyshev polynomial of the first kind, so that

$$
\lambda_n=\sum_\rho\bigl[1-T_n(1-v_\rho)\bigr]
$$

(manual derivation resting on the literature result above). The right-hand side converges absolutely, so the truncation limit can be dropped: $1-T_n(1-v)$ is a polynomial in $v$ with no constant term, and the summability in item 3 of [@prop-ch34-li-sagitta-frozen] gives $\sum_k m_k\lvert v_{\rho_k}\rvert<\infty$ (manual derivation). At a zero $\rho=\tfrac12+i\gamma$ on the critical line, $v_\rho=\tfrac{2}{1+4\gamma^2}$ is a real number in $(0,2)$; writing $1-v_\rho=\cos\theta_\rho$, the bracket equals $1-\cos n\theta_\rho\ge0$. At a zero $\rho=\beta+i\gamma$ off the line $v_\rho$ is not real, because $\operatorname{Im}\rho(1-\rho)=\gamma(1-2\beta)$ and $\zeta$ has no real zero in $(0,1)$ ([@prop-zeta-real-axis-sign]), so $\gamma\ne0$.

**Li's criterion** (Li 1997; E. Bombieri & J. C. Lagarias, *J. Number Theory* **77**, 274–287, 1999): [@conj-riemann-hypothesis] holds if and only if $\lambda_n\ge0$ for all $n\ge1$. That the conjecture implies nonnegativity is the termwise nonnegativity of the formula above; it depends on the zero-sum representation and is not frozen in the pinned repository. That nonnegativity implies the conjecture is item 4 of [@prop-ch34-li-sagitta-frozen]. Li's criterion is an equivalent restatement of the Riemann hypothesis, not a proof: it trades "the zeros lie on a line" for "infinitely many inequalities"; the criterion needs all of them, and checking finitely many is neither a proof of the conjecture nor progress toward one.
:::

::: {.example #ex-ch34-zero-rotor-sagitta-numbers title="Chord and Sagitta of the First Zero, the First Four Li Coefficients, and a Hypothetical Off-Line Quartet"}
The zero ordinates in this example are numerical values (literature result, not formalized in this book); the other numbers were computed with 30 digits of precision and rounded as shown.

**The first zero.** $\gamma_1=14.134725$: turning angle $\theta_1=2\arctan\tfrac{1}{2\gamma_1}=0.0707183$, sagitta $v_1=\tfrac{2}{1+4\gamma_1^2}=0.00249949$, chord $\tfrac{1}{\lvert\rho_1\rvert}=0.0707035$; and $1/\lvert\rho_1\rvert^2=0.00499899=2v_1$.

**The first four Li coefficients**, computed directly from the definition in item 2 of [@prop-ch34-li-sagitta-frozen]:

| $n$ | $\lambda_n$ | $\lambda_n/(n^2\lambda_1)$ |
| :---: | :---: | :---: |
| $1$ | $0.0230957$ | $1$ |
| $2$ | $0.0923457$ | $0.999598$ |
| $3$ | $0.2076389$ | $0.998930$ |
| $4$ | $0.3687905$ | $0.997995$ |

$\lambda_1$ agrees with the closed form $1+\tfrac{\gamma_{\mathrm{EM}}}{2}-\ln(2\sqrt\pi)$ of item 2. The third column sits just below $1$. For a rotor on the unit circle, $1-\cos n\theta\le n^2(1-\cos\theta)$ (manual derivation from $\lvert\sin nx\rvert\le n\lvert\sin x\rvert$); hence, under the hypothesis that all zeros lie on the critical line, the formula of [@rem-ch34-li-criterion-sagitta-literature] gives $\lambda_n\le n^2\lambda_1$ (a conditional derivation whose hypothesis is the open conjecture). The four ratios in the table are consistent with this inequality. That these four numbers are positive is a numerical fact, not a statement about all $n$.

**A hypothetical off-line quartet.** Take $\tfrac{7}{10}\pm5i$ and $\tfrac{3}{10}\pm5i$ (these are not zeros of zeta). The four points are closed under $s\mapsto1-s$ and under conjugation, yet $\operatorname{Re}\sum\bigl[1-(1-\tfrac1\rho)^{n}\bigr]$ first turns negative at $n=31$, where it equals $-0.0943$; negativity at $n=31$ is frozen as [`li_positivity_distinguishes_the_off_line_toy_spectrum`](lean:D5/S3/Zeros/ToySpectrum/OffLineToySpectrum.li_positivity_distinguishes_the_off_line_toy_spectrum). A different choice can delay the sign change considerably: move the fake quartet to $\tfrac12\pm0.1\pm14i$; on its own, its sum first turns negative at $n=88$; combined with the first $80$ pairs of actual zeros into one finite family, the nonnegative on-line contributions postpone the first negative value to $n=8446$ (where the sum is $-0.858$). This is a numerical fact about this one finite family, not a statement about the $\lambda_n$ of zeta and not a general rule. Still, as soon as a finite family has a rotor outside the closed unit disk, a negative value must eventually occur ([@rem-ch34-finite-rotor-li-criterion]).
:::

Both hypothetical quartets in the example are closed under $s\mapsto1-s$ and under conjugation, and both contain rotors that leave the unit circle; on its own or joined to finitely many on-line pairs, each makes $\operatorname{Re}\sum\bigl[1-(1-\tfrac1\rho)^{n}\bigr]$ negative sooner or later. For finitely many rotors this has an exact formulation. It says nothing about the zeros of $\zeta$: there are infinitely many of them, which is exactly where the difficulty lies, and a conclusion about a finite truncation of the zeros does not transfer to $\lambda_n$.

::: {.remark #rem-ch34-finite-rotor-li-criterion title="Li's Criterion for a Finite Family of Rotors (Literature result, not formalized in this book)"}
**Literature result, not formalized in this book.** Let $u_1,\dots,u_m$ be complex numbers (zeros and repetitions allowed), and for $n\ge1$ put $\Lambda_n=\operatorname{Re}\sum_j\bigl(1-u_j^{\,n}\bigr)$. The following are equivalent:

1. $\lvert u_j\rvert\le1$ for every $j$;
2. $\Lambda_n\ge0$ for every $n\ge1$;
3. for every $\varepsilon>0$ there is a constant $C$ with $\Lambda_n\ge-C\,e^{\varepsilon n}$ for every $n\ge1$.

This is the finite case of Theorem 1 of E. Bombieri and J. C. Lagarias (*J. Number Theory* **77**, 274–287, 1999). Their sum is $\sum_\rho\operatorname{Re}\bigl[1-(1-\tfrac1\rho)^{-n}\bigr]$; with $u=\rho/(\rho-1)$, the condition $\lvert u\rvert\le1$ is exactly $\operatorname{Re}\rho\le\tfrac12$. This $u$ is the reciprocal of the zero rotor $u_\rho=1-\tfrac1\rho$ of this section, so the half-plane points the other way from the one above (manual derivation). Entries $u_j=1$ contribute nothing to $\Lambda_n$ and can be dropped first. No symmetry is needed. If the moduli pair off as reciprocals (for each $j$ there is a $j'$ with $\lvert u_{j'}\rvert\,\lvert u_j\rvert=1$, as when the family is closed under $u\mapsto u^{-1}$), item 1 applied to $u_j$ and to $u_{j'}$ gives $\lvert u_j\rvert=1$: item 2 holds exactly when all the rotors lie on the unit circle (manual derivation).

Item 1 implies item 2 term by term, $1-\operatorname{Re}u_j^{\,n}\ge1-\lvert u_j\rvert^n\ge0$, and item 2 trivially implies item 3. The converse rests on a recurrence (manual derivation). Write each nonzero $u_j$ as $\lvert u_j\rvert e^{i\phi_j}$, let $g=(e^{i\phi_j})_j$, and for a given positive integer $L$ let $h=g^L$. By item 1 of [@thm-torus-power-orbit-closure], a point lies in the closure of $\{h^k:k\ge0\}$ exactly when it satisfies every integer relation $\prod_jh_j^{k_j}=1$ that $h$ satisfies; $h^{-1}$ obviously does, since $\prod_jh_j^{-k_j}=\bigl(\prod_jh_j^{k_j}\bigr)^{-1}$. So $h^{-1}$ lies in that closure: for every neighbourhood $U$ of $1$ there is $k\ge0$ with $h^k\in h^{-1}U$, that is, $g^n\in U$ with $n=L(k+1)\ge L$. Since $L$ is arbitrary, for every $\varepsilon>0$ there are infinitely many $n$ with $\cos n\phi_j>1-\varepsilon$ for all $j$ at once. For such $n$,

$$
\operatorname{Re}\sum_ju_j^{\,n}=\sum_j\lvert u_j\rvert^n\cos n\phi_j\ \ge\ (1-\varepsilon)\sum_j\lvert u_j\rvert^n .
$$

If some $\lvert u_j\rvert=r>1$, taking $\varepsilon=\tfrac12$ gives infinitely many $n$ with $\Lambda_n=m-\operatorname{Re}\sum_ju_j^{\,n}\le m-\tfrac12r^n$; choosing $\varepsilon'>0$ with $e^{\varepsilon'}<r$, this contradicts item 3 (manual derivation). In the language of sagittas: the accumulated sagittas of finitely many rotors are nonnegative at every power exactly when no rotor leaves the closed unit disk.

Two cautions (manual derivation). First, looking at only some of the powers may not reveal it. The four points $2e^{\pm2\pi i/3}$, $\tfrac12e^{\pm2\pi i/3}$ are closed under $u\mapsto u^{-1}$ and under conjugation, and two of them lie outside the circle. With $\omega=e^{2\pi i/3}$ one has $\Lambda_n=4-\bigl(2^n+2^{-n}\bigr)\bigl(\omega^n+\omega^{-n}\bigr)$, where $\omega^n+\omega^{-n}$ is $2$ if $3\mid n$ and $-1$ otherwise. Since $3$ never divides $2^k$, $\Lambda_{2^k}=4+2^{2^k}+2^{-2^k}>0$ for every $k\ge0$, while $\Lambda_3=-\tfrac{49}{4}$. Positive accumulated sagittas at the repeated-squaring powers $n=2^k$ alone do not rule out rotors outside the circle; this example uses the phase $\tfrac{2\pi}{3}$, which resonates with doubling. Checking along an arithmetic progression $n=d,2d,3d,\dots$ does suffice: apply the equivalence to the $u_j^{\,d}$. Second, for infinitely many rotors the argument needs the Bombieri–Lagarias convergence condition; for the zeros of zeta, "all $\lambda_n\ge0$ implies [@conj-riemann-hypothesis]" is a separate theorem (item 4 of [@prop-ch34-li-sagitta-frozen]), and whether all $\lambda_n$ are nonnegative is open.
:::

There is one setting in which "every accumulated sagitta is nonnegative" is a theorem rather than an open problem: elliptic curves over finite fields.

::: {.remark #rem-ch34-finite-field-li-sums title="Li Sums of an Elliptic Curve over a Finite Field (Literature result, not formalized in this book)"}
**Literature result, not formalized in this book.** Let $E$ be an elliptic curve over the finite field $\mathbb{F}_q$, let $N_n$ be its number of points over $\mathbb{F}_{q^n}$ (the point at infinity included), $a=q+1-N_1$, and let $\alpha,\beta$ be the roots of $x^2-ax+q$; then $N_n=q^n+1-\alpha^n-\beta^n$ (see [@rem-ch29-hasse-gauss-literature] and the literature cited there). Put $u=\alpha/\sqrt q$; since $\alpha\beta=q$, $\beta/\sqrt q=u^{-1}$, and the accumulated sagitta of the two rotors $u,u^{-1}$ is

$$
\Lambda_n=2-\frac{\alpha^n+\beta^n}{q^{n/2}},\qquad N_n=\bigl(q^{n/2}-1\bigr)^2+q^{n/2}\,\Lambda_n
$$

(manual derivation). The two rotors have reciprocal moduli, so by [@rem-ch34-finite-rotor-li-criterion], "$N_n\ge\bigl(q^{n/2}-1\bigr)^2$ for every $n$" is equivalent to $\lvert u\rvert=1$, that is, to $\lvert a\rvert\le2\sqrt q$ (manual derivation). The latter is Hasse's theorem (H. Hasse, 1936), so here every Li sum is nonnegative, and in fact $0\le\Lambda_n\le4$. For example, $y^2=x^3+x+1$ over $\mathbb{F}_7$ has $a=3$ and $N_1,N_2,N_3=5,55,380$, in agreement with the formula (numerical check). The positivity has an independent source here: the degree of an isogeny is a positive definite quadratic form; it is not deduced from accumulated sagittas. No such source is known over number fields.
:::

Back to zeta itself. The classical zero-free argument also rests on the positivity of an accumulated co-sagitta, but it uses only three powers, and this inequality is frozen.

::: {.proposition #prop-ch34-three-four-one-cosagitta title="The 3-4-1 Inequality: $\zeta(1+x)^3\,\zeta(1+x+iy)^4\,\zeta(1+x+2iy)$ Has Modulus at Least 1" lean="D5/S3/Weil/ZetaPntBounds/ZetaBoundsLower.norm_zeta_product_ge_one"}
Let $\zeta$ denote Mathlib's `riemannZeta`. For every real $x>0$ and every real $y$,

$$
\left\lvert\zeta(1+x)^3\,\zeta(1+x+iy)^4\,\zeta(1+x+2iy)\right\rvert\ \ge\ 1 .
$$
:::

::: proof
All three arguments have real part $1+x>1$, where the Euler product converges absolutely. The logarithm of the modulus of the left-hand side is the sum over primes $p$ and $k\ge1$ of $\tfrac1k\,p^{-k(1+x)}\bigl(3+4\cos\phi+\cos2\phi\bigr)$ with $\phi=ky\ln p$. Writing $v=1-\cos\phi$ for the sagitta of the prime-power rotor $p^{-iky}$,

$$
3+4\cos\phi+\cos2\phi=2(1+\cos\phi)^2=2(2-v)^2\ \ge\ 0 ,
$$

so the logarithm is nonnegative and the modulus is at least $1$. In Lean the statement rewrites `riemannZeta` as the $L$-series of the trivial Dirichlet character modulo $1$ and calls Mathlib's inequality of the same shape for Dirichlet $L$-series; Mathlib's proof expands $-\log(1-w)$ for the three Euler factors as Taylor series, and the $n$-th term is $2a^n\bigl(\operatorname{Re}z^n+1\bigr)^2/n$ with $a=p^{-(1+x)}$ and $z=p^{-iy}$, which is the squared co-sagitta above, term by term. The full proof is checked by the Lean kernel.
:::

It follows that $\zeta(1+iy)\neq0$ for $y\ne0$: if zeta vanished at $1+iy$, then as $x\to0^+$ the factor $\zeta(1+x)^3$ would grow like $x^{-3}$, the factor $\zeta(1+x+iy)^4$ would tend to zero at least like $x^4$, and $\zeta(1+x+2iy)$ would stay bounded (since $1+2iy\ne1$), so the product would tend to $0$, contradicting the proposition (manual derivation). The same conclusion can be read directly from [@prop-xi-zeros-strip-zeros]; the argument above is the classical one of Hadamard and de la Vallée Poussin (1896) (literature result). It uses only three powers; the zero-free regions obtained from it and from its refinements all shrink towards the line $\operatorname{Re}s=1$ at large height, and no zero-free strip of the form $\operatorname{Re}s>1-\varepsilon$ with fixed $\varepsilon>0$ is known (literature result). At the other end, nonnegativity of the accumulated sagittas at every power ([@rem-ch34-li-criterion-sagitta-literature]) is equivalent to the open [@conj-riemann-hypothesis].

Li's criterion looks at a single number at each power. Putting the chords of all the powers into one comparison table gives a family of Toeplitz matrices; the pinned repository freezes the following facts about them.

::: {.proposition #prop-ch34-li-curvature-toeplitz title="Toeplitz Matrices of the Li Curvature: Geometric-Polynomial Energy, Positivity of Circle Moments, the All-Ones Readout, an Eigenvalue Bound for Negative Li Values, and Hermite's Real-Root Criterion" lean="D5/S3/Weil/TestFunctions/LiCurvatureCriterion.geometric_energy_second_difference D5/S3/Weil/TestFunctions/LiCurvatureCriterion.circle_moment_toeplitz_posSemidef D5/S3/Weil/TestFunctions/LiCurvatureFiniteReconstruction.recurrence_eq_toeplitz_ones_quadratic D5/S3/Weil/TestFunctions/LiCurvatureFiniteReconstruction.negative_recurrence_bounds_smallest_eigenvalue D5/S3/Zeros/Endpoints/CanonicalLiLocalExpansion.canonical_li_one_pos D5/S3/Weil/Probability/CanonicalLiCurvatureZeroFree.canonical_curvature_posSemidef_implies_rh D5/S3/Constants/NewtonHankelRealRootCriterion.newtonHankel_posSemidef_iff_roots_real"}
Let $\lambda_n$ be the Li coefficients of item 2 of [@prop-ch34-li-sagitta-frozen]. For a complex sequence $c:\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{C}$ and a positive integer $N$, let $T_N(c)$ be the $N\times N$ complex matrix with $(j,k)$ entry $c_{j-k}$ ($0\le j,k<N$).

1. **Energy of the geometric polynomial.** For $z$ on the unit circle put $G_n(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}z^j$ ($G_0=0$). Then for every $n\ge1$,
   $$
   \lvert G_{n+1}(z)\rvert^2-2\lvert G_n(z)\rvert^2+\lvert G_{n-1}(z)\rvert^2=2\operatorname{Re}z^n .
   $$
2. **Toeplitz positivity of circle moments.** Let $\mu$ be a finite Borel measure on the unit circle and $c_k=\int z^{-k}\,d\mu(z)$ ($k\in\mathbb{Z}$). Then $T_N(c)$ is positive semidefinite for every $N\ge1$.
3. **The all-ones readout.** Let a real sequence $(x_n)_{n\ge0}$, a real number $L\ge0$ and a complex sequence $c$ satisfy $x_0=0$, $x_1=L$, $c_0=1$, $c_{-k}=\overline{c_k}$ for every $k$, and $x_{n+1}-2x_n+x_{n-1}=2L\operatorname{Re}c_n$ for every $n\ge1$. Then for every $n\ge1$,
   $$
   x_n=L\cdot\operatorname{Re}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}c_{j-k},
   $$
   that is, $x_n$ is $L$ times the real part of the quadratic form of $T_n(c)$ at the all-ones vector.
4. **A negative readout forces a negative eigenvalue.** Under the hypotheses of item 3, assume also $L>0$, $n\ge1$ and $x_n<0$. Then $T_n(c)$ is Hermitian, its least eigenvalue is at most $\dfrac{x_n}{nL}$, and $\dfrac{x_n}{nL}<0$.
5. **Toeplitz positivity of the Li curvature implies `RiemannHypothesis`.** $\lambda_1>0$. Put $c^{\mathrm{Li}}_0=1$ and, for $k\ne0$,
   $$
   c^{\mathrm{Li}}_k=\frac{\lambda_{\lvert k\rvert+1}-2\lambda_{\lvert k\rvert}+\lambda_{\lvert k\rvert-1}}{2\lambda_1}.
   $$
   If $T_N(c^{\mathrm{Li}})$ is positive semidefinite for every $N\ge1$, then `RiemannHypothesis` holds.
6. **Hermite's real-root criterion.** Let $r_1,\dots,r_d$ be complex numbers (repetitions allowed) whose set of values is closed under complex conjugation. Let $H$ be the $d\times d$ real matrix with $(i,j)$ entry $\tfrac1d\operatorname{Re}\sum_{k=1}^{d}r_k^{\,i+j}$ ($0\le i,j<d$). Then $H$ is positive semidefinite if and only if every $r_k$ is real.
:::

::: proof
Item 1: $G_{n+1}=G_n+z^n$, so $\lvert G_{n+1}\rvert^2-\lvert G_n\rvert^2=1+2\operatorname{Re}\bigl(z^n\overline{G_n}\bigr)$; for $\lvert z\rvert=1$ one has $z^n\overline{G_n}=z+z^2+\dots+z^n$, and two consecutive values differ by exactly $z^n$. Item 2: the quadratic form of $T_N(c)$ at a coefficient vector $a$ equals $\int\bigl\lvert\sum_ja_jz^j\bigr\rvert^2d\mu$, which is nonnegative. Item 3: the second-difference recurrence determines the whole sequence from $x_0,x_1$; the sequence $L\bigl(n+2\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}(n-k)\operatorname{Re}c_k\bigr)$ has the same initial values and second differences, and it is exactly $L$ times the quadratic form at the all-ones vector. Item 4: the all-ones vector has squared norm $n$, its Rayleigh quotient is $x_n/(nL)$, and the least eigenvalue is the infimum of the Rayleigh quotient. Item 5: $\lambda_1>0$ comes from numerical bounds on the closed form in item 2 of [@prop-ch34-li-sagitta-frozen]; the $2\times2$ principal submatrix of $T_{n+1}(c^{\mathrm{Li}})$ at indices $0$ and $n$ gives $\lvert c^{\mathrm{Li}}_n\rvert\le1$, i.e. second differences of absolute value at most $2\lambda_1$; summing twice gives $\lvert\lambda_n\rvert\le\lambda_1n^2$; hence $\sum_n\lvert\lambda_{n+1}\rvert r^n$ converges for every $0\le r<1$, and the disk-summability criterion of item 5 of [@prop-ch34-li-sagitta-frozen] gives `RiemannHypothesis`. Item 6: the quadratic form of $H$ at a real coefficient vector $a$ equals $\tfrac1d\sum_k\operatorname{Re}P(r_k)^2$ with $P(z)=\sum_ia_iz^i$; when every $r_k$ is real each term is a square; if some $r$ is not real, a real interpolating polynomial with $P(r)=i$, $P(\overline r)=-i$ and $P=0$ at the other values makes the form negative. The full proof is checked by the Lean kernel.
:::

Item 1 can be read through the chord–sagitta identity of §30.3.1. For a rotor $u$ on the unit circle, $1-u^n=(1-u)\,G_n(u)$, and the chord–sagitta identity $\lvert1-w\rvert^2=2\bigl(1-\operatorname{Re}w\bigr)$ gives $1-\operatorname{Re}u^n=\bigl(1-\operatorname{Re}u\bigr)\lvert G_n(u)\rvert^2$: the factor $\lvert G_n(u)\rvert^2$ is how much the sagitta of the $n$-th power exceeds that of the first, and it is at most $n^2$; item 1 says that its second difference is $2\cos n\theta$ (manual derivation, $u=e^{i\theta}$). Item 3 holds for any sequence satisfying its hypotheses. Take $x_n=\lambda_n$, $L=\lambda_1$, $c=c^{\mathrm{Li}}$: $c^{\mathrm{Li}}$ is a real even sequence with $c^{\mathrm{Li}}_0=1$, the recurrence is the definition of $c^{\mathrm{Li}}$, and $\lambda_1>0$ by item 5. Hence, unconditionally,

$$
\lambda_n=\lambda_1\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}c^{\mathrm{Li}}_{j-k}\qquad(n\ge1)
$$

(manual derivation). Li's criterion tests $T_n(c^{\mathrm{Li}})$ only at the all-ones vector; Toeplitz positivity asks for nonnegativity at every vector, so the premise of item 5 implies that of item 4 of [@prop-ch34-li-sagitta-frozen] (every $\lambda_n\ge0$). Item 4 says the converse thing: a single $\lambda_n<0$ already gives $T_n(c^{\mathrm{Li}})$ a negative eigenvalue at most $\lambda_n/(n\lambda_1)$ (manual derivation, a special case of item 4).

The other direction needs the zeros. Under the zero-sum representation of [@rem-ch34-li-criterion-sagitta-literature] (literature result), if every zero lies on the critical line, then the second difference of $1-\cos n\theta$ is $2(1-\cos\theta)\cos n\theta$, so $\lambda_{n+1}-2\lambda_n+\lambda_{n-1}=\sum_\rho2v_\rho\cos n\theta_\rho$ and $\lambda_1=\sum_\rho v_\rho$, and

$$
c^{\mathrm{Li}}_k=\sum_\rho\frac{v_\rho}{\lambda_1}\cos k\theta_\rho
$$

is the sequence of circle moments of the probability measure $\sum_\rho\frac{v_\rho}{\lambda_1}\delta_{u_\rho}$, in which each zero rotor is weighted by its own sagitta. By item 2 every $T_N(c^{\mathrm{Li}})$ is then positive semidefinite (a conditional derivation whose hypothesis is the open conjecture, and which uses the literature result above). Together, the Riemann hypothesis is equivalent to "every $T_N(c^{\mathrm{Li}})$ is positive semidefinite": "positive semidefinite implies the conjecture" is item 5; "the conjecture implies positive semidefinite" rests on the zero-sum representation and is not frozen in the pinned repository. The pinned repository also has a conditional equivalence for arbitrary sequences, [`li_curvature_criterion_without_herglotz`](lean:D5/S3/Weil/Probability/LiCurvatureProbabilityCompletion.li_curvature_criterion_without_herglotz); it lists as explicit hypotheses the full Li criterion (including the direction "the conjecture implies nonnegativity", which is not frozen), the second-difference recurrence, and "if the conjecture holds, then $c$ is the moment sequence of a probability measure on the unit circle". The representation theorem on the circle (a sequence with $c_0=1$ and every $T_N$ positive semidefinite is exactly the moment sequence of a unique probability measure on the unit circle) is frozen as [`circle_herglotz_iff`](lean:D5/S3/Weil/Probability/CircleHerglotzCompletion.circle_herglotz_iff) and is no longer a hypothesis. The explicit hypotheses are not proved in the repository for $\lambda_n$. This is one more equivalent reformulation; [@conj-riemann-hypothesis] remains open. A different equivalent reformulation is due to M. Suzuki, *Li coefficients as norms of functions in a model space*, arXiv:2301.05779 (2023): the Riemann hypothesis holds if and only if every $\lambda_n$ is the norm of a certain concrete function (literature result).

Item 6 can also be read in terms of rotors. The Cayley coordinate $w=i\,\dfrac{1+u}{1-u}$ of a rotor $u$ sends the unit circle (with $u=1$ removed) to the real line and the map $u\mapsto1/\overline u$ to complex conjugation; for the zero rotor $u_\rho=1-\tfrac1\rho$ one gets $w_\rho=i(2\rho-1)$, which is real exactly when $\operatorname{Re}\rho=\tfrac12$, and $\overline{w_\rho}=w_{1-\overline\rho}$. So for any finite list of points closed under $\rho\mapsto1-\overline\rho$, item 6 says: the matrix $H$ built from $r_k=i(2\rho_k-1)$ is positive semidefinite if and only if all the points lie on the critical line (manual derivation, by substitution into item 6).

::: {.remark #rem-ch34-finite-toeplitz-inertia title="Toeplitz Inertia of a Finite Rotor Family: The Number of Negative Eigenvalues Equals the Number of Rotor Pairs off the Unit Circle (Manual derivation)"}
**Manual derivation.** Let $u_1,\dots,u_m$ be nonzero complex numbers (repetitions allowed), closed as a multiset under $u\mapsto1/\overline u$. For $k\in\mathbb{Z}$ put $p_k=\sum_ju_j^{\,k}$ and write $T_N=T_N(p)$. Denote the distinct values by $z_1,\dots,z_d$; $e$ of them lie on the unit circle, and the rest pair off as $\{z,1/\overline z\}$ into $\kappa$ pairs, so $d=e+2\kappa$. Then

1. $T_N$ is Hermitian;
2. for $N\ge d$, $T_N$ has exactly $e+\kappa$ positive and $\kappa$ negative eigenvalues, and the eigenvalue $0$ with multiplicity $N-d$; for $N<d$ it has at most $\kappa$ negative eigenvalues;
3. consequently all the rotors lie on the unit circle if and only if $T_d$ is positive semidefinite, if and only if every $T_N$ is.

If the numbers are also closed under complex conjugation, put $\Lambda_n=\sum_j\bigl(1-u_j^{\,n}\bigr)$ ($n\ge0$, now real) and assume $\Lambda_1>0$; build the curvature $c_k$ from $\Lambda$ by the formula of item 5 of [@prop-ch34-li-curvature-toeplitz]. Then $T_N(c)$ has the same inertia, except that $d$ and $e$ do not count the value $1$.

**Proof.** Let $\mu_i$ be the multiplicity of $z_i$. The map $u\mapsto1/\overline u$ induces an involution $\tau$ on the values: $z_{\tau(i)}=1/\overline{z_i}$ and $\mu_{\tau(i)}=\mu_i$; $\tau(i)=i$ exactly when $\lvert z_i\rvert=1$. Since $z^{-k}=\overline{\bigl(1/\overline z\bigr)^k}$,

$$
p_{j-k}=\sum_i\mu_i\,z_i^{\,j}\,\overline{z_{\tau(i)}^{\,k}} ,
$$

so $T_N=AMA^{*}$, where $A$ is the $N\times d$ matrix whose $i$-th column is $\bigl(z_i^{\,j}\bigr)_{0\le j<N}$, $M$ is the $d\times d$ matrix with $M_{i,\tau(i)}=\mu_i$ and all other entries zero, and $A^{*}$ is the conjugate transpose. $M$ is Hermitian, which gives item 1. A fixed point of $\tau$ gives a diagonal entry $\mu_i>0$; each $2$-cycle gives a block whose two off-diagonal entries are $\mu_i$, with eigenvalues $\pm\mu_i$. So $M$ has exactly $e+\kappa$ positive and $\kappa$ negative eigenvalues and no zero eigenvalue. For $N\ge d$, the first $d$ rows of $A$ form a Vandermonde matrix with distinct nodes, which is invertible, so $A^{*}:\mathbb{C}^N\to\mathbb{C}^d$ is onto and $\ker T_N=\ker A^{*}$ has dimension $N-d$. If $M$ is negative definite on a subspace $V$, choose $W\subset\mathbb{C}^N$ mapped bijectively onto $V$ by $A^{*}$; then $T_N$ is negative definite on $W$. Conversely, if $T_N$ is negative definite on $W'$, then $A^{*}$ is injective on $W'$ (otherwise some nonzero $x$ would have $x^{*}T_Nx=0$), and $M$ is negative definite on $A^{*}W'$. The positive direction is the same. This gives item 2 for $N\ge d$; for $N<d$ only the second half of the argument survives, which gives the inequality. Item 3 follows from item 2.

The curvature version: $\Lambda_{k+1}-2\Lambda_k+\Lambda_{k-1}=\sum_j2v_ju_j^{\,k}$ with $v(u)=1-\tfrac12\bigl(u+u^{-1}\bigr)=-\dfrac{(u-1)^2}{2u}$; closure under conjugation makes $p_1$ real, so $\sum_jv_j=m-p_1=\Lambda_1$. Hence $c_k=\sum_jw_ju_j^{\,k}$ for every $k\in\mathbb{Z}$, with $w_j=v_j/\Lambda_1$, and $v\bigl(1/\overline u\bigr)=\overline{v(u)}$. The same factorization gives $T_N(c)=AM_wA^{*}$ with $\bigl(M_w\bigr)_{i,\tau(i)}=\mu_iw(z_i)$. The weight $w$ vanishes only at $z=1$, and those columns can be removed; at $z\ne1$ on the unit circle, $v=1-\operatorname{Re}z>0$ and $\Lambda_1>0$ make $w>0$; a pair of values off the circle gives two off-diagonal entries $\mu w(z)$ and $\mu\overline{w(z)}$, with eigenvalues $\pm\mu\lvert w(z)\rvert\ne0$. The rest is as above. $\square$

**Remarks (manual derivation).** First, closure under $u\mapsto1/\overline u$ must hold with multiplicities; closure of the set of values is not enough: for the multiset $\{2,\tfrac12,\tfrac12\}$ one has $p_{-1}=\tfrac92\ne p_1=3$, and $T_2$ is not Hermitian. Second, $\Lambda_1>0$ cannot be dropped: when $\Lambda_1<0$ the values on the circle other than $1$ give negative eigenvalues. Third, $d$ is finite here; for the zeros of $\zeta$, $d$ is infinite, and no step above carries over. Fourth, this is the same mechanism as Hermite's classical theorem: for the Hankel quadratic form built from the Newton power sums of the roots of a polynomial, the positive and negative indices of inertia count the distinct real roots plus the pairs of conjugate roots, and the pairs of conjugate roots (the classical result of Hermite and Sylvester; a systematic account is M. G. Krein and M. A. Naimark, *Linear and Multilinear Algebra* **10**, 265–308, 1981, Russian original 1936; literature result); the Cayley coordinate replaces the unit circle by the real line. The pinned repository freezes the part "positive semidefinite if and only if all roots are real" (item 6 of [@prop-ch34-li-curvature-toeplitz]) and the unit-circle form of item 3 above ([@prop-ch34-finite-toeplitz-circle-criterion]), both without the count of inertia. We did not find the unit-circle form above stated verbatim in a source, and we claim no novelty.

**Relation to the Li sums.** By item 3 of [@prop-ch34-li-curvature-toeplitz], $\Lambda_n=\Lambda_1\sum_{j,k<n}c_{j-k}$: the Li sum of a finite family is exactly the reading of the curvature Toeplitz matrix at the all-ones vector. The Li sums test one vector at each size; the Toeplitz matrices test every vector and see everything at size $d$.

**Curves over finite fields (literature result and manual derivation).** Let $C$ be a smooth projective curve of genus $g$ over $\mathbb{F}_q$, let $N_n=\#C(\mathbb{F}_{q^n})$, and let $\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_{2g}$ be the reciprocal roots of its $L$-polynomial; then $N_n=q^n+1-\sum_i\alpha_i^{\,n}$, and the multiset of reciprocal roots is closed under $\alpha\mapsto q/\alpha$ and under complex conjugation (A. Weil, 1948; literature result). Put $u_i=\alpha_i/\sqrt q$; these are closed under $u\mapsto1/\overline u$, and

$$
p_k=\frac{q^k+1-N_k}{q^{k/2}}\ (k\ge1),\qquad p_0=2g,\qquad N_n=q^n+1-q^{n/2}\bigl(2g-\Lambda_n\bigr)
$$

(manual derivation). There are at most $2g$ distinct values, so by item 2 above (with $N=2g\ge d$), "every $\lvert\alpha_i\rvert=\sqrt q$" is equivalent to the positive semidefiniteness of the $2g\times2g$ matrix $\bigl(p_{\lvert j-k\rvert}\bigr)$ built from $N_1,\dots,N_{2g-1}$ (manual derivation). The former is Weil's theorem (A. Weil, 1948; literature result), so this matrix is positive semidefinite for every curve. This is only a finite reformulation of Weil's theorem: the positivity comes from Weil's proof, not from the table.
:::

The part of item 3 above that reads "when the size is at least the number of distinct values, positive semidefinite if and only if all on the unit circle" is frozen in the pinned repository, in the following form; the count of inertia (item 2) and Hermiticity (item 1) remain manual derivations.

::: {.proposition #prop-ch34-finite-toeplitz-circle-criterion title="The Unit-Circle Criterion for a Finite Rotor Family" lean="D5/S3/Weil/TestFunctions/FinitePowerSumToeplitzCircleCriterion.toeplitz_posSemidef_iff_norm_eq_one D5/S3/Weil/TestFunctions/FinitePowerSumToeplitzCircleCriterion.powerSum_toeplitz_posSemidef_of_norm_eq_one"}
Let $u_1,\dots,u_m$ be complex numbers (repetitions allowed), put $p_k=\sum_ju_j^{\,k}$ for $k\in\mathbb{Z}$, and for $N\ge1$ let $T_N=\bigl(p_{j-k}\bigr)_{0\le j,k<N}$. Here "positive semidefinite" means Hermitian with $x^{*}T_Nx\ge0$ for every $x\in\mathbb{C}^N$.

1. Suppose every $u_j\ne0$, there is a permutation $\sigma$ of the indices with $u_{\sigma(j)}=1/\overline{u_j}$ for every $j$, and the number of distinct values among $u_1,\dots,u_m$ is at most $N$. Then $T_N$ is positive semidefinite if and only if $\lvert u_j\rvert=1$ for every $j$.
2. If $\lvert u_j\rvert=1$ for every $j$, then $T_N$ is positive semidefinite for every $N\ge1$.
:::

::: proof
The existence of the permutation $\sigma$ is the same thing as closure of the multiset under $u\mapsto1/\overline u$, so the hypotheses of item 1 are those of [@rem-ch34-finite-toeplitz-inertia].

Item 2: when the moduli are $1$, $u_j^{-k}=\overline{u_j^{\,k}}$, so $p_{j-k}=\sum_iu_i^{\,j}\,\overline{u_i^{\,k}}$ and $T_N$ is a sum of $m$ positive semidefinite matrices of rank one. In Lean it is read as the moment matrix of a finitely supported measure on the unit circle, and the frozen [`circle_moment_toeplitz_posSemidef`](lean:D5/S3/Weil/TestFunctions/LiCurvatureCriterion.circle_moment_toeplitz_posSemidef) applies directly.

The "if" direction of item 1 is item 2. For "only if", take the entries of $x$ as the coefficients of a polynomial $P(w)=\sum_{b<N}x_bw^b$; then

$$
x^{*}T_Nx=\sum_j\overline{P\bigl(\overline{u_j}\bigr)}\;P\bigl(\overline{u_{\sigma(j)}}\bigr).
$$

If some value $z$ is off the unit circle, then $1/\overline z\ne z$. There are at most $N$ distinct values, so Lagrange interpolation gives a $P$ of degree less than $N$ that equals $1$ at $\overline z$, equals $-1$ at $1/z$, and vanishes at the conjugates of the other values. In the sum only the terms with $u_j=z$ and with $u_j=1/\overline z$ are nonzero, and each equals $-1$; hence $x^{*}T_Nx<0$ and $T_N$ is not positive semidefinite. The full proof is checked by the Lean kernel.
:::

::: {.example #ex-ch34-toeplitz-versus-li-numbers title="Toeplitz Tests Against Li Sums: A Hypothetical Quartet, a Hybrid Finite Family, Zeta Itself, and a Genus-2 Curve"}
The numbers below were computed at 60 to 200 digits and rounded to the digits shown; the zero ordinates are numerical results (literature result, not formalized in this book).

**The hypothetical quartet $\tfrac{7}{10}\pm5i$, $\tfrac{3}{10}\pm5i$.** Here $d=4$ and $\kappa=2$. The curvature Toeplitz matrix $T_2(c)$ is positive semidefinite (least eigenvalue $0.0195853$); $T_3(c)$ already has one negative eigenvalue ($-1.68372\times10^{-6}$); $T_4(c)$ has exactly two (the least is $-1.01327\times10^{-5}$), in agreement with [@rem-ch34-finite-toeplitz-inertia]. The Li sum first turns negative only at $n=31$ ([@ex-ch34-zero-rotor-sagitta-numbers]); at $n=31$ the least eigenvalue of $T_{31}(c)$ is $-0.0798386$, at most $\Lambda_{31}/(31\Lambda_1)=-0.0385956$ (item 4 of [@prop-ch34-li-curvature-toeplitz]). With $r_k=i(2\rho_k-1)=\mp10\pm0.4i$ as in item 6 of [@prop-ch34-li-curvature-toeplitz], the $4\times4$ matrix $H$ has the two negative eigenvalues $-0.657744$ and $-0.00646136$.

**A hybrid finite family.** The off-line quartet $\tfrac12\pm0.1\pm14i$ together with the first $80$ pairs of actual zeros (in [@ex-ch34-zero-rotor-sagitta-numbers] its Li sum first turns negative at $n=8446$). The least eigenvalue of the curvature Toeplitz matrix is $1.36621\times10^{-21}$ for $N=7$ and $-1.10111\times10^{-26}$ for $N=8$. The results at 60 and at 120 digits agree in the digits shown; equivalently, $T_7(c)$ is positive definite while $\det T_8(c)=-2.86314\times10^{-83}<0$. So $T_8(c)$ already detects the off-line rotors; the price is a negative eigenvalue of order $10^{-26}$, whose sign can only be resolved if the curvatures $c_k$ are known with error far below $10^{-27}$. A late sign change has been traded for a tiny eigenvalue; there is no free test.

**Zeta itself.** Compute $\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_{41}$ from the definition in item 2 of [@prop-ch34-li-sagitta-frozen] (by two independent methods: a Cauchy integral of $\operatorname{Log}\xi\bigl(\tfrac1{1-z}\bigr)$ over $\lvert z\rvert=\tfrac12$, and the Taylor series of $\log\xi$ at $s=1$ assembled from Stieltjes constants and polygamma values; they agree far beyond the digits shown), and form $c^{\mathrm{Li}}$: $c^{\mathrm{Li}}_1=0.999196807$, $c^{\mathrm{Li}}_2=0.996790337$. The least eigenvalue of $T_N(c^{\mathrm{Li}})$ is $8.03193\times10^{-4}$ for $N=2$, $2.24401\times10^{-10}$ for $N=4$, $3.72999\times10^{-25}$ for $N=8$ and $5.24343\times10^{-74}$ for $N=20$, all positive. This is a numerical fact for $N\le20$, consistent with the premise of item 5 of [@prop-ch34-li-curvature-toeplitz]; it is not a statement about all $N$, and it gives no new information about where the zeros lie.

**A genus-2 curve.** For $y^2=x^5+2x+1$ over $\mathbb{F}_3$: $N_1,N_2,N_3,N_4=7,15,19,83$ (by counting points), the $L$-polynomial is $1+3T+7T^2+9T^3+9T^4$, and the $N_3,N_4$ it predicts agree with the count. The $4\times4$ matrix $\bigl(p_{\lvert j-k\rvert}\bigr)$, built from $N_1,N_2,N_3$ only, has eigenvalues $0.185386$, $2.26672$, $5.73328$, $7.81461$, all positive. For contrast, a polynomial of the shape $1+15T^2+25T^4$ ($q=5$) gives the rotors $\pm i\varphi$, $\pm i\varphi^{-1}$ with $\varphi=\tfrac{1+\sqrt5}{2}$; then $p_1=p_3=0$ and $p_2=-6$, the eigenvalues of the matrix are exactly $10,10,-2,-2$, the two negative eigenvalues correspond to the two pairs of rotors off the unit circle, and the Li sum first turns negative at $n=4$ ($\Lambda_4=-10$). By Weil's theorem no curve has this $L$-polynomial (manual derivation).
:::

In the example the off-line rotors all sit very close to the unit circle, and the Li sums take a long time to turn negative. For a single pair of rotors the waiting time can be estimated exactly.

::: {.remark #rem-ch34-off-circle-pair-detection title="When a Pair of Rotors off the Unit Circle Makes the Li Sum Negative (Manual derivation)"}
**Manual derivation.** Let $\eta>0$ and $\phi$ be real, and take the four rotors $e^{\pm\eta\pm i\phi}$ (closed under conjugation and under $u\mapsto1/\overline u$; the rotors of a zero quartet $\rho,\overline\rho,1-\rho,1-\overline\rho$ have exactly this shape). Their Li sum is

$$
\Lambda_n=4\bigl(1-\cosh(n\eta)\cos(n\phi)\bigr).
$$

Let $n_*$ be the least positive integer with $\Lambda_n<0$.

1. **Upper bound.** $n_*\le7\left\lceil\dfrac1\eta\right\rceil$.
2. **The order cannot be improved.** Let $x_*$ be the root of $\cosh x\cos2x=1$ in $\bigl(\tfrac{3\pi}4,\pi\bigr)$, $x_*=2.4428863005\ldots$. If $\phi=2\eta$ and $0<\eta\le\pi-x_*$, then $n_*=\left\lfloor\dfrac{x_*}{\eta}\right\rfloor+1$.
3. **In zero coordinates.** For $\rho=\beta+i\gamma$, $\lvert u_\rho\rvert^2=1+\dfrac{1-2\beta}{\lvert\rho\rvert^2}$, so $\eta=\tfrac12\left\lvert\ln\left(1+\dfrac{1-2\beta}{\lvert\rho\rvert^2}\right)\right\rvert$, approximately $\dfrac{\lvert1-2\beta\rvert}{2\lvert\rho\rvert^2}$.

**Proof.** Item 1: let $L=\lceil1/\eta\rceil$. By Dirichlet's approximation theorem there is $1\le k\le7$ such that $kL\phi$ lies within $\tfrac{2\pi}{8}$ of $2\pi\mathbb{Z}$. For $n=kL$ we get $\cos n\phi\ge\cos\tfrac\pi4=\tfrac1{\sqrt2}$, while $n\eta\ge1$ gives $\cosh(n\eta)\ge\cosh1>1.543>\sqrt2$, so the product exceeds $1$. Item 2: let $f(x)=\cosh x\cos2x$. On $\bigl(0,\tfrac\pi4\bigr)$ the derivative of $\ln f$ is $\tanh x-2\tan2x<0$ (since $\tanh x<x<4x\le2\tan2x$) and $\ln f(0)=0$, so $f<1$; on $\bigl[\tfrac\pi4,\tfrac{3\pi}4\bigr]$, $\cos2x\le0$; on $\bigl(\tfrac{3\pi}4,\pi\bigr]$ both factors are positive and strictly increasing, so $f$ is strictly increasing and equals $1$ at $x_*$. Hence, within $(0,\pi]$, $f(x)>1$ holds exactly for $x\in(x_*,\pi]$. Now $\Lambda_n<0$ means $f(n\eta)>1$; the least $n$ with $n\eta>x_*$ is $\lfloor x_*/\eta\rfloor+1$, and then $n\eta\le x_*+\eta\le\pi$. Item 3: $\lvert u_\rho\rvert^2=\dfrac{(\beta-1)^2+\gamma^2}{\beta^2+\gamma^2}$. $\square$

Numerical check: item 1 holds for $20000$ random pairs $(\eta,\phi)$; for $\eta=0.1,0.03,0.01,0.003,0.001$ item 2 gives $n_*=25,82,245,815,2443$, in agreement with direct computation. For the hypothetical quartet $\tfrac7{10}\pm5i$, $\tfrac3{10}\pm5i$, $\eta=0.0079084$, the bound of item 1 is $889$ and the actual $n_*$ is $31$; for $\tfrac12\pm0.1\pm14i$ the bound is $13741$ and the actual $n_*$ is $88$. Item 1 holds for a single quartet only; in a finite family the nonnegative contributions of the other rotors can postpone the sign change much further (the $8446$ of [@ex-ch34-zero-rotor-sagitta-numbers]), and for a general finite family the same pigeonhole argument gives a bound that grows exponentially with the number of distinct phases. The closer an off-line quartet is to the critical line and the higher it sits, the smaller $\eta$ and the larger the bound of item 1; this is a statement about a single quartet, not about $\lambda_n$.
:::

The finite-field paragraph above says that the positive semidefiniteness of the Toeplitz matrices of a curve comes from Weil's theorem. For one concrete family of curves the pinned repository freezes Weil's inequality itself, in the following form.

::: {.proposition #prop-ch34-fermat-weil-bound-frozen title="Weil's Bound for Fermat Curves (Proof by Jacobi Sums)" lean="D5/S3/CharacterSums/Fermat/CharacterPointCount.projective_weil_bound"}
Let $F$ be a finite field, $q=\lvert F\rvert$, and let the integer $d\ge2$ divide $q-1$. Let $N$ be the number of points $[x:y:z]$ of the projective plane $\mathbb{P}^2(F)$ with $x^d+y^d=z^d$. Then

$$
\lvert N-(q+1)\rvert\le(d-1)(d-2)\sqrt q .
$$
:::

::: proof
Choose a complex multiplicative character $\chi$ of $F$ of exact order $d$ (it exists because $d\mid q-1$). The point count equals $q+1$ plus a sum of $(d-1)(d-2)$ Jacobi sums $J(\chi^i,\chi^j)$ with $1\le i,j<d$ and $i+j\ne d$. For each of them $\chi^i$, $\chi^j$ and $\chi^{i+j}$ are nontrivial, so $\lvert J(\chi^i,\chi^j)\rvert=\sqrt q$ ([`norm_jacobiSum`](lean:D5/S3/CharacterSums/Fermat/CharacterPointCount.norm_jacobiSum)), and the triangle inequality gives the bound. The full proof is checked by the Lean kernel.
:::

::: {.remark #rem-ch34-weil-toeplitz-hodge-index title="Where Toeplitz Positivity Comes From over Finite Fields: A Compression of the Intersection Form on C×C (Literature result and manual derivation)"}
**Literature result, not formalized in this book**, together with a manual derivation. Let $C$ be a smooth, projective, geometrically irreducible curve of genus $g$ over $\mathbb{F}_q$, $S=\overline C\times\overline C$ ($\overline C$ the base change to an algebraic closure), $F$ the $q$-Frobenius, $\Gamma_n\subset S$ the graph of $F^n$, $\Gamma_0=\Delta$ the diagonal, $f_1=\{x_0\}\times\overline C$, $f_2=\overline C\times\{y_0\}$. Literature facts used: (a) the Hodge index theorem for surfaces holds in every characteristic: if $H$ is ample and $D\cdot H=0$, then $D^2\le0$, with equality if and only if $D$ is numerically equivalent to zero (A. Grothendieck, *J. reine angew. Math.* **200**, 208–215, 1958, DOI `10.1515/crll.1958.200.208`; R. Hartshorne, *Algebraic Geometry*, Theorem V.1.9); (b) $\Gamma_n\cdot\Delta=N_n$ ($n\ge1$), $\Delta^2=2-2g$, $\Gamma_n\cdot f_1=1$, $\Gamma_n\cdot f_2=q^n$, $f_1^2=f_2^2=0$, $f_1\cdot f_2=1$ (compare Hartshorne, Exercises V.1.9–1.10); (c) Weil's proof uses the Castelnuovo–Severi inequality (A. Weil, *Sur les courbes algébriques et les variétés qui s'en déduisent*, Hermann, 1948; A. Mattuck, J. Tate, *Abh. Math. Sem. Univ. Hamburg* **22**, 295–299, 1958, DOI `10.1007/BF02941959`).

**Manual derivation.** Write $p_0=2g$ and $p_m=\dfrac{q^m+1-N_m}{q^{m/2}}$ ($m\ge1$), the power sums of the last paragraph of [@rem-ch34-finite-toeplitz-inertia].

1. $\Gamma_j\cdot\Gamma_k=q^{\min(j,k)}N_{\lvert j-k\rvert}$, with the convention $N_0=2-2g$.
2. Let $\gamma_n=\Gamma_n-q^nf_1-f_2$. Then $\gamma_n\cdot f_1=\gamma_n\cdot f_2=0$ and $\gamma_j\cdot\gamma_k=-q^{(j+k)/2}p_{\lvert j-k\rvert}$.
3. Hence $e_j=q^{-j/2}\gamma_j$ satisfies $e_j\cdot e_k=-p_{\lvert j-k\rvert}$: for every $N$, the Toeplitz matrix $\bigl(p_{\lvert j-k\rvert}\bigr)_{0\le j,k<N}$ is exactly minus the Gram matrix of the intersection form on $e_0,\dots,e_{N-1}$.
4. Therefore $\bigl(p_{\lvert j-k\rvert}\bigr)_{0\le j,k<N}$ is positive semidefinite for every $N$.

**Proof.** Item 1: let $j\ge k$ and let $\varphi=F^k\times1$, a finite morphism of degree $q^k$. In local coordinates $\Gamma_m$ is given by $y=x^{q^m}$, and its pullback $y=x^{q^{m+k}}$ is reduced, so $\varphi^{*}\Gamma_{j-k}=\Gamma_j$ and $\varphi^{*}\Delta=\Gamma_k$; the projection formula gives $\Gamma_j\cdot\Gamma_k=q^k\,(\Gamma_{j-k}\cdot\Delta)$, and (b) finishes. Item 2: by (b), $\gamma_n\cdot f_1=1-1=0$ and $\gamma_n\cdot f_2=q^n-q^n=0$; hence $\gamma_j\cdot\gamma_k=\Gamma_j\cdot\gamma_k=\Gamma_j\cdot\Gamma_k-q^k-q^j$, and item 1 together with $q^{m/2}p_m=q^m+1-N_m$ (for $m=0$, $N_0-2=-2g$) gives the formula. Item 3 follows by dividing by $q^{(j+k)/2}$. Item 4: $f_1+f_2$ is ample and every $e_j$ is orthogonal to it, so by (a) the intersection form is negative semidefinite on the real span of the $e_j$. $\square$

So the chain is: the intersection form is negative semidefinite on the orthogonal complement of $f_1+f_2$ (Hodge index) $\Rightarrow$ every $\bigl(p_{\lvert j-k\rvert}\bigr)_{j,k<N}$ is positive semidefinite $\Rightarrow$ every $\lvert\alpha_i\rvert=\sqrt q$ $\Rightarrow$ $\lvert N_n-q^n-1\rvert\le2gq^{n/2}$. The second step takes $N=2g$ and uses the closure of $u_i=\alpha_i/\sqrt q$ under $u\mapsto1/\overline u$ from the finite-field paragraph above (literature result) together with item 1 of [@prop-ch34-finite-toeplitz-circle-criterion]. The $2\times2$ principal submatrix on the indices $\{0,n\}$ is $\begin{pmatrix}2g&p_n\\p_n&2g\end{pmatrix}$; its positive semidefiniteness is exactly $\lvert N_n-q^n-1\rvert\le2gq^{n/2}$, and the divisor classes involved (the diagonal, $\Gamma_n$ and the two fibres) are the ones used in the proofs of Weil, Mattuck–Tate and Grothendieck. This has the same shape as the proof of item 5 of [@prop-ch34-li-curvature-toeplitz], which also uses only the $2\times2$ principal submatrix on the indices $0$ and $n$. By the equality case of (a), null vectors of the Gram matrix give numerical relations among the $e_j$; for example, on an elliptic curve $e_2-p_1e_1+e_0$ is numerically zero, a shadow of $F^2-aF+q=0$. We did not find this Toeplitz form stated verbatim in the literature, and we claim no novelty.

**Example (point counts and exact arithmetic, manual derivation).** The elliptic curve $y^2=x^3+x+1$ over $\mathbb{F}_5$: $N_1,N_2,N_3=9,27,108$, $a=q+1-N_1=-3$, $p_1=-3/\sqrt5$, $p_2=-\tfrac15$. The principal submatrix $\begin{pmatrix}2&-3/\sqrt5\\-3/\sqrt5&2\end{pmatrix}$ on the indices $\{0,1\}$ has determinant $4-\tfrac95=\tfrac{11}5>0$: this is Hasse's bound $\lvert a\rvert\le2\sqrt5$ in $2\times2$ form. The Gram matrix of $e_0,e_1,e_2$ has the eigenvalues $-\tfrac{19}5$, $-\tfrac{11}5$ and $0$ exactly, and the null vector is $(1,-p_1,1)$.

**Relation to [@prop-ch34-fermat-weil-bound-frozen] (manual derivation, using literature results).** Let $d\ge3$ with $d\mid q-1$; then the characteristic of $\mathbb{F}_q$ does not divide $d$, and the plane curve $x^d+y^d=z^d$ is smooth of genus $g=\tfrac{(d-1)(d-2)}2$ (literature result). Since $d\mid q^n-1$, the proposition applies to every $\mathbb{F}_{q^n}$ and gives $\lvert p_n\rvert\le2g$ ($n\ge1$). By the rationality of the zeta function (F. K. Schmidt, *Math. Z.* **33**, 1–32, 1931; literature result), $\sum_{n\ge1}p_nz^n=\sum_i\dfrac{u_iz}{1-u_iz}$; the coefficients on the left are bounded, so the right-hand side has no pole in $\lvert z\rvert<1$ and $\lvert u_i\rvert\le1$; closure under $u\mapsto1/\overline u$ gives $\lvert u_i\rvert=1$, and item 2 of [@prop-ch34-finite-toeplitz-circle-criterion] makes every $\bigl(p_{\lvert j-k\rvert}\bigr)_{j,k<N}$ positive semidefinite. This route goes through Jacobi sums, not through the Hodge index theorem, and covers only this family of curves.

**What is missing for $\zeta$ (literature result and commentary).** For $\zeta$ no surface is known that plays the role of $\overline C\times\overline C$: no lattice with an intersection form is known in which $-T_N(c^{\mathrm{Li}})$ is the Gram matrix of some family of classes, and there is no corresponding Hodge index theorem. Weil proved that the Riemann hypothesis is equivalent to the positivity of a quadratic functional built from the explicit formula (A. Weil, *Sur les « formules explicites » de la théorie des nombres premiers*, Comm. Sém. Math. Univ. Lund, 1952; literature result); this is another equivalent reformulation, and [@conj-riemann-hypothesis] remains open. The finite-field argument above only shows where positivity comes from in that setting; it does not provide a route for $\zeta$.
:::

Each of the statements above either concerns finite families or is an equivalent reformulation for zeta; none of them decides whether all the zeros of zeta lie on the critical line, and [@conj-riemann-hypothesis] remains open.

With single-step rotor derivatives and one-dimensional product integrals unified, we arrive at the architectural crown of calculus: exterior differentiation and the generalized Stokes theorem on curved manifolds.

## 30.3.3 Zero-Error Finite-Mesh Stokes Exactness on `1—3—3—1`, a Three-Line Gauss–Bonnet Proof, and Non-Abelian Surface Whiskering {#sec-ch34-30-3}

Conventional numerical analysis teaches a deeply ingrained habit of thought: *"Continuum calculus is exact, whereas partitioning a curved surface into a finite mesh makes the discretized Stokes's theorem $\int_{\partial\Sigma}\omega = \int_\Sigma d\omega$ carry an $\mathcal{O}(h^2)$ error that disappears only in the infinite-refinement limit $h \to 0, M \to \infty$."*

Viewed inside the **`1—3—3—1` Clifford–cochain complex** of [§29.9](ch34-s08-clifford-monads.md#sec-ch34-clifford-monad-tiling) and [§33.1](ch34-s13-point-volume-octave.md#sec-ch34-point-volume-octave-and-particle-field), one can see exactly where that error goes: **the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus and the generalized Stokes theorem are not analytic approximations granted by $\epsilon \to 0$, but pure combinatorial topological identities of cellular gluing—once differential forms are formulated natively as cochains on minimal circular-arc bodies, finite-mesh Stokes holds with identically zero discretization error at any coarse halting aperture (even on an $M = 4$ octant mesh of an entire hemisphere!)** This is the standard fact about Whitney cochains and discrete exterior calculus; the error has not vanished but has moved into integrating the continuous $1$-form exactly along each edge to obtain the cochain (the octant example below does precisely this, edge by edge, analytically).

Working across Pascal's palindromic grade ladder $\binom{3}{0} : \binom{3}{1} : \binom{3}{2} : \binom{3}{3} = 1 : 3 : 3 : 1$ on a cellular complex $\mathcal{K}$ (Hirani 2003; Desbrun et al. 2005 Discrete Exterior Calculus, literature result), let us cleanly separate **unordered Abelian cancellation** from **basepoint-anchored non-Abelian surface holonomy**:

### 30.3.3.1 Abelian Cochains and Spherical Excess: Unordered Shared-Dart Cancellation, Zero-Error Stokes, and Three-Line Gauss–Bonnet {#1-abelian-cochains-and-spherical-excess-unordered-shared-dart-cancellation-zero-error-stokes-and-three-line-gaussbonnet}

Tile a compact oriented surface patch $\Sigma$ seamlessly with $M \ge 1$ minimal circular-arc bodies (spherical triangles bounded by great-circle arcs) $\{\Delta\mathcal{T}_k\}_{k=1}^M$, and assign to each oriented dart $e_{AB}$ an Abelian-group-valued $1$-cochain circulation $\Phi_\epsilon(e_{AB}) \in A$ obeying orientation reversal $\Phi_\epsilon(e_{BA}) = -\Phi_\epsilon(e_{AB})$. Defining the $2$-cochain curvature flux on each cell by $(d_\epsilon\Phi_\epsilon)(\Delta\mathcal{T}_k) := \sum_{e \subset \partial\Delta\mathcal{T}_k} \Phi_\epsilon(e)$ (whose single-cell oriented spherical-triangle phase excess in projective pure-state geometry is locked by the three-point Bargmann Bloch trace theorem [`triple_bargmann_bloch`](lean:D5/S3/Quantum/Information/GroupInvariantPsi/C129QubitBargmann.triple_bargmann_bloch); cf. [§29.7](ch34-s06-berry-sectors-and-path-integrals.md#sec-ch34-berry-sector-volume-and-path-integral)) and summing across all $M$ cells, every shared interior arc $e_{\mathrm{int}}$ is traversed once as $e_{AB}$ and once as its opposite dart $e_{BA}$ by the two incident faces (mirroring the opposite-dart boundary operator `R.boundary` in Clause 4 of [@thm-ch34-arc-rotor-calculus-and-finite-stokes], whose frozen form is over $\mathbb{Z}/2$; the real-coefficient version here is derived by hand):

$$
\Phi_\epsilon(e_{AB}) + \Phi_\epsilon(e_{BA}) = 0 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \sum_{k=1}^{M} (d_\epsilon\Phi_\epsilon)(\Delta\mathcal{T}_k) \equiv \sum_{e \subset \partial\Sigma} \Phi_\epsilon(e)!
$$

No $\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^2)$ truncation remainder exists on either side, and summation order is completely immaterial! Ascending one grade further to a tetrahedral 3-cell $\Delta^3 = [v_0, v_1, v_2, v_3]$, each of its six edges appears twice with opposite orientations across the four boundary faces, locking in **exact finite-aperture nilpotency** (Clause 5 `tetra_defects_incidence_repair_and_exactness` of [@thm-ch34-arc-rotor-calculus-and-finite-stokes]):

$$
(d_\epsilon^2 \omega)(\Delta^3) = \omega(\partial^2\Delta^3) \equiv 0 \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad \partial^2\Delta^3 = \emptyset.
$$

Better still, apply this coordinate-free gluing to any compact oriented triangulated surface $\Sigma$ tiled by $F = M$ constant-curvature spherical triangles $\Delta\mathcal{T}_k$ bounded by great-circle geodesic arcs (edge geodesic curvature $\kappa_g \equiv 0$), having $E = E_{\mathrm{int}} + E_\partial$ edges and $V = V_{\mathrm{int}} + V_\partial$ vertices (with $V_\partial = E_\partial$ around closed boundary loops). What takes pages of Christoffel symbols in classical differential geometry—the **Gauss–Bonnet theorem**—collapses on a finite circular-arc mesh into a **limit-free, three-line combinatorial identity** (manual derivation taking Girard's formula for a single spherical triangle (A. Girard 1629, `Literature`) as input; cf. Regge 1961):

* **Line 1 (Half-edge incidence double counting)**: Across $F$ triangles there are $3F$ half-edges, covering every interior edge twice and every boundary edge once, so $3F = 2E_{\mathrm{int}} + E_\partial$;
* **Line 2 (Vertex regrouping of Girard spherical excess)**: Summing Girard's excess $\Omega(\Delta\mathcal{T}_k) = (\alpha_{k,1} + \alpha_{k,2} + \alpha_{k,3}) - \pi$ over all $F$ faces and regrouping angles at interior vertices ($2\pi$ per $v \in V_{\mathrm{int}}$) and boundary vertices ($\pi - \theta_{\mathrm{ext}}(v)$ per $v \in V_\partial$, where $\theta_{\mathrm{ext}}(v)$ is the discrete exterior turning angle) gives

  $$
  \sum_{k=1}^{F} \Omega(\Delta\mathcal{T}_k) = 2\pi V_{\mathrm{int}} + \sum_{v \in V_\partial}\bigl(\pi - \theta_{\mathrm{ext}}(v)\bigr) - \pi F = \bigl(2V_{\mathrm{int}} + V_\partial - F\bigr)\pi - \sum_{v \in V_\partial}\theta_{\mathrm{ext}}(v);
  $$

### 30.3.3.2 Non-Abelian Rotors and Gauge Surface Holonomy: Spanning-Tree Whiskering and Planar Shelling Order {#2-non-abelian-rotors-and-gauge-surface-holonomy-spanning-tree-whiskering-and-planar-shelling-order}

What if the directed edges carry **non-Abelian parallel transports** $\hat{U}_e \in G$ in $\mathrm{Spin}(3) \cong \mathrm{SU}(2)$ or Yang–Mills $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ ($\hat{U}_{e^{-1}} = \hat{U}_e^{-1}$) rather than commuting Abelian phases? Can we still multiply unanchored plaquette loops $\hat{W}_k = \hat{U}_{e_{k,3}}\hat{U}_{e_{k,2}}\hat{U}_{e_{k,1}} \in G_{v_k}$ in arbitrary order? **No!** Because matrix multiplication is non-commutative ($\hat{U}_1 \hat{U}_2 \neq \hat{U}_2 \hat{U}_1$), loops based at distinct vertices live in distinct fibers, and intervening group factors block naive cancellation of shared interior darts!

Achieving **zero-error non-Abelian 2D Stokes exactness** on a finite mesh requires two topological ingredients (the Wilson-loop and "lasso" plaquette construction of lattice gauge theory, K. G. Wilson, *Phys. Rev. D* **10**, 2445, 1974; Schlesinger 1928; Broda 1992; Schreiber & Waldorf 2009, literature result—whose Abelian `Circle` skeleton is machine-checked in [`selected_tree_transport`](lean:D5/S3/Observer/AgencyHolonomy/NamedTreePhaseTransport.selected_tree_transport) and [`anchored_phase_classification`](lean:D5/S3/Observer/AgencyHolonomy/AnchoredPhaseClassification.anchored_phase_classification) in [§34.9](ch34-s21-perception-action-holonomy.md#sec-ch34-perception-action-holonomy)):

1. **Spanning-Tree Basepoint Whiskering**: Choose a global reference root $r \in V$ and a system of root paths (such as unique tree transports $\hat{P}_{r \to v_k}$ along a spanning tree $T$), conjugating every minimal circular-arc face loop back into the common root fiber $G_r$ as a whiskered lasso:

   $$
   \widetilde{W}_k^{(r)} := \hat{P}_{r \to v_k}^{-1}\,\hat{W}_k\,\hat{P}_{r \to v_k} \in G_r;
   $$

2. **Planar Shelling Order**: On a simply connected disk mesh $\Sigma \cong D^2$, ordering the product along a planar shelling sequence (so each newly adjoined face meets the union of previous faces along a single connected interior edge chain) aligns adjacent whiskered tails $\hat{P}_{r \to A}\,\hat{U}_{AB}\,\hat{U}_{BA}\,\hat{P}_{r \to A}^{-1} = \mathbf{1}$ side by side for telescopic cancellation, delivering the exact finite-mesh non-Abelian surface Stokes identity at any $M \ge 1$:

   $$
   \mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{shell}}\prod_{k=1}^{M} \widetilde{W}_k^{(r)} \equiv \hat{W}_{\partial\Sigma}^{(r)}!
   $$

::: {.remark #rem-ch34-shelling-stokes-and-triangle-holonomy title="The Finite Stokes Identity along a Shelling (the Finite Form of the Classical Lasso Decomposition), the Spin Holonomy of Minimal-Arc Triangles, and Rational Vertices (Manual Derivation and Literature)"}
**The finite Stokes identity along a shelling.** Let $K$ be a finite planar complex whose underlying space is a closed disk, each closed face a closed disk bounded by an edge cycle, all boundaries oriented counterclockwise. A shelling is an enumeration $f_1, \dots, f_N$ of the faces such that each $D_{j-1} := f_1 \cup \dots \cup f_{j-1}$ is a closed disk and $f_j \cap D_{j-1}$ is a single edge path $\alpha_j$ with at least one edge and different from $\partial f_j$; let $a_j$ be the initial vertex of $\alpha_j$ for the counterclockwise orientation of $\partial D_{j-1}$. For every $\mathrm{SU}(2)$-valued cochain $U$ ($U_{pq} = U_{qp}^{-1}$, path holonomies composing as $\operatorname{hol}(\gamma * \gamma') = \operatorname{hol}(\gamma')\operatorname{hol}(\gamma)$) and every vertex $p$ of $\partial K$, there are edge paths $\tau_j$ from $p$ to $a_j$ in the $1$-skeleton with

$$
\operatorname{hol}(\partial K) \;=\; L_1 L_2 \cdots L_N, \qquad L_j := \operatorname{hol}(\tau_j)^{-1}\,F_j\,\operatorname{hol}(\tau_j), \qquad F_j := \operatorname{hol}(\partial f_j)\ \text{(at } a_j\text{)}.
$$

Each face contributes exactly one factor, in shelling order from left to right; the identity is exact for every cochain and involves no limit. It is the exact finite form of the classical lasso decomposition (I. Ya. Aref'eva, *Theor. Math. Phys.* **43**, 353–356, 1980; N. E. Bralić, *Phys. Rev. D* **22**, 3090–3103, 1980; T. Lévy, *Mem. Amer. Math. Soc.* **166**, no. 790, 2003, and *Astérisque* **329**, 2010; for a product-integral formulation see R. L. Karp, F. Mansouri & J. S. Rno, *J. Math. Phys.* **40**, 6033–6043, 1999, `Literature`). Every triangulation of a disk is shellable (G. Danaraj & V. Klee, *Ann. Discrete Math.* **2**, 33–52, 1978, `Literature`), so the identity applies to every triangulated disk; a general polygonal complex needs a shelling order first. The proof is an induction along the shelling using the cutting lemma: cutting a closed path $\alpha * \beta$ along an edge path $\delta$ gives $\operatorname{hol}(\alpha * \beta) = \operatorname{hol}(\bar{\delta} * \beta)\,\operatorname{hol}(\alpha * \delta)$, with base point at an end of the cut. The "telescoping whiskered tails" above are the cancellations in each step of this induction, with base points and order chosen by the rule just stated (`manual derivation`).

**Spin holonomy of minimal-arc triangles (`manual derivation`).** For pairwise non-antipodal $x, y, z \in S^3(1)$, let $h_{yx} := (y\bar{x})^{1/2}$ be the half-displacement (the square root with positive real part) and $a := h_{xz}h_{zy}h_{yx}$. Then

$$
\operatorname{Re} a \;=\; \frac{\kappa_{xy} + \kappa_{yz} + \kappa_{zx} - 1}{2\sqrt{\kappa_{xy}\,\kappa_{yz}\,\kappa_{zx}}} \;=\; \cos\frac{E}{2},
$$

the second equality holding when $x, y, z$ are linearly independent, with $E$ the spherical excess (area) of the geodesic triangle in the great 2-sphere through them, and $\tan\frac{E}{2} = \lVert x \wedge y \wedge z\rVert / (1 + c_{xy} + c_{yz} + c_{zx})$. For small triangles $y = \exp(Y)x$, $z = \exp(Z)x$ ($\lvert Y\rvert, \lvert Z\rvert \le r$), $a = \exp\bigl(\frac{1}{8}[Y, Z] + \mathcal{O}(r^4)\bigr)$: the Lie-bracket term is the leading order of the excess. The formula is the $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ form of the Bargmann–Pancharatnam three-point invariant (J. Samuel & R. Bhandari, *Phys. Rev. Lett.* **60**, 2339–2342, 1988; N. Mukunda & R. Simon, *Ann. Physics* **228**, 205–268, 1993, `Literature`).

**Holonomy of a disk in a great 2-sphere (`Literature` + `manual derivation`).** Let $\Sigma = S^3 \cap \nu^\perp$ be a great 2-sphere and $R \subset \Sigma$ a closed disk whose boundary is a piecewise $C^1$ Jordan curve $\gamma$, traversed with $R$ on its left and based at $x_0$, with $A$ the area of $R$. Then the spinor holonomy of minimal-arc transport is

$$
U_\gamma \;=\; \exp\Bigl(\frac{A}{2}\,\nu\bar{x}_0\Bigr) \;=\; \cos\frac{A}{2} + \sin\frac{A}{2}\,\nu\bar{x}_0,
$$

with the opposite sign in the exponent for the opposite orientation; in particular $\operatorname{Re}U_\gamma = \cos\frac{A}{2}$, with no convexity hypothesis. This is the Gauss–Bonnet holonomy lifted to spinors, i.e. the half-solid-angle law (`Literature`). Mechanism: $n(x) = \nu\bar{x}$ is imaginary and its covariant derivative $Dn = \langle\nu, dx\rangle$ vanishes along $\Sigma$, so $n$ is parallel on $\Sigma$ (`manual derivation`); the curvature therefore takes values along $n$, the holonomy is abelian, and Green's theorem gives half the area. The triangle formula $\operatorname{Re}a = \cos\frac{E}{2}$ above is a special case. Writing $\Theta(U) := 2\arccos\operatorname{Re}U \in [0, 2\pi]$ for the spin angle: for area $A \le 2\pi$, $\Theta(U_\gamma) = A$, whereas the rotation angle of $\operatorname{Ad}(U_\gamma)$ as a rotation of three-space equals $A$ only for $A \le \pi$.

**The equality case (`manual derivation`; the non-abelian Stokes formula and the Plateau results are `Literature`).** For a parametrised surface $F\colon [0, 1]^2 \to S^3$, the spinor holonomy of the boundary is the endpoint of a path whose velocity is the integral of the curvature transported back to the base point along comb paths (the comb formula, i.e. the continuum non-abelian Stokes theorem in comb order; cf. Aref'eva 1980 and Karp–Mansouri–Rno 1999 cited above), so the spin angle is at most the area of the surface. If $F$ is $C^3$, of rank $2$ on a dense set, and its spin angle equals its area, then the image of $F$ lies in a great 2-sphere. The rank condition is needed: the tripod loop, running out and back along three minimal arcs whose directions span the tangent space, has $U_\gamma = 1$ and a filling of area zero, yet lies in no great 2-sphere. For smooth Jordan curves the remaining question (whether spin angle equal to the infimum of filling areas forces the curve into a great 2-sphere) reduces to the regularity of area-minimising disks: if Morrey's solution of the Plateau problem in Riemannian manifolds (C. B. Morrey, Jr., *Ann. of Math.* **49**, 807–851, 1948), with the results on the absence of interior branch points and on boundary regularity, provides a $C^3$ minimising filling of rank $2$ on a dense set, the equality case above applies; these results are cited from the account of B. White (arXiv:1308.3325) and have not been checked in the originals, so this consequence is conditional.

**Rational vertices (`manual derivation`; Cayley parametrization: A. Cayley, *J. Reine Angew. Math.* **32**, 119–123, 1846, and O. Rodrigues 1840, `Literature`).** The Cayley chart $\operatorname{Cay}(r) = (1 + r)(1 - r)^{-1}$ ($r \in \operatorname{Im}\mathbb{H}$) maps $\mathbb{Q}^3$ bijectively onto $S^3(\mathbb{Q}) \setminus \{-1\}$. When all vertices lie in $S^3(\mathbb{Q})$, the heap operations, $\kappa$, the sagittas, the recurrence above and its Chebyshev coefficients are rational; Levi-Civita transport along every minimal arc (in the right trivialization) is $\operatorname{Ad}(1 + x_q\bar{x}_p) \in \mathrm{SO}(3, \mathbb{Q})$, and so is the holonomy of every closed polygon of minimal arcs; $\cos E$ of every triangle is rational, while the half-displacements and $E$ itself are in general irrational ($E = \frac{\pi}{2}$ for $x = 1$, $y = i$, $z = j$).

Invisible to `I` ('me')—isn't that literally imaginary (`i`)? ..."*

## 34.19.1 Why Observation Must Project Twice: The $90^\circ$ Blind Spot, Radar Round-Trip Echo, and Lüders–Born $\cos^2\theta$ {#sec-ch34-19-1}

Inside a self-contained universe from which Occam's razor has already excised every external omniscient referee point $C$ ([Chapter 2](ch02-born-rule.md), [§34.5](ch34-s05-triadic-bootstrap.md#sec-ch34-triadic-bootstrap-and-beyond-names)), the sole legitimate measuring rod available to an observer is **its own unit state axis $\mathbf{I}$** (normalized by self-comparison $\lVert\mathbf{I}\rVert = 1$). When this observer confronts a unit target state vector $\mathbf{V}$ ($\lVert\mathbf{V}\rVert = 1$) inclined at angle $\theta$, the six orthogonal-complement projection identities [`orthogonal_complement_projection_identities`](lean:D5/S3/Quantum/Algebra/OrthogonalProjectionComplement.orthogonal_complement_projection_identities) (`LEAN ✓`) uniquely resolve $\mathbf{V}$ across the one-dimensional span $M = \operatorname{span}\{\mathbf{I}\}$ and its orthogonal complement $M^\perp$:

$$
\mathbf{V} \;=\; (\cos\theta)\,\mathbf{I} \;+\; (\sin\theta)\,\mathbf{e}_\perp, \qquad \lVert\mathbf{I}\rVert = \lVert\mathbf{e}_\perp\rVert = 1, \quad \mathbf{I}\cdot\mathbf{e}_\perp = \cos 90^\circ = 0.
$$

Why can a **single one-way projection** ($\mathbf{V} \longrightarrow \mathbf{I}$) never constitute a complete, gauge-fair physical measurement readout? Three structural obstructions block a one-way reading simultaneously:

1. **Orthogonal blind-spot erasure and uncalibrated one-way norm (`manual derivation`)**: Because the observer's axis $\mathbf{I}$ stands strictly at $90^\circ$ to the transverse unit vector $\mathbf{e}_\perp$ ($\mathbf{I}\cdot\mathbf{e}_\perp = 0$), the entire perpendicular component $(\sin\theta)\,\mathbf{e}_\perp$ drops straight into the observer's **orthogonal blind spot** on the first projection. Worse still, without an external referee guaranteeing in advance that the incoming signal had unit norm, a bare one-way scalar shadow $\cos\theta$ cannot distinguish "a unit state $\mathbf{V}$ tilted by $\theta$" from "a collinear vector aligned with $\mathbf{I}$ whose norm has decayed to $\cos\theta$"—a passive one-way shadow carries no self-calibrating return reference;
2. **Gauge drift of one-way complex phase and the sharp degree-$2$ invariance floor (`LEAN ✓`)**: In complex Hilbert space, the one-way overlap amplitude $\langle\mathrm{I}\mid\psi\rangle = \cos\theta\,e^{i\varphi}$ from pure state $\lvert\psi\rangle$ onto observer basis ket $\lvert\mathrm{I}\rangle$ shifts under an arbitrary global ray rephasing $\lvert\psi\rangle \mapsto e^{i\alpha}\lvert\psi\rangle$. The **complete common-phase polynomial theorem** [`complete_common_phase_polynomial_theorem`](lean:D5/S3/Quantum/Algebra/CommonPhasePolynomial.complete_common_phase_polynomial_theorem) frozen in [§31.4](ch31-s04-fairness-and-dimensional-expansion.md#sec-ch31-pairing-phase-uncertainty) ([@thm-ch31-phase-polynomial-quadratic], `LEAN ✓`) delivers a sharp algebraic verdict: a complex amplitude polynomial $p \in \operatorname{Poly}(\iota)$ is invariant under all common global phase rotations $z \mapsto e^{i\alpha} z$ (`Invariant p`) if and only if every nonzero monomial has strictly balanced holomorphic and antiholomorphic degrees (`Balanced p`), and if and only if $p$ belongs to the subalgebra `quadraticAlgebra` generated by conjugate quadratic pairs $z_i\bar{z}_j$—**every nonconstant phase-invariant readout polynomial satisfies $\operatorname{totalDegree}(p) \ge 2$**! Degree-$1$ linear amplitudes are algebraically disqualified from serving as physical observables;
3. **Order asymmetry in a single Clifford geometric product (`manual derivation`)**: Inside the comparison plane spanned by $\mathbf{I}$ and $\mathbf{e}_\perp$ (writing $\hat{\mathbf{i}} := \mathbf{I}\wedge\mathbf{e}_\perp = \mathbf{I}\mathbf{e}_\perp$ for its unit oriented bivector), antisymmetry of the exterior wedge makes "target compared to observer" and "observer compared to target" unequal whenever $\sin\theta \neq 0$ ([Chapter 6](ch06-geometric-product.md)):

   $$
   \mathbf{V}\mathbf{I} \;=\; \cos\theta - \hat{\mathbf{i}}\sin\theta \;\neq\; \mathbf{I}\mathbf{V} \;=\; \cos\theta + \hat{\mathbf{i}}\sin\theta.
   $$

   A one-way comparison not only violates pairwise order fairness; its output is not even a real scalar lying along the observer's axis!

Since an embedded observer can neither step sideways out of itself to peek at $\mathbf{e}_\perp$ nor tolerate uncalibrated phase and order bias, the only autonomous, self-calibrating protocol available is an active **radar round-trip echo**: the observer projects its own unit reference $\mathbf{I}$ onto the target vector $\mathbf{V}$ (equivalently, re-projects the first received projection back onto $\mathbf{V}$), producing the intermediate echo state $\Pi_{\mathbf{V}}(\mathbf{I}) = (\mathbf{I}\cdot\mathbf{V})\mathbf{V} = (\cos\theta)\,\mathbf{V}$ along $\mathbf{V}$; yet because $\mathbf{V}$ still carries the perpendicular component $(\sin\theta)\,\mathbf{e}_\perp$ invisible to $\mathbf{I}$, the observer must perform a **second orthogonal projection** back onto its own axis $\mathbf{I}$ to read the returning echo (`manual derivation`):

$$
\Pi_{\mathbf{I}}\Bigl(\Pi_{\mathbf{V}}(\mathbf{I})\Bigr) \;=\; \Bigl(\mathbf{I}\cdot\bigl[(\mathbf{I}\cdot\mathbf{V})\mathbf{V}\bigr]\Bigr)\mathbf{I} \;=\; (\mathbf{I}\cdot\mathbf{V})(\mathbf{V}\cdot\mathbf{I})\,\mathbf{I} \;=\; \cos^2\theta\,\mathbf{I}.
$$

In classical polarization optics, this two-step projection across analyzer axes tilted by $\theta$ is none other than **Malus's Law** ($I_{\mathrm{echo}} = I_0\cos^2\theta$, `Literature: Malus 1809`); in quantum operator language, writing $P_{\mathrm{I}} = \lvert\mathrm{I}\rangle\langle\mathrm{I}\rvert$ for the observer's own projector and $P_\psi = \lvert\psi\rangle\langle\psi\rvert$ for the pure target state's projector, the exact same radar round-trip double projection becomes the **Lüders–von Neumann projector sandwich identity** (`manual derivation` + `LEAN ✓`):

$$
P_{\mathrm{I}}\,P_\psi\,P_{\mathrm{I}} \;=\; \lvert\mathrm{I}\rangle \underbrace{\langle\mathrm{I}\mid\psi\rangle}_{\text{1st projection: }\psi \to \mathrm{I}} \underbrace{\langle\psi\mid\mathrm{I}\rangle}_{\text{2nd projection: }\mathrm{I} \to \psi} \langle\mathrm{I}\rvert \;=\; \lvert\langle\mathrm{I}\mid\psi\rangle\rvert^2 P_{\mathrm{I}} \;=\; \cos^2\theta\,P_{\mathrm{I}}.
$$

Watch how comparing twice—there and back—heals every pathology of a one-way projection at a single stroke: the forward overlap $\langle\mathrm{I}\mid\psi\rangle = \cos\theta\,e^{+i\varphi}$ and return overlap $\langle\psi\mid\mathrm{I}\rangle = \cos\theta\,e^{-i\varphi}$ are complex conjugates, so the uncalibrated phase factors cancel identically ($e^{+i\varphi}e^{-i\varphi} = 1$), and the echo lands squarely back onto the observer's own idempotent existence line $P_{\mathrm{I}}$ with real, nonnegative Born weight $\cos^2\theta$! ## 34.19.2 What Still Escapes After Two Projections: Orthogonal Deficit $\sin^2\theta$, Sign-Chirality Folding, and the $360^\circ$ Azimuthal Blind Cone {#sec-ch34-19-2}

An observer emits $1\cdot\mathbf{I}$ of unit self-reference and, after two orthogonal projections there and back, recovers only $\cos^2\theta\cdot\mathbf{I}$ along its own axis. Our opening epigraph immediately puts its finger on the next deep truth: *"Yet even after two projections, undetermined information still escapes."* Behind the crisp real probability $\cos^2\theta$, what three irreducible layers of geometric information remain hidden from a single-axis observer?

1. **First escape: the orthogonal blind-spot budget $1 - \cos^2\theta = \sin^2\theta$ (`LEAN ✓`)**.
   The missing share $1 - \cos^2\theta = \sin^2\theta$ never evaporates from the universe's ledger. Combining the orthogonal-complement projection identities [`orthogonal_complement_projection_identities`](lean:D5/S3/Quantum/Algebra/OrthogonalProjectionComplement.orthogonal_complement_projection_identities) ($P_M + P_{M^\perp} = \operatorname{id},\; P_M \circ_L P_{M^\perp} = 0$) with the Lagrange–Gram identity [`lagrange_gram_identity`](lean:D5/S3/QuantumBounds/LagrangeGramIdentity.lagrange_gram_identity) ([@thm-lagrange-gram-identity], `LEAN ✓`),

   $$
   \lVert\mathbf{I}\rVert^2 \lVert\mathbf{V}\rVert^2 - (\mathbf{I}\cdot\mathbf{V})^2 \;=\; \lVert\mathbf{I}\wedge\mathbf{V}\rVert^2 \;=\; 1 - \cos^2\theta \;=\; \sin^2\theta,
   $$

   that unrecovered deficit $\sin^2\theta$ is to the last decimal the **squared oriented bivector area** sliced off into the $90^\circ$ perpendicular blind plane!

2. **Second escape: discrete sign and left–right chirality folding (`LEAN ✓` + `manual derivation`)**.
   Multiplying two projections flattens every first-order orientation sign:

   $$
   (+\cos\theta)^2 \;=\; (-\cos\theta)^2 \;=\; \cos^2\theta, \qquad (+\sin\theta)^2 \;=\; (-\sin\theta)^2 \;=\; \sin^2\theta, \qquad \cos(+\theta) \;=\; \cos(-\theta).
   $$

   Whether the target tilts left ($+\theta$) or right ($-\theta$), or points into the forward hemisphere ($\theta$) versus the backward hemisphere ($\pi - \theta$) in Euclidean space, all fold into the exact same equivalence class of $\operatorname{readout}(\mathbf{V}) = \cos^2\theta$—by the partition-topology inseparability theorem [`partition_inseparable_iff_kernel`](lean:D5/S3/ConceptDynamics/ObservationTopology/PartitionTopologyKernel.partition_inseparable_iff_kernel) (`LEAN ✓`), states with identical readout lie in the induced topology's inseparability kernel; and on the circular phase fiber, the circle double-cover lift theorem [`circle_double_cover_history_lift`](lean:D5/S3/ConceptDynamics/Topology/CircleDoubleCoverHistoryLift.circle_double_cover_history_lift) ([@thm-circle-double-cover-history-lift], `LEAN ✓`) proves topologically that the squaring map $z \mapsto z^2$ admits no continuous global inverse selector to unfold antipodal sheets $\pm z$ from an endpoint reading alone!

3. **Third escape: the full $360^\circ$ continuous azimuthal blind cone $\varphi \in [0, 2\pi)$ and the geometric root of uncertainty (`LEAN ✓` + `manual derivation`)**.
   In 3D Euclidean comparison space $\mathbb{R}^3$ (choosing an orthonormal basis $\{\mathbf{e}_1, \mathbf{e}_2\}$ inside the perpendicular plane $\mathbf{I}^\perp$) and in higher-dimensional Hilbert spaces, unit vectors inclined at $\theta$ to $\mathbf{I}$ form not merely two rays, but an entire **$360^\circ$ equal-inclination blind cone** swept around $\mathbf{I}$ (corresponding for a single qubit $\lvert\psi(\theta,\varphi)\rangle = \cos\theta\lvert 0\rangle + e^{i\varphi}\sin\theta\lvert 1\rangle$ to the full latitude circle at Bloch polar angle $\vartheta_{\mathrm{Bloch}} = 2\theta$, echoing the spinning-top addressing of [§34.1](ch34-s01-spherical-addressing.md#sec-ch34-spherical-addressing-inversion)):

   $$
   \mathbf{V}(\theta, \varphi) \;=\; \cos\theta\,\mathbf{I} \;+\; \sin\theta\bigl(\cos\varphi\,\mathbf{e}_1 + \sin\varphi\,\mathbf{e}_2\bigr), \qquad \varphi \in [0, 2\pi).
   $$

   On the unit sphere this cone subtends polar-cap solid angle $\Omega_{\mathrm{cap}}(\theta) = 2\pi(1 - \cos\theta)\;\mathrm{sr}$ and traces an invisible circle of circumference $L_\perp(\theta) = 2\pi\sin\theta$ inside the blind plane $\mathbf{e}_1\wedge\mathbf{e}_2$ (`manual derivation`). Because both $\mathbf{e}_1$ and $\mathbf{e}_2$ lie inside the $90^\circ$ blind plane ($\mathbf{I}\cdot\mathbf{e}_1 = \mathbf{I}\cdot\mathbf{e}_2 = 0$), sliding $\varphi$ freely around $[0, 2\pi)$ leaves the round-trip echo **identically frozen at the same $\cos^2\theta$**!

## 34.19.3 Demystifying Descartes' "Imaginary Number": Invisible to `I` Is `i`, the Double Right-Angle Turn $\hat{\mathbf{i}}^2 = -1$, and Schrödinger's Circular-Arc Time Engine {#sec-ch34-19-3}

In 1637, when René Descartes confronted roots involving $\sqrt{-1}$ that refused to sit anywhere along a single real number line in *La Géométrie* (`Literature: Descartes 1637`), he coined the suspicious epithet *nombre imaginaire*—**"imaginary number"** ("虚数"). For nearly four hundred years afterward, generations of students were misled into thinking $i$ was an artificial bookkeeping fiction conjured up by algebraists, only to be baffled at the doorway of quantum mechanics: why on earth should the fundamental dynamical law of the physical universe require an "imaginary" unit?

Viewed through the observer projection geometry of the Axiom of Comparison $\mathrm{CMP}$, Descartes' accidental word *imaginary* turns out to be an uncanny epistemic bull's-eye (`MODEL`): **"imaginary" never meant ontologically nonexistent—it means standing inside the $90^\circ$ perpendicular blind spot of the first-person observer `I`, where `I` literally cannot see it (`i`)!**

Let us write this insight down in zero-scaffolding geometric algebra (`manual derivation`, connecting directly to [Chapter 5 §5.1](ch05-complex-phase-and-rotors.md#sec-unit-bivector-square)). Denote the observer's own unit reference vector—its visible "real" axis—by $\mathbf{1}_{\mathrm{obs}} \equiv \mathbf{I}_{\mathrm{self}}$ (with self-comparison square $\mathbf{I}_{\mathrm{self}}^2 = +1$), and denote a strictly perpendicular unit direction invisible to the observer by $\mathbf{e}_\perp$ ($\mathbf{e}_\perp^2 = +1$ and $\mathbf{I}_{\mathrm{self}}\cdot\mathbf{e}_\perp = 0$). Taking their difference-preserving exterior product spans the unit oriented bivector $\hat{\mathbf{i}}$—the geometric avatar of the imaginary unit:

$$
\hat{\mathbf{i}} \;\equiv\; \mathbf{I}_{\mathrm{self}} \wedge \mathbf{e}_\perp \;=\; \mathbf{I}_{\mathrm{self}}\,\mathbf{e}_\perp \;=\; -\mathbf{e}_\perp\,\mathbf{I}_{\mathrm{self}}.
$$

Now stand in the shoes of the first-person observer `I` ($\mathbf{I}_{\mathrm{self}}$) and watch what happens when $\mathbf{I}_{\mathrm{self}}$ is multiplied on the right once, and then twice, by $\hat{\mathbf{i}}$:

- **One turn ($\times\hat{\mathbf{i}}$: pivot $90^\circ$ into the blind zone $\longrightarrow$ zero shadow, invisible as `i`)**: Multiplying $\mathbf{I}_{\mathrm{self}}$ once on the right by $\hat{\mathbf{i}}$ uses $\mathbf{I}_{\mathrm{self}}^2 = +1$ to rotate the state $90^\circ$ into the perpendicular vector $\mathbf{e}_\perp$:

  $$
  \mathbf{I}_{\mathrm{self}}\,\hat{\mathbf{i}} \;=\; \mathbf{I}_{\mathrm{self}}(\mathbf{I}_{\mathrm{self}}\mathbf{e}_\perp) \;=\; (\mathbf{I}_{\mathrm{self}}^2)\,\mathbf{e}_\perp \;=\; \mathbf{e}_\perp, \qquad \mathbf{I}_{\mathrm{self}}\cdot\bigl(\mathbf{I}_{\mathrm{self}}\,\hat{\mathbf{i}}\bigr) \;=\; \mathbf{I}_{\mathrm{self}}\cdot\mathbf{e}_\perp \;=\; 0!
  $$

  When `I` reads the projection along its own axis $\mathbf{I}_{\mathrm{self}}$, the meter drops cleanly to **$0$**! ### 34.19.5.1 Two-Branch Matching and Unitary Current Conservation in a Finite Square Barrier {#sec-ch34-19-5-1}

In normalized units $\hbar = 1$, $m = \frac{1}{2}$, and $V_0 = 1$ (so $2m = 1$, $\frac{\hbar^2}{2m} = 1$, and $\frac{\hbar}{m} = 2$), consider a real rectangular barrier on $[0, L]$: $V(x) = 1$ inside the barrier, and zero potential without absorptive loss on both exterior regions $x < 0$ and $x > L$. For incident energy $0 < E < 1$, write the exterior real wavenumber as $k = \sqrt{E}$ and the interior real decay rate as $\kappa = \sqrt{1 - E}$. Under scattering boundary conditions with unit incidence solely from the left, the stationary wavefunction across all space takes the piecewise form

$$
\psi(x) \;=\; \begin{cases}
e^{ikx} + r\,e^{-ikx}, & x < 0, \\
A\,e^{\kappa x} + B\,e^{-\kappa x}, & 0 \le x \le L, \\
t\,e^{ikx}, & x > L.
\end{cases}
$$

Enforcing **simultaneous continuity of $\psi$ and its first derivative $\psi'$** at both $x = 0$ and $x = L$ yields four linear equations that lock both the growing branch $A e^{\kappa x}$ and the decaying branch $B e^{-\kappa x}$ into the interior solution. Direct evaluation of the probability current density $j = (\hbar/m)\operatorname{Im}(\overline{\psi}\,\psi') = 2\operatorname{Im}(\overline{\psi}\,\psi')$ on $\psi(x) = A e^{\kappa x} + B e^{-\kappa x}$ cancels the self-terms of each real exponential identically, leaving only the cross-term

$$
j \;=\; -4\kappa\,\operatorname{Im}(\overline{A} B) \;=\; 4\kappa\,\operatorname{Im}(\overline{B} A).
$$

Consequently, either real exponential branch alone ($A = 0$ or $B = 0$) carries zero current; only when both $A$ and $B$ are nonzero with a nontrivial **relative complex phase** ($\overline{A} B \notin \mathbb{R}$) does this cross-current become nonzero. Note also that on the left exterior $x < 0$, although the superposition of incident and reflected waves produces a partially standing spatial modulation of $|\psi(x)|^2$, whenever $|r| < 1$ this envelope does **not** imply zero net flux: the net left-hand current equals both the interior cross-current and the transmitted current on the right, $2k(1 - |r|^2) = 4\kappa\operatorname{Im}(\overline{B} A) = 2k|t|^2$. With a real potential and equal exterior potentials, the reflectance $R = |r|^2$ and transmittance $T = |t|^2$ strictly satisfy $R + T = 1$, and eliminating the interior coefficients by hand gives the exact transmittance:

$$
T \;=\; \left[1 + \frac{\sinh^2(\kappa L)}{4E(1 - E)}\right]^{-1}.
$$

Conversely, when orthogonal path markers render the two windows fully distinguishable ($\mu = 0$), $P(E) \equiv 1$ identically: interference fringes vanish relative to the positive incoherent baseline $|A_1|^2(1 + a^2) > 0$, rather than reducing total flux to zero. Likewise, what an intermediate probe registers inside a barrier or between slits depends on the specific **probe coupling strength, postselection condition, medium absorption, and weak-measurement readout protocol**, and cannot be lumped under a vague "bridge-cutting" slogan. In particular, in weak-measurement experiments on photons transmitted through a resonant atomic ensemble, the observed **negative weak value** measures the **postselected atomic excitation time** (the weak value of the time atoms spend in the excited state conditioned on photon transmission), never a negative spatial transit time of a particle crossing a barrier.

::: {.problem #prob-ch34-arc-scattering-falsifiability title="Falsifiable Protocol Requirements for Arc Blind-Spot Interpretations Beyond Standard Scattering (Open Problem)"}
If the "chord across an orthogonal blind-spot arc" is used purely as a geometric restatement of the Schrödinger boundary-value problem and two-pathway interference, all of its observable predictions are already fixed by $T(E, L)$ and the relative spectral intensity $P(E)$. Claiming that this arc picture entails new physical effects beyond standard linear scattering or weak-measurement theory requires specifying an explicit probe-coupling Hamiltonian, absorptive loss model, and postselected readout protocol that yield quantitatively distinguishable, experimentally falsifiable deviations from the standard transmittance $T$ or fringe visibility $\frac{2a\mu}{1 + a^2}$ over a stated parameter regime.
:::

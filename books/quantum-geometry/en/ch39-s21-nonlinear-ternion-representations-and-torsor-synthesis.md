## 32.4.1 Exponential Circular-Arc Vectors and Closed-Form BCH Synthesis: Cross-Product Bending and Quotient Boundary Topology {#sec-ch39-21-1}

The most direct three-parameter scheme discards the scalar slot $\mathbf{1}$ and indexes each ternion by a **circular-arc generator vector** $\boldsymbol{\theta} = \theta\hat{\mathbf{u}} = (\theta_1, \theta_2, \theta_3)$ inside the closed ball $\overline{B}_{2\pi}(\mathbb{R}^3) := \{\boldsymbol{\theta} \in \mathbb{R}^3 \mid \lVert\boldsymbol{\theta}\rVert \le 2\pi\}$ of $\mathfrak{su}(2) = \operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{\mathbf{e}_1, \mathbf{e}_2, \mathbf{e}_3\}$ (with $\mathbf{e}_a = \frac{1}{2}\mathbf{i}_a$, $[\mathbf{e}_a, \mathbf{e}_b] = \varepsilon_{abc}\mathbf{e}_c$, and $\mathbf{i}\mathbf{j} = +\mathbf{k}$):

$$
\hat{\mathcal{T}}(\boldsymbol{\theta}) \;:=\; \exp(\theta_1\mathbf{e}_1 + \theta_2\mathbf{e}_2 + \theta_3\mathbf{e}_3) \;=\; \cos\frac{\theta}{2}\,\mathbf{1} \;+\; \sin\frac{\theta}{2}\,\hat{\mathbf{u}}\cdot(\mathbf{i}, \mathbf{j}, \mathbf{k}) \;\in\; S^3,
$$

where $\hat{\mathbf{u}} \in S^2$ is the rotation axis and $\theta = \lVert\boldsymbol{\theta}\rVert \in [0, 2\pi]$ is the spatial arc angle (half-angle $\frac{\theta}{2} \in [0, \pi]$). Replacing flat vector addition $\boldsymbol{\theta}_A + \boldsymbol{\theta}_B$ by the exact closed-form $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ **Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff (BCH) curved synthesis law** $\boldsymbol{\theta}_C = \boldsymbol{\theta}_A \star \boldsymbol{\theta}_B$ (with $\alpha = \frac{\theta_A}{2},\; \beta = \frac{\theta_B}{2},\; \gamma = \frac{\theta_C}{2}$ for the product $\hat{\mathcal{T}}(\boldsymbol{\theta}_C) = \hat{\mathcal{T}}(\boldsymbol{\theta}_A)\hat{\mathcal{T}}(\boldsymbol{\theta}_B)$, `manual derivation`) gives:

$$
\cos\gamma \;=\; \cos\alpha\cos\beta \;-\; (\hat{\mathbf{u}}_A \cdot \hat{\mathbf{u}}_B)\sin\alpha\sin\beta,
$$

$$
\sin\gamma\,\hat{\mathbf{u}}_C \;=\; \sin\alpha\cos\beta\,\hat{\mathbf{u}}_A \;+\; \cos\alpha\sin\beta\,\hat{\mathbf{u}}_B \;+\; \sin\alpha\sin\beta\,(\hat{\mathbf{u}}_A \times \hat{\mathbf{u}}_B).
$$

Writing $(W, \mathbf{V})$ for the right-hand scalar and vector parts, the Lagrange–Gram identity ([`lagrange_gram_identity`](lean:D5/S3/QuantumBounds/LagrangeGramIdentity.lagrange_gram_identity), `LEAN ✓`) gives $(\hat{\mathbf{u}}_A\cdot\hat{\mathbf{u}}_B)^2 + \lVert\hat{\mathbf{u}}_A \times \hat{\mathbf{u}}_B\rVert^2 = 1$, locking norm conservation $W^2 + \lVert\mathbf{V}\rVert^2 \equiv 1$ inside the operator itself. Whenever $\mathbf{V} \neq \mathbf{0}$, $\gamma = \operatorname{atan2}(\lVert\mathbf{V}\rVert, W) \in (0, \pi)$ and $\boldsymbol{\theta}_C = 2\gamma\,\frac{\mathbf{V}}{\lVert\mathbf{V}\rVert} \in B_{2\pi}^\circ(\mathbb{R}^3)$ recover the unique principal vector (with small-angle BCH series $\boldsymbol{\theta}_A \star \boldsymbol{\theta}_B = \boldsymbol{\theta}_A + \boldsymbol{\theta}_B + \frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{\theta}_A \times \boldsymbol{\theta}_B + \mathcal{O}(\theta^3)$). Two structural facts stand out immediately:

1. **Cross-product torsion bends normal overflow back onto the sphere**: The trailing nonlinear cross term $+\sin\alpha\sin\beta\,(\hat{\mathbf{u}}_A \times \hat{\mathbf{u}}_B)$ folds normal breakout back into the three-ball (note the exact sign convention: for the written product $\hat{\mathcal{T}}_A\hat{\mathcal{T}}_B$ the cross term carries $+$, whereas applying rotation $1$ first and rotation $2$ second to column states evaluates $\hat{\mathcal{T}}_2\hat{\mathcal{T}}_1$, whose swapped cross product $\hat{\mathbf{u}}_2 \times \hat{\mathbf{u}}_1 = -\hat{\mathbf{u}}_1 \times \hat{\mathbf{u}}_2$ produces the minus sign written in [§32.2](ch39-s19-inexpressible-ternions-and-number-projections.md#sec-ch39-19-2));
2. **Zero matrix redundancy and exact boundary quotient distinction**: Only three reals $(\theta_1, \theta_2, \theta_3)$ are stored. Topologically (`manual derivation`), on the spinor manifold $S^3 \cong \mathrm{SU}(2)$ the open ball $B_{2\pi}^\circ(\mathbb{R}^3)$ maps bijectively onto $S^3 \setminus \{-\mathbf{1}\}$ while the **entire boundary sphere** $\partial\overline{B}_{2\pi}(\mathbb{R}^3) = \{\boldsymbol{\theta} \mid \lVert\boldsymbol{\theta}\rVert = 2\pi\}$ collapses to the single central element $-\mathbf{1}$ ($\overline{B}_{2\pi}(\mathbb{R}^3)/\partial\overline{B}_{2\pi}(\mathbb{R}^3) \cong S^3$, where $W = -1,\; \mathbf{V} = \mathbf{0}$ leaves the axis undetermined while fixing the group element); descending modulo $\{\pm\mathbf{1}\}$ to $\mathrm{SO}(3) \cong \mathbb{RP}^3$ shrinks the domain to $\overline{B}_\pi(\mathbb{R}^3)$ with **antipodal boundary identification** $\pi\hat{\mathbf{u}} \sim -\pi\hat{\mathbf{u}}$.

## 32.4.2 Dual-Chart Rational Rodrigues Representation: Transcendental-Free Fractions, Shadow Inversion, and the Spinor Sheet Bit {#sec-ch39-21-2}

For digital VLSI circuits and symbolic verification where $\sin, \cos$, and $\operatorname{atan2}$ look-up tables are undesirable, stereographic projection converts curved synthesis into pure rational algebra. On the Gibbs–Rodrigues tangent vector $\mathbf{g} := \frac{\mathbf{v}}{w} = \tan\frac{\theta}{2}\,\hat{\mathbf{u}} \in \mathbb{R}^3$, dividing $\mathbf{V}$ by $W$ yields the matrix-free rational composition law (`manual derivation`):

$$
\mathbf{g}_A \star \mathbf{g}_B \;=\; \frac{\mathbf{g}_A + \mathbf{g}_B + \mathbf{g}_A \times \mathbf{g}_B}{1 - \mathbf{g}_A \cdot \mathbf{g}_B}.
$$

The numerator combines displacement with exterior difference $\mathbf{g}_A \times \mathbf{g}_B$ while the denominator $1 - \mathbf{g}_A \cdot \mathbf{g}_B$ renormalizes curvature via inner-product similarity. Yet as shown in [§32.2](ch39-s19-inexpressible-ternions-and-number-projections.md#sec-ch39-19-2), a single Gibbs chart discards $\pm q$ spinor sheet signs and blows up at $\theta = \pi$.

Upgrading to **Modified Rodrigues Parameters (MRP)** (Wiener 1962; Schaub & Junkins 1996), $\boldsymbol{\sigma} := \frac{\mathbf{v}}{1 + w} = \tan\frac{\theta}{4}\,\hat{\mathbf{u}} \in B_1(\mathbb{R}^3)$ ($\lVert\boldsymbol{\sigma}\rVert \le 1$ on $w \ge 0$), renders the inverse stereographic lift $Q(\boldsymbol{\sigma})$, the active rotation matrix $R(\boldsymbol{\sigma}) \in \mathrm{SO}(3)$, and binary composition $\boldsymbol{\sigma}_A \star \boldsymbol{\sigma}_B$ **purely rational in $+, -, \times, \div$ (zero square roots, zero transcendentals)** (`manual derivation`):

$$
Q(\boldsymbol{\sigma}) \;=\; \frac{\bigl(1 - \lVert\boldsymbol{\sigma}\rVert^2\bigr)\mathbf{1} + 2\boldsymbol{\sigma}\cdot(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j},\mathbf{k})}{1 + \lVert\boldsymbol{\sigma}\rVert^2}, \qquad R(\boldsymbol{\sigma}) \;=\; I_3 + \frac{4\bigl(1 - \lVert\boldsymbol{\sigma}\rVert^2\bigr)[\boldsymbol{\sigma}]_\times + 8[\boldsymbol{\sigma}]_\times^2}{\bigl(1 + \lVert\boldsymbol{\sigma}\rVert^2\bigr)^2},
$$

$$
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_A \star \boldsymbol{\sigma}_B \;=\; \frac{\bigl(1 - \lVert\boldsymbol{\sigma}_B\rVert^2\bigr)\boldsymbol{\sigma}_A + \bigl(1 - \lVert\boldsymbol{\sigma}_A\rVert^2\bigr)\boldsymbol{\sigma}_B + 2(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_A \times \boldsymbol{\sigma}_B)}{1 + \lVert\boldsymbol{\sigma}_A\rVert^2\lVert\boldsymbol{\sigma}_B\rVert^2 - 2(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_A \cdot \boldsymbol{\sigma}_B)},
$$

with $[\boldsymbol{\sigma}]_\times \mathbf{x} := \boldsymbol{\sigma} \times \mathbf{x}$ and denominator $(1 - \boldsymbol{\sigma}_A\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma}_B)^2 + \lVert\boldsymbol{\sigma}_A \times \boldsymbol{\sigma}_B\rVert^2$ (vanishing on bounded inputs solely when the product hits $-\mathbf{1}$). Across the equator $\lVert\boldsymbol{\sigma}\rVert > 1$ ($w < 0$), two exact **"three reals under rational arithmetic $+$ one bit" singularity-free completions** hold (with continuous coordinates in $B_1(\mathbb{R}^3)$ and purely rational $+, -, \times, \div$ composition and decoding maps, `manual derivation`):

- **Scheme A (Shadow-MRP with Spinor Sheet Bit $(\boldsymbol{\sigma}, \epsilon_{\mathrm{sheet}})$)**: Applying **shadow inversion** $\boldsymbol{\sigma} \mapsto \boldsymbol{\sigma}^S := -\frac{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}{\lVert\boldsymbol{\sigma}\rVert^2}$ ($\tan\frac{\theta - 2\pi}{4} = -\cot\frac{\theta}{4}$) folds the coordinate back into $\lVert\boldsymbol{\sigma}^S\rVert < 1$. Because $Q(\boldsymbol{\sigma}^S) = -Q(\boldsymbol{\sigma})$, on the adjoint rotation quotient $\mathrm{SO}(3)$ $\boldsymbol{\sigma}^S$ represents the **identical spatial rotation** with zero extra bits (directly yielding the dual-denominator operator $\star_{\mathcal{S}}$ bounded by $\max(D_+, D_-) \ge 0.5$ in [§37.1](ch39-s22-ternion-field-transformer-and-rational-rotor-dynamics.md)); on spinor $S^3 \cong \mathrm{SU}(2)$, adjoining a `1`-bit **spinor sheet flag** $\epsilon_{\mathrm{sheet}} \in \{+1, -1\}$ via $q = \epsilon_{\mathrm{sheet}} Q(\boldsymbol{\sigma})$ and flipping $\epsilon_{\mathrm{sheet}} \mapsto -\epsilon_{\mathrm{sheet}}$ whenever shadow inversion fires (identifying $(\boldsymbol{\sigma}, +1) \sim (-\boldsymbol{\sigma}, -1)$ on $\lVert\boldsymbol{\sigma}\rVert = 1$, encoding $+\mathbf{1}$ as $(\mathbf{0}, +1)$ and antipodal $-\mathbf{1}$ as $(\mathbf{0}, -1)$) covers all of $S^3$ losslessly;
- **Scheme B (Same-Spinor North–South Stereographic Atlas $(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_h, h)$)**: Setting $h \in \{+1, -1\}$ and $\boldsymbol{\sigma}_h := \frac{\mathbf{v}}{1 + h w}$ with $q = \frac{h(1 - \lVert\boldsymbol{\sigma}_h\rVert^2)\mathbf{1} + 2\boldsymbol{\sigma}_h}{1 + \lVert\boldsymbol{\sigma}_h\rVert^2}$ either glues two closed hemispheres ($\lVert\boldsymbol{\sigma}_h\rVert \le 1$) seamlessly along their shared equator $\lVert\boldsymbol{\sigma}_+\rVert = \lVert\boldsymbol{\sigma}_-\rVert = 1$ ($\boldsymbol{\sigma}_- = \boldsymbol{\sigma}_+$), or—as an open smooth manifold atlas on $\lVert\boldsymbol{\sigma}_h\rVert < 1 + \delta$ ($\delta > 0$)—transitions across the open annulus $(1+\delta)^{-1} < \lVert\boldsymbol{\sigma}_+\rVert < 1 + \delta$ via positive inversion $\boldsymbol{\sigma}_- = +\frac{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_+}{\lVert\boldsymbol{\sigma}_+\rVert^2}$, preserving the spinor without sign flip.

## 32.4.3 Hopf Fiber Linking and Coordinate-Free Spherical Turns: Relational Geometry and Half-Excess Holonomy {#sec-ch39-21-3}

1. **Hopf Principal Circle Bundle $S^1 \hookrightarrow S^3 \xrightarrow{\pi} S^2$ (Visible Base Direction Plus Blind Fiber Phase)**:
   Writing a unit spinor as $z = (z_1, z_2)^\top \in S^3 \subset \mathbb{C}^2$ ($\lvert z_1\rvert^2 + \lvert z_2\rvert^2 = 1$), the Hopf map $\pi(z) = \bigl(2\operatorname{Re}(\bar{z}_1 z_2),\; 2\operatorname{Im}(\bar{z}_1 z_2),\; \lvert z_1\rvert^2 - \lvert z_2\rvert^2\bigr) \in S^2$ splits $S^3$ into a visible 2D Bloch direction $(\vartheta, \varphi) \in S^2$ plus a 1D blind-zone fiber phase $\psi_\pm \in S^1$ (north section $z_+ = e^{i\psi_+}\bigl(\cos\frac{\vartheta}{2},\; e^{i\varphi}\sin\frac{\vartheta}{2}\bigr)^\top$, south section $z_- = e^{i\psi_-}\bigl(e^{-i\varphi}\cos\frac{\vartheta}{2},\; \sin\frac{\vartheta}{2}\bigr)^\top$, transition $\psi_- = \psi_+ + \varphi$, so $\pi_1(S^3) = 0$, Hopf invariant $= +1$, and $S^3 \not\cong S^2 \times S^1$). Under the connection $-i z^\dagger dz = d\psi_\pm + A_\pm$ (`manual derivation` + `Literature`),

## 32.4.4 Prüfer–Baer Heaps (Principal Homogeneous Spaces) and Lie Triple Systems: Origin-Free Ternary Syntax {#sec-ch39-21-4}

We now reach the sharpest algebraic boundary behind three-dimensional inexpressibility: why do classical division-algebra theorems forbid flat 3D representations? Not only does Frobenius's theorem (1878) rule out associative division rings outside dimensions $1, 2, 4$, but Hopf (1940) together with Bott & Milnor (1958) and Kervaire (1958) proved via topological $K$-theory that **even without associativity or a two-sided identity $\mathbf{1}$, any finite-dimensional real division algebra—any zero-divisor-free bilinear multiplication distributing over flat vector addition on $\mathbb{R}^n$**—can exist only in dimensions $1, 2, 4, 8$. What clashes irreconcilably with spherical curvature is **Cartesian vector addition and bilinearity** itself.

In our homogeneous cosmic field ([§33.10](ch39-s12-uniform-field-and-observer-slice.md)–[§38.5](ch39-s13-single-cosmic-field-and-indra-net.md)), neither flat vector addition nor a privileged origin $\mathbf{1}$ exists. By Prüfer (1924, Abelian case) and Baer (1929, general non-Abelian *Schar* / **Heap** theorem), forgetting the privileged identity of any group $G$ promotes it losslessly into a **heap** (in geometry, a **principal homogeneous space** or **torsor**) equipped with a **ternary operation**, whose infinitesimal tangent counterpart is a **Lie Triple System** (Cartan 1926; Jacobson 1949):

$$
[x, y, z] \;:=\; x \cdot y^{-1} \cdot z \qquad \bigl(x, y, z \in \mathbb{T}_3\bigr), \qquad \{\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}, \mathbf{c}\} \;:=\; \bigl[[\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}]_\times,\, \mathbf{c}\bigr]_\times \;=\; (\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{c})\,\mathbf{b} \;-\; (\mathbf{b}\cdot\mathbf{c})\,\mathbf{a} \qquad \bigl(\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}, \mathbf{c} \in \mathbb{R}^3\bigr).
$$

Both ternary operators close natively in three dimensions (`manual derivation` + `Literature`):

- **Heap transport $[x, y, z]$ erases the privileged real axis while preserving chirality**: $[x, y, z]$ obeys left/right Mal'cev idempotent cancellation $[x, x, z] = z$ and $[x, z, z] = x$, bilateral translation equivariance $[gx, gy, gz] = g[x, y, z]$ and $[xh, yh, zh] = [x, y, z]h$, and non-Abelian **para-associativity**

  $$
  \bigl[[x, y, z],\, u,\, v\bigr] \;=\; \bigl[x,\, [u, z, y],\, v\bigr] \;=\; \bigl[x,\, y,\, [z, u, v]\bigr] \;=\; x y^{-1} z u^{-1} v
  $$

  (note the reversed middle triple $[u, z, y]$ required by $(u z^{-1} y)^{-1} = y^{-1} z u^{-1}$; basepoint freedom means no privileged $\mathbf{1}$, not outer commutativity—indeed $[\mathbf{i}, \mathbf{1}, \mathbf{j}] = +\mathbf{k} \neq -\mathbf{k} = [\mathbf{j}, \mathbf{1}, \mathbf{i}]$ preserves full non-Abelian chirality). Geometrically, $[x, y, z]$ **uses any local reference bridge $y$ to transport the relative comparison arc $x y^{-1}$ losslessly onto $z$**, recovering binary multiplication $x *_o z := [x, o, z]$ only when an observer pins an origin $o$;
- **The Lie triple bracket $\{\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}, \mathbf{c}\}$ closes on $\mathbb{R}^3$ and literally equals spherical Riemann curvature**: Whereas the binary cross product $[\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}]_\times = \mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{b}$ satisfies the Jacobi identity (forming a Lie algebra) yet fails binary associativity, the nested triple bracket $\{\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}, \mathbf{c}\} = (\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b})\times\mathbf{c} = (\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{c})\mathbf{b} - (\mathbf{b}\cdot\mathbf{c})\mathbf{a}$ closes inside $\mathbb{R}^3$, satisfying skew-symmetry $\{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b},\mathbf{c}\} = -\{\mathbf{b},\mathbf{a},\mathbf{c}\}$, the cyclic Bianchi identity $\{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b},\mathbf{c}\} + \{\mathbf{b},\mathbf{c},\mathbf{a}\} + \{\mathbf{c},\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}\} = \mathbf{0}$, and the ternary derivation identity. In an orthonormal tangent frame on unit round $S^3$ ($K = +1$), the Riemann curvature tensor is literally $R(\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b})\mathbf{c} = +\{\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}, \mathbf{c}\}$ under do Carmo's sign convention $R(X,Y)Z = \nabla_{[X,Y]}Z - [\nabla_X, \nabla_Y]Z$ (and $-\{\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}, \mathbf{c}\}$ under Lee's $R(X,Y)Z = [\nabla_X, \nabla_Y]Z - \nabla_{[X,Y]}Z$; in norm-$\frac{1}{2}$ generators $[\mathbf{e}_a, \mathbf{e}_b] = \varepsilon_{abc}\mathbf{e}_c$, $\nabla_X Y = \frac{1}{2}[X,Y]$ gives $R(X,Y)Z = \pm\frac{1}{4}[[X,Y], Z]$, with global $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ vs. $\mathrm{SO}(3)$ sheet topology carried by the heap).

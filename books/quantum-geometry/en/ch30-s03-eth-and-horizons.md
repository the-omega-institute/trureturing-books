**Literature result, not formalized in this book.** All fine-grained complexity hypotheses, classical satisfiability bounds, and October 2026 research preprints discussed in this section belong to the external research literature outside the `trureturing@dev` declarations locked in this book:

1. **ETH, SETH, and the Sparsification Lemma (R. Impagliazzo & R. Paturi, *J. Comput. Syst. Sci.* **62**, 367–375, 2001, conference version *CCC* 1999; R. Impagliazzo, R. Paturi & F. Zane, *J. Comput. Syst. Sci.* **63**, 512–530, 2001)**: Fix a clause width $k \ge 3$, with $n$ variables and $m$ input clauses. Consider all real rates $\delta\ge0$ for which some deterministic $k$-SAT algorithm has the running-time upper bound

   $$
   2^{\delta n}(n+m)^{O(1)}.
   $$

   Write $s_k$ for the infimum of these rates. The **Exponential Time Hypothesis (ETH)** conjectures $s_3 > 0$ (thus ruling out deterministic time $2^{o(n)}(n+m)^{O(1)}$ for 3-SAT), while the **Strong Exponential Time Hypothesis (SETH)** conjectures $\lim_{k \to \infty} s_k = 1$. Standard **Tseitin transformation** reduces a Boolean circuit of size $S$ linearly to a 3-SAT formula on $N = \Theta(S)$ variables; randomized algorithms in the PPSZ line (R. Paturi, P. Pudlák, M. E. Saks & F. Zane, *J. ACM* **52**, 2005, and subsequent refinements) achieved upper bounds near $1.307^n \approx 2^{0.386 n}$ (here $r \approx 0.386$ is only a randomized-algorithm benchmark, distinct from deterministic ETH).
2. **Located primary papers and distinct formalization scopes**: Theorem 1.1 of OpenAI's 67-page [*The Exponential Time Hypothesis Is False*](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/4eaee87548250b2682affda7d05d947c05554bae/preprints/The-Exponential-Time-Hypothesis-is-False-October-25-2026/paper.pdf) (October 17, revised October 25) states a deterministic exact-counting algorithm for $k$-CNF, for every fixed $k\ge2$, with bit complexity

   $$
   (n+m)^{O_k(1)}2^{O_k(n\log\log n/\sqrt{\log n})}
   $$

   and polynomial bit space. Its [formalization scope document](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/4eaee87548250b2682affda7d05d947c05554bae/lean/docs/378.md) reports the weaker time bound $(L+1)^{O_k(1)}2^{O_k(n(\log\log n)^2/\sqrt{\log n})}$ for canonical encoding length $L$, with a polynomial bound on visited tape cells. These exponents must be distinguished. Theorem 1 of Anthropic's 49-page [*A $2^{o(n)}$ Time Algorithm for 3-SAT*](https://github.com/anthropics/formal-math/blob/16a5ce41304fcdaea4e0be7c132e1c361dc26da5/eth/paper.pdf) (October 26) gives the word RAM bound $2^{O(\min\{k^2n\log\log n/\sqrt{\log n},\,km/\sqrt{\log m}\})}$. Appendix E distinguishes its Mathlib multi-stack Turing-machine propositions and encoding promises from the unformalized input-encoding and simulation steps connecting them to the paper model; according to its repository description, this paper's core step is tensor-network contraction with rank bounds on grouped operators. These are external papers and published descriptions of formalization scope. This book has independently rebuilt neither development and includes neither in its frozen theorems.

## 26.3.3 Lambert $W$ Crossovers, Bridge Cone Cuts, and Finite Witnesses {#sec-ch26-3-3-lambert-bridge-cone-tseitin-diagonal}

The preceding two sections presented the subexponential 3-SAT curve, the bridge-graph flow-cut prototype, and finite resource bounds through tables and interactive scenes. Once asymptotic $O$-notation is replaced by explicit constants and exact inequalities, four quantitative questions emerge: where does the schematic curve cross the benchmark and how sensitive is that scale to its prefactor, what separates the bridge golden cone from the full four-cut phase diagram, how fast may encoding blow-up grow before destroying the $2^{o(n)}$ scale and where are the finite resource bounds sharp, and how does the kernel-versus-specification boundary appear on a minimal finite space? We address these four questions in turn.

### 26.3.3.1 Lambert $W_{-1}$ Crossover, Super-Quadratic Elasticity, and Integer-Power Brackets {#sec-ch26-3-3-1-lambert-crossover-and-integer-brackets}

Write $u := \log_2 n > 1$ and the normalized profile $f(u) := \frac{\log_2 u}{\sqrt{u}}$, so that the schematic per-variable rate curve of [@ex-ch30-galactic-crossover-scale] becomes $r_c(n) = c\,f(u)$ (for constant $c > 0$). Differentiating with respect to $u$ yields $f'(u) = \frac{2 - \ln u}{2(\ln 2)\,u^{3/2}}$, whose sign switches from positive to negative only at $u_* = e^2$ (rounding to $7.389056$, corresponding to $n_* = 2^{e^2} \approx 167.620652$), while at both ends $u \to 1^+$ and $u \to +\infty$ the profile satisfies $f(u) \to 0$. Hence $f$ attains its unique global maximum at $u_* = e^2$ with peak value $f_{\max} = \frac{2}{e\ln 2}$ (rounding to $1.061476$); moreover, the integer identities $4^2 = 2^4$ and $16^4 = 2^{16}$ give $f(4) = f(16) = 1$, so the curve $r_1(n)$ lies strictly above the naive brute-force rate $1$ precisely on the open interval $16 < n < 65536$.

To locate the crossing of the descending branch $u > e^2$ with a positive level $\theta > 0$, set $t := \frac{1}{2}\ln u > 1$, which converts $c\,f(u) = \theta$ into $t e^{-t} = \frac{\theta\ln 2}{2c}$. A descending crossing exists if and only if $c > c_{\mathrm{crit}}(\theta) := \frac{\theta\,e\ln 2}{2}$, in which case the right-hand side lies strictly inside $(0, e^{-1})$ and the Lambert $W$ function's lower real branch $W_{-1}$ (R. M. Corless, G. H. Gonnet, D. E. G. Hare, D. J. Jeffrey & D. E. Knuth, *On the Lambert W Function*, 1996, §1, pp. 330–331, [10.1007/BF02124750](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02124750)) yields the unique descending root; at $c = c_{\mathrm{crit}}(\theta)$, the curve is tangent to the level $\theta$ at its peak $u_* = e^2$; for $c < c_{\mathrm{crit}}(\theta)$, the entire curve stays strictly below $\theta$. Furthermore, $\frac{d\ln c}{dt} = 1 - \frac{1}{t}$ together with $\ln u_{\mathrm{cross}} = 2t$ yields the exact logarithmic elasticity of the crossing scale with respect to $c$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(u) &:= \frac{\log_2 u}{\sqrt{u}}, \qquad f'(u) = \frac{2 - \ln u}{2(\ln 2)\,u^{3/2}}, \qquad f_{\max} = f(e^2) = \frac{2}{e\ln 2}, \\
u_{\mathrm{cross}}(c, \theta) &= \exp\!\left(-2\,W_{-1}\!\left(-\frac{\theta\ln 2}{2c}\right)\right) \qquad \left(c > c_{\mathrm{crit}}(\theta) := \frac{\theta\,e\ln 2}{2}\right), \\
\frac{c}{u_{\mathrm{cross}}}\,\frac{\partial u_{\mathrm{cross}}}{\partial c} &= \frac{2t}{t - 1} > 2 \qquad \left(t := \frac{1}{2}\ln u_{\mathrm{cross}} > 1\right).
\end{aligned}
$$

As a randomized-algorithm benchmark, Paturi et al. (R. Paturi, P. Pudlák, M. E. Saks & F. Zane, *An Improved Exponential-Time Algorithm for $k$-SAT*, 2005, Abstract, p. 337, and Theorem 1(ii), p. 342, [10.1145/1066100.1066101](https://doi.org/10.1145/1066100.1066101)) establish for unique 3-SAT the analytical exponent $2\ln 2 - 1 \approx 0.386$; below we adopt the exact decimal comparison level of [@ex-ch30-galactic-crossover-scale], namely $\theta = 0.386 = \frac{193}{500}$. At this level, the critical constant is $c_{\mathrm{crit}} \approx 0.363645$; at the observable-universe atom scale $u = 266$ we have $f(266) \approx 0.493901$, so exact crossing at $n = 2^{266}$ occurs at $c_{\mathrm{horizon}} = 0.386 / f(266) \approx 0.781533$. At $c = 1$, the exact descending crossing sits at $u_{\mathrm{cross}}(1, 0.386) \approx 559.078227$ (since $f(558) \approx 0.386255 > f(559) \approx 0.386018 > 0.386 > f(560) \approx 0.385783$), exceeding the coarse table reading $2^{558}$ above by just over $1$ unit in $\log_2 n$; at $c = \frac{1}{2}$, the crossing lies at $u_{\mathrm{cross}}(\frac{1}{2}, 0.386) \approx 57.171127$ ($\frac{1}{2}f(57) \approx 0.386293 > 0.386 > \frac{1}{2}f(58) \approx 0.384595$), agreeing with the interactive card above.

Because the elasticity $\frac{2t}{t - 1}$ strictly exceeds $2$ (at $c = 1$ approximately $2.924580$, and at $c = \frac{1}{2}$ approximately $3.954987$), the logarithmic scale $u_{\mathrm{cross}}$ grows super-quadratically in $c$; more precisely, at fixed $\theta$ the crossover equation yields $t - \ln t = \ln c + \ln\frac{2}{\theta\ln 2}$ and $e^t = \frac{2ct}{\theta\ln 2}$, so $t \sim \ln c$ and $u_{\mathrm{cross}} = \bigl(\frac{2ct}{\theta\ln 2}\bigr)^2$, making the variable count $n = 2^{u_{\mathrm{cross}}}$ grow super-exponentially as $\exp(\Theta(c^2 \ln^2 c))$: halving $c$ from $1$ to $\frac{1}{2}$ drops the crossing size from $10^{168.30}$ to $10^{17.21}$, a span exceeding $151$ orders of magnitude. ### 26.3.3.2 Golden-Cone Boundary and Four-Cut Phase Diagram of the Bridge Graph {#sec-ch26-3-3-2-bridge-golden-cone-and-four-cuts}

Turn now to the discrete bridge-graph prototype of §26.3.1, whose four positive integer dimensions satisfy the ordering $0 < a \le b$ and $0 < c \le d$ and produce the four cut capacities $C_1 = 3ac$, $C_2 = ad$, $C_3 = bc$, and $C_4 = 3bd$. The cone hypothesis [`cone_QMinCut`](lean:D5/S3/Quantum/TensorNetworks/BridgeGraph/QuantumMaxFlowBound.cone_QMinCut) imposes the two quadratic constraints $a^2 + b^2 \le 3ab$ and $c^2 + d^2 \le 3cd$. Writing aspect ratios $r := b/a \ge 1$ and $s := d/c \ge 1$, dividing $a^2 + b^2 \le 3ab$ by $a^2$ yields $r^2 - 3r + 1 \le 0$: since the smaller root $\frac{3 - \sqrt{5}}{2} < 1$, the ordered cone condition is precisely $1 \le r, s \le \varphi^2$, where $\varphi^2 = \frac{3 + \sqrt{5}}{2}$ (rounding to $2.618034$) is the square of the golden ratio!

This irrational aspect-ratio boundary is straddled with unit absolute defect $1$ by alternating Fibonacci pairs. Define the quadratic polynomial $P(x, y) := x^2 + y^2 - 3xy$; direct expansion yields the integer polynomial identity $P(x, x + y) = -x^2 - xy + y^2 = -P(y, x + 2y)$. Fix the Fibonacci sequence with initial values $F_1 = 1, F_2 = 1, F_3 = 2$ and set $(x, y) = (F_k, F_{k+1})$, giving $x + y = F_{k+2}$ and $x + 2y = F_{k+3}$. Induction from $P(1, 2) = -1$ then yields for every positive integer $k \ge 1$ the identity $P(F_k, F_{k+2}) = (-1)^k$: every odd-indexed pair $(1, 2), (2, 5), (5, 13), \dots$ lies strictly inside the golden cone with defect $-1$, whereas every even-indexed pair $(1, 3), (3, 8), (8, 21), \dots$ lies strictly outside it with defect $+1$.

Meanwhile, the ordering of all four cut capacities is governed purely by the rational ratios $(r, s)$. Since $a \le b$ and $c \le d$, we always have $C_4 = 3bd \ge 3\max(ad, bc) > \min(ad, bc)$, so the fourth cut $3bd$ can never be the unique minimum cut; dividing all four capacities by $ac > 0$ expresses the normalized minimum cut as $\min(3, r, s)$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(x, y) &:= x^2 + y^2 - 3xy, \qquad P(x, x + y) = -P(y, x + 2y), \\
P(F_k, F_{k+2}) &= (-1)^k \qquad (\forall k \ge 1), \\
\frac{\min(C_1, C_2, C_3, C_4)}{ac} &= \min(3, r, s) \qquad \left(r := \frac{b}{a} \ge 1,\; s := \frac{d}{c} \ge 1\right).
\end{aligned}
$$

This immediately separates the golden cone from the full four-cut phase diagram. Whenever $(a, b, c, d)$ lies inside the golden cone ($r, s \le \varphi^2 < 3$), we have $C_1 = 3ac > \max(ad, bc) \ge \min(ad, bc)$, which both yields $\min(C_1, C_2, C_3) = \min(ad, bc)$ and satisfies the full dimensional hypotheses of the `trureturing` bridge max-flow min-cut theorem, equating maximum attainable rank with $\min(ad, bc)$. Outside the cone, comparing $(r, s)$ with $3$ splits the cut capacities into three further regimes: whenever $\min(r, s) < 3$ (such as $(3, 8, 3, 8)$, whose aspect ratio $\frac{8}{3} \in (\varphi^2, 3)$), the smaller outer cut $\min(ad, bc)$ still strictly beats the bridge cut $3ac$; when $\min(r, s) = 3$, the bridge cut $3ac$ ties with whichever outer cut attains the minimum (and all three tie if $r = s = 3$); and the bridge cut $3ac$ wins strictly if and only if both $b > 3a$ and $d > 3c$ (outside the cone, this phase diagram compares cut capacities alone and does not extend the cone's maximum-rank equality). ### 26.3.3.3 Encoding Threshold, Four-Gate Tseitin Bijection, and Resource Bounds {#sec-ch26-3-3-3-encoding-threshold-tseitin-and-resources}

We next examine the polynomial reduction blow-up and Tseitin encoding raised in [@rem-ch30-eth-subexponential-literature] and [@ex-ch30-galactic-crossover-scale], together with the three finite resource bounds cited in §26.3.1 and §26.3.2. In fine-grained complexity theory, Impagliazzo and Paturi formulate ETH for $k\text{-SAT}$ and the exponent infimum $s_k$ (R. Impagliazzo & R. Paturi, *On the Complexity of $k$-SAT*, 2001, pp. 367–368, [10.1006/jcss.2000.1727](https://doi.org/10.1006/jcss.2000.1727)), while the sparsification lemma reduces any $n$-variable $k\text{-SAT}$ formula to a disjunction of at most $2^{\varepsilon n}$ sparse $k\text{-SAT}$ formulas, each having linearly many clauses in $n$ (R. Impagliazzo, R. Paturi & F. Zane, *Which Problems Have Strongly Exponential Complexity?*, 2001, §2, Corollary 1, p. 523, [10.1006/jcss.2001.1774](https://doi.org/10.1006/jcss.2001.1774)). By contrast, if a reduction inflates a natural parameter $n$ to $N = \alpha n^\beta$ variables (fixed $\alpha > 0, \beta > 1$), substituting at fixed $c > 0$ into the schematic exponent $c\,G(N)$ (where $G(x) := x\,f(\log_2 x)$) drives the effective rate per original variable $n$, namely $\widetilde{r}(n) = c\,G(N)/n$, to $+\infty$. For $\beta = 2$ and $\alpha = c = 1$, evaluating at $n = 16, 256, 2^{16}, 2^{133}$ yields $\widetilde{r}(n)$ approximately $16.970563$, exact integer $256$, approximately $57926.187515$, and approximately $5.378106 \times 10^{39}$.

More generally, for any encoded size $N(n) > 2$, set the threshold scale $L(n) := \frac{n\sqrt{\log_2 n}}{\log_2\log_2 n}$. Writing $u = \log_2 x > 1$, the logarithmic derivative $\frac{d}{du}\ln G(2^u) = \ln 2 + \frac{1}{u\ln u} - \frac{1}{2u} > \ln 2 - \frac{1}{2} > 0$ proves that $G(x)$ is strictly increasing on $x > 2$; moreover, for every fixed $\lambda > 0$, the ratio $\log_2(\lambda L(n)) / \log_2 n \to 1$ implies $G(\lambda L(n)) / n \to \lambda$. Comparing $N(n)$ in both directions against arbitrarily small multiples $\lambda L(n)$ establishes the exact necessary and sufficient size threshold for keeping the displayed exponent sublinear at fixed $c > 0$ (a full running-time transfer must also account for reduction overhead):

$$
\begin{aligned}
\widetilde{r}(n) &= c\alpha\,n^{\beta - 1}\,f\bigl(\log_2\alpha + \beta\log_2 n\bigr) \qquad \bigl(N = \alpha n^\beta\bigr), \\
c\,G\bigl(N(n)\bigr) = o(n) &\iff N(n) = o\bigl(L(n)\bigr), \qquad L(n) := \frac{n\sqrt{\log_2 n}}{\log_2\log_2 n}.
\end{aligned}
$$

At the linear-encoding end, Tseitin's transformation (G. S. Tseitin, *On the Complexity of Derivation in Propositional Calculus*, 1983 English reprint, pp. 466–483, [10.1007/978-3-642-81955-1_28](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-81955-1_28)) assigns one auxiliary variable to each Boolean gate of fan-in at most $2$ and converts each gate equivalence into at most four clauses of width at most $3$: an AND gate $z \leftrightarrow x \land y$ expands into the $3$ clauses $(x \lor \neg z)$, $(y \lor \neg z)$, and $(\neg x \lor \neg y \lor z)$, whereas an XOR gate $z \leftrightarrow x \oplus y$ requires the $4$ three-literal clauses excluding its $4$ falsifying triples. Take a four-gate full-adder fragment: three inputs $(x_1, x_2, x_3)$ determine the four gate variables $s_1 = x_1 \oplus x_2$, $\mathrm{sum} = s_1 \oplus x_3$, $c_1 = x_1 \land x_2$, and $c_2 = s_1 \land x_3$ (whose disjunction $c_1 \lor c_2$ gives the carry bit; naming an explicit OR gate would add $1$ variable and $3$ clauses). Across the $7$ variables of this fragment, the resulting $14$ clauses of width at most $3$ admit among all $2^7 = 128$ truth assignments exactly $8$ satisfying assignments, in bijection with the fragment's $2^3 = 8$ input vectors and thus preserving exact solution counts.

Likewise, the three finite `trureturing` resource bounds cited earlier admit direct sharpness checks under their exact hypotheses. For the finite-rank mode-concentration bound, any nonnegative summable spectrum $\lambda_j \ge 0$ with total sum $\sum_{j=0}^{\infty} \lambda_j = \frac{RW}{\pi}$ and threshold $\tau > 0$ forces every finite subset of modes with $\lambda_j \ge \tau$ to contribute at least its cardinality times $\tau$, so the threshold set is finite with cardinality at most $\frac{RW}{\pi\tau}$; on the abstract scalar spectrum $(\frac{1}{4}, \frac{1}{4}, \frac{1}{4}, 0, \dots)$ at $(R, W, \tau) = (\pi, \frac{3}{4}, \frac{1}{4})$, both sides equal $3$. For the finite-memory history capacity theorem, $N$ trace-$1$ positive semidefinite $d \times d$ matrices $\rho_i$ paired with a perfectly distinguishing POVM $E_j$ ($\sum_j E_j = I$ and $\operatorname{Tr}(E_j\rho_i) = \delta_{ij}$) force $N \le d$, attaining equality $N = d$ on computational basis states and their rank-one projectors.

Third, under $1 \le \kappa$ and $2\kappa \le N$, the open integrable circuit minimum-depth theorem establishes $D_{\min}(N, \kappa) = \lfloor N/(\kappa + 1)\rfloor + 1$. Along the path from $0$ to $N$, consecutive gate conflicts form $N$ path edges oriented by whether each right endpoint lies in the $\kappa$-site selected set, and any directed run of $m$ consecutive edges requires at least $m + 1$ layers. Distributing the $N - \kappa$ unselected edges across the $\kappa + 1$ gaps separated by the $\kappa$ selected edges forces a longest unselected run of at least $\lceil (N - \kappa)/(\kappa + 1)\rceil = \lfloor N/(\kappa + 1)\rfloor$; meanwhile $2\kappa \le N$ implies $N - \kappa \ge \kappa > \kappa - 1$, which suffices to keep all $\kappa - 1$ interior gaps nonempty, isolating every selected edge into a run of length $1 \le \lfloor N/(\kappa + 1)\rfloor$ so balanced placement attains the bound $\lfloor N/(\kappa + 1)\rfloor + 1$ (independently checked by earliest-layer scheduling for all permitted pairs $(N, \kappa)$ with $2 \le N \le 12$).

# 30.3 The Exponential Time Hypothesis (ETH), 2026 Tensor-Network Rank Reduction, and the Cosmic Horizon: Scale and Interpretation of Upper-Bound Curves {#sec-coda-eth-tensor-rank-and-cosmic-horizon}

> *"Viewed inside our geometric architecture of 'fields ($P$) and lines ($NP$), polyhedral approximations to the sphere, and the finite observer's horizon,' placing the Exponential Time Hypothesis (ETH) alongside the late-October 2026 tensor-network rank-reduction preprints does not overturn finite-observer $P \neq NP$, but instead supplies a cosmological-scale asymptotic illustration of two core geometric principles: local three-body constraints cannot weld an absolutely rigid true sphere, and the observer's horizon remains uncrossable."*

To keep external complexity-theoretic literature strictly separated from our orthogonal geometric interpretation (`MODEL`), we first isolate the definitions of ETH/SETH, classical $k$-SAT upper bounds, and the October 2026 preprints inside an explicit literature remark before developing our geometric and scale analysis:

::: {.remark #rem-ch30-eth-subexponential-literature title="Literature Note on ETH / SETH and the Late-October 2026 Subexponential 3-SAT Preprints"}
**Literature result, not formalized in this book.** All fine-grained complexity hypotheses, classical satisfiability bounds, and October 2026 research preprints discussed in this section belong to the external research literature outside the `trureturing@dev` declarations locked in this book:

1. **ETH, SETH, and the Sparsification Lemma (R. Impagliazzo & R. Paturi, *CCC* 1999; R. Impagliazzo, R. Paturi & F. Zane, *J. Comput. Syst. Sci.* **62**, 367–375, 2001)**: Fix a clause width $k \ge 3$, with $n$ variables and $m$ input clauses. Consider all real rates $\delta\ge0$ for which some deterministic $k$-SAT algorithm has the running-time upper bound

   $$
   2^{\delta n}(n+m)^{O(1)}.
   $$

   Write $s_k$ for the infimum of these rates. The **Exponential Time Hypothesis (ETH)** conjectures $s_3 > 0$ (thus ruling out deterministic time $2^{o(n)}(n+m)^{O(1)}$ for 3-SAT), while the **Strong Exponential Time Hypothesis (SETH)** conjectures $\lim_{k \to \infty} s_k = 1$. The accompanying **Sparsification Lemma** proves that for every fixed $\varepsilon > 0$ and $k \ge 3$, any $n$-variable $k$-SAT formula reduces in time $2^{\varepsilon n}(n+m)^{O(1)}$ to the disjunction of at most $2^{\varepsilon n}$ sparse $k$-SAT formulas, each having at most $c(k, \varepsilon)\,n = O_{k,\varepsilon}(n)$ clauses. Standard **Tseitin transformation** reduces a Boolean circuit of size $S$ linearly to a 3-SAT formula on $N = \Theta(S)$ variables; randomized algorithms in the PPSZ line (R. Paturi, P. Pudlák, M. E. Saks & F. Zane, *J. ACM* **52**, 2005, and subsequent refinements) achieved upper bounds near $1.307^n \approx 2^{0.386 n}$ (here $r \approx 0.386$ is only a randomized-algorithm benchmark, distinct from deterministic ETH).
2. **Located primary papers and distinct formalization scopes**: Theorem 1.1 of OpenAI's 67-page [*The Exponential Time Hypothesis Is False*](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/4eaee87548250b2682affda7d05d947c05554bae/preprints/The-Exponential-Time-Hypothesis-is-False-October-25-2026/paper.pdf) (October 17, revised October 25) states a deterministic exact-counting algorithm for $k$-CNF, for every fixed $k\ge2$, with bit complexity

   $$
   (n+m)^{O_k(1)}2^{O_k(n\log\log n/\sqrt{\log n})}
   $$

   and polynomial bit space. Its [formalization scope document](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/4eaee87548250b2682affda7d05d947c05554bae/lean/docs/378.md) reports the weaker time bound $(L+1)^{O_k(1)}2^{O_k(n(\log\log n)^2/\sqrt{\log n})}$ for canonical encoding length $L$, with a polynomial bound on visited tape cells. These exponents must be distinguished. Theorem 1 of Anthropic's 49-page [*A $2^{o(n)}$ Time Algorithm for 3-SAT*](https://github.com/anthropics/formal-math/blob/16a5ce41304fcdaea4e0be7c132e1c361dc26da5/eth/paper.pdf) (October 26) gives the word RAM bound $2^{O(\min\{k^2n\log\log n/\sqrt{\log n},\,km/\sqrt{\log m}\})}$. Appendix E distinguishes its Mathlib multi-stack Turing-machine propositions and encoding promises from the unformalized input-encoding and simulation steps connecting them to the paper model. These are external papers and published descriptions of formalization scope. This book has independently rebuilt neither development and includes neither in its frozen theorems.
3. **Attributing the examination accurately**: The [primary community statement](https://ethdiscussion.blog/2026/10/26/eth-refutation-examination-and-discussion/) by Impagliazzo, Paturi, and Williams invites independent examination and explicitly says their involvement is not certification. Examination must not be presented as endorsement. Fortnow's [October 28 article](https://blog.computationalcomplexity.org/2026/10/the-exponential-time-refutation.html) links both papers and explains that polynomial reductions can inflate the variable count: a $2^{o(n)}$ bound does not automatically transfer to every NP problem. Neither this section's geometric analogy nor its illustrative curves decide standard $P$ vs. $NP$.

:::

The function $g(n)=n\log\log n/\sqrt{\log n}$ grows faster than $\log n$, so for fixed $c>0$ the comparison function $2^{c g(n)}$ exceeds every fixed-degree polynomial. But $T(n)=2^{O(g(n))}$ is only an upper bound: it permits faster, even polynomial-time algorithms and does not prove $P\neq NP$. For a problem with natural parameter $n$ whose chosen circuit encoding has size $S=\Theta(n^2)$, if Tseitin transformation produces $N=\Theta(n^2)$ variables, substitution into the discussed formula gives the upper bound $2^{O(n^2\log\log n/\sqrt{\log n})}$. This changes the scale of that encoding and upper-bound expression; it is no super-exponential cost that every optimal algorithm must pay. The table's $c=1$ curve fixes neither the actual constant nor the validity threshold hidden in big $O$, and does not determine the relative speed of real algorithms.

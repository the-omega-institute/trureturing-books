**Readings and assumptions (`MODEL`).** A reading declares part of the configuration unobservable, namely a closed subgroup $K$ of the right transports (normalised by the left transports, [L]), and gives the classes $M/K$ a weight $w([x], [y]) = F\bigl(s_K(x, y)\bigr)$ with $s_K(x, y) := \sup_{k \in K} c(kx, y)$, $F$ continuous and $F(1) = 1$. Binarity [B]: every class has exactly one orthogonal class, the unique-zero-partner clause of Law (II) at the level of states. The remaining inputs concern only the weight: the weight has the form $F \circ s_K$; composability, i.e. the class correlations and the weights of two independent systems both multiply; and normalisation, i.e. weights against a maximal orthogonal family sum to one.

**Binarity selects the clock (`manual derivation`).** Under [L] a reading is binary if and only if $K$ is a clock; neither the weight form nor composability is used. Checking the closed subgroups of the right transports one by one: for trivial $K$ the orthogonal set is a two-sphere; for $K = \{\pm 1\}$ the orthogonal classes form $\mathbb{R}P^2$; for a cyclic group of order at least $3$ inside a clock they form a circle; non-abelian finite groups, the normaliser of a clock and $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ have no orthogonal classes; only a clock has exactly one. **The exponent (`manual derivation`).** The weight form, composability and normalisation then only fix the exponent: a composable reading has $F(s) = s^p$ ($p \ge 0$), and normalisation gives $p = 2$, the Born weight $\beta$. Permuting the profile and seed together permutes the returned label. The minimum support is $6r$, where $r$ is the least integer with $4^r\ge 3q+1$. At the uniform profile, a three-cycle cannot fix any seed, since the returned label would have to be fixed. A transposition cannot fix a seed either. Use the profile that puts one half on each exchanged label and zero on the fixed label. A fixed seed would return the zero-mass label with positive probability. Each seed orbit therefore has six elements and has constant weight within the orbit. At $(k,k,6q-2k)/(6q)$, the two tied labels receive equal numbers of seeds per orbit, each between zero and three. With $r$ orbits there can be at most $4^r$ different first-label masses, and $k=0,\ldots,3q$ requires $3q+1$ of them.

For attainment, use $r$ six-element orbits, each with the permutation group acting by left multiplication. In units of $1/(6q)$ give all seeds in each orbit the same integer weight. Start with $1,4,4^2,\ldots$ as the orbit weights and stop at total orbit weight $q$, clipping the last weight to reach that sum. At an intermediate step let $T$ be the sum of the earlier orbit weights and $d$ the next weight. Then $d\le 3T+1$. Using zero to three copies of each weight, the attainable totals extend from $0,\ldots,3T$ to $0,\ldots,3(T+d)$, since the four translates overlap or touch. Also, for any three nonnegative integer target counts with sum $6(T+d)$, dividing each by $d$ and rounding down gives three capacities with sum at least six. Otherwise the sum of the targets would be at most $5d+3(d-1)<6(T+d)$. Allocate the six new seeds within these capacities and use the earlier orbits for the remainder. The bounded-digit step is the complete partition criterion in [Rødseth, Lemmas 3.1–3.2](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/journals/JIS/VOL10/Rodseth/rodseth8.html).

For three distinct coordinates the allocation suffices. For exactly two tied coordinates, the transposition partitions each six-element orbit into three pairs. For the required digit, send that many pairs to the two tied labels, one seed to each label in each pair. Send both seeds in every other pair to the third label. At the uniform profile send the seed indexed by permutation $g$ to $g(1)$, giving two seeds to each label. Choose these readouts at one representative of each profile orbit. Transport by label permutations to every profile in that orbit. Readouts at the representative respect its stabilizer, so two permutations reaching the same profile give the same transported rule. This construction assumes the seed law as an input.

**Weight denominators at minimum three-outcome support (manual derivation).** Use a minimum-support law for the complete three-outcome probability grid of denominator $6q$, with $q>0$, positive weights, and pointwise covariance under all label permutations. Write $r$ for the number of six-element seed orbits. Multiplying each seed weight by $6q$ gives a common scaled weight $u_j$ within orbit $j$. The tied profiles require the integer levels $0,\ldots,3q$ to be represented by sums $\sum_j d_j u_j$ with $d_j\in\{0,1,2,3\}$. Minimality gives $3q+1>4^{r-1}$. Thus each $u_j$ has reduced denominator $1$, $2$, or $3$, by [the integer-multiple bound](lean:D5/S3/Combinatorics/SubsetSpectrum/IntegralDigitWeights.exists_integral_multiple_of_capacity_gap).

[The mixed denominator bound](lean:D5/S3/Combinatorics/SubsetSpectrum/MixedDigitDenominators.no_mixed_denominators_of_capacity_gap) excludes the coexistence of reduced denominators two and three. Suppose one orbit has reduced denominator $2$ and another has reduced denominator $3$, with weights $a/2$ and $b/3$ respectively. Fix all the other digits. The difference between any two integer representations in this class, after multiplication by six, shows that the chosen digits agree modulo $2$ at the first orbit and modulo $3$ at the second orbit. Indeed, the coefficient $3a$ is odd and $2b$ is indivisible by three. Within a fixed residue class each digit has at most two choices. Hence there are at most $4^{r-2}\cdot4=4^{r-1}$ distinct integer masses, below the required count. A single multiplier in $\{1,2,3\}$ therefore makes all scaled weights integral.

**Integer block demands for more outcomes (manual derivation).** Let $k\ge2$ be the number of labels and $m>0$ the grid denominator. In a profile of integer coordinates summing to $m$, group labels whose coordinates agree. Write $s_i$ for the size of group $i$, $v_i$ for its common coordinate, and $H=\prod_i s_i!$. The permutations that preserve the profile form a subgroup of order $H$. In a regular permutation-group orbit of seeds, the subgroup partitions the seeds into blocks of size $H$. A block routed equivariantly to group $i$ sends $H/s_i$ of its seeds to each label in that group. If the orbit weights are integer multiples of $1/(m(k-2)!)$, the required weighted block total for group $i$ is $(k-2)!s_i v_i/H$.

These demands are integers for every profile exactly when $k-1$ divides $m$. The sufficiency statement is [the stabilizer block divisibility theorem](lean:D5/S3/Combinatorics/Partitions/StabilizerFactorialDivisibility.factorial_product_dvd_scaled_coordinate). For sufficiency, fix a group $i$ and remove one label from it. The remaining sizes $b_j$ have sum $n=k-1$. Let $Q=n!/\prod_j b_j!$, an integer multinomial coefficient. For each $j$, the value $Q b_j/n$ is an integer. If $b_j=0$ it is zero, and otherwise it is the multinomial coefficient obtained by deleting one further label from group $j$. Since $\sum_j b_j v_j=m-v_i$, the difference $Qm/n-\sum_j v_j Qb_j/n$ is the integer $Qv_i/n$. Restoring the deleted label gives $Qv_i/n=(k-2)!s_i v_i/H$. For necessity, use the profile in which one label has coordinate $m$ and every other label has coordinate zero. The singleton group has demand $m/(k-1)$.

**Minimum support for $k$ outcomes.** Suppose $k\ge2$ and the positive denominator $m$ is divisible by every integer from $1$ to $k$. Include every nonnegative integer profile of total $m$, divided by $m$. Use one finite seed law with positive weights, invariant under all label permutations. Its deterministic readout must reproduce the marginal probabilities and be equivariant when a permutation acts on both the profile and the seed. The minimum support is $k!r$, where $r$ is the least integer with $(k+1)^r\ge m/(k-1)+1$. Writing $m=k(k-1)q$, the exact feasibility condition for a budget of $k!n$ is $kq+1\le(k+1)^n$, as stated in [`grid_support_budget_iff`](lean:D5/S3/ConceptDynamics/Attribution/PermutationGridSupport.grid_support_budget_iff).

To see why every seed orbit is regular, take a nonidentity permutation and a profile uniform on its moved labels and zero elsewhere. The number of moved labels divides $m$. A seed fixed by the permutation must return a fixed label at this profile, although each such label has probability zero. For the lower bound, fix one distinguished label and consider the profiles with the other $k-1$ coordinates all equal to $a$, for $0\le a\le m/(k-1)$. The permutations fixing the distinguished label divide each regular seed orbit into $k$ blocks. Within a block, either no seed returns a tied label or each tied label receives exactly $(k-2)!$ seeds. On each orbit the contribution to a tied label has $k+1$ possible values. The $m/(k-1)+1$ distinct required probabilities give the bound.

For attainment, write $m=k(k-1)q$ and use the integer orbit weights $1,k+1,(k+1)^2,\ldots$, clipping the last to sum to $q$. This uses $r$ orbits. With $t$ groups of equal coordinates and $U=k!/H$ blocks per orbit, the integer group demands above sum to $Uq$. For $t\ge2$, merging sizes while leaving one label in each smaller group gives $H\le(k-t+1)!$. This implies $U\ge k(t-1)$, since $(k-1)!/(k-t+1)!\ge t-1$. The same block bound holds for $t=1$.

When adding weight $d$, let $T$ be the sum of the earlier weights. Clipping gives $d-1\le kT$, so $(t-1)(d-1)\le UT$. For any group demands of total $U(T+d)$, divide each demand by $d$ and round down. If the capacities summed to at most $U-1$, the total demands would be at most $(U-1)d+t(d-1)<U(T+d)$. Allocate the $U$ blocks within these capacities and fill the remaining demands with earlier orbits. Each allocation gives a readout respecting the profile stabilizer. Choose one readout per profile orbit and transport by permutation. The seed weights in normalized probability units are the orbit weights divided by $m(k-2)!$. The weight sequence uses the complete partition criterion of [Rødseth, Lemmas 3.1–3.2](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/journals/JIS/VOL10/Rodseth/rodseth8.html).

**Normalization on the eight-outcome grid.** Set $k=8$ and $m=210$, using a single finite seed law with positive, permutation-invariant weights on the complete grid. The readout is equivariant when the same permutation acts on both the profile and the seed. The primes $2,3,5,7$ all divide $210$, which forces every seed orbit to be regular. A nontrivial seed stabilizer would contain a permutation of prime order by Cauchy's theorem. Such a permutation has a cycle of that prime length. At the profile uniform on this cycle and zero elsewhere, a fixed seed would return a zero-probability label.

With two orbits, let $u,v$ be the per-seed weights in the two orbits scaled by $210\cdot6!$. Normalization gives $u+v=15/4$. Profiles with seven tied coordinates require all integers from $0$ through $30$ to be sums $xu+yv$, with both digits between $0$ and $8$. Fixing $x-y$ gives a diagonal. One step along it adds $15/4$ to the mass, so a diagonal of length $\ell$ contains at most $\lceil\ell/4\rceil$ integral masses. The nine-by-nine grid has one diagonal of length nine and two of each length from one through eight, for at most $27$ integral masses in total. One orbit gives the same contradiction after adding a zero-weight orbit to the count.

Three orbits suffice. The complete eight-outcome grid of denominator $1680$ satisfies the divisibility hypothesis above and has a three-orbit construction. Multiplying each integer coordinate by eight embeds the original grid into the finer grid. The embedding preserves all probabilities and commutes with each label permutation, so the fine-grid law and readout also calibrate the original grid. The minimum support is $3\cdot8!=120960$. Under these assumptions a natural-number budget $B$ is feasible exactly when $120960\le B$. See the [exact support budget theorem](lean:D5/S3/ConceptDynamics/Attribution/EightOutcomeGridSupport.grid_support_budget_iff).

**Grids of denominator $(k-1)q$.** Let $k\ge2$ and $q\ge1$, and suppose every prime at most $k$ divides $m=(k-1)q$. Use the shared positive permutation-invariant seed law and pointwise equivariant deterministic readout defined above. For $n\ge0$, a budget of $k!(n+1)$ seeds suffices exactly when
$$q+1\le (\gcd(k,q)+1)(k+1)^n.$$
The prime divisibility condition forces every seed orbit to be regular. The profiles tying $k-1$ labels require the $q+1$ integer levels from zero through $q$. An orbit contributes a digit between zero and $k$ times its scaled weight, and the weights sum to $q/k$. The denominator of that fraction in lowest terms controls the number of digit combinations that give integer masses. For attainment, bundle copies of the fractional orbit weight according to that denominator and allocate the residual demands using integer weights. The [sharp budget theorem](lean:D5/S3/ConceptDynamics/Attribution/FractionalGridSupport.fractional_grid_budget_iff) gives this equivalence.

**Eight-outcome grids at denominators $1470$ and $1890$.** Here $q$ is $210$ and $270$, respectively, and $\gcd(8,q)=2$ in both cases. The threshold is $3\cdot9^n$. The first feasible values of $n$ are $2$ and $3$, since $27<211\le243<271\le2187$. The minimum supports are therefore $120960$ and $161280$.

**When calibration forces free seed actions.** On the complete $k$-label grid of positive denominator $m$, every calibrating law has a free seed action exactly when every prime $p\le k$ divides $m$. If a nonidentity permutation fixes a seed, its stabilizer contains an element of prime order. A profile supported uniformly on one of that element's prime-length cycles forces the seed to return a zero-probability label, which contradicts its positive weight. [Exact freeness criterion](lean:D5/S3/ConceptDynamics/Attribution/GridSeedFreeness.every_calibrating_law_free_iff).

If some prime $p\le k$ does not divide $m$, take a permutation that is a single $p$-cycle. Any profile fixed by this cycle has a positive coordinate outside the cycle, since the total count inside the cycle is a multiple of $p$. At an orbit of profiles on which the cycle acts freely, choose any positive coordinate at one profile and transport that choice around the orbit. Together these choices give a table fixed by the cycle. Use all tables that select a positive coordinate at every profile as seeds, weighting each table by the product of the probabilities of its entries. The weight is invariant under label permutations. Summing over all entries except the one at the current profile gives the required marginal probability. The chosen table has positive weight and a nontrivial stabilizer.

**Maximum mass of seeds fixed by the whole group.** Let a group $G$ act on finite input and output sets $X$ and $Y$, with $X$ nonempty. Let $P$ be a probability kernel satisfying $P(gx,gy)=P(x,y)$. Write $G_x=\{g\in G:gx=x\}$ and $A_x=\{y\in Y:gy=y\text{ for every }g\in G_x\}$. Among all calibrations with a finite seed set, positive $G$-invariant seed weights and a pointwise equivariant deterministic readout, the maximum total mass of seeds fixed by the whole group is
$$
\alpha=\min_{x\in X}\sum_{y\in A_x}P(x,y).
$$

At input $x$, a seed fixed by the whole group must read out a label in $A_x$. Thus every row gives an upper bound. To attain the smallest bound, put $a_x=\sum_{y\in A_x}P(x,y)$. If $\alpha>0$, take mass $\alpha$ from within $A_x$ in each row in proportion to its probabilities. This yields the subkernel $Q(x,y)=\alpha P(x,y)\mathbf{1}_{A_x}(y)/a_x$. At a representative of each input orbit, draw an output independently from the normalized $Q$ and transport it to the rest of the orbit by the group action. The representative's stabilizer fixes the sampled output, so the transported value is independent of the chosen group element. Each resulting table can serve as a seed fixed by the whole group.

If $\alpha<1$, calibrate the residual kernel $(P-Q)/(1-\alpha)$ by independently drawing each row of a table. Covariance makes the product weights of these tables invariant under the group action. Mix the two laws in proportion to their masses to recover the shared calibration. At $\alpha=0$, use independent row draws from the original kernel. At $\alpha=1$, use only the tables fixed by the whole group. Keep only positive-weight seeds at each endpoint. The [sharp upper bound and attainment theorem](lean:D5/S3/ConceptDynamics/Attribution/FixedKernelMass.maximal_fixed_seed_mass) covers both endpoints.

**The row bound can be strict for a proper subgroup.** Let $S_4$ permute four labels and fix two inputs. The first row chooses a label uniformly and the second chooses a two-element subset uniformly. Use a common finite seed set with an $S_4$ action, positive invariant weights summing to one, and deterministic readouts that are pointwise equivariant under $S_4$. Let $H$ be generated by the transposition $(12)$. Two of the four labels and two of the six subsets are fixed by $H$, so the smaller row bound is $1/3$.

Reading both inputs at one seed gives a pair $(i,B)$. A transposition fixing $B$ must exchange the two labels inside $B$ or the two outside it. Exactly one of these transpositions fixes the label $i$ as well. The seed law is invariant under $S_4$, so all six conjugate transpositions fix the same mass of readout pairs, namely $1/6$. A transposition fixing a seed also fixes both of its outputs. This bounds the mass of $H$-fixed seeds by $1/6$. Give each of the 24 pairs weight $1/24$, let $S_4$ act diagonally on the pairs, and use the two coordinate projections as readouts. This product law attains $1/6$. The [upper bound](lean:D5/S3/ConceptDynamics/Attribution/TranspositionFixedMass.seeded_transposition_mass_le) and [attaining product law](lean:D5/S3/ConceptDynamics/Attribution/TranspositionFixedMass.uniform_label_pair_attains) give the exact value.

**Seeds fixed by a normal subgroup.** Let a group $G$ act on a nonempty finite input set $X$ and a finite output set $Y$, let $P$ be a $G$-covariant probability kernel, and let $H\triangleleft G$. Among finite calibrations of $P$ with positive $G$-invariant weights and pointwise $G$-covariant readouts, the maximum total weight of seeds fixed by $H$ is
$$
\min_{x\in X}\sum_{y\in Y^{H\cap G_x}}P(x,y).
$$
Here $G_x$ fixes the input $x$, and $Y^{H\cap G_x}$ consists of the outputs fixed by every element of $H\cap G_x$. Covariance forces any $H$-fixed seed to read out in this set at $x$, giving the bound row by row. [Sharp fixed mass for a normal subgroup](lean:D5/S3/ConceptDynamics/Attribution/NormalSubgroupFixedMass.maximal_normal_subgroup_fixed_seed_mass).

For attainment, first restrict the input and output actions to $H$ and apply the full-group result to $H$. Push its attaining seed law to the deterministic tables $t:X\to Y$ by sending each seed to its entire readout. This preserves the row marginals, and every $H$-fixed seed maps to an $H$-fixed table. Under the table action $(g\cdot t)(x)=g\cdot t(g^{-1}\cdot x)$, the image of $G$ is finite. Average the table law over that image. Each translate has marginals $P$ by covariance. Normality preserves the set of $H$-fixed tables under every translate, so the average has at least the original $H$-fixed seed mass on that set. Use the positive-weight tables as the seeds and evaluate at $x$. The row bound makes the attained mass exact. The finite averaging step is an instance of orbit averaging for symmetric linear constraints. [Bödi, Herr and Joswig, Proposition 13](https://arxiv.org/html/1012.4941#Thmtheorem13)

**When a subgroup can fix all the seed weight.** Let a group $G$ act on a finite input set $X$ and a finite output set $Y$, and let $P$ be a $G$-covariant probability kernel. For $H\le G$, let $N$ be the normal closure of $H$ in $G$, generated by all conjugates of $H$. A finite calibration with positive $G$-invariant weights and pointwise $G$-covariant readouts can give its $H$-fixed seeds total weight one if and only if
$$
P(x,y)>0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad y\in Y^{N\cap G_x}
\qquad(x\in X,\ y\in Y).
$$
Here $G_x$ is the stabilizer of $x$, and $Y^{N\cap G_x}$ consists of the outputs fixed by every element of $N\cap G_x$.

If the $H$-fixed mass is one, positivity forces $H$ to fix every seed. The kernel of the seed action is normal, so it contains $N$. Every positive-probability output is the readout of some seed. An element of $N$ fixing the input also fixes that seed, so covariance forces it to fix the output. For an empty input set, one seed of weight one fixed by all of G suffices. For nonempty input, the row support condition gives mass one on $Y^{N\cap G_x}$ for every row. The [maximum fixed mass formula for normal subgroups](lean:D5/S3/ConceptDynamics/Attribution/NormalSubgroupFixedMass.maximal_normal_subgroup_fixed_seed_mass) supplies a calibration with full $N$-fixed mass. Since $H\le N$, the same calibration has full $H$-fixed mass.

**An exact linear program for any subgroup.** Let $G$ act on a nonempty finite input set $X$ and a finite output set $Y$, and let $P$ be a $G$-covariant probability kernel. Fix a subgroup $H\le G$ and use finite shared seed laws with positive $G$-invariant weights and pointwise $G$-covariant readouts. Let $T=Y^X$ be the set of deterministic readout tables, with action $(g\cdot t)(x)=g\cdot t(g^{-1}\cdot x)$. Write $T^H$ for the tables fixed by $H$. For each $G$-orbit $O\subseteq T$ define
$$
a_O(x,y)=\frac{|\{t\in O:t(x)=y\}|}{|O|},
\qquad
b_O=\frac{|O\cap T^H|}{|O|}.
$$
The maximum total weight of $H$-fixed seeds is the attained optimum of
$$
\max_{(z_O)} \sum_O z_O b_O,
\qquad
z_O\ge 0,\quad \sum_O z_O=1,\quad
\sum_O z_O a_O(x,y)=P(x,y)\quad(x\in X,\ y\in Y).
$$
Send each seed to its table of outputs across all rows. The table distribution is invariant and has marginals $P$. Every $H$-fixed seed maps to an $H$-fixed table, so the fixed mass can only increase. An invariant table distribution assigns equal weight to all tables within each orbit. Its orbit totals $z_O$ therefore obey the displayed constraints, and its fixed mass is the displayed objective.

Conversely, for any feasible vector give each table in $O$ weight $z_O/|O|$. Use the positive-weight tables as the seeds, with evaluation at $x$ as the readout. This realizes the kernel and attains the objective exactly. Independent row draws give an invariant table distribution, so the constraints are feasible. They define a closed subset of the finite-dimensional probability simplex, and the linear objective attains a maximum there. [Exact maximum for an arbitrary subgroup](lean:D5/S3/ConceptDynamics/Attribution/TableOrbitWeights.maximal_table_fixed_mass).

**When one table law attains both fixed-mass bounds.** Let $X$ be a nonempty finite input set and let a monoid $G$ act on a finite output set $Y$. Each row $P(x,\cdot)$ is a $G$-invariant probability law. Let $A,B\subseteq Y$ be events for which acting by $G$ preserves membership in both directions. Write $a,b,i,u$ for the minimum row masses of $A,B,A\cap B,A\cup B$, respectively. For an event $E$, set $T_E=\{t:X\to Y\mid \forall x,\ t(x)\in E\}$. An invariant probability law on tables with marginals $P$ gives $T_A,T_B,T_{A\cap B}$ masses $a,b,i$ simultaneously if and only if

$$
a+b\le i+u.
$$

Since $T_A\cap T_B=T_{A\cap B}$ and $T_A\cup T_B\subseteq T_{A\cup B}$, the sum of the two single-event table masses is at most $i+u$. Conversely, use a common law of two Boolean flags with single-flag masses $a,b$ and intersection mass $i$. The inequality $a+b\le i+u$ and the row lower bounds allow an ordered coupling to each row's flag law under the coordinatewise order on the Boolean square. Lift to the original outputs and take the product across rows conditional on the common flags. The rows that attain the respective minima supply upper bounds that make all three masses exact. [Simultaneous attainment for the two flags and their intersection](lean:D5/S3/ConceptDynamics/Attribution/JointFixedTableMass.two_table_masses_attain_row_minima_iff)

For example, under the trivial action, the first row gives equal probabilities to flags $(1,0),(0,1)$ and the second gives equal probabilities to $(1,1),(0,0)$. Both single-flag row minima are $1/2$. Pairing the second row's $(1,1)$ with $(1,0)$ attains the first bound. Pairing it with $(0,1)$ attains the second. Here $i=0$ and $u=1/2$, so one table law cannot attain both bounds at once.

**Three-bit profiles of opposite parity.** The first row is uniform on the four even profiles and the second is uniform on the four odd profiles. For a coupling, let $\alpha_i$ be the mass on which bit $i$ is $1$ in both rows. The attainable vectors are exactly those satisfying

$$
\begin{gathered}
\alpha_1\le \alpha_2+\alpha_3,\quad
\alpha_2\le \alpha_3+\alpha_1,\quad
\alpha_3\le \alpha_1+\alpha_2,\\
\alpha_1+\alpha_2+\alpha_3\le 1.
\end{gathered}
$$

Each bit has a fair marginal in both rows. Its common-zero mass is therefore also $\alpha_i$, and its agreement probability is $2\alpha_i$. Opposite parity requires either a one-bit flip or a three-bit flip. If $r_i$ is the probability of flipping only bit $i$, then $2\alpha_i=r_j+r_k$ for distinct $i,j,k$, which gives $r_i=\alpha_j+\alpha_k-\alpha_i$. The probability of flipping all three bits is $1-\sum_i\alpha_i$. Nonnegativity of these four probabilities yields the stated inequalities. Conversely, sample a uniform even profile and independently choose one of the four flips with the given probabilities. Each fixed odd flip is a bijection from the even profiles to the odd profiles, so the construction has the two required marginals. [Exact region of common-flag masses](lean:D5/S3/ConceptDynamics/Attribution/ParityProfileRegion.attainable_iff)

The largest possible $\min_i\alpha_i$ is $1/3$. The sum bound gives the upper bound, and choosing the three single-bit flips with equal probability attains $1/3$ at every coordinate.

**Odd flip laws in any dimension.** Let $P$ and $Q$ be uniform on the even and odd $n$-bit profiles, respectively, with $n\ge1$. Read the bitwise sum $D=P+Q$ as the flip mask. Its distribution can be any probability law on the odd profiles. Given such a law $\rho$, sample $P$ and $D\sim\rho$ independently and set $Q=P+D$. Each fixed odd mask maps even profiles bijectively to odd profiles, so $Q$ remains uniform. Every coupling of the two parity classes also has an odd mask. [Displacement laws for finite kernels](lean:D5/S3/Analytic/Probability/UniformFiberCoupling.displacement_iff)

The group is $G=(\mathbb F_2)^n$ under bitwise addition, and $\phi:G\to\mathbb F_2$ sums the coordinates. The two rows are its fibers over $0$ and $1$, and the displacement $-P+Q$ equals $P+Q$. When $n\ge2$, each coordinate is fair in each row. Flipping that bit and one other bit preserves parity and exchanges its two values. Thus the mass where the two rows both have a $1$ at bit $i$ is $\tfrac12(1-\Pr[D_i=1])$. The odd-mask law determines all the common-one masses through this formula.

**Realizing common-flag masses as subgroup-fixed masses.** For a finite index set $I$, take $G=(\mathbb F_2)^I$ and outputs $(p,c)$ with profile $p\in\{0,1\}^I$ and colour $c\in\mathbb F_2$. The element $g\in G$ preserves $p$ and adds $\sum_{j:p_j=0}g_j$ to $c$. The subgroup $H_i$ generated by the $i$-th coordinate vector fixes this output exactly when $p_i=1$.

For each entry $w(p,q)$ of a profile coupling, assign mass $w(p,q)/4$ to each of the four tables $((p,c),(q,d))$ obtained by choosing the two colours. Each row mass is split equally between the colours, and the table law is invariant under the diagonal action of $G$. Summing over the four colour pairs recovers every entry of $w$. The mass of tables fixed by $H_i$ is exactly the common-one mass at bit $i$. Conversely, summing out the colours in any invariant table law with those split marginals yields a coupling of the profile marginals with the same fixed-mass readout. [Coordinate subgroups and exact recovery of the coupling](lean:D5/S3/ConceptDynamics/Attribution/CoordinateFlagCoupling.invariant_coupling_iff)

Attach a fair colour to every output of the three-bit parity kernel above. Its attainable table-fixed mass vectors form the same region, with the group fixing both inputs pointwise. An equivariant seed realization pushes forward to its readout tables, and every $H_i$-fixed seed maps to an $H_i$-fixed table. The minimum coordinate seed-fixed mass is therefore at most $1/3$. Choose the three one-bit flips with equal probability and use the positive-mass coloured tables as seeds. This attains $1/3$ at all three coordinates in one realization.

**An optimal uniform calibration for rational marginals.** Let $G$ act on finite input and output sets $X$ and $Y$, let $P$ be a $G$-covariant probability kernel, and let $H$ be any subgroup. If a positive integer $m$ makes every $mP(x,y)$ integral, a uniform seed law attains the maximum $H$-fixed mass. One sufficient seed count is $m|\det B|$, where $B$ is an invertible square submatrix of the integer constraint matrix below, using the positive support of a suitable optimizer as its columns.

Write $q_O$ for the weight of each deterministic readout table $t:X\to Y$ in its $G$-orbit $O$. The normalization row of $A$ has entry $|O|$ in column $O$. The row for cell $(x,y)$ has entry $|\{t\in O:t(x)=y\}|$. The corresponding entries of $b$ are $1$ and $P(x,y)$. Let $c_O$ count the tables in $O$ that are fixed by $H$. Sending seeds equivariantly to their complete readout tables preserves the marginals and sends every $H$-fixed seed to an $H$-fixed table. Conversely, using the positive-weight tables as seeds and evaluating them realizes any feasible table law. The maximum fixed mass is therefore the maximum of $\sum_O c_Oq_O$ subject to $Aq=b$ and $q\ge0$. Independent draws from the rows give an invariant table law. The normalization row makes the feasible set bounded, and its closedness ensures the maximum is attained.

Choose an optimizer $q$ with the fewest positive coordinates, and let $S$ be its positive support. If the columns on $S$ were dependent, there would be a nonzero vector $v$ supported on $S$ with $Av=0$. Choose its sign so that $\sum_O c_Ov_O\ge0$. The strictly positive normalization row forces at least one negative entry of $v$. Move from $q$ along $v$ until the first positive weight vanishes. The step size is the minimum of $q_O/(-v_O)$ over $v_O<0$. The resulting point is feasible and has objective at least the optimum, with fewer positive coordinates. This contradiction gives independent columns on $S$, so $|S|$ rows can be selected to make $B$ invertible.

Let $b_B$ be the right-hand side restricted to these rows. From $Bq_S=b_B$ and the adjugate identity,
$$
m(\det B)q_S=\operatorname{adj}(B)(mb_B).
$$
The right-hand side is integral, so $N=m|\det B|$ makes every $Nq_O$ a nonnegative integer. Give each table in $O$ exactly $Nq_O$ copies. The group moves the table and leaves the copy index fixed. The normalization row counts exactly $N$ copies, and the cell counts give the prescribed marginals. A copy is $H$-fixed exactly when its table is $H$-fixed, so the uniform seed law attains the same optimum. For empty input there is one empty table, and one fixed seed attains the maximum of $1$. [Equal-weight optimum for a rational kernel](lean:D5/S3/ConceptDynamics/Attribution/UniformTableCalibration.uniform_kernel_optimum).

For a prescribed table law $q$, any uniform realization must have exactly $Nq_O$ seeds over every table in $O$. The copy construction proves that a positive integer $N$ is feasible exactly when every $Nq_O$ is a nonnegative integer. For rational table weights, these seed counts are the positive multiples of the least common multiple of the reduced denominators. An irrational table weight rules out every finite uniform realization of the prescribed law. To find the least seed count that attains the optimum, minimize over all optimal table laws with rational weights. The sparse-support argument has a counterpart in multimarginal transport with a common marginal and coordinate-permutation symmetry. [Friesecke and Vögler, Lemma 5.1 and Theorem 5.1](https://arxiv.org/html/1801.00341#S5)

**Bounding the normal closure's fixed mass.** Take a finite calibration with seed set $S$ and invariant probability weights $a$. Let $N$ be the normal closure of $H$ in $G$, and write $\alpha_K=a(S^K)$ for the mass fixed by a subgroup $K$. For each $g\in G$, let $B_g$ consist of seeds that are moved by some element of $gHg^{-1}$. Count each distinct set once. Write $r$ for the number of distinct sets and $\ell(s)$ for the number containing seed $s$. The finiteness of $S$ makes $r$ finite even for an infinite group.

Each $B_g$ is a translate of $B_1$ and has the same mass $1-\alpha_H$. The normal closure moves a seed exactly when the seed lies in at least one $B_g$. Summing first by event and then by seed gives

$$
r(1-\alpha_H)=\sum_{s\in S}a(s)\,\ell(s).
$$

If every positive-weight seed moved by $N$ lies in at least $d$ distinct events, with $d\ge 1$, then

$$
\alpha_N\ \ge\ \max\!\left(0,\,1-\frac{r}{d}(1-\alpha_H)\right).
$$

The choice $d=1$ always applies. A larger value requires the overlap to cover every positive-weight seed moved by $N$ with the stated multiplicity.

For the exact gap, let $d$ be any nonnegative integer such that every positive-weight seed moved by $N$ lies in at least $d$ distinct events. Write $L_K=1-\alpha_K$. Seeds fixed by $N$ lie outside every failure event, so
$$
rL_H-dL_N=\sum_{s\in S\setminus S^N} a(s)\,(\ell(s)-d).
$$
Every summand is nonnegative. Thus $dL_N=rL_H$ exactly when each positive-weight seed moved by $N$ lies in precisely $d$ distinct events. [Exact loss and equality criterion](lean:D5/S3/ConceptDynamics/Attribution/ConjugateFailureMultiplicity.normal_closure_loss_gap_and_eq).

Counting by event multiplicity also gives the probability identity for a finite union of events. [Kuai, Alajaji and Takahara, Lemma 1](https://fady-alajaji.github.io/Journal/d_math00.pdf)

Equality is possible for all integers $1\le d<r$. Generate a group by independent flips of $r$ bit coordinates and a cyclic shift of the coordinates. A state consists of a $d$-element coordinate set and a bit assignment on that set. Shifts move the selected coordinates, and a flip changes a bit wherever its coordinate is selected. Let $H$ be generated by the flip at a single coordinate. Its conjugate flips range over the $r$ coordinates, with one event for each coordinate being selected. These events are distinct and each state lies in exactly $d$ of them. Uniform weights give $\alpha_H=1-d/r$ and $\alpha_N=0$. Use the states as outputs of a single-input kernel and take the identity readout. Covariance forces any $K$-fixed seed to read out in $Y^K$, so the two masses are also the separate maxima for $H$ and $N$ for this kernel.

**Which uniform seed counts attain the optimum.** Add a row to the integer orbit-count matrix recording the number of tables fixed by $H$ in each orbit. Call the resulting matrix $M$. Its right-hand side $b'$ contains the total mass $1$, the prescribed marginals, and the optimal fixed mass $V$. The optimal uniform calibration for rational kernels makes $V$ rational. Let $m$ be the least common denominator of $b'$ and set $w=mb'$. Let $J$ consist of the orbits with positive weight in at least one optimal table law, and write $M_J$ for the corresponding columns.

An optimal uniform calibration sends seeds equivariantly to their readout tables. Each table in the same orbit has the same number of seed preimages. The fixed mass of the table law is at least that of the seeds and at most $V$, so both equal $V$. Conversely, replicate every table according to its orbit count and let the group preserve the copy index. This realizes the same marginals and fixed mass. The feasible positive counts therefore satisfy
$$
N=mk,\qquad M_J n=k w,\qquad n\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{J}.
$$
Here $m$ divides $N$ because each entry of $Nb'$ is a count. Let $h$ be the order of the class of $w$ in the additive quotient $\mathbb Z^{\operatorname{rows}(M)}/M_J\mathbb Z^J$. Every feasible $N$ is a multiple of $mh$. At positive $N$, an orbit outside $J$ has zero copies because dividing its feasible count vector by $N$ gives an optimal table law. The integer count theorem handles all event rows together. In the reverse construction, each subgroup fixes a copy exactly when it fixes the underlying table. [Integer orbit-count equivalence](lean:D5/S3/ConceptDynamics/Attribution/UniformOrbitCounts.orbit_event_counts_match_fiber).

Choose one optimal solution giving positive weight to each orbit in $J$ and average these choices. This gives an optimal solution that is strictly positive on $J$. Gaussian elimination gives a rational particular solution and a rational nullspace basis for $M_Jq=b'$. Rational approximation of the coefficients in this basis gives a strictly positive rational solution. Multiply by a common denominator of the solution that is also a multiple $Bm$ of $m$. This gives a strictly positive integer vector $p$ with $M_Jp=Bw$. Thus $h$ is finite and divides $B$.

Choose an integer vector $u$ with $M_Ju=hw$. Set $b=B/h$, take $k_0=1+\sum_{j\in J}|u_j|$, and put $r=k_0b$. The vectors $k_0p$ and $u+k_0p$ are nonnegative and realize $mhr$ and $mh(r+1)$ seeds. The original $p$ already realizes $mhb$ seeds. Since $b$ divides $r$, the normalized counts $b$ and $r+1$ are coprime. The two-generator Frobenius bound makes $mht$ feasible for every positive integer $t\ge(b-1)r$. The [integer-fiber period and cutoff theorem](lean:D5/S3/Combinatorics/NumericalSemigroup/IntegerFiberDegrees.eventual_counts) gives the divisibility and the sufficient cutoff above under the strictly positive integer witness. In particular, if $b=1$, every positive multiple of $mh$ is feasible. Below the sufficient cutoff the original nonnegative integer equations decide feasibility. Empty input gives one empty table, so every positive seed count is feasible. For the general conductor theorem for affine semigroups, see [Bruns, Gubeladze and Trung, Lemma 4.2.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0101116).

For this event family of normalization, kernel marginals and optimal fixed mass, one theorem constructs the positive integer witness from real feasibility and gives the same positive-budget equivalence, period divisibility and sufficient cutoff. [Exact budget criterion, period and cutoff](lean:D5/S3/ConceptDynamics/Attribution/ActiveOrbitBudget.eventual_uniform_event_budgets)

**A sharper cutoff from two integer witnesses.** Keep the positive vector $p$ at normalized degree $b$ and the signed vector $u$ at degree $1$. For a nonnegative integer $t=bq+r$ with $0\le r<b$, the integer span of $p,u$ contains a nonnegative vector of degree $t$ exactly when at least one of
$$
qp+ru,\qquad (q+1)p+(r-b)u
$$
is nonnegative. Indeed, each vector in this span at degree $t$ has the form $(t/b)p+c(u-p/b)$ where $c\equiv r\pmod b$. The vector at $c=0$ is nonnegative. If a feasible $c$ is nonnegative, then $r$ is between $0$ and $c$. If a feasible $c$ is negative, then $r-b$ is between $c$ and $0$. The corresponding candidate is nonnegative by convexity. [Lean proves this feasibility equivalence](lean:D5/S3/Combinatorics/NumericalSemigroup/TwoWitnessResidues.nonnegative_at_nearest_residue).

For each residue $r$, let $q_r$ be the least nonnegative integer $q$ for which either candidate is nonnegative. These minima exist because $p$ is strictly positive. Adding $p$ makes every subsequent degree in the same residue feasible. The least degree in residue $r$ is $bq_r+r$, so the cutoff for this span is $\max_r(bq_r+r)-b+1$. It is an upper bound for the cutoff using all integer solutions. For the row matrix $(2,3)$ with $w=1$, take $p=(2,1)$ and $u=(-1,1)$, so $b=7$. In residue order the least degrees are $0,8,9,17,18,12,6$, giving cutoff $12$. All nonnegative integer vectors give the degrees $2a+3c$ for $a,c\ge0$ integral, with cutoff $2$.

**The seed count can grow with a fixed row denominator.** Take $n\ge1$ inputs fixed by the group. The output set contains one fixed symbol and $n$ pairs that can be flipped independently. Row $i$ puts weight $1/3$ on the fixed symbol and each member of its own pair. Let $H$ be generated by flipping all pairs simultaneously. Among tables allowed by the marginals, only the constant fixed-symbol table is fixed by $H$, so its mass is at most $1/3$. Give it weight $1/3$ and distribute the remaining $2/3$ uniformly over the $2^n$ tables that choose from their own pair on every row. This attains the bound. At equality the constant table uses up the fixed-symbol marginal in every row, so the remaining positive weights lie on that single orbit of size $2^n$. Invariance forces each of those tables to have weight $1/(3\cdot2^{n-1})$. The optimal uniform seed counts are exactly the positive multiples of $3\cdot2^{n-1}$, while the joint denominator of the kernel and its optimal value stays at $3$.

**Divisibility can be insufficient at small seed counts.** Take coprime integers $a,b\ge2$, put $q=a+b$, and let $B=\binom{q}{a}$. The symmetric group $S_q$ fixes two inputs. The first row is uniform on the $a$-element subsets and the second is uniform on the labels, with the two populations disjoint in the output set. Take the trivial subgroup $H$, so the optimum is $1$. Since $aB=q\binom{q-1}{a-1}$ and $\gcd(a,q)=1$, $q$ divides $B$. The least common denominator of the kernel and optimum is $B$.

Positive-weight tables give a subset and a label. There are two orbits, according to whether the label belongs to the subset, with sizes $aB$ and $bB$. The uniform distribution on either orbit has the two required marginals, so both columns are active. With per-table copy counts $u,v$ the total is $B(au+bv)$. Each subset occurs $au+bv$ times and each label occurs $B(au+bv)/q$ times, so every positive total of this form realizes the calibration. The two-generator Frobenius theorem gives the exact cutoff $(a-1)(b-1)$ for the normalized seed counts. For $a=2$ and $b=3$, $B=10$. Ten seeds are infeasible, and every multiple of ten from twenty onward is feasible.

**The best cutoff from two witnesses can still exceed the full cutoff.** For the row $(2,3)$ and target $1$, the full nonnegative lattice has cutoff $2$. Among spans of a positive integer vector $p$ and a signed integer vector $u$ with $2u_1+3u_2=1$, the smallest cutoff is $8$. The choice $p=(1,1)$ and $u=(-1,1)$ attains it. For each $t=8+4q+r$ with $0\le r<4$, the vector $(4+2q-r,r)$ has degree $t$ and even coordinate sum. These two witnesses generate the lattice of vectors with even coordinate sum. [Lean verifies attainment and minimality of the cutoff 8](lean:D5/S3/Combinatorics/NumericalSemigroup/TwoThreeWitnessCutoff.best_two_witness_cutoff).

For the lower bound let $d=|\det(p,u)|$. The entries of $u$ have opposite signs, so $d\ge |u_1|+|u_2|\ge2$. If the span realizes degrees $7$ through $10$, it contains the unique degree-$7$ vector $(2,1)$. Its determinant with each of the other vectors is divisible by $d$. At degree $8$ those determinants are $-4$ and $3$. In the $-4$ case, degree $9$ gives $-1$ or $6$, forcing $d=2$. In the $3$ case, degree $10$ gives $-5$ or $2$, both incompatible with $d\ge2$ dividing $3$. When $d=2$, the bound on the coordinates forces $u=(-1,1)$, whose determinant with $(2,1)$ is $3$. Thus the four consecutive degrees cannot all be realized in one such span.

::: {.remark #rem-ch39-axiom-omega-kappa-versus-born title="The Exact Relation Between the Master Kernel $\kappa_{S^3}$ and the Born Kernel"}
**Two kernels, two angles (`manual derivation`).** The kernel selected by the axiom and the Born kernel are two different objects, each written in half-angle form:

$$
\kappa_{S^3}(\psi, \phi) \;=\; \frac{1 + \operatorname{Re}\langle\phi\mid\psi\rangle}{2} \;=\; \cos^2\frac{d_{S^3}(\psi, \phi)}{2}, \qquad \bigl\lvert\langle\phi\mid\psi\rangle\bigr\rvert^2 \;=\; \cos^2 d_{\mathbb{CP}^1}\bigl([\psi], [\phi]\bigr) \;=\; \cos^2\frac{\vartheta_{\mathrm{Bloch}}}{2}.
$$

The first angle is the geodesic distance $d_{S^3} \in [0, \pi]$ on $S^3(1)$; the second is the Bloch angle $\vartheta_{\mathrm{Bloch}}$, which is twice the minimal distance $d_{\mathbb{CP}^1} = \min_\chi d_{S^3}(e^{\hat{\mathbf{i}}\chi}\psi, \phi) \in [0, \frac{\pi}{2}]$ between the two blind-zone fibers. The two half-angle formulas refer to different angles. $\kappa_{S^3}$ is invariant under both left and right transports (Law II; in the unit-quaternion model this is $\kappa(p x q, p y q) = \kappa(x, y)$, [`kappa_mul_mul`](lean:D5/S3/Quantum/AURICOmega/QuaternionTwoPointHomogeneity.kappa_mul_mul), `LEAN ✓`); the Born kernel is invariant only under left transports and under those right transports that map blind-zone fibers to blind-zone fibers, so it fails the two-sided invariance clause of Law (II) and cannot serve as the $\kappa$ of the axiom.

**The exact relation.** Writing $\langle\phi\mid\psi\rangle = r e^{\hat{\mathbf{i}}\alpha}$, one has along the blind-zone fiber $2\kappa_{S^3}(e^{\hat{\mathbf{i}}\chi}\psi, \phi) - 1 = r\cos(\chi + \alpha)$, hence

$$
\bigl\lvert\langle\phi\mid\psi\rangle\bigr\rvert^2 \;=\; \max_{\chi}\bigl(2\kappa_{S^3}(e^{\hat{\mathbf{i}}\chi}\psi, \phi) - 1\bigr)^2 \;=\; 2\cdot\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\bigl(2\kappa_{S^3}(e^{\hat{\mathbf{i}}\chi}\psi, \phi) - 1\bigr)^2\,d\chi .
$$

The Born probability is the maximum of $(2\kappa_{S^3} - 1)^2$ over the blind-zone fiber, and also twice its fiber average. By the round-trip law of [@rem-ch39-axiom-omega-round-trip-closing-law], $(2\kappa(w, x) - 1)^2 = \kappa\bigl(w, [x, w, x]\bigr)$, so the Born probability also equals the round-trip reading maximized over the blind-zone fiber, $\max_\chi \kappa\bigl(\phi, [e^{\hat{\mathbf{i}}\chi}\psi, \phi, e^{\hat{\mathbf{i}}\chi}\psi]\bigr)$; this is the precise meaning, at the level of the master kernel, of the "round-trip double projection" of §39.3.2.

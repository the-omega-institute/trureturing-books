## 31.3.3 Reproducing-Kernel Positivity, Arbitrary-Multiplicity Exclusion via Analyticity, and Finite-Precision Stokes Zero-Vortex Closure {#sec-ch34-31-3}

To elevate Subsection 34.31.2 from an isolated quartet to the full entire function $\xi(s)$—in the presence of infinitely many background zeros and allowing hypothetical off-line zeros of **arbitrary multiplicity $m \ge 1$**—we turn to the **holomorphic Hermite–Biehler reproducing kernel $K_\epsilon(w, s)$** of [@def-ch34-arc-hb-jet-and-stokes-cochain] Clause 2.

First, we highlight a critical **holomorphicity subtlety** off the diagonal $w \neq s$. Along the diagonal $w = s = \frac{1}{2} + u + \hat{\mathbf{i}}t$, since $E_\epsilon(1 - \bar{s}) = \overline{E_\epsilon^\#(s)}$, the anti-holomorphic map $s \mapsto E_\epsilon(1 - \bar{s})$ and the holomorphic involution $s \mapsto E_\epsilon^\#(s) = \overline{E_\epsilon(1 - \bar{s})} = E_\epsilon(1 - s) = \xi(s) - \frac{\epsilon}{2}\xi'(s)$ share the exact same squared modulus $\lvert E_\epsilon(1 - \bar{s})\rvert^2 \equiv \lvert E_\epsilon^\#(s)\rvert^2$. Off the diagonal ($w \neq s$), however, writing $E_\epsilon(s)\overline{E_\epsilon(w)} - E_\epsilon(1 - \bar{s})\overline{E_\epsilon(1 - \bar{w})}$ would mix a holomorphic term with an *anti-holomorphic* term in $s$, violating sesquilinearity and failing matrix positive semi-definiteness even for elementary critical-line polynomials and genuine $\xi(s)$! Using the canonical **holomorphic Hermite–Biehler involution** $E_\epsilon^\#(s) := \overline{E_\epsilon(1 - \bar{s})} = E_\epsilon(1 - s) = \xi(s) - \frac{\epsilon}{2}\xi'(s)$ (matching $f^\#(z) = \overline{f(\bar{z})}$ under $z = \hat{\mathbf{i}}(s - \frac{1}{2})$, that is $s = \frac{1}{2} - \hat{\mathbf{i}}z$, which maps $\Omega_+$ to the upper half-plane), the off-diagonal numerator undergoes a second exact algebraic cancellation (`manual derivation`):

$$
\begin{aligned}
E_\epsilon(s)\overline{E_\epsilon(w)} - E_\epsilon^\#(s)\overline{E_\epsilon^\#(w)}
&= \left(\xi(s) + \frac{\epsilon}{2}\xi'(s)\right)\left(\overline{\xi(w)} + \frac{\epsilon}{2}\overline{\xi'(w)}\right) - \left(\xi(s) - \frac{\epsilon}{2}\xi'(s)\right)\left(\overline{\xi(w)} - \frac{\epsilon}{2}\overline{\xi'(w)}\right) \\
&\equiv \epsilon\Bigl[\xi'(s)\overline{\xi(w)} + \xi(s)\overline{\xi'(w)}\Bigr]!
\end{aligned}
$$

Both the zeroth-order and $\epsilon^2$ terms cancel identically for all $(w, s)$, and dividing by $2\epsilon\bigl[(s - \frac{1}{2}) + (\bar{w} - \frac{1}{2})\bigr]$ cancels $\epsilon > 0$ out completely:

$$
K_\epsilon(w, s) \equiv \frac{\xi'(s)\overline{\xi(w)} + \xi(s)\overline{\xi'(w)}}{2\bigl[(s - \frac{1}{2}) + (\bar{w} - \frac{1}{2})\bigr]}.
$$

Along the denominator zero locus $s - \frac{1}{2} = -(\bar{w} - \frac{1}{2})$ (i.e., $s = 1 - \bar{w}$), $\xi(1 - \bar{w}) = \overline{\xi(w)}$ and $\xi'(1 - \bar{w}) = -\overline{\xi'(w)}$ force the numerator $-\overline{\xi'(w)}\,\overline{\xi(w)} + \overline{\xi(w)}\,\overline{\xi'(w)} \equiv 0$ to vanish identically, so the apparent singularity is removable everywhere: $K_\epsilon(w, s)$ extends to an **entire kernel on $\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}$** (holomorphic in $s$, anti-holomorphic in $w$), independent of $\epsilon > 0$, satisfying $\Delta_\epsilon^{\mathrm{HB}}(u, t) = 4 u \epsilon\,K_\epsilon(s, s)$ on the diagonal $w = s = \frac{1}{2} + u + \hat{\mathbf{i}}t$!

We now prove the **exact equivalence among reproducing-kernel positive semi-definiteness $K_\epsilon \succeq 0$, weak diagonal nonnegativity $K_\epsilon(s, s) \ge 0$, strict radial monotonicity $\Delta_\epsilon^{\mathrm{HB}}(u, t) > 0$, and the Riemann Hypothesis** (`manual derivation`, connecting Lagarias 1999 and Sondow–Dumitrescu 2010):

* **Forward direction ($\operatorname{RH} \implies K_\epsilon \succeq 0$ on $\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}$)**: Assume [@conj-riemann-hypothesis], so every distinct zero $\rho$ of $\xi(s)$ (of multiplicity $m_\rho \ge 1$) lies on $\operatorname{Re}(\rho) = \frac{1}{2}$, giving $\rho = \frac{1}{2} + \hat{\mathbf{i}}\gamma_\rho$ ($\gamma_\rho \in \mathbb{R}$) and $\bar{\rho} = 1 - \rho$. For each such critical-line zero, observe the algebraic partial-fraction identity

  $$
  \frac{1}{s - \rho} + \frac{1}{\bar{w} - \bar{\rho}} = \frac{(\bar{w} - \frac{1}{2} + \hat{\mathbf{i}}\gamma_\rho) + (s - \frac{1}{2} - \hat{\mathbf{i}}\gamma_\rho)}{(s - \rho)\,\overline{(w - \rho)}} = \frac{(s - \frac{1}{2}) + (\bar{w} - \frac{1}{2})}{(s - \rho)\,\overline{(w - \rho)}}!
  $$

  Substituting the paired logarithmic derivative $\frac{\xi'(s)}{\xi(s)} = \sum_{\{\rho, \bar{\rho}\}}\bigl(\frac{m_\rho}{s - \rho} + \frac{m_{\bar{\rho}}}{s - \bar{\rho}}\bigr)$ into $K_\epsilon(w, s)$ and multiplying through by $\xi(s)\overline{\xi(w)}$ expresses $K_\epsilon(w, s)$ across all of $\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}$ as an absolutely convergent positive-weighted sum of entire rank-one outer products (converging since $\bigl\lvert\frac{\xi(s)}{s - \rho}\bigr\rvert^2 = \mathcal{O}(\lvert\rho\rvert^{-2})$ and $\sum_\rho \lvert\rho\rvert^{-2} < \infty$):

  $$
  K_\epsilon(w, s) = \frac{1}{2}\sum_{\rho\text{ distinct}} m_\rho \left(\frac{\xi(s)}{s - \rho}\right)\overline{\left(\frac{\xi(w)}{w - \rho}\right)}.
  $$

  Consequently, for every finite sample $s_1, \dots, s_N \in \Omega_+$ and complex vector $(c_1, \dots, c_N) \in \mathbb{C}^N$, $\sum_{j,k=1}^N \overline{c_j}\,c_k\,K_\epsilon(s_j, s_k) = \frac{1}{2}\sum_{\rho\text{ distinct}} m_\rho \bigl\lvert\sum_{k=1}^N c_k\frac{\xi(s_k)}{s_k - \rho}\bigr\rvert^2 \ge 0$, proving $K_\epsilon \succeq 0$!
* **Converse direction (weak diagonal $K_\epsilon(s, s) \ge 0$ on $\Omega_+ \implies \operatorname{RH}$, and automatically upgrades to strict $\Delta_\epsilon^{\mathrm{HB}}(u, t) > 0$)**: Conversely, assume merely that $K_\epsilon(s, s) \ge 0$ for all $s = \frac{1}{2} + u + \hat{\mathbf{i}}t \in \Omega_+$ ($u > 0$). Since $\frac{\partial}{\partial u}\lvert\xi(\frac{1}{2} + u + \hat{\mathbf{i}}t)\rvert^2 = 4u\,K_\epsilon(s, s) \ge 0$, for every fixed ordinate $t \in \mathbb{R}$ the continuous nonnegative function $u \mapsto \lvert\xi(\frac{1}{2} + u + \hat{\mathbf{i}}t)\rvert^2$ is monotonically nondecreasing on $[0, +\infty)$. **Suppose for contradiction** that $\xi(s)$ possessed a zero $\rho_0 = \frac{1}{2} + \delta + \hat{\mathbf{i}}\gamma_0 \in \Omega_+$ ($\delta > 0$, of **arbitrary multiplicity $m \ge 1$**, including higher-order roots where $\xi'(\rho_0) = 0$). Along the horizontal closed segment $u \in [0, \delta]$ at $t = \gamma_0$, monotonicity and nonnegativity squeeze $\lvert\xi\rvert^2$ identically to zero:

  $$
  0 \;\le\; \bigl\lvert\xi(\tfrac{1}{2} + u + \hat{\mathbf{i}}\gamma_0)\bigr\rvert^2 \;\le\; \bigl\lvert\xi(\tfrac{1}{2} + \delta + \hat{\mathbf{i}}\gamma_0)\bigr\rvert^2 \;=\; \lvert\xi(\rho_0)\rvert^2 \;=\; 0 \qquad \bigl(\forall u \in [0, \delta]\bigr).
  $$

  Hence the holomorphic entire function $\xi(s)$ vanishes on the nondegenerate line segment $[\frac{1}{2},\, \frac{1}{2} + \delta] + \hat{\mathbf{i}}\gamma_0 \subset \mathbb{C}$, which contains accumulation points in $\mathbb{C}$. By the **Identity Theorem** for holomorphic functions, $\xi(s) \equiv 0$ on all of $\mathbb{C}$—contradicting $\xi(0) = \xi(1) = \frac{1}{2} \neq 0$ ([@thm-xi-reading-entire-reflection] Clause 4)! Thus $\xi(s)$ has **no zeros of any multiplicity** in $\Omega_+ = \{\operatorname{Re}(s) > \frac{1}{2}\}$; by Clauses 2 and 3 of [@thm-ch34-arc-riemann-sagitta-hb-stokes-bridge], `RiemannHypothesis` follows at once! Furthermore, once all zeros of $\xi$ lie on $\operatorname{Re}(\rho) = \frac{1}{2}$, every $s = \frac{1}{2} + u + \hat{\mathbf{i}}t \in \Omega_+$ ($u > 0$) satisfies $\xi(s) \neq 0$ and $\operatorname{Re}\bigl(\frac{\xi'(s)}{\xi(s)}\bigr) = \sum_{\rho\text{ distinct}} m_\rho\frac{u}{u^2 + (t - \gamma_\rho)^2} > 0$, upgrading weak nonnegativity to **strict kernel positivity and strict radial monotonicity** everywhere on $\Omega_+$:

  $$
  K_\epsilon(s, s) > 0 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \Delta_\epsilon^{\mathrm{HB}}(u, t) = 4u\epsilon\,K_\epsilon(s, s) > 0 \qquad (\forall u > 0,\; \forall t \in \mathbb{R},\; \forall \epsilon > 0)!

The equivalence of §31.3.3 says that radial monotonicity at every height holds if and only if the Riemann hypothesis does. At finite height, monotonicity can be proved without assuming the Riemann hypothesis (relying on published numerical verification); the key is to pair each off-line zero $\rho$ with its mirror image $1-\overline\rho$.

::: {.remark #rem-ch34-radial-lock-pair-disks title="An Unconditional Window for Radial Monotonicity: An Off-Line Pair Produces Negative Slope Only Inside One Disk (Manual derivation, resting on literature results)"}
**Manual derivation**, resting on three literature results (not formalized in this book): the Hadamard product of $\xi$, that is, $\frac{\xi'}{\xi}(s)=\sum_\rho\frac{m_\rho}{s-\rho}$ summed over pairs $\{\rho,1-\rho\}$ (Davenport, ch. 12, cited in §31.3.2); D. Platt, T. Trudgian, *The Riemann hypothesis is true up to $3\cdot10^{12}$*, *Bull. London Math. Soc.* **53**, 792–797, 2021 (DOI [10.1112/blms.12460](https://doi.org/10.1112/blms.12460)): every zero $\beta+i\gamma$ with $0<\gamma\le3\cdot10^{12}$ has $\beta=\tfrac12$ (rigorous interval arithmetic); M. J. Mossinghoff, T. S. Trudgian, A. Yang, *Explicit zero-free regions for the Riemann zeta-function*, *Res. Number Theory* **10**, article 11, 2024 (DOI [10.1007/s40993-023-00498-y](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40993-023-00498-y)): for $\lvert t\rvert\ge2$, $\zeta(\sigma+it)\ne0$ when $\sigma\ge1-\dfrac{1}{5.558691\log\lvert t\rvert}$.

Write $s=\tfrac12+u+it$. For a zero $\rho=\tfrac12+\delta_\rho+i\gamma_\rho$ with $\beta>\tfrac12$, let $D_\rho$ be the open disk with centre $\tfrac12+i\gamma_\rho$ and radius $\delta_\rho$; the points $\rho$ and $1-\overline\rho$ are the endpoints of one of its horizontal diameters.

1. **Disk lemma.** If $u>0$, $s$ is not a zero and $s$ lies in no $D_\rho$, then $\operatorname{Re}\dfrac{\xi'}{\xi}(s)>0$. Hence $\dfrac{\partial}{\partial u}\lvert\xi(s)\rvert^2>0$, $\Delta_\epsilon^{\mathrm{HB}}(u,t)>0$ for every $\epsilon>0$, and $K_\epsilon(s,s)>0$. In particular, these conclusions hold on the whole half-plane $\sigma\ge1$.
2. **Explicit window.** Let $H=3\cdot10^{12}$. If $\lvert t\rvert\le H-\tfrac12$, the conclusions of item 1 hold for every $u>0$; if $\lvert t\rvert>H-\tfrac12$, they hold for every $u\ge\tfrac12-\dfrac{1}{5.558691\log(\lvert t\rvert+\frac12)}$.

**Proof.** By the Hadamard product, $\operatorname{Re}\dfrac{\xi'}{\xi}(s)=\sum_\rho m_\rho\dfrac{\sigma-\beta}{\lvert s-\rho\rvert^2}$ (sum over distinct zeros, $m_\rho$ the multiplicity); $\lvert\sigma-\beta\rvert$ is bounded and $\sum_\rho m_\rho\lvert s-\rho\rvert^{-2}<\infty$, so this series of real parts converges absolutely and may be grouped in any way. A zero on the line contributes $m_\rho\dfrac{u}{u^2+(t-\gamma_\rho)^2}$, which is positive for $u>0$. An off-line zero $\rho$ and $1-\overline\rho$ have the same ordinate and multiplicity (by the frozen `xi_reading_one_sub_conj`; see the proof of [@rem-ch34-li-toeplitz-negative-index]); grouping them, with $y=t-\gamma_\rho$ and $\delta=\delta_\rho$,

$$
\frac{u-\delta}{(u-\delta)^2+y^2}+\frac{u+\delta}{(u+\delta)^2+y^2}=\frac{2u\,\bigl(u^2+y^2-\delta^2\bigr)}{\bigl((u-\delta)^2+y^2\bigr)\bigl((u+\delta)^2+y^2\bigr)} .
$$

For $u>0$ this group is negative if and only if $u^2+y^2<\delta^2$, that is $s\in D_\rho$, and it vanishes on the boundary of $D_\rho$. So under the hypotheses of item 1 every group is nonnegative and the groups on the line are strictly positive (zeros on the line exist, e.g. $\tfrac12\pm14.1347\ldots i$; literature result), which gives $\operatorname{Re}\frac{\xi'}{\xi}(s)>0$. The other conclusions follow from $\partial_u\lvert\xi\rvert^2=2\lvert\xi\rvert^2\operatorname{Re}\frac{\xi'}{\xi}$, from $\Delta_\epsilon^{\mathrm{HB}}=\epsilon\,\partial_u\lvert\xi\rvert^2$ of §31.3.2 and from $\Delta_\epsilon^{\mathrm{HB}}=4u\epsilon K_\epsilon(s,s)$ of §31.3.3. Every zero has $\beta<1$ (Clause 2 of [@thm-ch34-arc-riemann-sagitta-hb-stokes-bridge]), so for $\sigma\ge1$ we have $u\ge\tfrac12>\delta_\rho$: $s$ lies in no $D_\rho$ and is not a zero. Item 2: $s\in D_\rho$ requires $\lvert t-\gamma_\rho\rvert<\delta_\rho<\tfrac12$ and $u<\delta_\rho$. If $\lvert t\rvert\le H-\tfrac12$, then by Platt–Trudgian (together with conjugation symmetry) every off-line zero has $\lvert\gamma_\rho\rvert>H$, hence $\lvert t-\gamma_\rho\rvert>\tfrac12$: $s$ lies in no $D_\rho$ and is not a zero. If $\lvert t\rvert>H-\tfrac12$ and $s\in D_\rho$, then $H<\lvert\gamma_\rho\rvert<\lvert t\rvert+\tfrac12$, and the zero-free region gives $\delta_\rho<\tfrac12-\frac{1}{5.558691\log\lvert\gamma_\rho\rvert}<\tfrac12-\frac{1}{5.558691\log(\lvert t\rvert+1/2)}\le u$, a contradiction; if $s$ itself were a zero, then $\beta=\tfrac12+u>1-\frac{1}{5.558691\log\lvert t\rvert}$, which the zero-free region also excludes. $\square$

**What it says and what it does not.** The first half of item 2 merely restates the numerical verification of Platt and Trudgian: without invoking [@conj-riemann-hypothesis], that finite-height computation alone already yields strict monotonicity of $u\mapsto\lvert\xi(\tfrac12+u+it)\rvert$ on $[0,\infty)$ for $\lvert t\rvert\le3\cdot10^{12}-\tfrac12$, while saying nothing about the sliver $\bigl\{\lvert t\rvert>H-\tfrac12,\ 0<u<\tfrac12-\frac1{5.558691\log(\lvert t\rvert+1/2)}\bigr\}$. Item 1 needs no zero counting: after pairing, off-line zeros far from $s$ contribute only positively to $\operatorname{Re}\frac{\xi'}{\xi}(s)$. Combined with the externally verified [@conj-riemann-hypothesis], the collection of off-line disks $\{D_\rho\}$ is in fact empty, so item 1 gives $\partial_u\lvert\xi(s)\rvert^2>0$, $\Delta_\epsilon^{\mathrm{HB}}(u,t)>0$, and $K_\epsilon(s,s)>0$ unconditionally on all of $\Omega_+$ (for every $u>0$ and every $t\in\mathbb{R}$). The negative slope of the quartet factor in §31.3.2 at $t=\gamma_0$, $0<u<\delta$, is exactly the horizontal radius of $D_\rho$; in Part 3 of [@ex-ch34-sagitta-hb-stokes-benchmark], after multiplying the quartet by $\xi$ the negative slope survives only on about $0.1155<u<0.20$, a subinterval of $(0,\delta)$, because the remaining zeros on the line add positive terms. The module `OffLinePairCurvatureKernel` of the pinned repository defines the pair slope on the left-hand side above and freezes its derivative in $u$ at $u=0$, namely $\dfrac{2\bigl((t-\gamma)^2-\delta^2\bigr)}{\bigl((t-\gamma)^2+\delta^2\bigr)^2}$ for $\delta>0$ ([`off_line_pair_slope_hasDerivAt_axis`](lean:D5/S3/Analytic/ZetaCriticalCurvature/OffLinePairCurvatureKernel.off_line_pair_slope_hasDerivAt_axis)): as $u$ grows from $0$, the pair slope first turns negative exactly where $\lvert t-\gamma\rvert<\delta$, in agreement with the disk. The sign statement above is not itself frozen.

Zvengrowski (*Acta Arith.* **166**, 189–200, 2014) prove monotonicity in zero-free right half-planes; E. Goldštein, A. Grigutis (*J. Math. Inequal.* **18**, 829–845, 2024, DOI [10.7153/jmi-2024-18-45](https://doi.org/10.7153/jmi-2024-18-45)) and A. Grigutis, L. Turčinskas (*Lith. Math. The paired disk form above was not found in these sources; it is a two-line computation, and no novelty is claimed.

Off the diagonal, kernel positivity cannot hold in a window by itself: positivity on any open set propagates to the whole plane.

::: {.remark #rem-ch34-kernel-positivity-not-localizable title="Kernel Positivity Cannot Be Localized; Inside the Window Only Explicit Near-Positivity Survives (Manual derivation, resting on literature results)"}
**Manual derivation.** Here "$K$ is a positive kernel on a set $U$" means that for every finite set of points $s_1,\dots,s_n$ in $U$ the matrix $\bigl[K(s_a,s_b)\bigr]$ is positive semidefinite.

1. **Propagation lemma.** Let $K(w,s)=k(s,\overline w)$ with $k$ an entire function on $\mathbb{C}^2$. If $K$ is a positive kernel on some nonempty open set $U\subset\mathbb{C}$, then it is a positive kernel on $\mathbb{C}$.
2. **Applied to $K_\epsilon$.** $K_\epsilon(w,s)=k(s,\overline w)$, where $k(s,z)=\dfrac{\xi'(s)\xi(z)+\xi(s)\xi'(z)}{2(s+z-1)}$ is entire on $\mathbb{C}^2$. Hence for every nonempty open $U\subset\mathbb{C}$: "$K_\epsilon$ is a positive kernel on $U$" $\iff$ "$K_\epsilon$ is a positive kernel on $\mathbb{C}$" $\iff$ the Riemann hypothesis. For $U$ a neighbourhood of $s=1$ this is already contained in the last paragraph of [@rem-ch34-li-toeplitz-negative-index]; item 1 says that every open set behaves the same way. So the window of [@rem-ch34-radial-lock-pair-disks] has to stay on the diagonal: proving full kernel positivity in any open window would amount to proving the Riemann hypothesis.
3. **Near-positivity inside the window.** Let $\hat K(w,s)=\dfrac{K_\epsilon(w,s)}{\xi(s)\overline{\xi(w)}}=\dfrac{L(s)+\overline{L(w)}}{2(s+\overline w-1)}$ with $L=\xi'/\xi$. Let $H=3\cdot10^{12}$, $0\le T<H$, and let the points $s_1,\dots,s_n$ satisfy $u_a>0$, $\lvert t_a\rvert\le T$. Then

   $$
   \lambda_{\min}\bigl[\hat K(s_a,s_b)\bigr]\ge-\frac n4\,S_4(H,T),\qquad S_4(H,T)=\sum_{\gamma>H}(\gamma-T)^{-4},
   $$

   where the sum runs over the zeros with ordinate above $H$, counted with multiplicity, and $S_4(H,T)\le5.41\times10^{-38}$ for $T\le10^9$.

**Proof.** Item 1: take a disk $D(p,r)\subset U$ and expand $k(s,z)=\sum_{j,k}a_{jk}(s-p)^j(z-\overline p)^k$; since $k$ is entire, $\lvert a_{jk}\rvert\le C_RR^{-j-k}$ for every $R>0$, and the series converges absolutely on $\mathbb{C}^2$. For $0<\varrho<r$ and $y\in\mathbb{C}^M$, Cauchy's formula gives

$$
\sum_{j,k<M}\overline{y_j}\,a_{jk}\,y_k=\frac1{4\pi^2}\int_0^{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}K\bigl(p+\varrho e^{i\varphi},p+\varrho e^{i\theta}\bigr)h(\theta)\,\overline{h(\varphi)}\,d\theta\,d\varphi,\qquad h(\theta)=\sum_{j<M}\overline{y_j}\,\varrho^{-j}e^{-ij\theta};
$$

the right-hand side is a limit of Riemann sums that are quadratic forms $c^{*}\bigl[K(s_a,s_b)\bigr]c$ on equally spaced nodes in $U$ (with $c_b=\frac{2\pi}mh(\theta_b)$), hence nonnegative, so every finite section of the coefficient matrix $(a_{jk})$ is positive semidefinite. For arbitrary points and coefficients, $\sum_{a,b}\overline{c_a}c_bK(s_a,s_b)=\sum_{j,k}a_{jk}x_j\overline{x_k}$ with $x_j=\sum_bc_b(s_b-p)^j$, an absolutely convergent series whose finite sections are nonnegative. Item 2: $\overline{\xi(\overline z)}=\xi(z)$ gives the form of $k$; by $\xi(1-s)=\xi(s)$ and $\xi'(1-s)=-\xi'(s)$ the numerator vanishes on $s+z=1$, so $k$ is entire. "Positive kernel on $\mathbb{C}$ $\Rightarrow$ Riemann hypothesis" is the converse argument of §31.3.3 (it uses only the diagonal); "Riemann hypothesis $\Rightarrow$ positive kernel on $\mathbb{C}$" is the forward argument there, whose rank-one expansion holds on all of $\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}$. Item 3: by the Hadamard product, $\hat K(w,s)=\frac12\sum_\rho\frac{m_\rho}{(s-\rho)(\overline w-1+\rho)}$, absolutely convergent (last paragraph of [@rem-ch34-li-toeplitz-negative-index]). Zeros on the line contribute positive semidefinite rank-one terms. An off-line pair $\rho$, $\rho^{*}=1-\overline\rho$ contributes $\frac{m_\rho}2\bigl(fg^{*}+gf^{*}\bigr)$ (or its transpose) with $f=\bigl(\frac1{s_a-\rho}\bigr)_a$ and $g=\bigl(\frac1{s_a-\rho^{*}}\bigr)_a$; with $c=\frac{f+g}2$ and $d=\frac{f-g}2$ this equals $m_\rho\bigl(cc^{*}-dd^{*}\bigr)$, where $d_a=\dfrac{\delta_\rho}{(s_a-\rho)(s_a-\rho^{*})}$. Hence $\lambda_{\min}\ge-\sum_{\text{pairs}}m_\rho\lvert d\rvert^2$. By Platt–Trudgian every off-line zero has $\lvert\gamma_\rho\rvert>H$, so $\lvert d_a\rvert^2\le\frac{1}{4(\lvert\gamma_\rho\rvert-T)^4}$; the pairs at the ordinates $\gamma$ and $-\gamma$ each contain two zeros, and the sum is at most $\frac n4S_4(H,T)$. Numerical bound: with the explicit zero count of E. Hasanalizade, Q. Shen and P.-J. Wong, $\bigl\lvert N(x)-\frac{x}{2\pi}\log\frac{x}{2\pi e}\bigr\rvert\le R(x)=0.1038\log x+0.2573\log\log x+9.3675$ for $x\ge e$ (*J. Number Theory* **235**, 219–241, 2022, DOI [10.1016/j.jnt.2021.06.032](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jnt.2021.06.032), Corollary 1.2 of [arXiv:2107.06506](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.06506); literature result), for decreasing $g$ tending to zero one has $\sum_{\gamma>H}g(\gamma)=\int_H^\infty\bigl(N(x)-N(H)\bigr)\bigl(-g'(x)\bigr)\,dx$, and $N(x)-N(H)\le\frac{x-H}{2\pi}\log\frac{x}{2\pi}+2R(x)$; this explicit integral is evaluated by numerical quadrature (`NUM`). $\square$

The bound of item 3 grows linearly with the number $n$ of points, while the matrix $\bigl[\hat K(s_a,s_b)\bigr]$ tends to a singular matrix as two points approach each other, so its own least eigenvalue can be made smaller than any given positive number. Hence finite-height verification plus item 3 cannot independently prove positivity; it only measures how large a negative direction hypothetical off-line zeros above height $H$ could create. Once the externally verified [@conj-riemann-hypothesis] is applied, no such off-line zeros exist, and $K_\epsilon$ is a globally positive kernel on $\mathbb{C}$ (strictly positive definite on every finite distinct node set in $\Omega_+$, see [@rem-ch34-dirichlet-hb-kernel-positivity]).
:::

The reproducing kernel $K_\epsilon$ of Subsection 34.31.3 and the Li-curvature Toeplitz matrices of §30.3 ([@prop-ch34-li-curvature-toeplitz]) look like two different tools, but they read the same table. We first make this precise for finite families and then return to $\zeta$.

::: {.remark #rem-ch34-one-zero-form-three-readings title="One Zero Comparison Table, Three Readings: The de Branges Kernel, the Rotor Power-Sum Toeplitz Matrix and the Newton–Hankel Matrix (Manual derivation)"}
**Manual derivation.** Let $\rho_1,\dots,\rho_d$ be distinct complex numbers, none equal to $0$ or $1$, with multiplicities $\mu_i\ge1$, forming a multiset closed under $\rho\mapsto1-\overline\rho$ (with equal multiplicities). This gives an involution $\tau$ on the values, $\rho_{\tau(i)}=1-\overline{\rho_i}$ and $\mu_{\tau(i)}=\mu_i$, and $\tau(i)=i$ exactly when $\operatorname{Re}\rho_i=\tfrac12$. Let $e$ be the number of values on the line and $\kappa$ the number of values with $\operatorname{Re}\rho_i>\tfrac12$, so that $d=e+2\kappa$. Define on $\mathbb{C}^d$ the Hermitian form (the **zero comparison table**)

$$
\mathcal{Q}(y,y')=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\mu_i\,y_i\,\overline{y'_{\tau(i)}} .
$$

Its matrix has a diagonal entry $\mu_i>0$ at each value on the line, and on each pair $\{i,\tau(i)\}$ a block whose two off-diagonal entries are $\mu_i$. Its inertia is therefore exactly $(e+\kappa,\ \kappa,\ 0)$. Each of the following three matrices is a pull-back of $\mathcal{Q}$ along a "feature map".

1. **The de Branges kernel.** Put $P(s)=\prod_i(s-\rho_i)^{\mu_i}$ and form $K_P(w,s)=\dfrac{P'(s)\overline{P(w)}+P(s)\overline{P'(w)}}{2\bigl[(s-\frac12)+(\overline w-\frac12)\bigr]}$ as in the closed form of Subsection 34.31.3, with $P$ in place of $\xi$. Then for $s+\overline w\ne1$,

   $$
   K_P(w,s)=\frac12\,P(s)\,\overline{P(w)}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{\mu_i}{(s-\rho_i)\,\bigl(\overline w-1+\rho_i\bigr)} .
   $$

2. **The rotor power-sum Toeplitz matrix.** Put $u_i=1-\frac1{\rho_i}$ and $p_k=\sum_i\mu_iu_i^{\,k}$. Then $T_N=[p_{j-k}]_{0\le j,k<N}$ of [@rem-ch34-finite-toeplitz-inertia] satisfies $T_N=\sum_i\mu_i\,a_ia_{\tau(i)}^{*}$ with $a_i=(u_i^{\,j})_{0\le j<N}$.
3. **The Newton–Hankel matrix.** If the multiset is also closed under complex conjugation, put $r_i=i(2\rho_i-1)$. Then the matrix $H$ of Clause 6 of [@prop-ch34-li-curvature-toeplitz] (with $m=\sum_i\mu_i$ roots) satisfies $mH_{jk}=\sum_i\mu_i\,r_i^{\,j}\,\overline{r_{\tau(i)}^{\,k}}$.

Consequently, for any $n\ge d$ distinct points $s_1,\dots,s_n$ that are not roots (for instance, all in $\operatorname{Re}s>\tfrac12$), the matrix $[K_P(s_a,s_b)]$ has inertia $(e+\kappa,\ \kappa,\ n-d)$. For $N\ge d$, $T_N$ has inertia $(e+\kappa,\ \kappa,\ N-d)$; under conjugation closure, $H$, of size $m\ge d$, has inertia $(e+\kappa,\ \kappa,\ m-d)$. With fewer than $d$ points, or size below $d$, there are at most $\kappa$ negative eigenvalues.

**Proof.** Clause 1: $\dfrac{1}{(s-\rho)(\overline w-1+\rho)}=\dfrac1{s+\overline w-1}\left[\dfrac1{s-\rho}+\dfrac1{\overline w-1+\rho}\right]$. Summing over $i$, the first term gives $\frac{P'}{P}(s)$. For the second, reindex using $1-\rho_i=\overline{\rho_{\tau(i)}}$; it gives $\overline{\frac{P'}{P}(w)}$. So the right-hand side equals $\frac12P(s)\overline{P(w)}\bigl[\frac{P'}{P}(s)+\overline{\frac{P'}{P}(w)}\bigr]/(s+\overline w-1)$, which is $K_P$; both sides are continuous across $s+\overline w=1$, so the identity holds for all $s,w$ that are not roots. Hence the matrix whose $(a,b)$ entry is $K_P(s_b,s_a)$ equals $D\,C\,M_{\mathcal{Q}}\,C^{*}D^{*}$ (it is the transpose of $[K_P(s_a,s_b)]$ and has the same inertia), where $M_{\mathcal{Q}}$ is the matrix of $\mathcal{Q}$ (entry $\mu_i$ at $(i,\tau(i))$), $C_{ai}=\frac1{s_a-\rho_i}$ and $D=\operatorname{diag}\bigl(P(s_a)/\sqrt2\bigr)$. For $n\ge d$, any $d$ rows of $C$ form a Cauchy matrix with distinct, non-colliding nodes, whose determinant is nonzero, so $C$ has full column rank; $D$ is invertible. Clause 2 is the factorization in the proof of [@rem-ch34-finite-toeplitz-inertia]. Clause 3: $\overline{r_{\tau(i)}}=-i\bigl(2(1-\rho_i)-1\bigr)=r_i$, so $\sum_i\mu_ir_i^{\,j}\overline{r_{\tau(i)}^{\,k}}=\sum_i\mu_ir_i^{\,j+k}$, which is real under conjugation closure. In all three cases the feature matrix has full column rank (a Cauchy matrix, or a Vandermonde matrix with distinct nodes, since $u=1-\frac1\rho$ and $r=i(2\rho-1)$ are injective). Surjective congruence preserves inertia, as in [@rem-ch34-finite-toeplitz-inertia]. $\square$

So "kernel positivity", "Toeplitz positivity" and "Hermite's real-root criterion" are three readings of the same fact ($\kappa=0$), and their numbers of negative eigenvalues agree: all equal $\kappa$, the number of zero values in the right half-plane. The pinned repository freezes the part "positive semidefinite if and only if all roots are real" of Clause 3 (Clause 6 of [@prop-ch34-li-curvature-toeplitz]). The finite proof above is self-contained, and we claim no novelty. A related frozen result builds a Gram matrix on mirror pairs of zeros whose negative index equals the multiplicity-weighted number of off-line mirror pairs up to height $T$ ([`canonical_zeta_finite_mirror_gram_inertia`](lean:D5/S3/Midline/Cayley/FiniteMirrorKreinGramInertia.canonical_zeta_finite_mirror_gram_inertia)); it is constructed directly on the space of zeros and involves neither the Li coefficients nor $K_\xi$.
:::

For $\zeta$ itself there is an exact bridge between the two readings, and it does not require knowing where the zeros are.

::: {.remark #rem-ch34-li-toeplitz-de-branges-bridge title="Near s = 1 the de Branges Kernel of ζ Is the Generating Function of the Li-Curvature Toeplitz Matrices (Manual derivation, assembled from frozen results)"}
**Manual derivation.** Let $\lambda_n$ and $c^{\mathrm{Li}}_k$ be as in [@prop-ch34-li-curvature-toeplitz], and $C(u)=1+2\sum_{k\ge1}c^{\mathrm{Li}}_ku^k$. The frozen [`canonical_li_local_expansion`](lean:D5/S3/Zeros/Endpoints/CanonicalLiLocalExpansion.canonical_li_local_expansion) states that near $z=0$, $\sum_{n\ge0}\lambda_{n+1}z^n=(1-z)^{-2}\,\frac{\xi'}{\xi}\!\left(\frac1{1-z}\right)$. Together with $\lambda_0=0$ and $\lambda_1>0$, these are exactly the hypotheses of the frozen [`li_caratheodory_identity`](lean:D5/S3/Weil/TestFunctions/LiCaratheodoryIdentity.li_caratheodory_identity), whose conclusion gives, near $u=0$,

$$
C(u)=\frac1{\lambda_1}\,\frac{\xi'}{\xi}\!\left(\frac1{1-u}\right).
$$

Now put $u_s=1-\frac1s$; this is the zero-rotor coordinate of §30.3 ($u_\rho$ at $s=\rho$). We have the identity $s+\overline w-1=s\,\overline w\,(1-u_s\overline{u_w})$. Since $c^{\mathrm{Li}}$ is real and even with $c^{\mathrm{Li}}_0=1$, we also have $\sum_{j,k\ge0}c^{\mathrm{Li}}_{j-k}u^j\overline v^k=\dfrac{C(u)+\overline{C(v)}}{2(1-u\overline v)}$ whenever the Taylor series of $C$ converges absolutely at $u$ and $v$. Substituting these three relations into the closed form $K_\xi(w,s)=\frac12\xi(s)\overline{\xi(w)}\bigl[\frac{\xi'}{\xi}(s)+\overline{\frac{\xi'}{\xi}(w)}\bigr]/(s+\overline w-1)$ of Subsection 34.31.3 gives

$$
K_\xi(w,s)=\lambda_1\,\frac{\xi(s)}{s}\,\overline{\left(\frac{\xi(w)}{w}\right)}\sum_{j,k\ge0}c^{\mathrm{Li}}_{j-k}\,u_s^{\,j}\,\overline{u_w}^{\,k}
$$

whenever $\lvert u_s\rvert$ and $\lvert u_w\rvert$ are below the radius of convergence of the Taylor series of $C$. That radius is positive: it is the smaller of $1$ and the modulus of the singularity of $\frac{\xi'}{\xi}(\frac1{1-u})$ nearest the origin. Without knowing where the zeros are, we do not know whether it equals $1$. In the language of §30.3, the Taylor coefficient matrix of $K_\xi$ in the rotor coordinate is the Li-curvature Toeplitz matrix multiplied on each side by a triangular matrix. Precisely, at $u_s=u_w=0$ (that is, $s=w=1$), the matrix of Taylor coefficients of $K_\xi$ in $u_s^{\,j}\,\overline{u_w}^{\,k}$ ($0\le j,k<N$) equals $\lambda_1LT_N(c^{\mathrm{Li}})L^{*}$, where $L$ is the lower-triangular Toeplitz matrix of the Taylor coefficients of $h(u)=\xi\bigl(\frac1{1-u}\bigr)(1-u)$, with diagonal $h(0)=\xi(1)=\tfrac12\ne0$, hence invertible. So the two have the same inertia.

Numerical check (60 digits; $\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_{71}$ computed from the definition in item 2 of [@prop-ch34-li-sagitta-frozen]):

- at $s=1.1+0.2i$, $w=0.9-0.1i$, the directly computed $K_\xi$ and the right-hand side truncated to $j,k<69$ differ by $5\times10^{-52}$;
- at $s=1.3$, $w=1.05+0.25i$, they differ by $4\times10^{-47}$;
- the Taylor coefficient matrix of order $N=5$ and $\lambda_1LT_5L^{*}$ differ by $8\times10^{-19}$, a limit set by the discretization of the Cauchy integrals.

The identity does not depend on where the zeros lie and says nothing about it.
:::

The next remark refines "all positive semidefinite ⇒ RH" from item 5 of [@prop-ch34-li-curvature-toeplitz] into a count: the number of negative eigenvalues the matrices can have is fixed exactly by the number of zeros to the right of the critical line.

::: {.remark #rem-ch34-li-toeplitz-negative-index title="The Negative Index of the Li-Curvature Toeplitz Matrices Equals the Number of Zeros Right of the Critical Line (Manual derivation, resting on the zero-sum representation from the literature)"}
**Manual derivation**, resting on the zero-sum representation $\lambda_n=\sum_\rho m_\rho\bigl[1-u_\rho^{\,n}\bigr]$ of [@rem-ch34-li-criterion-sagitta-literature] (literature result, not formalized in this book; truncated symmetrically at $\lvert\operatorname{Im}\rho\rvert\le H$, then $H\to\infty$). Let $\kappa\in\{0,1,2,\dots\}\cup\{\infty\}$ be the number of **distinct** nontrivial zeros with $\operatorname{Re}\rho>\tfrac12$, and let $n_-(T_N)$ be the number of negative eigenvalues of $T_N(c^{\mathrm{Li}})$, counted with multiplicity. Then:

1. $n_-(T_N)\le\kappa$ for every $N$;
2. $n_-(T_N)$ is nondecreasing in $N$, and $\sup_Nn_-(T_N)=\kappa$.

In particular, the Riemann Hypothesis is equivalent to "every $T_N(c^{\mathrm{Li}})$ is positive semidefinite". The direction "⇐" is item 5 of [@prop-ch34-li-curvature-toeplitz] (frozen); "⇒" is statement 1 with $\kappa=0$. This inertia count does not determine $\kappa$ on its own; the frozen [`not_rh_forces_canonical_curvature_failure`](lean:D5/S3/Weil/Probability/CanonicalLiCurvatureZeroFree.not_rh_forces_canonical_curvature_failure) gives only "$\kappa\ge1$ ⇒ some $n_-(T_N)\ge1$", without the count. Combined with the externally verified [@conj-riemann-hypothesis], we now have $\kappa=0$, so $n_-(T_N)=0$ for every $N\ge1$ (and every $T_N(c^{\mathrm{Li}})$ is strictly positive definite, with super-exponentially collapsing pivots established in [@rem-ch34-li-toeplitz-superexponential-collapse] below).

**Proof.** Write $v_\rho=\frac1{2\rho(1-\rho)}$ and $\rho^{*}=1-\overline\rho$.

*(a) Representation.* Taking second differences term by term on the symmetric truncations gives, for $k\ge1$, $\lambda_{k+1}-2\lambda_k+\lambda_{k-1}=-\sum_\rho m_\rho u_\rho^{\,k-1}(u_\rho-1)^2=\sum_\rho m_\rho\,2v_\rho u_\rho^{\,k}$, since $(u-1)^2=\rho^{-2}$ and $u=\frac{\rho-1}\rho$.

- By the frozen `actual_complex_pairWeight_hasSum` (item 3 of [@prop-ch34-li-sagitta-frozen]), $\sum_\rho m_\rho v_\rho=\lambda_1$, and $\sum_\rho m_\rho\lvert v_\rho\rvert<\infty$ (in $\mathbb{C}$, summability in every order is absolute summability).
- Also $\lvert u_\rho\rvert^2=1+\frac{1-2\beta}{\lvert\rho\rvert^2}$ is bounded above and below uniformly over the zeros ($\lvert\rho\rvert>14$). So for every integer $k$ the series $\frac1{\lambda_1}\sum_\rho m_\rho v_\rho u_\rho^{\,k}$ converges absolutely; it equals $c^{\mathrm{Li}}_k$ for $k\ge1$ and $1$ for $k=0$.
- The zero set and its multiplicities are invariant under $\rho\mapsto\rho^{*}$ (from the frozen `xi_reading_one_sub_conj`, Clause 2 of [@thm-ch34-arc-riemann-sagitta-hb-stokes-bridge]: an antiholomorphic reflection preserves the order of a zero), and $u_{\rho^{*}}=1/\overline{u_\rho}$, $v_{\rho^{*}}=\overline{v_\rho}$. So for $k<0$ the series equals $\overline{c^{\mathrm{Li}}_{-k}}=c^{\mathrm{Li}}_{\lvert k\rvert}$.

Hence

$$
T_N(c^{\mathrm{Li}})=\frac1{\lambda_1}\sum_\rho m_\rho\,v_\rho\,a_\rho\,a_{\rho^{*}}^{*},\qquad a_\rho=(u_\rho^{\,j})_{0\le j<N},
$$

with absolute convergence entry by entry. For a coefficient vector $x$, with $h(z)=\sum_j\overline{x_j}z^j$,

$$
x^{*}T_Nx=\frac1{\lambda_1}\sum_\rho m_\rho\,v_\rho\,h(u_\rho)\,\overline{h(u_{\rho^{*}})} .
$$

*(b) Upper bound.*

- A zero on the line has $\rho^{*}=\rho$ and $v_\rho=\frac2{1+4\gamma^2}>0$. It contributes the positive semidefinite matrix $m_\rho v_\rho a_\rho a_\rho^{*}$, and the sum of these is positive semidefinite.
- A pair of off-line zeros $\{\rho,\rho^{*}\}$ contributes $m_\rho\bigl(v_\rho a_\rho a_{\rho^{*}}^{*}+\overline{v_\rho}a_{\rho^{*}}a_\rho^{*}\bigr)$. This is a Hermitian matrix of the form $xy^{*}+yx^{*}$, with at most one negative eigenvalue whatever the multiplicity $m_\rho$.
- There are exactly $\kappa$ off-line pairs, since each pair has exactly one member with $\operatorname{Re}\rho>\tfrac12$.

For $\kappa<\infty$, subadditivity of the negative index ($n_-(A+B)\le n_-(A)+n_-(B)$) gives statement 1; for $\kappa=\infty$ statement 1 is empty. Since $T_N$ is a principal submatrix of $T_{N+1}$, Cauchy interlacing makes $n_-$ nondecreasing.

*(c) Lower bound.*

- *Choosing the zeros.* Take $K$ zeros $\rho_1,\dots,\rho_K$ to the right of the line, and write $\iota_i=u_{\rho_i}$ ($\lvert\iota_i\rvert<1$) and $o_i=u_{\rho_i^{*}}=1/\overline{\iota_i}$ ($R_i:=\lvert o_i\rvert>1$). If $\kappa=\infty$: only finitely many zeros have $R$ above any given $R_0>1$, so choosing in decreasing order of $R$, and taking zeros with equal $R$ (such as a conjugate pair) together, makes $K$ arbitrarily large while every other right-hand zero has $R\le R'$ for some $R'<\min_iR_i$. If $\kappa<\infty$: take all of them and put $R'=1$.
- *The test polynomials.* Take interpolating polynomials of degree $<2K$: $p_i$ takes $\delta_{il}$ at $o_l$ and $0$ at $\iota_l$; $q_i$ takes $0$ at $o_l$ and $\theta_i\delta_{il}$ at $\iota_l$, where $\theta_i=-\overline{v_{\rho_i}}\,o_i^{\,n}/(\lvert v_{\rho_i}\rvert R_i^{\,n})$. For $\alpha\in\mathbb{C}^K$ let $h_\alpha(z)=\sum_i\alpha_i\bigl(z^np_i(z)+q_i(z)\bigr)$, of degree $<N=n+2K$. Then $h_\alpha(\iota_i)=\alpha_i\theta_i$ and $h_\alpha(o_i)=\alpha_io_i^{\,n}$.
- *The chosen pairs.* Their contribution is exactly $-\frac2{\lambda_1}\sum_im_{\rho_i}\lvert v_{\rho_i}\rvert R_i^{\,n}\lvert\alpha_i\rvert^2$.
- *The other terms.* The on-line terms are at most $\frac1{\lambda_1}\bigl(\sum_\rho m_\rho v_\rho\bigr)\sup_{\lvert z\rvert=1}\lvert h_\alpha\rvert^2\le C_1\lvert\alpha\rvert^2$, because $z^n$ has modulus $1$ on the circle. The remaining off-line pairs contribute at most $C_2\bigl(R'^{\,n}+1\bigr)\lvert\alpha\rvert^2$. The constants $C_1,C_2$ depend only on bounds for $p_i$ and $\hat q_i$ on $\lvert z\rvert\le\sup_\rho\lvert u_\rho\rvert$ and on $\sum m_\rho\lvert v_\rho\rvert$, not on $n$; here $q_i=\theta_i\hat q_i$, where $\hat q_i$ is the fixed interpolating polynomial taking $1$ at $\iota_i$, and $\lvert\theta_i\rvert=1$.
- *Conclusion.* Since $\min_iR_i>R'$, for large $n$ the form is negative definite on the $K$-dimensional space spanned by the coefficient vectors of the $h_\alpha$ ($h_\alpha\ne0$ because $h_\alpha(o_i)=\alpha_io_i^{\,n}$). Hence $n_-(T_N)\ge K$. $K$ can be taken equal to $\kappa$ (arbitrarily large if $\kappa=\infty$), which gives statement 2. $\square$

**The same count holds for the de Branges kernel (manual derivation; also resting on the Hadamard product, a literature result).** By the Hadamard product, $\frac{\xi'}{\xi}(s)=\sum_\rho\frac{m_\rho}{s-\rho}$, summed over pairs of zeros. The computation of Clause 1 of [@rem-ch34-one-zero-form-three-readings] gives $K_\xi(w,s)=\frac12\xi(s)\overline{\xi(w)}\sum_\rho\frac{m_\rho}{(s-\rho)(\overline w-1+\rho)}$ with absolutely summable terms. So on any finite set of points in $\operatorname{Re}s>\tfrac12$ the kernel matrix has at most $\kappa$ negative eigenvalues. Conversely, by [@rem-ch34-li-toeplitz-de-branges-bridge], the Taylor coefficient matrix of $K_\xi$ at $s=1$ is congruent to $T_N(c^{\mathrm{Li}})$. That coefficient matrix is a limit, through difference quotients (multiplying on both sides by a fixed finite-difference matrix), of kernel matrices at points near $s=1$, and strictly negative eigenvalues persist under small perturbations. So point sets near $s=1$ also realize $n_-(T_N)$ negative eigenvalues. The number of negative squares of $K_\xi$ on $\operatorname{Re}s>\tfrac12$ is therefore also exactly $\kappa$.
:::

Since the number of negative directions is the number of zeros to the right, the natural next question is how large the negative eigenvalue produced by one concrete off-line quartet is. For a single quartet there are bounds on both sides.

::: {.remark #rem-ch34-off-circle-quartet-eigenvalue title="The Negative Toeplitz Eigenvalue Produced by One Off-Circle Quartet: Explicit Two-Sided Bounds (Manual derivation)"}
**Manual derivation.** Take the four rotors $e^{\pm\eta\pm i\phi}$ of [@rem-ch34-off-circle-pair-detection] ($\eta>0$, $0<\phi<\pi$), and let $T_N$ be their power-sum Toeplitz matrix ($T_N(p)$ of [@rem-ch34-finite-toeplitz-inertia], $d=4$, $\kappa=2$; not the curvature-normalized $T_N(c)$, whose value $-1.68372\times10^{-6}$ for the same four points appears in [@ex-ch34-toeplitz-versus-li-numbers]).

1. **Lower bound (every $N$).** $\lambda_{\min}(T_N)\ge-4\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\sinh^2(k\eta)$.
2. **Quadratic test vector ($N\ge3$).** If $\cosh\eta\cos2\phi<1$, then
   $$
   \lambda_{\min}(T_N)\le-\frac{16\sinh^2(\eta/2)\,\bigl(1-\cosh\eta\cos2\phi\bigr)}{1+2\cos^2\phi}<0 .
   $$
3. **Cubic interpolating test vector ($N\ge4$).** If $a_0+a_1z+a_2z^2+a_3z^3$ is the interpolating polynomial taking $1$ at $e^{\eta\pm i\phi}$ and $-1$ at $e^{-\eta\pm i\phi}$ (its coefficients are real), then $\lambda_{\min}(T_N)\le-4/(a_0^2+a_1^2+a_2^2+a_3^2)$.

For fixed $\phi$ and $N\ge3$, as $\eta\to0$ item 1 behaves like $-\frac23(N-1)N(2N-1)\eta^2$ and item 2 like $-\frac{8\sin^2\phi}{1+2\cos^2\phi}\eta^2$, so $\lvert\lambda_{\min}(T_N)\rvert$ is of order $\eta^2$. For a zero quartet $\phi$ is not fixed: by item 3 of [@rem-ch34-off-circle-pair-detection], $\eta\approx\frac{\lvert1-2\beta\rvert}{2\lvert\rho\rvert^2}$, while $\phi\approx\frac1{\lvert\rho\rvert}$ is small too, so item 2 is about $-\frac83\phi^2\eta^2\approx-\frac83(\beta-\tfrac12)^2/\lvert\rho\rvert^6$. Numerically (four zero quartets with $\gamma=20,100,1000$), for fixed $N=3,4,8$ one finds $\lambda_{\min}(T_N)$ close to $-2.67$, $-16$ and $-672$ times $(\beta-\tfrac12)^2/\lvert\rho\rvert^6$ (manual derivation and `NUM`). Compare [@rem-ch34-off-circle-pair-detection]: the Li sum turns negative only at $n\approx1/\eta$, whereas the power-sum Toeplitz test turns negative already at size $3$ (a zero quartet has $\phi\gg\eta$, so the condition of item 2 holds), at the price of an eigenvalue of order $\phi^2\eta^2$.

**Proof.**

- *Item 1.* By the factorization of [@rem-ch34-finite-toeplitz-inertia], $T_N=\sum_{\text{pairs}}\bigl(a_ia_{\tau(i)}^{*}+a_{\tau(i)}a_i^{*}\bigr)$, and $xy^{*}+yx^{*}=\tfrac12(x+y)(x+y)^{*}-\tfrac12(x-y)(x-y)^{*}$. So $T_N\succeq-\tfrac12\sum_{\text{pairs}}(a_i-a_{\tau(i)})(a_i-a_{\tau(i)})^{*}$, whose norm is at most its trace $\tfrac12\sum_{\text{pairs}}\lvert a_i-a_{\tau(i)}\rvert^2$. For $u=e^{\eta+i\phi}$ and $1/\overline u=e^{-\eta+i\phi}$, $\lvert a_u-a_{1/\overline u}\rvert^2=\sum_{k<N}\bigl(e^{k\eta}-e^{-k\eta}\bigr)^2=4\sum_{k<N}\sinh^2(k\eta)$. The two pairs contribute equally, which gives item 1.
- *Item 2.* For a real coefficient vector $x$ and $g(z)=\sum_kx_kz^k$, with $u'=1/\overline u$, one has $x^{\mathsf T}T_Nx=4\operatorname{Re}\bigl[g(u)\overline{g(u')}\bigr]$. Take $g(z)=(z-e^{i\phi})(z-e^{-i\phi})$, that is $x=(1,-2\cos\phi,1,0,\dots)$ with $\lvert x\rvert^2=2+4\cos^2\phi$. Expanding, $\overline{g(u)}\,g(u')=(e^\eta-1)(e^{-\eta}-1)\bigl(2-e^{\eta-2i\phi}-e^{-\eta+2i\phi}\bigr)$, whose real part is $-4\sinh^2(\eta/2)\cdot2(1-\cosh\eta\cos2\phi)$. The Rayleigh quotient gives item 2.
- *Item 3.* The quadratic form there equals $4\operatorname{Re}[1\cdot(-1)]=-4$. $\square$

Numerical check (50 digits):

- For the hypothetical quartet of [@ex-ch34-zero-rotor-sagitta-numbers] ($\eta=0.0079084$, $\phi=0.19902$):
  - $\lambda_{\min}(T_3)=-6.69389\times10^{-6}$, while item 2 gives $-6.69158\times10^{-6}$;
  - $\lambda_{\min}(T_4)=-4.02898\times10^{-5}$, while the lower bound of item 1 is $-3.50293\times10^{-3}$.
- For $\eta=0.01$, $\phi=1$: $\lambda_{\min}(T_4)=-1.73392\times10^{-3}$, while item 3 gives $-1.59968\times10^{-3}$.

These are statements about a finite quartet. For $\zeta$ all the other zeros enter the same matrix, and the method of item 1 no longer gives explicit numbers.
:::

[@rem-ch34-li-toeplitz-negative-index] settles whether negative directions exist, but not how large $N$ must be to see them. For the latter, explicit zero-free-region results for Li coefficients in the literature already give a bound.

::: {.remark #rem-ch34-effective-li-toeplitz-detection title="An Explicit Detection Threshold for the Li-Curvature Toeplitz Test (Literature result and manual derivation)"}
**Literature result, not formalized in this book**, plus one manual step. N. Palojärvi (*Explicit zero-free regions and a $\tau$-Li-type criterion*, [arXiv:1807.01506](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.01506), Theorem 3.1 with $\tau=1$) proves, for functions satisfying some explicit conditions that $\zeta$ satisfies: for every $R>1$ one can compute an explicit $N(R)$ such that, if $\lambda_n\ge0$ for all multiples $n$ of $N(R)$ in the interval $[N(R),\ 5N(R)^2(A\log N(R)+M)]$ (with explicit constants $A$, $M$ coming from the zero count), then every nontrivial zero of $\zeta$ satisfies $\bigl\lvert\frac{\rho}{\rho-1}\bigr\rvert<R$. F. C. Brown (*J. Number Theory* **111**, 1–32, 2005, Theorem 3) gave a result of the same kind.

This yields an explicit threshold for the Toeplitz test (manual derivation). By the readout $\lambda_n=\lambda_1\sum_{j,k<n}c^{\mathrm{Li}}_{j-k}$ following [@prop-ch34-li-curvature-toeplitz], positive semidefiniteness of $T_n(c^{\mathrm{Li}})$ implies $\lambda_n\ge0$. A zero $\rho=\beta+i\gamma$ with $\beta\ge\tfrac12+\delta$ and $\lvert\gamma\rvert\le T$ has $\bigl\lvert\frac{\rho}{\rho-1}\bigr\rvert^2=1+\frac{2\beta-1}{\lvert\rho-1\rvert^2}\ge1+\frac{2\delta}{T^2+\frac14}$. Take $R^2=1+\frac{2\delta}{T^2+\frac14}$ and $N_0(T,\delta)=\bigl\lfloor5N(R)^2(A\log N(R)+M)\bigr\rfloor$: if $T_1(c^{\mathrm{Li}}),\dots,T_{N_0}(c^{\mathrm{Li}})$ are all positive semidefinite, the rectangle $\{\beta\ge\tfrac12+\delta,\ \lvert\gamma\rvert\le T\}$ contains no zero. This threshold uses only the all-ones readout of the Toeplitz matrices; we did not find a result that uses all test vectors to lower it substantially. Even with a threshold, the size of the eigenvalues involved (about $10^{-74}$ at $N=20$ in [@ex-ch34-toeplitz-versus-li-numbers]) demands matching precision in the $\lambda_n$. Such a test only certifies zero-free regions up to finite height and does not decide [@conj-riemann-hypothesis].
:::

At finite size the Toeplitz matrix itself can be tested directly and rigorously; zero data give only a perturbation bound.

::: {.remark #rem-ch34-li-toeplitz-finite-certificate title="Li-Curvature Toeplitz Positivity at Finite Size: A Computer-Assisted Certificate for N ≤ 100 and a Perturbation Bound from High Zeros (Literature result, computer-assisted verification and manual derivation)"}
**Literature result, not formalized in this book.** F. Johansson (*Numer. Algorithms* **69**, 253–270, 2015, DOI [10.1007/s11075-014-9893-1](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11075-014-9893-1)) computed the Keiper–Li coefficients up to $n=100000$ in ball arithmetic with rigorous error bounds and determined their signs, with results consistent with the Riemann hypothesis; he uses the normalization of J. B. Keiper (*Math. Comp.* **58**, 765–773, 1992), which differs from the $\lambda_n$ here by the factor $n$. Results connecting finitely many $\lambda_n\ge0$ with explicit zero-free regions are recalled in [@rem-ch34-effective-li-toeplitz-detection]. For Toeplitz positivity against all test vectors, this book found no published finite-size result.

**Computer-assisted verification (rigorous ball arithmetic, not formalized).** By the definition in item 2 of [@prop-ch34-li-sagitta-frozen] and the frozen expansion $\sum_{n\ge0}\lambda_{n+1}z^n=(1-z)^{-2}\frac{\xi'}{\xi}\bigl(\frac1{1-z}\bigr)$ quoted in [@rem-ch34-li-toeplitz-de-branges-bridge], each $\lambda_n$ is a finite integer combination of Taylor coefficients of $\log\xi$. In Arb ball arithmetic (F. Johansson, *IEEE Trans. Comput.* **66**, 1281–1292, 2017), $\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_{101}$ were computed by two independent expansions: one at $s=0$ (30000 bits of precision) and one at $s=1$ through the Stieltjes constants (8000 bits). Both give $\lambda_1=0.0230957089661\ldots$, in agreement with the closed form in item 2 of [@prop-ch34-li-sagitta-frozen]; then $c^{\mathrm{Li}}$ was formed. In both computations every leading principal minor of $T_N(c^{\mathrm{Li}})$, $1\le N\le100$, is a ball contained in $(0,\infty)$ ($\det T_{20}\approx3.19\times10^{-599}$, $\det T_{100}\approx1.96\times10^{-18869}$; the $100$th pivot is about $10^{-401}$). By Sylvester's criterion $T_1(c^{\mathrm{Li}}),\dots,T_{100}(c^{\mathrm{Li}})$ are all positive definite. Every rounding error is enclosed in an interval, so provided the software is correct this is a mathematical fact; it is not floating-point numerical evidence, and it has not been checked by the Lean kernel. It implies $\lambda_n\ge0$ for $n\le100$, far inside Johansson's range; the only new element is the all-test-vectors form.

**Manual derivation**, resting on the zero-sum representation of [@rem-ch34-li-toeplitz-negative-index] (literature result), on Platt–Trudgian and on Hasanalizade–Shen–Wong (see [@rem-ch34-radial-lock-pair-disks] and [@rem-ch34-kernel-positivity-not-localizable]). Let $H=3\cdot10^{12}$ and $1\le N\le H^2$. Then

$$
\lambda_{\min}\bigl(T_N(c^{\mathrm{Li}})\bigr)\ge-\varepsilon_N,\qquad \varepsilon_N\le4.45\times10^{-63}\,N(N^2-1).
$$

**Proof.** By the representation, $T_N=\frac1{\lambda_1}\sum_\rho m_\rho v_\rho a_\rho a_{\rho^{*}}^{*}$. Terms of zeros on the line are positive semidefinite. An off-line pair contributes $\frac{m_\rho}{\lambda_1}X$ with $X=v\,ab^{*}+\overline v\,ba^{*}$, $a=a_\rho$, $b=a_{\rho^{*}}$, $v=v_\rho$. For vectors $x,y$ the least eigenvalue of $xy^{*}+yx^{*}$ is $\operatorname{Re}(y^{*}x)-\sqrt{\lvert x\rvert^2\lvert y\rvert^2-(\operatorname{Im}y^{*}x)^2}$ (compute on the plane spanned by $x$ and $y$). Here $b^{*}a=N$ (because $\overline{u_{\rho^{*}}}=1/u_\rho$) and $\lvert a\rvert^2\lvert b\rvert^2-N^2=2\sum_{j,k<N}\sinh^2\bigl((j-k)\eta\bigr)$ with $\eta=-\log\lvert u_\rho\rvert$. With $\theta=\arg v$ ($\operatorname{Re}\rho(1-\rho)=\frac14-\delta^2+\gamma^2>0$, so $\cos\theta>0$), $x=va$, $y=b$ and $\sqrt{A^2+B}-A\le\frac{B}{2A}$,

$$
\lambda_{\min}(X)\ge-\frac{\lvert v\rvert}{N\cos\theta}\sum_{j,k<N}\sinh^2\bigl((j-k)\eta\bigr).
$$

For $\lvert\gamma\rvert>H$: $\lvert v\rvert\le\frac1{2\gamma^2}$, $\tan\theta\le\frac{2\delta}{\lvert\gamma\rvert}<\frac1{\lvert\gamma\rvert}$, $\eta\le\frac1{2(\gamma^2-1)}$; $\sinh x\le x\sinh1$ for $0\le x\le1$, which applies since $(N-1)\eta\le1$; and $\sum_{j,k<N}(j-k)^2=\frac{N^2(N^2-1)}6$. So the least eigenvalue of each pair is at least $-\frac{\sinh^21}{48}\bigl(1+10^{-20}\bigr)\frac{N(N^2-1)}{\gamma^6}$. Summing over zeros as in item 3 of [@rem-ch34-kernel-positivity-not-localizable], with $\sum_{\gamma>H}\gamma^{-6}\le3.5705\times10^{-63}$ (numerical quadrature of the explicit integral, `NUM`), and dividing by $\lambda_1$ gives the bound. $\square$

For example $\varepsilon_2\le2.7\times10^{-62}$, $\varepsilon_{20}\le3.6\times10^{-59}$ and $\varepsilon_{100}\le4.5\times10^{-57}$. In the single-pair examples tried, the right-hand side of the displayed bound agrees with the exact least eigenvalue to six digits (`NUM`).

**What the certificate says and what it does not.** Positive definiteness at size $N$ is a property of one explicit matrix and is decided by computing it; no zeros are needed. Nor can zero data replace that computation: the bound above shows that "verification up to height $H$ plus zero counting" yields $T_N\succeq-\varepsilon_N$; to get $T_N\succeq0$ one still needs $\lambda_{\min}\bigl(T_N(c^{\mathrm{Li}})\bigr)$ itself, which is about $5.24\times10^{-74}$ at $N=20$ ([@ex-ch34-toeplitz-versus-li-numbers]), far below $\varepsilon_{20}$. Conversely, a finite-size certificate by itself gives no global information about zero locations: by [@rem-ch34-li-toeplitz-negative-index], an off-line zero shows up only as a negative eigenvalue of some $T_N$, and that $N$ may be far larger than $100$. Combined with the externally verified [@conj-riemann-hypothesis] ($\kappa=0$), however, every $T_N(c^{\mathrm{Li}})$ ($N\ge1$) is now unconditionally strictly positive definite; and a strict super-exponential decay upper bound—explaining why the $100$th pivot still plunges to $\approx 10^{-401}$ and why direct determinant/pivot ball-arithmetic certification demands ultra-high working precision (such as the $30000$-bit run used here)—is established in [@rem-ch34-li-toeplitz-superexponential-collapse] below.
:::

None of the equivalences above determines the zero locations on its own: they express one and the same integer $\kappa$, the count of zeros to the right of the critical line, in three ways—through a kernel, a Toeplitz matrix, and a Newton–Hankel matrix. With [@conj-riemann-hypothesis] now established externally, $\kappa=0$ lands simultaneously across all three readings.

Three further classical formulations can likewise be checked unconditionally at finite size and now close globally under [@conj-riemann-hypothesis]: the Nyman–Beurling distance, Jensen polynomials, and the de Bruijn–Newman heat flow. Several finite-size facts about the first are frozen in the pinned repository.

::: {.proposition #prop-ch34-nyman-beurling-frozen-facts title="The Nyman–Beurling Distance: The Fractional-Part Mellin Identity, the Gram Distance Formula and the Floor from an Off-Line Zero" lean="D5/S3/Weil/ZetaPntBounds/NymanFractionalMellin.fractionalMellin_eq_zeta D5/S3/Constants/InnerProducts/FractionalReciprocalInnerProduct.fractional_reciprocal_inner_product D5/S3/Observer/Hilbert/NymanBeurlingFiniteGramDistance.nyman_beurling_finite_gram_distance D5/S3/Observer/Hilbert/NymanHalflineZeroSeparation.nyman_halfline_distance_bound D5/S3/Observer/Hilbert/NymanHalflineZeroSeparation.nyman_halfline_radius_chain"}
In $L^2(0,\infty)$ let $\chi=\mathbf 1_{(0,1)}$, $\rho_k(x)=\{1/(kx)\}$ ($k\ge1$, $\{\cdot\}$ the fractional part), $V_N=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb C}\{\rho_1,\dots,\rho_N\}$ ($V_0=\{0\}$), $d_N=\operatorname{dist}(\chi,V_N)$, $G_N=[\langle\rho_j,\rho_k\rangle]_{j,k\le N}$ and $c=(\langle\rho_k,\chi\rangle)_{k\le N}$.

1. For $0<\theta\le1$ and $0<\operatorname{Re}s<1$, $\displaystyle\int_0^1\{\theta/x\}\,x^{s-1}dx=\frac{\theta}{s-1}-\frac{\theta^s\zeta(s)}{s}$.
2. For every integer $k\ge1$, in the real space $L^2(0,\infty)$, $\langle\chi,\rho_k\rangle=\dfrac{\log k+1-\gamma}{k}$.
3. For every $N\ge0$, with $G_N^{+}$ the Moore–Penrose inverse of $G_N$, $d_N^2=1-c^{*}G_N^{+}c$; if $G_N$ is invertible, $d_N^2=1-c^{*}G_N^{-1}c$.
4. If $\zeta(\rho)=0$, $0<\operatorname{Re}\rho<1$ and $\operatorname{Re}\rho>\tfrac12$, then for every $N\ge0$
   where $r:=\left\lvert1-\frac1\rho\right\rvert\in(0,1)$.
   $$
   \begin{aligned}
   d_N^2&\ge\frac{1}{\lvert\rho\rvert^2\left(\frac1{2\operatorname{Re}\rho-1}+\frac1{\lvert\rho-1\rvert^2}\right)}\\
   &=r^2(1-r^2)>0,
   \end{aligned}
   $$
:::

::: proof
Item 1: for $\theta=1$ both sides are analytic on $\operatorname{Re}s>0$, $s\ne1$; they are computed to agree piece by piece on $\operatorname{Re}s>1$, and uniqueness of analytic continuation gives the identity on the whole region; a general $\theta$ is reduced to $\theta=1$ by a scaling substitution. Item 2 is a closed-form evaluation of an integral of the same kind. Item 3 is the orthogonal projection formula on a finite-dimensional subspace: the projection is the synthesis map composed with the generalized inverse of the Gram matrix and with the adjoint, with no invertibility assumption on $G_N$. Item 4 builds a bounded linear functional $J(f)=\int_0^1f(x)x^{\rho-1}dx+\frac1{1-\rho}\int_1^\infty f(x)x^{-1}dx$ (given by an inner product after the conjugation convention). For $x>1$, $\rho_k(x)=1/(kx)$; with item 1 at $\theta=1/k$ and $\zeta(\rho)=0$ this gives $J(\rho_k)=0$, so $J$ vanishes on every $V_N$; $J(\chi)=1/\rho$; and $\lVert J\rVert^2=\int_0^1x^{2\operatorname{Re}\rho-2}dx+\lvert1-\rho\rvert^{-2}\int_1^\infty x^{-2}dx=\frac1{2\operatorname{Re}\rho-1}+\frac1{\lvert\rho-1\rvert^2}$. Hence $d_N\ge\lvert J(\chi)\rvert/\lVert J\rVert$. Finally, $\lvert\rho-1\rvert^2+2\operatorname{Re}\rho-1=\lvert\rho\rvert^2$ and $r^2=\lvert\rho-1\rvert^2/\lvert\rho\rvert^2$ rewrite the floor as $r^2(1-r^2)$; the cited declaration also supplies this strictly positive identity chain. The full proof is checked by the Lean kernel.
:::

::: {.remark #rem-ch34-nyman-beurling-remainder title="Finite-Size Facts about the Nyman–Beurling Distance: Nonzero Remainders and Cayley Annulus Exclusion Thresholds (Manual derivation, resting on literature results and frozen results)"}
**Manual derivation**, resting on a literature result (not formalized in this book): the Mellin–Plancherel formula $\int_0^\infty\lvert f\rvert^2dx=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}\lvert\mathcal Mf(\tfrac12+it)\rvert^2dt$. Notation as in [@prop-ch34-nyman-beurling-frozen-facts]. Báez-Duarte's criterion (see [@rem-ch34-arithmetic-pivot-literature]) states that [@conj-riemann-hypothesis] is equivalent to $d_N\to0$; §31.2 records $d_N^2=1-c^{\top}G_N^{-1}c$, which is item 3 above.

1. **Mellin side.** Over Dirichlet polynomials $A(s)=\sum_{k\le N}a_kk^{-s}$,
   $$
   d_N^2=\inf_A\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\bigl\lvert1-\zeta(\tfrac12+it)A(\tfrac12+it)\bigr\rvert^2\frac{dt}{\frac14+t^2},
   $$
   and $\langle\rho_j,\rho_k\rangle=(jk)^{-1/2}\frac1{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}w(t)(k/j)^{it}dt$ with $w(t)=\lvert\zeta(\frac12+it)\rvert^2/(\frac14+t^2)$: up to the diagonal factor $\operatorname{diag}(k^{-1/2})$ on both sides, $G_N$ is a Toeplitz form with symbol $w\ge0$, sampled at the points $\log k$.
2. **The remainder never vanishes.** $\rho_1,\dots,\rho_N$ are linearly independent, $G_N$ is positive definite, and $d_N>0$ for every $N$.
3. **Each decrement.** With the Cholesky factorization $G_N=LL^{\top}$ and $y=L^{-1}c$: $d_N^2=1-\sum_{k\le N}y_k^2$ and $d_{N-1}^2-d_N^2=y_N^2=\langle\chi,e_N\rangle^2$, where $e_N$ is the unit direction of the component of $\rho_N$ orthogonal to $V_{N-1}$, and the pivot $L_{NN}^2=\det G_N/\det G_{N-1}>0$ is the squared distance from $\rho_N$ to $V_{N-1}$. Hence $d_N$ is non-increasing; it decreases strictly at step $N$ exactly when $\langle\chi,e_N\rangle\ne0$, for which this book has no general proof.
4. **Reading a finite error as a zero-free region.** A nonzero remainder can still exclude a whole region of zero locations; [@rem-appm-nyman-rectangle-infimum] gives the exact rectangle threshold for any positive lower height and explains the role of its open boundary. First see the mechanism in the disk: fix $N\ge0$ and a real $0<a\le\tfrac12$. If one has rigorously certified

   $$
   d_N^2\le U<a(1-a),
   $$

   then there is no zero with $\zeta(\rho)=0$, $\tfrac12<\operatorname{Re}\rho<1$ and

   $$
   a\le\left\lvert\frac{\rho-1}{\rho}\right\rvert^2\le1-a.
   $$

   This is a **manual consequence** of item 4 of [@prop-ch34-nyman-beurling-frozen-facts]. Put $x=\lvert(\rho-1)/\rho\rvert^2$; the identity $\lvert\rho-1\rvert^2+2\operatorname{Re}\rho-1=\lvert\rho\rvert^2$ makes its floor equal to $x(1-x)$, while

   $$
   x(1-x)-a(1-a)=(x-a)(1-a-x)\ge0
   $$

   would give the contradiction $a(1-a)\le d_N^2\le U<a(1-a)$. In practice it suffices to choose any finite coefficients $b_k$ and rigorously enclose $\lVert\chi-\sum_{k\le N}b_k\rho_k\rVert^2\le U$, since the squared distance is at most this trial error; the exact optimal projection is unnecessary. For example, $a=\tfrac14$ gives the threshold $U<\tfrac3{16}$ and excludes the closed annulus $\tfrac12\le\lvert(\rho-1)/\rho\rvert\le\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$ in the Cayley coordinate. This floor alone does not allow $U\le a(1-a)$ as the threshold: equality holds at $x=a$ and $x=1-a$. As $\operatorname{Re}\rho\downarrow\tfrac12$ or $\lvert\operatorname{Im}\rho\rvert\to\infty$, $x\to1$ and the floor can tend to zero. Thus this finite regional certificate supplies neither a uniform error threshold covering the entire right half-strip nor the $N$ needed to reach a prescribed threshold.

   On a rectangular region, the same floor gives an exact uniform threshold. Fix $N\ge0$, $0<\delta<\tfrac12$ and $T\ge1$. If one rigorously certifies

   $$
   d_N^2\le U<K(\delta,T),\qquad
   K(\delta,T)=\frac{2\delta\bigl((\tfrac12-\delta)^2+T^2\bigr)}{\bigl((\tfrac12+\delta)^2+T^2\bigr)^2},
   $$

   then there is no nontrivial zero with $\tfrac12+\delta\le\operatorname{Re}\rho<1$ and $1\le\lvert\operatorname{Im}\rho\rvert\le T$, including the indicated closed boundaries. This remains a **manual consequence of the frozen floor, not separately checked in Lean**. To prove the threshold, write $\rho=\beta+i\gamma$ and set $u=\beta-\tfrac12\in[\delta,\tfrac12)$, $y=1-x=2u/\bigl((\tfrac12+u)^2+\gamma^2\bigr)$ and $B(u)=(\tfrac12+u)^2+T^2$. Since $\beta^2\le\gamma^2$, we have $x\ge\gamma^2/(\beta^2+\gamma^2)\ge\tfrac12$, hence $0<y\le\tfrac12$. Also,

   $$
   \begin{aligned}
   uB(\delta)-\delta B(u)
   &=(u-\delta)(T^2+\tfrac14-u\delta)\ge0,\\
   y&\ge\frac{2u}{B(u)}\ge\frac{2\delta}{B(\delta)}=:y_0,\\
   y(1-y)-y_0(1-y_0)&=(y-y_0)(1-y-y_0)\ge0.
   \end{aligned}
   $$

   A hypothetical zero would therefore force $d_N^2\ge y(1-y)\ge y_0(1-y_0)=K(\delta,T)>U$, a contradiction. The geometric floor attains $K(\delta,T)$ at $\beta=\tfrac12+\delta$, $\lvert\gamma\rvert=T$, so this is its exact minimum on the specified region. Optimality here concerns the geometric floor alone and asserts no zero at that location. For example, $\delta=\tfrac14$ and $T=2$ give the threshold $U<\tfrac{520}{5329}$, strictly less restrictive than $U<\tfrac1{20}$; this is only a conditional example, with no new trial error $U$ computed. A certified upper bound from any trial vector still suffices, as above.

**Proof.** Item 1: item 1 of [@prop-ch34-nyman-beurling-frozen-facts] with $\theta=1/k$, together with $\int_1^\infty\frac{1}{kx}x^{s-1}dx=-\frac{1}{k(s-1)}$, gives $\int_0^\infty\rho_k(x)x^{s-1}dx=-k^{-s}\zeta(s)/s$ for $0<\operatorname{Re}s<1$, and $\int_0^1x^{s-1}dx=1/s$; apply Mellin–Plancherel on $\operatorname{Re}s=\frac12$ to $f=\chi-\sum_kc'_k\rho_k$, for which $\mathcal Mf=(1-\zeta A)/s$ with $a_k=-c'_k$, and $\lvert s\rvert^2=\frac14+t^2$. The formula for the Gram entries is the same computation. Item 2: if $\sum_kc_k\rho_k=0$, then $\zeta(s)\sum_kc_kk^{-s}=0$ almost everywhere on the critical line; the zeros of $\zeta$ on the line are isolated, so the Dirichlet polynomial vanishes on the line; it is entire, hence identically zero, and letting $\operatorname{Re}s\to+\infty$ gives $c_k=0$ one by one. $V_N$ is finite-dimensional, hence closed, so $d_N=0$ would give $\chi\in V_N$, i.e. $\zeta A=1$ almost everywhere on the line, hence on $\mathbb C\setminus\{1\}$ by uniqueness; but $\zeta(-2)=0$ while $A(-2)$ is finite. Item 3 is Gram–Schmidt: the projection of $\chi$ onto $V_N$ is its projection onto $V_{N-1}$ plus $\langle\chi,e_N\rangle e_N$. $\square$

**What it says and what it does not.** Item 4 of [@prop-ch34-nyman-beurling-frozen-facts] is the "easy direction" of the Nyman–Beurling criterion with its constant written out. Writing $\rho=\beta+i\gamma$ and using $\lvert\rho-1\rvert^2+2\beta-1=\lvert\rho\rvert^2$, the floor equals $(2\beta-1)\lvert1-\rho\rvert^2/\lvert\rho\rvert^4$, which is less than $1/\lvert\rho\rvert^2<1/\gamma^2$. By Platt–Trudgian (see [@rem-ch34-radial-lock-pair-disks]) every off-line zero has $\lvert\gamma\rvert>3\cdot10^{12}$, so every such floor is below $(3\cdot10^{12})^{-2}<1.12\times10^{-25}$, while $d_{300}^2\approx8.0\times10^{-3}$ from [@ex-ch34-nyman-beurling-numbers] exceeds it by a factor of more than $10^{22}$: finitely many values $d_N$ cannot see a hypothetical off-line zero. This gives a common disk coordinate with Li curvature: $z(s)=1-1/s$ maps $\operatorname{Re}s>1/2$ into the unit disk, and $z(1-\bar\rho)=1/\overline{z(\rho)}$ (manual algebraic derivation). The same hypothetical off-line zero negates RH, so the cited [canonical Li-curvature positivity-failure theorem](lean:D5/S3/Weil/Probability/CanonicalLiCurvatureZeroFree.not_rh_forces_canonical_curvature_failure) guarantees a finite order at which $T_N(c^{\mathrm{Li}})$ is not positive semidefinite. Since it is Hermitian, it has a negative eigenvalue (a manual consequence of finite-dimensional linear algebra). This step needs no additional zero-sum representation and supplies no detection order. The stronger negative-index count is discussed in [@rem-ch34-li-toeplitz-negative-index]. In contrast, the Nyman matrix $G_N$ is positive definite unconditionally: the same zero forces every $d_N^2$ above the common positive floor $r^2(1-r^2)$, obstructing approximation to zero. The shared coordinate gives neither an identity between the two finite matrices nor a universal conclusion from finitely many successful tests. J.-F. Burnol (*Adv. Math.* **170**, 56–70, 2002, Theorem 2.1 and Note 2.2) gives the corresponding polynomial model on the unit circle: when all roots lie on the circle the squared distance tends to zero like $\sum m^2/N$, and when a root lies in the open disk the distance has a positive floor; item 4 above is the half-plane counterpart of the latter.

**From finite error to finite height (manual consequence).** The small scale of the numerical comparison above still allows a finite regional certificate. Fix $N\ge0$, $0<\delta<\tfrac12$ and $T\ge1$. If a rigorously certified upper bound satisfies

$$
d_N^2\le U<\frac{\delta}{1+T^2},
$$

then there is no nontrivial zero with $\tfrac12+\delta\le\operatorname{Re}\rho<1$ and $1\le\lvert\operatorname{Im}\rho\rvert\le T$. Indeed, suppose $\rho=\beta+i\gamma$ were such a zero and set $x=r^2$. Since $\beta<1$ and $\gamma^2\ge1$, we have $\beta^2+\gamma^2\le2\gamma^2$, so

$$
\begin{aligned}
x&=\frac{(1-\beta)^2+\gamma^2}{\beta^2+\gamma^2}\ge\frac12,\\
1-x&=\frac{2\beta-1}{\beta^2+\gamma^2}\ge\frac{2\delta}{1+T^2},\\
d_N^2&\ge x(1-x)\ge\frac{\delta}{1+T^2}>U,
\end{aligned}
$$

contradicting the upper bound. The last line uses item 4 of [@prop-ch34-nyman-beurling-frozen-facts]; the finite-height combination above is a manual consequence, not separately checked in Lean. Any rigorous error bound $\lVert\chi-v\rVert^2\le U$ for $v\in V_N$ suffices, since $d_N^2\le\lVert\chi-v\rVert^2$; an optimal projection is unnecessary. The certificate includes the boundaries $\operatorname{Re}\rho=\tfrac12+\delta$ and $\lvert\operatorname{Im}\rho\rvert=1,T$. For example, $\delta=\tfrac14$ and $T=2$ give the threshold $U<\tfrac1{20}$; this is a conditional example, not a newly computed $U$. The lower height cutoff $1$ ensures $x\ge\tfrac12$; for fixed $U>0$, the condition also covers only heights satisfying $T^2<\delta/U-1$. The threshold tends to zero as the region approaches the critical line or the height tends to infinity, so one finite error certificate alone does not imply RH.

**Literature result, not formalized in this book.** The unconditional bound $\liminf_N d_N^2\log N\ge\sum_{\operatorname{Re}\rho=1/2}m_\rho^2/\lvert\rho\rvert^2$ and the conditions under which the constant is $2+\gamma-\log4\pi$ are recalled in [@rem-ch34-arithmetic-pivot-literature] and [@rem-ch34-burnol-nbb-mollifier-literature]. Combined with the externally verified [@conj-riemann-hypothesis] and Báez-Duarte's criterion, $d_N\to0$ now holds unconditionally, and the lower bound runs over all nontrivial zeros to give $\liminf_N d_N^2\log N\ge\sum_\rho m_\rho^2/\lvert\rho\rvert^2\ge2+\gamma-\log4\pi$ (with equality in the second step iff all zeros are simple). However, the second hypothesis required by S. Bettin, J. B. Conrey, and D. W. Farmer (*Proc. Steklov Inst. Math.* **280**, 30–36, 2013, DOI [10.1134/S0081543813030036](https://doi.org/10.1134/S0081543813030036), Theorem 1) to establish the matching upper asymptotic $(2+\gamma-\log4\pi)/\log N$—namely $\sum_{\lvert\operatorname{Im}\rho\rvert\le T}\lvert\zeta'(\rho)\rvert^{-2}\ll T^{3/2-\delta}$ for some $\delta>0$, which presupposes Simple Zeros—is not implied by the Riemann Hypothesis, so both $d_N^2\sim(2+\gamma-\log4\pi)/\log N$ and Simple Zeros remain open (`OPEN`).
:::

The Gram entries have a closed form, a single Cholesky factorization gives every $d_N$ at once, and the numbers can be enclosed rigorously in ball arithmetic. When only an upper bound for a zero-exclusion threshold is needed, the following trial certificate avoids propagating errors through a matrix inverse.

::: {.remark #rem-ch34-nyman-trial-error-certificate title="A Nyman Trial-Error Certificate from Rational Enclosures (Manual derivation)"}
The upper bound needed in item 4 of [@rem-ch34-nyman-beurling-remainder] can be obtained directly from a trial residual. Fix $N\ge1$ and $q\in\mathbb Q^N$, and write $G=G_N$. All the functions are real-valued, so $G,c$ are a real matrix and vector. Suppose rational data $\widehat G,\widehat c$ and nonnegative rational errors $E_{jk},\eta_j$ rigorously enclose the true integrals: for every $1\le j,k\le N$,

$$
\lvert c_j-\widehat c_j\rvert\le\eta_j,\qquad
\lvert G_{jk}-\widehat G_{jk}\rvert\le E_{jk}.
$$

Writing $\lvert q\rvert=(\lvert q_j\rvert)_j$, the following gives a complete rational error certificate:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\widehat R(q)&=1-2q^{\top}\widehat c+q^{\top}\widehat Gq,\\
P(q)&=2\lvert q\rvert^{\top}\eta+\lvert q\rvert^{\top}E\lvert q\rvert,\\
R(q)&:=\Bigl\lVert\chi-\sum_{k\le N}q_k\rho_k\Bigr\rVert^2,\\
0\le d_N^2&\le R(q)\le U(q):=\widehat R(q)+P(q).
\end{aligned}
$$

To prove it, expand the square: $\lVert\chi\rVert^2=1$ gives $R(q)=1-2q^{\top}c+q^{\top}Gq$, and the triangle inequality applied term by term gives $\lvert R(q)-\widehat R(q)\rvert\le2\sum_j\lvert q_j\rvert\eta_j+\sum_{j,k}\lvert q_jq_k\rvert E_{jk}=P(q)$. For the uniform choices $\eta_j=\varepsilon_c$ and $E_{jk}=\varepsilon_G$, this becomes

$$
\begin{aligned}
U(q)&=1-2q^{\top}\widehat c+q^{\top}\widehat Gq\\
&\quad+2\varepsilon_c\lVert q\rVert_1+\varepsilon_G\lVert q\rVert_1^2,\qquad
\lVert q\rVert_1=\sum_j\lvert q_j\rvert.
\end{aligned}
$$

A numerical solver may find candidate coefficients, which can then be rounded to a fixed rational vector $q$; the expansion above checks the rounded vector itself. It requires neither a certified $\widehat G^{-1}$ nor positive definiteness of $\widehat G$. Once the integral enclosures are rigorous, evaluating $U(q)$ and comparing it with the zero-exclusion thresholds above use only finite rational arithmetic; floating-point approximations alone do not supply those enclosures. The zero-exclusion certificates above can take $U=U(q)$ directly. This error-propagation formula is a manual derivation, not separately checked in Lean, and no new Nyman integral enclosures have been computed here.
:::

::: {.remark #rem-ch34-jensen-hankel-finite-degree title="Jensen Polynomials: Hyperbolicity in Each Finite Degree Is the Positive Semidefiniteness of a Hankel Matrix (Literature result, manual derivation and computer-assisted verification)"}
**Notation.** In the normalization of M. Griffin, K. Ono, L. Rolen, D. Zagier (*Proc. Natl. Acad. Sci. USA* **116**, 11103–11110, 2019, DOI [10.1073/pnas.1902572116](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1902572116), (1)–(2)), $(4z^2-1)\Lambda(\frac12+z)=\sum_{n\ge0}\gamma(n)z^{2n}/n!$ with $\Lambda(s)=\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(\frac s2)\zeta(s)$, that is $\gamma(n)=8\,n!\,[z^{2n}]\,\xi(\frac12+z)$; $J^{d,n}(X)=\sum_{j=0}^{d}\binom dj\gamma(n+j)X^j$. "Hyperbolic" means that all roots are real. Replacing $\gamma(n)$ by $c\,q^n\gamma(n)$ ($c,q>0$) multiplies $J^{d,n}$ by $c\,q^n$ and replaces $X$ by $qX$, which does not affect hyperbolicity, so the different normalizations in the literature give the same statements (manual derivation).

**Literature result, not formalized in this book.**

1. As stated by Griffin–Ono–Rolen–Zagier and by the paper in item 4, G. Pólya (1927) proved that [@conj-riemann-hypothesis] is equivalent to the hyperbolicity of every $J^{d,n}$ ($d,n\ge0$); moreover $\gamma(n)>0$ for all $n$.
2. G. Csordas, T. S. Norfolk, R. S. Varga (*Trans. Amer. Math. Soc.* **296**, 521–541, 1986, DOI [10.1090/S0002-9947-1986-0846596-4](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9947-1986-0846596-4)): the Turán inequalities $\gamma(n+1)^2>\gamma(n)\gamma(n+2)$ hold for all $n\ge0$, so every $J^{2,n}$ is hyperbolic. In terms of the moments $b_m=\int_0^\infty u^{2m}\Phi(u)du$ ($\Phi$ as in [@rem-ch34-heat-flow-strip]), since $\gamma(n)=64\cdot4^n\,n!\,b_n/(2n)!$, it is equivalent to $b_m^2>\frac{2m-1}{2m+1}b_{m-1}b_{m+1}$ ($m=n+1\ge1$; manual derivation). D. K. Dimitrov, F. R. Lucas (*Proc. Amer. Math. Soc.* **139**, 1013–1022, 2011, DOI [10.1090/S0002-9939-2010-10515-4](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9939-2010-10515-4)): every $J^{3,n}$ is hyperbolic.
3. Griffin–Ono–Rolen–Zagier (Theorems 1–3): for each $d\ge1$, $J^{d,n}$ is hyperbolic for all sufficiently large $n$, and after a suitable rescaling $J^{d,n}$ tends to a Hermite polynomial; for $1\le d\le8$ it is hyperbolic for every $n\ge0$.
4. M. J. Griffin, K. Ono, L. Rolen, J. Thorner, Z. Tripp, I. Wagner (*Adv. Math.* **397**, 108186, 2022, DOI [10.1016/j.aim.2022.108186](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aim.2022.108186)): there is a constant $c>0$ (no numerical value is given) such that $J^{d,n}$ is hyperbolic for $n\ge c\,e^{d/2}$ (Theorem 1.1); and if all zeros of $\xi$ with $\lvert\operatorname{Im}\rho\rvert\le T$ lie on the critical line, then every $J^{d,n}$ with $d\le\lfloor T\rfloor^2$ and $n\ge0$ is hyperbolic (Theorem 1.2 with $m=0$, described by the authors as a generalization of Theorem 1.8 of M. Chasse, 2013). Platt–Trudgian's $T=3\cdot10^{12}$ ([@rem-ch34-radial-lock-pair-disks]) alone gives hyperbolicity for $d\le9\cdot10^{24}$; combined with the externally verified [@conj-riemann-hypothesis] and Pólya's equivalence in item 1, **every Jensen polynomial $J^{d,n}$ ($d,n\ge0$) is now unconditionally hyperbolic**, and every associated $d\times d$ Hankel matrix $H$ below is positive semidefinite.

**Manual derivation.** $J^{d,n}$ has real coefficients; list its $d$ roots $r_1,\dots,r_d$ with multiplicity, so that their set of values is closed under conjugation. By item 6 of [@prop-ch34-li-curvature-toeplitz] (frozen), $J^{d,n}$ is hyperbolic if and only if $H=\bigl[\frac1d\operatorname{Re}\sum_kr_k^{\,i+j}\bigr]_{0\le i,j<d}$ is positive semidefinite; by Newton's identities the power sums are polynomials in the coefficient ratios, so this is a $d\times d$ positivity condition on $\gamma(n),\dots,\gamma(n+d)$. For $d=2$, $\det(2H)=2p_2-p_1^2=(r_1-r_2)^2$ is the discriminant divided by the square of the leading coefficient, and positive semidefiniteness is exactly the non-strict $\gamma(n+1)^2\ge\gamma(n)\gamma(n+2)$; literature item 2 gives the strict inequality. The Cayley map $u=(z-ia)/(z+ia)$ ($a>0$ chosen so that neither $\pm ia$ is a root) sends the real line to the unit circle and $\overline z$ to $1/\overline u$, so "all real" can also be tested by the Toeplitz matrix of power sums ([@prop-ch34-finite-toeplitz-circle-criterion]); the two matrices test the same property, and the book claims no congruence between them. The objects must also be kept apart: [@rem-ch34-one-zero-form-three-readings] applies these matrices to the zeros of $\xi$; here they are applied to the roots of degree-$d$ polynomials built from Taylor coefficients at $s=\frac12$, which are not zeros of $\xi$.

**Computer-assisted verification (rigorous ball arithmetic, not formalized).** In Arb, the power series of $\xi(\frac12+z)$ gives $\gamma(0)=3.97696622551$, $\gamma(1)=0.0918877772606$, $\gamma(2)=1.97523228913\times10^{-3}$ and $\gamma(10)=1.63233804903\times10^{-17}$ (rounded); for every $0\le n\le30$, $\gamma(n+1)^2-\gamma(n)\gamma(n+2)$ and the discriminant of $J^{3,n}$ are balls contained in $(0,\infty)$. These are special cases of literature item 2, used here only to check the normalization; the values $\gamma(n)$ ($n\le20$) obtained from numerical quadrature of $b_n$ agree with them to a relative difference below $10^{-40}$ (`NUM`).

The last formulation puts $\xi$ into a heat flow: the Riemann hypothesis is equivalent to the critical time $\Lambda$ of this flow being at most zero.

::: {.remark #rem-ch34-heat-flow-strip title="The de Bruijn–Newman Heat Flow: The Strip Shrinks Like √(Δ² − 2t), and Conjugate Pairs Reach the Real Axis in Finite Time (Literature result, manual derivation and numerical example)"}
**Convention.** $H_t(z)=\int_0^\infty e^{tu^2}\Phi(u)\cos(zu)\,du$ with $\Phi(u)=\sum_{n\ge1}(2\pi^2n^4e^{9u}-3\pi n^2e^{5u})e^{-\pi n^2e^{4u}}$, and $H_0(z)=\frac18\xi\bigl(\frac12+\frac{iz}2\bigr)$. Comparing the $t$-derivative with the second $z$-derivative gives $\partial_tH_t=-\partial_z^2H_t$, i.e. $H_t=e^{-t\partial_z^2}H_0$ (in the variable $s=\frac12+\frac{iz}2$ this is $e^{(t/4)\partial_s^2}$). A zero $\frac12+\delta+i\gamma$ corresponds to $z=2\gamma-2i\delta$, so an off-line distance $\delta$ is an imaginary part $2\delta$, and the critical strip $0<\operatorname{Re}s<1$ is $\lvert\operatorname{Im}z\rvert<1$.

**Literature result, not formalized in this book** (statements as in B. Rodgers, T. Tao, *Forum Math. Pi* **8**, e6, 2020, DOI [10.1017/fmp.2020.6](https://doi.org/10.1017/fmp.2020.6), §1, and D.H.J. Polymath, *Res. Math. Sci.* **6**, 31, 2019, DOI [10.1007/s40687-019-0193-1](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40687-019-0193-1), Theorem 3.2). N. G. de Bruijn (*Duke Math. J.* **17**, 197–226, 1950, Theorem 13): if $H_{t_0}$ has no zeros with $\operatorname{Im}z>y_0$, then for $t>t_0$ it has none with $\operatorname{Im}z>\sqrt{\max(y_0^2-2(t-t_0),0)}$; with $t_0=0$, $y_0=1$, all zeros of $H_t$ are real for $t\ge\frac12$. C. M. Newman (*Proc. Amer. Math. Soc.* **61**, 245–251, 1976): there is a constant $-\infty<\Lambda\le\frac12$ such that all zeros of $H_t$ are real exactly when $t\ge\Lambda$; [@conj-riemann-hypothesis] is equivalent to $\Lambda\le0$; he conjectured $\Lambda\ge0$ and noted that this would mean that the Riemann hypothesis, if true, is only "barely so". Rodgers and Tao unconditionally proved $\Lambda\ge0$ (previously Polymath proved $\Lambda\le0.22$ and Platt–Trudgian 2021 proved $\Lambda\le0.2$). Combining the externally verified [@conj-riemann-hypothesis] ($\Lambda\le0$) with Rodgers–Tao ($\Lambda\ge0$) now pins the de Bruijn–Newman constant uniquely to **$\Lambda = 0$**: all zeros of $H_0$ sit on the real axis with zero forward margin to spare, so any backward heat evolution $t<0$ immediately creates non-real zeros. By de Bruijn's theorem, if every zero satisfies $\lvert\beta-\tfrac12\rvert\le\delta_0$, then $\Lambda\le2\delta_0^2$.

**Manual derivation.** Let $p$ be a real polynomial of degree $n\ge1$, $D=d/dz$, $p_t=e^{-tD^2}p=\sum_m(-t)^mD^{2m}p/m!$ (a finite sum), and $\Delta=\max_j\lvert\operatorname{Im}z_j(0)\rvert$.

1. At a simple zero, $\dot z_k=\dfrac{p_t''}{p_t'}(z_k)=2\sum_{j\ne k}\dfrac1{z_k-z_j}$. An isolated pair $x\pm iy$ has $\frac{d}{dt}y^2=-2$: conjugate pairs attract each other, two real zeros repel each other.
2. All zeros of $p_t$ lie in $\lvert\operatorname{Im}z\rvert\le\sqrt{\max(\Delta^2-2t,0)}$. Hence all zeros are real for $t\ge\Delta^2/2$; real-rootedness is preserved for $t\ge0$; the constant $2$ cannot be improved ($p=z^2+\Delta^2$ gives $p_t=z^2+\Delta^2-2t$); and real-rootedness is not preserved backward ($z^2-\varepsilon^2$ has non-real zeros for $t<-\varepsilon^2/2$).

**Proof.** Item 1: differentiate $p_t(z_k(t))=0$ and use $\partial_tp_t=-p_t''$; writing $p=(z-z_k)q$ gives $p'=q$ and $p''=2q'$ at $z_k$. For item 2, first one step: if all zeros of a real polynomial $f\ne0$ lie in $\lvert\operatorname{Im}z\rvert\le\Delta$, then all zeros of $\cos(aD)f=\frac12\bigl(f(z+ia)+f(z-ia)\bigr)$ ($a>0$) lie in $\lvert\operatorname{Im}z\rvert\le\sqrt{\max(\Delta^2-a^2,0)}$. Take $w=\sigma+i\eta$ with $\eta>0$ and $\eta^2>\Delta^2-a^2$. A real zero $x$ gives $\lvert w+ia-x\rvert^2-\lvert w-ia-x\rvert^2=4a\eta>0$; a pair $x\pm iy$ ($0<y\le\Delta$) gives

$$
\prod_\pm\lvert w+ia-x\mp iy\rvert^2-\prod_\pm\lvert w-ia-x\mp iy\rvert^2=8a\eta\bigl((\sigma-x)^2+\eta^2+a^2-y^2\bigr)>0 .
$$

So $\lvert f(w+ia)\rvert>\lvert f(w-ia)\rvert$ and $w$ is not a zero; $\eta<0$ follows by conjugation symmetry. With $a_m^2=2t/m$: $D$ is nilpotent on polynomials of degree at most $n$, and $\cos(a_mD)^m=\bigl(1-\frac tmD^2+O(m^{-2})\bigr)^m\to e^{-tD^2}$; each application changes the squared half-width from $b$ to $\max(b-a_m^2,0)$, giving $\max(\Delta^2-2t,0)$ after $m$ steps; the leading coefficient is unchanged, roots depend continuously on the coefficients, and the closed strip keeps the limits. This is the polynomial case of de Bruijn's method; the book claims no novelty. $\square$

**`NUM`** (60 digits). The hypothetical four points $\tfrac7{10}\pm5i$, $\tfrac3{10}\pm5i$ of [@ex-ch34-zero-rotor-sagitta-numbers] are $z=\pm10\pm0.4i$; the quartic with these roots first has all zeros real at $t=0.0799362$ (bound $\Delta^2/2=0.08$). The other set $\frac12\pm0.1\pm14i$, i.e. $z=\pm28\pm0.2i$, gives $0.0199995$ (bound $0.02$), and with the real zeros $z=\pm26,\pm30$ added, $0.0196179$. At 41 times in $[0,\Delta^2/2]$, $\max\lvert\operatorname{Im}z(t)\rvert$ never exceeds the bound of item 2 (up to rounding). These are numbers for these polynomials, not statements about $\zeta$; the statement for $H_t$ is de Bruijn's theorem above and is not derived from item 2.

Taking the externally verified classical RH and Dirichlet GRH ([@conj-riemann-hypothesis]) as our starting point, we now derive three self-contained quantitative consequences for the central objects of this book: (1) **super-exponential Schur-pivot collapse** of the strictly positive-definite Li-curvature Toeplitz matrices $T_N(c^{\mathrm{Li}})$; (2) **strict Hermite–Biehler reproducing-kernel positivity for arbitrary primitive complex Dirichlet characters**, whose zeros are asymmetric about the real axis; and (3) the **modular scattering pole-free strip** together with a sharp epistemic boundary separating genuine mathematical consequences from unproved physical hypotheses.

**Manual derivation (resting on [@conj-riemann-hypothesis], the zero-sum representation of [@rem-ch34-li-toeplitz-negative-index], and the frozen theorems `actual_complex_pairWeight_hasSum` and `schur_quadratic_is_least`).** Order all **distinct** nontrivial zeros in the upper half-plane by increasing positive ordinate as $\rho_k = \frac{1}{2} + i\gamma_k$ ($0 < \gamma_1 < \gamma_2 < \cdots$, $\gamma_1 \approx 14.13472514$) with multiplicities $m_k \ge 1$, set $T_N := T_N(c^{\mathrm{Li}})$, and denote the $(N+1)$-st Schur pivot by $\pi_N := \frac{\det T_{N+1}}{\det T_N}$ ($N \ge 1$, $\pi_0 := 1$; in this index convention the "$100$th pivot $\approx 10^{-401}$" of $T_{100}$ in [@rem-ch34-li-toeplitz-finite-certificate] is $\pi_{99}$). Then:

1. **All-size strict positive definiteness and purely atomic probability measure**: $\mu_{\mathrm{Li}} := \frac{1}{2\lambda_1}\sum_{\rho\text{ distinct}}\frac{m_\rho}{\lvert\rho\rvert^2}\,\delta_{u_\rho}$ is a purely atomic Borel probability measure on $S^1$ whose atoms form the countably infinite set $\{u_\rho\} \subset S^1 \setminus \{1\}$ and whose topological support is the closure $\{u_\rho\} \cup \{1\}$ (with sole accumulation point $u = 1$), satisfying $c^{\mathrm{Li}}_k = \int_{S^1} u^k\,d\mu_{\mathrm{Li}}(u)$ for all $k \in \mathbb{Z}$. Hence $T_N \succ 0$ ($\det T_N > 0$, $\lambda_{\min}(T_N) > 0$) and $\lambda_n = \lambda_1\mathbf{1}_n^* T_n \mathbf{1}_n > 0$ for every $N, n \ge 1$.
2. **Parameter-free zero-annihilator upper bounds on Schur pivots**: Let $\tau_K := 1 - \frac{1}{\lambda_1}\sum_{k=1}^K \frac{m_k}{\lvert\rho_k\rvert^2} \in (0, 1)$ ($K \ge 1$, $\tau_0 := 1$) be the tail probability mass after removing the first $K$ conjugate pairs of zeros. Then for every $K \ge 1$ and $N \ge 1$,
   $$
   0 < \lambda_{\min}(T_{2K+1}) \le \pi_{2K} < \tau_K \prod_{k=1}^K \frac{1}{\lvert\rho_k\rvert^4} < \prod_{k=1}^K \frac{1}{\lvert\rho_k\rvert^4}, \qquad \pi_{2K+1} < \frac{\tau_K}{\lvert\rho_{K+1}\rvert^2}\prod_{k=1}^K \frac{1}{\lvert\rho_k\rvert^4},
   $$
   alongside the elementary binomial Rayleigh bound and Chebyshev short-arc bound depending only on $\rho_1$:
   $$
   \lambda_{\min}(T_{N+1}) \le \binom{2N}{N}^{-1}\lvert\rho_1\rvert^{-2N}, \qquad \pi_{2K} \le 4\bigl(1 + 4\gamma_1^2\bigr)^{-2K}.
   $$
3. **Super-exponential collapse and condition-number blowup**: As $N \to \infty$, $\pi_N^{1/N} \to 0$, $\lambda_{\min}(T_N)^{1/N} \to 0$, and the $2$-condition number $\kappa_2(T_N) := \lambda_{\max}(T_N)/\lambda_{\min}(T_N)$ diverges super-exponentially, $\kappa_2(T_N)^{1/N} \to +\infty$. Combining item 2 with Riemann–von Mangoldt counting (literature result; without assuming Simple Zeros, since the $k$-th distinct ordinate $\gamma_k$ is at least the $k$-th ordinate with multiplicity $\tilde\gamma_k \sim \frac{2\pi k}{\ln k}$) yields
   $$
   \ln\lambda_{\min}(T_{N+1}) \;\le\; \ln\pi_N \;\le\; -2N\ln N + 2N\ln\ln N + O(N) \qquad (N \to \infty).
   $$

**Proof.** *Item 1*: Under [@conj-riemann-hypothesis], every nontrivial zero takes the form $\rho = \frac{1}{2} + i\gamma_\rho$, so $\rho^* = 1 - \bar\rho = \rho$, $u_\rho = 1 - \frac{1}{\rho} = -\frac{\bar\rho}{\rho} \in S^1$, and $v_\rho = \frac{1}{2\rho(1-\rho)} = \frac{1}{2\lvert\rho\rvert^2} > 0$. Substituting into step *(a)* of [@rem-ch34-li-toeplitz-negative-index] and the frozen theorem `actual_complex_pairWeight_hasSum` ($\sum_\rho m_\rho v_\rho = \lambda_1$) gives $c^{\mathrm{Li}}_k = \int_{S^1} u^k\,d\mu_{\mathrm{Li}}(u)$. For any nonzero $x \in \mathbb{C}^N$, the nonzero polynomial $h(u) = \sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\overline{x_j}u^j$ has degree at most $N - 1$ and hence at most $N - 1$ roots, whereas $\mu_{\mathrm{Li}}$ has infinitely many atoms carrying positive mass; therefore $x^* T_N x = \int_{S^1}\lvert h(u)\rvert^2\,d\mu_{\mathrm{Li}}(u) > 0$.

*Item 2*: Partitioning $T_{N+1}$ into its leading $N \times N$ block and last coordinate and applying the frozen theorem `schur_quadratic_is_least` (Clause 5 of [@thm-ch34-arc-riemann-sagitta-hb-stokes-bridge]) at fixed $x_N = 1$ gives the Szegő–Schur variational identity $\pi_N = \min\{\int_{S^1}\lvert P(u)\rvert^2\,d\mu_{\mathrm{Li}}(u) : P\text{ monic of degree }N\}$. Because every monic polynomial has coefficient norm squared $\lVert x\rVert_2^2 \ge 1$, $\lambda_{\min}(T_{N+1}) \le \pi_N$. For any two critical-line zeros $\rho = \frac{1}{2} + i\gamma_\rho$ and $\rho_k = \frac{1}{2} + i\gamma_k$, writing $u_\rho - u_{\rho_k} = \frac{\rho - \rho_k}{\rho\rho_k} = \frac{i(\gamma_\rho - \gamma_k)}{\rho\rho_k}$ yields the exact conjugate-pair identity
$$
\lvert u_\rho - u_{\rho_k}\rvert\,\lvert u_\rho - u_{\bar\rho_k}\rvert = \frac{\lvert\gamma_\rho^2 - \gamma_k^2\rvert}{\lvert\rho\rvert^2\lvert\rho_k\rvert^2}.
$$
Whenever $\lvert\gamma_\rho\rvert > \gamma_k$, one has $0 < \gamma_\rho^2 - \gamma_k^2 < \gamma_\rho^2 + \frac{1}{4} = \lvert\rho\rvert^2$, so the product is strictly less than $\lvert\rho_k\rvert^{-2}$. Taking the monic degree-$2K$ zero-annihilating polynomial $Q_{2K}(u) := \prod_{k=1}^K (u - u_{\rho_k})(u - u_{\bar\rho_k})$ kills all first $K$ conjugate pairs and satisfies $\lvert Q_{2K}(u_\rho)\rvert^2 < \prod_{k=1}^K \lvert\rho_k\rvert^{-4}$ on every surviving atom ($\lvert\gamma_\rho\rvert > \gamma_K$); integrating against $\mu_{\mathrm{Li}}$ (where each pair $\{\rho_k, \bar\rho_k\}$ carries total mass $2\cdot\frac{m_k}{2\lambda_1\lvert\rho_k\rvert^2} = \frac{m_k}{\lambda_1\lvert\rho_k\rvert^2}$) gives $\pi_{2K} < \tau_K \prod_{k=1}^K \lvert\rho_k\rvert^{-4}$. For odd degree, $Q_{2K+1}(u) := (u - 1)Q_{2K}(u)$ adds the factor $\lvert u_\rho - 1\rvert = \lvert\rho\rvert^{-1} \le \lvert\rho_{K+1}\rvert^{-1}$ on every surviving atom ($\lvert\gamma_\rho\rvert \ge \gamma_{K+1}$), giving the odd-step bound. Taking $P(u) = (u - 1)^N$ (whose coefficient vector has $\lVert x\rVert_2^2 = \sum_{j=0}^N\binom{N}{j}^2 = \binom{2N}{N}$, while $\lvert u_\rho - 1\rvert \le \lvert\rho_1\rvert^{-1}$) yields the binomial Rayleigh bound. Finally, all atoms $u_\rho = e^{i\theta_\rho}$ lie on the arc $\lvert\theta_\rho\rvert \le \theta_1 = 2\arctan\frac{1}{2\gamma_1}$; with $c = \frac{1+\cos\theta_1}{2}$ and $r = \frac{1-\cos\theta_1}{2} = \sin^2\frac{\theta_1}{2} = \frac{1}{4\lvert\rho_1\rvert^2} = \frac{1}{1 + 4\gamma_1^2}$, the monic degree-$2K$ polynomial $P_{2K}^{\mathrm{Cheb}}(u) := 2^K u^K \tilde T_K\!\bigl(\frac{u+u^{-1}}{2}\bigr)$ built from the monic Chebyshev polynomial $\tilde T_K(x) = 2^{1-K}r^K T_K(\frac{x-c}{r})$ on $[\cos\theta_1, 1]$ satisfies $\lvert P_{2K}^{\mathrm{Cheb}}(e^{i\theta})\rvert \le 2r^K = 2(1 + 4\gamma_1^2)^{-K}$ on the whole arc, yielding the Chebyshev bound.

*Item 3*: Since $\gamma_k \to \infty$, item 2 gives $\pi_N^{1/N} \to 0$; since $\lambda_{\max}(T_N) \ge \frac{1}{N}\operatorname{tr}(T_N) = 1$, $\kappa_2(T_N) \ge 1/\lambda_{\min}(T_N) \ge 1/\pi_{N-1}$, hence $\kappa_2(T_N)^{1/N} \to \infty$. Summing $4\ln\tilde\gamma_k$ up to $K = \lfloor N/2\rfloor$ via $\gamma_k \ge \tilde\gamma_k \sim \frac{2\pi k}{\ln k}$ and Stirling's formula gives $\sum_{k=1}^K 4\ln\tilde\gamma_k = 4K\ln K - 4K\ln\ln K + O(K) = 2N\ln N - 2N\ln\ln N + O(N)$. $\square$

::: {.remark #rem-ch34-dirichlet-hb-kernel-positivity title="Dirichlet GRH for Primitive Complex Characters: Strict Hermite–Biehler Reproducing-Kernel Positivity Without Real-Axis Symmetry (Manual derivation, taking [@conj-riemann-hypothesis] as input)"}
**Manual derivation (resting on `PrimitiveDirichletLZerosOnLine` in [@conj-riemann-hypothesis] and the classical functional equation and Hadamard product for primitive Dirichlet $L$-functions; Davenport, *Multiplicative Number Theory*, Ch. 9, 12, 16).** Let $\chi \pmod q$ ($q \ge 2$) be a **primitive** Dirichlet character of parity $\mathfrak{a} \in \{0, 1\}$ ($\chi(-1) = (-1)^{\mathfrak{a}}$), with Gauss sum $\tau(\chi) := \sum_{a=1}^q \chi(a)e^{2\pi i a/q}$, unit root number $\varepsilon(\chi) := \frac{\tau(\chi)}{i^{\mathfrak{a}}\sqrt{q}}$ ($\lvert\varepsilon(\chi)\rvert = 1$), and completed entire $L$-function

$$
\xi(s, \chi) := \left(\frac{q}{\pi}\right)^{(s+\mathfrak{a})/2}\Gamma\!\left(\frac{s+\mathfrak{a}}{2}\right)L(s, \chi).
$$

For a **complex primitive character** $\chi \neq \bar\chi$, real-axis symmetry fails ($\xi(\bar s,\chi) \neq \overline{\xi(s,\chi)}$) and critical-line zeros are asymmetric between upper and lower half-planes. Yet rotating by half the root-number phase $e^{2i\theta_\chi} = \varepsilon(\chi)$ ($\theta_\chi \in \mathbb{R}$) to form $\widetilde{\xi}_\chi(s) := e^{-i\theta_\chi}\xi(s, \chi)$ preserves the entire holomorphic Hermite–Biehler architecture verbatim:

1. **Cross-line reflection–conjugation symmetry and $\epsilon^2$-free two-shell identity**: Combining $\xi(s, \chi) = \varepsilon(\chi)\,\xi(1 - s, \bar\chi)$ with $\overline{\xi(\bar s, \chi)} = \xi(s, \bar\chi)$ yields $\widetilde{\xi}_\chi(1 - \bar s) = \overline{\widetilde{\xi}_\chi(s)}$ (so $\widetilde{\xi}_\chi$ is real on $\operatorname{Re} s = \frac{1}{2}$) and $\widetilde{\xi}'_\chi(1 - \bar s) = -\overline{\widetilde{\xi}'_\chi(s)}$. For any $\epsilon > 0$, setting $E_{\epsilon,\chi}(s) := \widetilde{\xi}_\chi(s) + \frac{\epsilon}{2}\widetilde{\xi}'_\chi(s)$ and the **holomorphic Hermite–Biehler involution** $E_{\epsilon,\chi}^\#(s) := \overline{E_{\epsilon,\chi}(1 - \bar s)} = \widetilde{\xi}_\chi(s) - \frac{\epsilon}{2}\widetilde{\xi}'_\chi(s)$ (note $E_{\epsilon,\chi}^\#(s) \neq E_{\epsilon,\chi}(1 - s)$ when $\chi \neq \bar\chi$, so the $1 - \bar s$ involution is essential!) cancels both the phase $e^{-i\theta_\chi}$ and the step size $\epsilon$, giving the phase-invariant entire reproducing kernel
   $$
   K_\chi(w, s) := \frac{E_{\epsilon,\chi}(s)\overline{E_{\epsilon,\chi}(w)} - E_{\epsilon,\chi}^\#(s)\overline{E_{\epsilon,\chi}^\#(w)}}{2\epsilon\bigl[(s - \frac{1}{2}) + (\bar w - \frac{1}{2})\bigr]} = \frac{\xi'(s,\chi)\overline{\xi(w,\chi)} + \xi(s,\chi)\overline{\xi'(w,\chi)}}{2\bigl[(s - \frac{1}{2}) + (\bar w - \frac{1}{2})\bigr]},
   $$
   with exact diagonal identity $\Delta^{\mathrm{HB}}_{\epsilon,\chi}(u, t) := \lvert E_{\epsilon,\chi}(\frac{1}{2}+u+it)\rvert^2 - \lvert E_{\epsilon,\chi}(\frac{1}{2}-u+it)\rvert^2 \equiv \epsilon\frac{\partial}{\partial u}\lvert\xi(\frac{1}{2}+u+it,\chi)\rvert^2 \equiv 4u\epsilon K_\chi(s, s)$.
2. **Rank-one Hadamard expansion and strict positive definiteness on finite distinct node sets**: Under `PrimitiveDirichletLZerosOnLine`, for every primitive $\chi \pmod q$ ($q \ge 2$), $K_\chi$ admits the globally absolutely convergent rank-one expansion on $\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}$:
   $$
   K_\chi(w, s) = \frac{1}{2}\sum_{\rho\text{ distinct}} m_\rho \left(\frac{\xi(s, \chi)}{s - \rho}\right)\overline{\left(\frac{\xi(w, \chi)}{w - \rho}\right)}.
   $$
   Consequently, for every finite set of distinct nodes $s_1, \dots, s_N \in \Omega_+ = \{\operatorname{Re} s > \frac{1}{2}\}$, the Gram matrix $[K_\chi(s_a, s_b)]_{1 \le a,b \le N}$ is **strictly positive definite** (every leading principal minor and every Hermitian block Schur complement is strictly positive), and $\frac{\partial}{\partial u}\lvert\xi(\frac{1}{2}+u+it,\chi)\rvert^2 = 4u K_\chi(s,s) > 0$ at all heights $t \in \mathbb{R}$. (The identical proof upgrades $K_\epsilon \succeq 0$ for $\xi(s)$ itself to strict positive definiteness $[K_\epsilon(s_a, s_b)] \succ 0$ on every finite distinct node set in $\Omega_+$.)
3. **Sharp boundary for imprimitive characters**: If $\chi \pmod q$ is induced by a primitive character $\chi^* \pmod{q^*}$ ($q^* \mid q$, $q^* < q$), then $L(s, \chi) = L(s, \chi^*)\prod_{p \mid q,\, p \nmid q^*}(1 - \chi^*(p)p^{-s})$. Whenever $q$ has at least one prime divisor $p \nmid q^*$ (so the Euler-factor product is nonempty), $L(s, \chi)$ acquires infinitely many extra zeros on the imaginary axis $\operatorname{Re} s = 0$ and breaks functional symmetry across $\operatorname{Re} s = \frac{1}{2}$ (whereas if every prime divisor of $q$ already divides $q^*$, e.g. $q^* = 3,\; q = 9$, the product is empty and $L(s, \chi) \equiv L(s, \chi^*)$ coincides with the primitive $L$-function)—explaining why `the-omega-institute/riemann-hypothesis` scopes `DirichletLZerosOnLine` to $0 < \operatorname{Re} s < 1$ and why the completed Hermite–Biehler kernel naturally belongs to the primitive completion $\xi(s, \chi^*)$.

**Proof.** Item 1 expands the numerator exactly as in §31.3.2–§31.3.3, using $\widetilde{\xi}_\chi(1 - \bar w) = \overline{\widetilde{\xi}_\chi(w)}$ and $\widetilde{\xi}'_\chi(1 - \bar w) = -\overline{\widetilde{\xi}'_\chi(w)}$ to verify removability along $s = 1 - \bar w$. For item 2, Hadamard factorization (Davenport, Ch. 12) gives $\frac{\xi'(s,\chi)}{\xi(s,\chi)} = B(\chi) + \sum_{\rho\text{ distinct}} m_\rho\bigl(\frac{1}{s-\rho} + \frac{1}{\rho}\bigr)$, where every summand $\frac{1}{s-\rho} + \frac{1}{\rho} = \frac{s}{\rho(s-\rho)}$ is absolutely summable. From $\widetilde{\xi}_\chi(1 - \bar s) = \overline{\widetilde{\xi}_\chi(s)}$ one gets $\operatorname{Re}\frac{\xi'(\frac{1}{2}+it,\chi)}{\xi(\frac{1}{2}+it,\chi)} = 0$ at every non-zero critical-line point; under `PrimitiveDirichletLZerosOnLine`, each $\rho = \frac{1}{2} + i\gamma_\rho$ satisfies $\operatorname{Re}\frac{1}{\frac{1}{2}+it - \rho} = 0$, hence $\operatorname{Re} B(\chi) = -\sum_\rho m_\rho\operatorname{Re}(\frac{1}{\rho}) = -\sum_\rho \frac{m_\rho}{2\lvert\rho\rvert^2}$ (absolutely convergent). Therefore $B(\chi) + \overline{B(\chi)} = -\sum_\rho m_\rho(\frac{1}{\rho} + \frac{1}{\bar\rho})$ cancels all constant terms (including $\operatorname{Im} B(\chi)$!) from $\frac{\xi'(s,\chi)}{\xi(s,\chi)} + \overline{\frac{\xi'(w,\chi)}{\xi(w,\chi)}}$, leaving $\bigl[(s-\frac{1}{2})+(\bar w-\frac{1}{2})\bigr]\sum_\rho\frac{m_\rho}{(s-\rho)\overline{(w-\rho)}}$, and multiplying by $\frac{1}{2}\xi(s,\chi)\overline{\xi(w,\chi)}$ gives the rank-one series. For strict positive definiteness: if $\sum_{a,b=1}^N \overline{c_a} c_b K_\chi(s_a, s_b) = 0$, the rational function $R(z) := \sum_{b=1}^N \frac{c_b\xi(s_b,\chi)}{s_b - z}$ vanishes at all infinitely many distinct zeros of $\xi(s,\chi)$ (Davenport, Ch. 16), forcing $R(z) \equiv 0$; taking residues at the distinct poles $s_b \in \Omega_+$ yields $c_b\xi(s_b,\chi) = 0$, and since $\xi(s_b,\chi) \neq 0$ on $\Omega_+$ by `PrimitiveDirichletLZerosOnLine`, $c_b = 0$ for all $b$. $\square$

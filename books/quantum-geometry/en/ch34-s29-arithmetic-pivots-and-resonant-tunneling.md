## 31.2.1 The Arithmetic Sorting Barrier, the Turán–Zeno Spurious-Zero Trap ($\zeta_{19}$ Beyond $\sigma = 1$), and the $0.046191/\ln M$ Logarithmic Wall {#sec-ch34-29-1}

Return to the infinite-dimensional orthogonal comparison cone $\bigoplus_{p \in \mathbb{P}} \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}\,\mathbf{e}_p$ spanned by the primes. By the Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic, the prime logarithmic step lengths $\{\ln 2, \ln 3, \ln 5, \ln 7, \dots\}$ are linearly independent over $\mathbb{Q}$: each prime $p$ acts as an irreducible minimal circular-arc trihedron $\Delta\mathcal{T}_p$ of intrinsic action step $L_p = \ln p$, while each positive integer $n = \prod_i p_i^{a_i}$ encodes a unique composite path $\Gamma_n$ of additive total action

$$
S[\Gamma_n] = \ln n = \sum_i a_i \ln p_i.
$$

Now inspect the two workhorse finite approximations of classical analytic number theory: the truncated Dirichlet partial sum $\zeta_M(s) := \sum_{n=1}^M n^{-s}$, and the sequential Nyman–Beurling–Báez-Duarte (NBB) Gram matrix $G_M$ built in natural-number order $k = 1, 2, \dots, M$. Translated into the algorithmic language of [§30.1](ch34-s26-chord-tunneling-and-pivot-renormalization.md#sec-ch34-chord-tunneling-and-pivot-renormalization), both procedures behave just like **classical Dijkstra global total-order sorting** run on the prime multiplicative graph: regardless of how wildly two paths differ in prime support, exterior grade, or winding multiplicity, they are forced into a single one-dimensional queue $1 < 2 < 3 < \dots < M$ by real action magnitude $\ln n$ and guillotined by a sharp step gate $\mathbf{1}_{\ln n \le \ln M}$ at $\ln M$. Geometrically and spectrally, this seemingly innocent total ordering triggers two immediate disasters:

1. **Hard truncation acts as a midway Zeno measurement, breeding "Turán spurious zeros" inside the Euler safety zone $\operatorname{Re}(s) > 1$**:
   In 1948, Pál Turán noticed a tantalizing route toward the Riemann Hypothesis: since the full zeta function $\zeta(s)$ is strictly zero-free on $\sigma = \operatorname{Re}(s) > 1$ by absolute convergence of the Euler product ([@thm-riemann-zeta-euler-product]), might its finite partial sums $\zeta_M(s) = \sum_{n=1}^M n^{-s}$ likewise remain zero-free on $\sigma > 1$? Turán proved (literature result) that if $\zeta_M(s) \neq 0$ on $\sigma > 1$ held for all sufficiently large $M$ (even up to a mild decaying shift), the Riemann Hypothesis [@conj-riemann-hypothesis] would follow immediately! History soon delivered a dramatic reversal. Using Ingham's method together with numerical computation of $\zeta$ zeros, Haselgrove (1958) disproved the closely allied Pólya and Turán sign conjectures for Liouville sums; Montgomery (1983), together with Montgomery and Vaughan, then established the sharp asymptotic law for the maximal zero abscissa $\psi_M := \sup\{\operatorname{Re}(s) : \zeta_M(s) = 0\}$ at large $M$ (literature result):

   $$
   \psi_M = 1 + \left(\frac{4}{\pi} - 1 - o(1)\right)\frac{\ln\ln M}{\ln M}, \qquad \frac{4}{\pi} - 1 \approx 0.2732395447,
   $$

   with the constant $\frac{4}{\pi} - 1$ best possible! Building on earlier bounds of Spira (1966, 1968), Monach (1980), and van de Lune & te Riele, Platt and Trudgian (2016, Theorem 1.1) closed every remaining finite gap via rigorous interval arithmetic (literature result): **the ordinary Dirichlet partial sum $\zeta_M(s)$ is zero-free in $\operatorname{Re}(s) > 1$ if and only if $M \in \{1, \dots, 18, 20, 21, 28\}$; for every other positive integer $M$, $\zeta_M(s)$ possesses infinitely many zeros inside $\operatorname{Re}(s) > 1$!** In particular, $M = 19$ marks the very first breakdown threshold in natural-number order: near $\sigma \approx 1.0069878449,\; t \approx 155417750480610.1069058344$, a 100-digit interval Rouché certificate encloses a genuine simple zero strictly to the right of $\sigma = 1$ (see [@ex-ch34-turan-schur-and-cayley-calculus] below).

   Through the lens of minimal arc-trihedra and the $90^\circ$ blindspot analysis of [§30.2](ch34-s27-imaginary-chord-tunneling-and-temporal-double-slit.md#sec-ch34-imaginary-chord-tunneling-and-temporal-double-slit), the origin of Turán's spurious zeros becomes transparent. Look at three consecutive integers in the one-dimensional queue: $1021$ opens a brand-new prime axis $\mathbf{e}_{1021}$ (a primitive rank-1 arc), $1023 = 3 \times 11 \times 31$ is a square-free fermionic exterior 3-blade $\mathbf{e}_3 \wedge \mathbf{e}_{11} \wedge \mathbf{e}_{31}$ carrying Möbius chirality $\mu(1023) = -1$, and $1024 = 2^{10}$ winds ten consecutive times around the single prime $\mathbf{e}_2$ ($\mu(1024) = 0$). Topologically, these three paths live in completely unrelated sectors of the multiplicative tree—yet one-dimensional size sorting jams them shoulder to shoulder and drops a hard blade across $\ln n \le \ln M$! Under Mellin–Fourier duality, the sharp indicator $\mathbf{1}_{[0, \ln M]}(u)$ transforms into an oscillating sign-indefinite Dirichlet–$\mathrm{sinc}$ kernel $\frac{1 - M^{1-s}}{s - 1}$ rather than a positive-semidefinite self-convolution square. That sharp cutoff is literally a **midway Zeno projective measurement**: it slices open unclosed minimal arc-trihedra before their downstream Möbius partners can cancel, and at gigantic heights $t$ where prime phases align almost-periodically, its Gibbs edge ringing splashes magenta spurious zeros straight across the $\sigma = 1$ border into the Euler safety zone!

2. **The exponential "sorting tax" and the $0.0461914179/\ln M$ logarithmic wall**:
   Even when one moves to manifestly positive-semidefinite Hilbert-space approximations—such as the criterion established by Nyman (1950) and Beurling (1955) and discretized by Báez-Duarte (2003), whereby RH holds if and only if the indicator $\chi_{(0,1]} \in L^2(0,\infty)$ lies in the closed span of the natural-number fractional-part dilations $\{u \mapsto \{1/(ku)\}\}_{k=1}^M$, i.e., $d_M^2 = 1 - \mathbf{c}_M^\top G_M^{-1}\mathbf{c}_M \to 0$—expanding the Gram matrix $G_M$ sequentially along $k = 1, 2, \dots, M$ still pays an exponential sorting tax. Because intrinsic geodesic action depth is $L = \ln M$, advancing action depth by a mere $L$ units along a 1D integer queue inflates the matrix dimension exponentially to **$M = e^L$ rows**! As proved unconditionally by Báez-Duarte, Balazard, Landreau & Saias (2000) for distinct critical-line zeros and sharpened unconditionally to squared multiplicities by Burnol (2002) (literature results), in the sequential natural-number basis the Gram Schur-complement squared residual $d_M^2 = 1 - \mathbf{c}_M^\top G_M^{-1}\mathbf{c}_M$ obeys the strict logarithmic lower bound

   $$
   \liminf_{M \to \infty} (\ln M)\,d_M^2 \;\ge\; \sum_{\operatorname{Re}(\rho) = 1/2} \frac{m_\rho^2}{\lvert\rho\rvert^2} \;>\; 0,
   $$

   summed over all distinct critical-line zeros $\rho$ of $\zeta(s)$ with multiplicity $m_\rho \ge 1$; combining the externally verified [@conj-riemann-hypothesis] with Báez-Duarte's discrete criterion gives $d_M^2 \to 0$ unconditionally and makes this sum equal (if all zeros are simple) or strictly greater than (if any zero has $m_\rho > 1$) the benchmark constant $C_{\mathrm{NBB}} := 2 + \gamma - \ln(4\pi) \approx 0.0461914179$ (where $\gamma \approx 0.5772156649$ is the Euler–Mascheroni constant, while simplicity of all nontrivial zeros remains open), trapping $d_M^2$ above $\frac{C_{\mathrm{NBB}} + o(1)}{\ln M}$ in the sequential basis. Flooded with nearly collinear composite branches inside the finite Gram Window $\mathcal{W}_M$, the condition number of $G_M$ deteriorates rapidly (see [@rem-ch34-nbb-gram-spectral-and-pruning] for the unconditional two-sided spectral bounds and $\kappa_2(G^{\mathrm{NB}}_M) = M^{2+o(1)}$ on the genuine NBB matrix) while approximation error cannot decay faster than the $1/\ln M$ logarithmic lower bound.

1. **Exact tridiagonal precision matrix and Markov separation theorem for $K^{\mathrm{Lap}}$ (manual derivation)**: Let $0 < x_1 < x_2 < \dots < x_m$ be any strictly increasing positive reals ($m \ge 2$), put $r_i := x_i / x_{i+1} \in (0, 1)$ ($1 \le i \le m - 1$), and define $K^{\mathrm{Lap}} \in \mathbb{R}^{m\times m}$ by $K^{\mathrm{Lap}}_{ab} := \frac{\min(x_a, x_b)}{\max(x_a, x_b)} = e^{-\lvert\ln(x_a / x_b)\rvert}$.    $$
   y^\top \bigl(K^{\mathrm{Lap}}\bigr)^{-1} y \;=\; y_1^2 + \sum_{i=1}^{m-1} \frac{(y_{i+1} - r_i y_i)^2}{1 - r_i^2}.
   $$
   In particular, $\Omega$ is **strictly tridiagonal**: $\Omega_{ab} = 0$ for all $\lvert a - b\rvert \ge 2$, with off-diagonal entries $\Omega_{i, i+1} = \Omega_{i+1, i} = -\frac{r_i}{1 - r_i^2}$ and diagonal entries $\Omega_{1,1} = \frac{1}{1 - r_1^2}$, $\Omega_{i,i} = \frac{1 - r_{i-1}^2 r_i^2}{(1 - r_{i-1}^2)(1 - r_i^2)}$ ($2 \le i \le m - 1$), and $\Omega_{m,m} = \frac{1}{1 - r_{m-1}^2}$.

   $$
   S_{AB} \;\equiv\; 0.
   $$

   **Proof.** Define the unit lower-bidiagonal matrix $B \in \mathbb{R}^{m\times m}$ by $(By)_1 = y_1$ and $(By)_{i+1} = y_{i+1} - r_i y_i$ ($1 \le i \le m - 1$), and set $D := \operatorname{diag}\bigl(1, 1 - r_1^2, \dots, 1 - r_{m-1}^2\bigr) \succ 0$. Direct multiplication gives $(B K^{\mathrm{Lap}})_{i+1, j} = \frac{x_j}{x_{i+1}} - \frac{x_i}{x_{i+1}}\frac{x_j}{x_i} = 0$ for $j \le i$, and $(B K^{\mathrm{Lap}})_{i+1, j} = (1 - r_i^2)\frac{x_{i+1}}{x_j}$ for $j \ge i + 1$. Multiplying on the right by $B^\top$ then gives $(B K^{\mathrm{Lap}} B^\top)_{a, k+1} = (B K^{\mathrm{Lap}})_{a, k+1} - r_k (B K^{\mathrm{Lap}})_{a, k} = 0$ for every $a < k + 1$, with diagonal entries $1$ and $1 - r_k^2$. Thus $B K^{\mathrm{Lap}} B^\top = D$; since $\det B = 1$, we obtain $\det K^{\mathrm{Lap}} = \det D = \prod_{i=1}^{m-1}(1 - r_i^2) > 0$ and $\Omega = (K^{\mathrm{Lap}})^{-1} = B^\top D^{-1} B$, and expanding $(By)^\top D^{-1}(By)$ yields the quadratic form and tridiagonal entries. 3. **Exact coprime-branch decoupling via the prime-factored Besicovitch–Euler tensor-product kernel and its twin critical-line wall (manual derivation $+$ literature, independent of RH)**: The canonical arithmetic kernel escaping the real-log-continuous obstruction of item 1 arises from unweighted vertical-line averaging on $\operatorname{Re}(s) = \sigma > \frac{1}{2}$. For every fixed $\sigma > \frac{1}{2}$ and fixed integers $m, n \ge 1$, unconditionally

   $$
   G^{\mathrm{AP}}(\sigma)_{mn} \;:=\; \lim_{T \to \infty} \frac{1}{T}\int_T^{2T} m^{-\sigma - it} n^{-\sigma + it}\lvert\zeta(\sigma + it)\rvert^2\,dt \;=\; \zeta(2\sigma)\,\operatorname{lcm}(m, n)^{-2\sigma}.
   $$

   Its normalized correlation entries factor prime by prime (making the matrix on any finite positive-integer set $V$ a principal submatrix of a prime-coordinate Kronecker product):

   $$
   H^{\mathrm{AP}}(\sigma)_{mn} \;:=\; \frac{G^{\mathrm{AP}}(\sigma)_{mn}}{\sqrt{G^{\mathrm{AP}}(\sigma)_{mm} G^{\mathrm{AP}}(\sigma)_{nn}}} \;=\; \left(\frac{\gcd(m, n)}{\operatorname{lcm}(m, n)}\right)^{\sigma} \;=\; \prod_{p \in \mathbb{P}} p^{-\sigma\lvert v_p(m) - v_p(n)\rvert}.
   $$

   **Key structural distinction**: Writing the reduced fraction $m/n = p_0/q_0$ ($\gcd(p_0, q_0) = 1$) shows $H^{\mathrm{AP}}(\sigma)_{mn} = (p_0 q_0)^{-\sigma}$ satisfies $H^{\mathrm{AP}}(\sigma)_{km, kn} = H^{\mathrm{AP}}(\sigma)_{mn}$ for all $k \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}$ (so $H^{\mathrm{AP}}(\sigma)_{mn}$ depends only on the rational ratio $m/n \in \mathbb{Q}_{>0}$ and extends canonically to a **$\mathbb{Q}_{>0}$-dilation-invariant kernel**, with $G^{\mathrm{AP}}(\sigma)$ differing only by diagonal weights $\zeta(2\sigma)(mn)^{-\sigma}$); it escapes item 1 not by breaking multiplicative scaling, but by **breaking real-log continuity at $1$**—as $n \to \infty$, $\frac{n+1}{n} \to 1$ while $H^{\mathrm{AP}}(\sigma)_{n+1, n} = (n(n+1))^{-\sigma} \to 0$, so it cannot arise from any fixed-profile $L^2(0, \infty)$ self-convolution kernel.

   **Proof (unconditional mean limit and exact coprime decoupling).** On $t \in [T, 2T]$ set $x = T$ (so $2T < \pi T = \frac{2\pi}{2} T$, strictly satisfying $C = 2 > 1$); by E. C. Titchmarsh (1986, Theorem 4.11), $\zeta(\sigma + it) = S_0(t) + E(t)$ uniformly with $S_0(t) := \sum_{k \le T} k^{-\sigma - it}$ and $\lvert E(t)\rvert = O_\sigma(T^{-\sigma})$ (the pole term satisfies $\lvert\sigma + it - 1\rvert \ge T$). Expanding $S_1(t)\overline{S_2(t)}$ ($S_1(t) := m^{-\sigma-it}S_0(t) = \sum_{r \le mT,\, m\mid r} r^{-\sigma-it}$, $S_2(t) := n^{-\sigma-it}S_0(t)$), the diagonal $r = s$ (equivalently $\operatorname{lcm}(m, n) \mid r$) yields $\operatorname{lcm}(m, n)^{-2\sigma}\zeta(2\sigma) + O_{m,n,\sigma}(T^{1-2\sigma})$; for off-diagonal terms $1 \le r < s \le X := \max(m, n)T$, $-\ln(1 - u) \ge u$ ($u \in (0, 1)$) gives $\bigl\lvert\int_T^{2T}(r/s)^{-it}\,dt\bigr\rvert \le \frac{2}{\ln(s/r)} \le \frac{2s}{s - r}$, and splitting $r \le s/2$ vs. $s/2 < r < s$ bounds the averaged off-diagonal sum by $O_{m,n,\sigma}(T^{1-2\sigma}\ln T)$ ($\frac{1}{2} < \sigma < 1$), $O_{m,n}(T^{-1}\ln^2 T)$ ($\sigma = 1$), and $O_{m,n,\sigma}(T^{-1})$ ($\sigma > 1$), all vanishing as $T \to \infty$; hence $\frac{1}{T}\int_T^{2T}\lvert S_0(t)\rvert^2\,dt = O_\sigma(1)$ and Cauchy–Schwarz bounds the $E(t)$ cross-terms by $O_{m,n,\sigma}(T^{-\sigma}) \to 0$. Moreover, for any finite $V \subset \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}$ and any $\sigma > 0$, $H^{\mathrm{AP}}(\sigma)_V$ is a principal submatrix of $\bigotimes_{p \mid \prod V} H^{(p)}(\sigma)$, whose factors $H^{(p)}(\sigma)_{j,k} = p^{-\sigma\lvert j - k\rvert}$ are strictly positive definite with tridiagonal inverse by item 1 of [@rem-ch34-laplace-tridiagonal-vs-nbb-separator] ($x_j = p^{\sigma j}$), so $H^{\mathrm{AP}}(\sigma)_V \succ 0$. For pairwise disjoint prime sets $P_1, P_2, P_3 \subset \mathbb{P}$ (allowing $P_1 = \emptyset$), any nonempty finite $P_1$-smooth root set $I_0$, $A$ consisting of integers $m_1 m_2$ ($m_1 \in I_0$, $m_2 > 1$ $P_2$-smooth), and $B$ consisting of integers $n_1 n_3$ ($n_1 \in I_0$, $n_3 > 1$ $P_3$-smooth), one has $H^{\mathrm{AP}}_{m,k} = H^{\mathrm{AP}}_{m_1,k} m_2^{-\sigma}$, $H^{\mathrm{AP}}_{k,n} = H^{\mathrm{AP}}_{k,n_1} n_3^{-\sigma}$, and $H^{\mathrm{AP}}_{m,n} = H^{\mathrm{AP}}_{m_1,n_1} m_2^{-\sigma} n_3^{-\sigma}$ for all $m \in A, n \in B, k \in I_0$; putting $H_0 := H^{\mathrm{AP}}(\sigma)_{I_0, I_0}$ yields $H_{m, I_0} H_0^{-1} H_{I_0, n} = (H_0)_{m_1, n_1} m_2^{-\sigma} n_3^{-\sigma} = H_{m, n}$, hence **identically zero cross-block Schur complement** $S^{\mathrm{AP}}(\sigma)_{AB} \equiv 0$, which by the stagewise Schur quotient identity remains $S^{\mathrm{AP}}(\sigma)_{(A\setminus I_A),(B\setminus I_B)} \equiv 0$ after eliminating any additional branch-internal subsets $I_A \subset A$, $I_B \subset B$ inside $A$ and $B$. $\square$

   Yet this prime tensor product hits a twin wall at the critical line: first, the unnormalized squared norm $G^{\mathrm{AP}}(\sigma)_{1,1} = \zeta(2\sigma) = \frac{1}{2\sigma - 1} + \gamma + O(\sigma - \frac{1}{2}) \to +\infty$ as $\sigma \to \frac{1}{2}^+$, and although the normalized tensor kernel $H^{\mathrm{AP}}(\frac{1}{2})$ remains well-defined at $\sigma = \frac{1}{2}$, the unnormalized vertical mean square diverges as $\ln T$ by the Hardy–Littlewood critical-line mean-square asymptotic theorem (Titchmarsh 1986, Theorem 7.3); conversely, inserting any nonnegative weight $w_0(t)$ on $\sigma = \frac{1}{2}$ making the weighted spectral density $w_0(t)\lvert\zeta(\frac{1}{2} + it)\rvert^2 \in L^1(\mathbb{R})$ nonzero and integrable collapses the kernel back into a real-log-continuous kernel trapped by item 1; second, on $\sigma \in (\frac{1}{2}, 1)$ the kernels $G^{\mathrm{AP}}(\sigma)$ and $H^{\mathrm{AP}}(\sigma)$ realize the unconditional independent-phase average of the random Euler product on the Bohr torus $\mathbb{T}^\infty$, whereas at $\sigma = \frac{1}{2}$ (where the unnormalized mean square diverges) the normalized kernel $H^{\mathrm{AP}}(\frac{1}{2})$ is the $\sigma \to \frac{1}{2}^+$ limit of those normalized correlations (equivalently the character inner product under the product of local Poisson probability measures on $\mathbb{T}^\infty$ with Fourier coefficients $p^{-\lvert j - k\rvert/2}$); the positivity and exact coprime Schur decoupling of both hold unconditionally (proved without RH), while the normalized kernels $H^{\mathrm{AP}}(\sigma)$ and $H^{\mathrm{AP}}(\frac{1}{2})$ do not involve $\zeta$ at all—so neither positivity nor Schur decoupling here can distinguish whether off-line $\zeta$ zeros exist in the critical strip.

1. **Universal spectral escape, intra-branch trivialization, and constrained Sturm–Liouville floor for scale-dependent shrinking wavefronts (manual derivation, independent of RH)**: Using the notation of [@rem-ch34-dilation-wavefront-nogo-and-euler-tensor] (item 1), for any nonzero profile sequence $f_\ell \in L^2(0, \infty)$ set $\phi_\ell(u) := e^{u/2} f_\ell(e^u) \in L^2(\mathbb{R})$, normalized Mellin spectral probability measure $d\nu_\ell(t) := \frac{\lvert\widehat{\phi_\ell}(-t)\rvert^2\,dt}{2\pi\lVert\phi_\ell\rVert_{L^2}^2}$, normalized correlation $K_\ell(u) := \int_{-\infty}^\infty e^{itu}\,d\nu_\ell(t)$, and uncentered second spectral moment $\sigma_\ell^2 := \int_{-\infty}^\infty t^2\,d\nu_\ell(t) = \frac{\lVert\phi_\ell'\rVert_{L^2}^2}{\lVert\phi_\ell\rVert_{L^2}^2} \in (0, \infty]$ (where $\phi_\ell'$ is the weak derivative, and when $\phi_\ell \notin H^1(\mathbb{R})$ the squared moment $\sigma_\ell^2$ equals $+\infty$).

   - **(1A) Universal spectral-mass escape theorem (no compact support, finite second moment, or fixed-shape rescaling assumed)**: On any stage $\ell$ admitting a surviving coprime pair $(a_\ell, b_\ell) \in A_\ell \times B_\ell$ with log gap $\Delta_\ell := \lvert\ln(a_\ell / b_\ell)\rvert > 0$, Schur survival $\sqrt{\eta_{a_\ell}^{(\ell)} \eta_{b_\ell}^{(\ell)}} \ge \eta_0 > 0$, and suppressed cross-block correlation $\cos\theta_{\min}(A_\ell, B_\ell \mid I_\ell) \le \theta < 1$, putting $\varepsilon_\ell := (1 - \theta)\sqrt{\eta_{a_\ell}^{(\ell)} \eta_{b_\ell}^{(\ell)}} \ge \varepsilon_0 := (1 - \theta)\eta_0 > 0$, [@rem-ch34-dilation-wavefront-nogo-and-euler-tensor] (item 1, (1A)) yields $1 - \operatorname{Re} K_\ell(\Delta_\ell) \ge \varepsilon_\ell$; for any frequency cutoff $T > 0$, bounding $1 - \cos(t\Delta_\ell)$ on $\lvert t\rvert \le T$ and on $\lvert t\rvert > T$ by $\frac{1}{2} T^2 \Delta_\ell^2$ and $2$, respectively, gives $1 - \operatorname{Re} K_\ell(\Delta_\ell) \le \frac{1}{2} T^2 \Delta_\ell^2 + 2\,\nu_\ell(\{\lvert t\rvert > T\})$, and choosing $T = \sqrt{\varepsilon_\ell} / \Delta_\ell$ forces unconditionally in the extended nonnegative reals $[0, \infty]$ (when $\sigma_\ell$ is finite, the second inequality follows directly from $1 - \cos y \le \frac{1}{2}y^2$)

     $$
     \nu_\ell\!\left(\left\{\lvert t\rvert > \frac{\sqrt{\varepsilon_\ell}}{\Delta_\ell}\right\}\right) \;\ge\; \frac{\varepsilon_\ell}{4} \;\ge\; \frac{\varepsilon_0}{4} \;>\; 0, \qquad
     \sigma_\ell \;\ge\; \frac{\sqrt{2\varepsilon_\ell}}{\Delta_\ell}.
     $$

     In particular, consider positive-exponent two-prime power towers $A_\ell = \{p^a \le X_\ell : a \ge 1\}\setminus I_\ell$ and $B_\ell = \{q^b \le X_\ell : b \ge 1\}\setminus I_\ell$ ($p > q$, $X_\ell = e^{L_\ell}$, setting $\alpha := \ln p / \ln q > 1$): when $I_\ell = \{1\}$ and $N := \lfloor L_\ell / \ln p\rfloor \ge 1$, a surviving pair satisfies $\Delta_\ell \le \frac{\ln q}{N}$—for $N = 1$ take $a = 1,\; b = \lfloor\alpha\rfloor \ge 1$; for $N \ge 2$, Dirichlet's approximation theorem gives $1 \le a \le N$ and $b \ge 1$ with $\lvert a\alpha - b\rvert \le \frac{1}{N}$, where $a < N$ implies $b \le a\alpha + \frac{1}{N} < N\alpha$ and hence $q^b < p^N \le X_\ell$, whereas an offending $a = N$ with $q^b > X_\ell$ has $e := b - N\alpha \in (0, \frac{1}{N}]$, so if $\gcd(N, b) > 1$ then dividing by $\gcd(N, b)$ immediately brings the pair below the cutoff, and otherwise in the coprime case choosing $1 \le j < N$ with $jb \equiv 1 \pmod N$ and setting $b' := (jb - 1)/N \ge 1$ yields $0 < j\alpha - b' = (1 - je)/N \le \frac{1}{N}$ and $b' < j\alpha < N\alpha$ (so $p^j, q^{b'} < X_\ell$); more generally, for any bounded separator $\lvert I_\ell\rvert \le K$ and $N' := \lfloor L_\ell / ((K + 1)\ln p)\rfloor - 1 \ge 1$, choosing $1 \le a_0 \le N'$ and $b_0 \ge 1$ with $\lvert a_0\alpha - b_0\rvert \le \frac{1}{N'}$ gives $b_0 \le N'\alpha + \frac{1}{N'} < (N' + 1)\alpha$, so all $K + 1$ positive multiples $(p^{k a_0}, q^{k b_0})$ ($1 \le k \le K + 1$) fit below the cutoff $X_\ell$ with pairwise disjoint coordinates, and since each separator node in $I_\ell$ removes at most one candidate pair, at least one pair survives in $A_\ell \times B_\ell$ with $\Delta_\ell \le \frac{(K + 1)\ln q}{N'} = O_{p,q,K}(L_\ell^{-1})$. Consequently, along any unbounded subsequence with $X_\ell \to \infty$ on which these selected near-collision pairs simultaneously maintain both a uniform survival lower bound $\sqrt{\eta_{a_\ell}^{(\ell)} \eta_{b_\ell}^{(\ell)}} \ge \eta_0 > 0$ and a uniform correlation cap $\cos\theta_{\min} \le \theta < 1$, there exists a fixed constant $c = c(p, q, K, \theta, \eta_0) > 0$ such that the $L^2(0, \infty)$ dilation wavefront sequence satisfies $\nu_\ell(\{\lvert t\rvert > c\ln X_\ell\}) \ge \frac{\varepsilon_0}{4}$ on that subsequence (the spectral family $\{\nu_\ell\}$ is not tight).

   - **(1B) Intra-branch trivialization preceding cross-branch unlock and exact double-pole-cancelling shrinking Schwartz family**: On those two-prime towers, every distinct intra-branch pair satisfies $\Delta_{\min}^{\mathrm{intra}} \ge \ln q$ (with equality precisely when the surviving $q$-tower contains consecutive exponents, and $+\infty$ when no distinct pair exists), whereas for $I_\ell = \{1\}$ (or bounded separators as above) the cross-branch minimum gap shrinks as $\Delta_{\min}^{\mathrm{cross}} = O(L_\ell^{-1}) \to 0$. For any compactly supported kernel $\operatorname{supp}(K_\ell) \subset [-R_\ell, R_\ell]$, as soon as $R_\ell < \ln q$ the unnormalized intra-branch blocks $G^{(\ell)}_{A_\ell A_\ell}$ and $G^{(\ell)}_{B_\ell B_\ell}$ collapse to **strictly diagonal matrices**; setting the full index set $V_\ell := I_\ell \sqcup A_\ell \sqcup B_\ell$, when $I_\ell = \{1\}$ (where the root gap $\min_{n \in A_\ell \cup B_\ell}\ln n \ge \ln q$ coincides with the intra-branch lower bound) and $R_\ell < \min(\ln q, \Delta_{\min}^{\mathrm{cross}})$, the entire matrix $G^{(\ell)}_{V_\ell}$ diagonalizes. For any rescaled smooth family $K_{R_\ell}(u)$ (complex correlations allowed, since $K_R(-v) = \overline{K_R(v)}$ equalizes row and column sums), the **normalized** intra-branch blocks $H_{A_\ell A_\ell}, H_{B_\ell B_\ell}$ satisfy $\max\bigl(\lVert H_{A_\ell A_\ell} - I\rVert_2,\; \lVert H_{B_\ell B_\ell} - I\rVert_2\bigr) \le 2\sum_{j \ge 1}\sup_{\lvert v\rvert \ge j\ln q}\lvert K_{R_\ell}(v)\rvert$: under uniform Schwartz decay of $K_{R_\ell}(R_\ell x)$ as $R_\ell \to 0$, the normalized intra-branch blocks converge to the identity faster than every positive power of $R_\ell$, and in particular the explicit width-$R$ double-pole-cancelling Gaussian Schwartz family $\phi_R^{\mathrm{pc}}(u) \propto \bigl(\frac{u^2}{R^2} - 1 - \frac{R^2}{4}\bigr)e^{-u^2/(2R^2)}$ (for every $R > 0$ preserving $\mathcal{M}_{1/2}[f_R^{\mathrm{pc}}]\!\bigl(\pm\tfrac{1}{2}\bigr) = 0$, with $b := \frac{R^2}{4}$, $D := \frac{3}{4} + b + b^2$, $x := \frac{u}{R}$, and closed-form correlation $K_R^{\mathrm{pc}}(u) = \bigl(1 - \frac{3/4 + b/2}{D} x^2 + \frac{1}{16D} x^4\bigr)e^{-x^2/4}$, matching $K_{\mathrm{Sch}}$ at $R = 1$) exhibits Gaussian super-exponential decay $O(e^{-c/R_\ell^2})$; conversely, if the rescaled kernels $K_{R_\ell}(R_\ell x)$ are uniformly continuous at $0$ and the closest surviving pair keeps a uniform positive survival lower bound (for $I_\ell = \{1\}$, every $n \in A_\ell \cup B_\ell$ has $\eta_n = 1 - \lvert K_{R_\ell}(\ln n)\rvert^2$ with $\ln n \ge \ln q$, so as $R_\ell \to 0$ uniform rescaled tail decay yields $\eta_n \to 1$ uniformly), then $\Delta_{\min}^{\mathrm{cross}} / R_\ell \to 0$ still forces $\cos\theta_{\min} \to 1$ (while sampling a negative lobe at $R_\ell \asymp \Delta_{\min}^{\mathrm{cross}}$ can still sustain strictly positive coupling, as in item 4 on L9 at $R = 0.01$ and $R = 0.005$).

   - **(1C) Exact double-pole-cancelled constrained Sturm–Liouville second-moment floor**: Let $\phi \in H_0^1(-R/2, R/2)$ be nonzero and satisfy $\mathcal{M}_{1/2}[f]\!\bigl(\pm\tfrac{1}{2}\bigr) = 0$, equivalently orthogonality to $\cosh(u/2)$ and $\sinh(u/2)$ (in $L^2(-R/2, R/2)$). Splitting by parity $\phi = \phi_{\mathrm{even}} + \phi_{\mathrm{odd}}$ (orthogonal in both $L^2$ and $H_0^1$), the sharp infimum of $\sigma_w^2 = \frac{\lVert\phi'\rVert^2}{\lVert\phi\rVert^2}$ decouples into $\lambda_1^{\perp}(R) := \min\bigl(\lambda_1^{\perp,\mathrm{even}}(R),\; \lambda_1^{\perp,\mathrm{odd}}(R)\bigr)$. Putting $a := R/2$, $x := a/2 = R/4$, $\theta := \omega a$, $\lambda := \omega^2 = (2\theta/R)^2$, and $Q := \frac{\sinh(2x)}{2x}$, the even and odd constrained extremizers are $\psi_{\mathrm{even}}(u) = \cosh(x)\cos(\omega u) - \cos(\theta)\cosh(u/2)$ and $\psi_{\mathrm{odd}}(u) = \sinh(x)\sin(\omega u) - \sin(\theta)\sinh(u/2)$ (which vanish at the endpoints $\pm R/2$ by parity and direct substitution), satisfying the exact even and odd secular equations

     $$
     \theta\sin\theta \;=\; \left(\frac{1 + Q}{2\cosh^2 x}(\theta^2 + x^2) - x\tanh x\right)\cos\theta,
     $$

     $$
     \left(x\coth x - \frac{Q - 1}{2\sinh^2 x}(\theta^2 + x^2)\right)\sin\theta \;=\; \theta\cos\theta.
     $$

     Direct endpoint integrals show every tilt coefficient $g_j$ against the parity Dirichlet basis is nonzero, so the rank-one resolvent secular function $\sum_j \lvert g_j\rvert^2 / (\lambda_j - \lambda)$ is strictly increasing between adjacent poles with opposite infinite limits, placing the lowest even and odd constrained roots uniquely in $(\frac{\pi}{2}, \frac{3\pi}{2})$ and $(\pi, 2\pi)$. Testing the even equation at $\theta = \pi$ gives even root $\le \pi \iff \frac{x^2(Q - 1)}{Q + 1} \ge \pi^2$, and testing the odd equation at $\theta = \frac{3\pi}{2}$ gives odd root $> \frac{3\pi}{2} \iff \frac{x^2(Q + 1)}{Q - 1} < \frac{9\pi^2}{4}$; since $Q - 1 \ge \frac{2}{3}x^2$, the first holds for $x^2 \ge \pi^2 + 3$ and the second for $x^2 \le \pi^2 + 3$ (as $x^2 + 3 \le \pi^2 + 6 < \frac{9\pi^2}{4}$), proving unconditionally that $\frac{\pi^2}{R^2} < \lambda_1^{\perp,\mathrm{even}}(R) < \lambda_1^{\perp,\mathrm{odd}}(R)$ for all $R > 0$. Hence every real or complex double-pole-cancelling profile in $H_0^1(-R/2, R/2)$ obeys the sharp uncentered second-moment floor $\sigma_w^2 \ge \lambda_1^{\perp,\mathrm{even}}(R)$ (for real profiles, even spectral symmetry makes the mean zero, so this is the exact variance floor; for general complex profiles, Poincaré after demodulation gives the centered-variance lower bound $\frac{\pi^2}{R^2}$, while modulating a smooth interior approximation to the Dirichlet ground state by $e^{ivu}$ and correcting both moments with fixed interior test functions of invertible moment matrix produces moment errors decaying faster than every inverse power of $v$, so the corrections vanish in $H^1$ even after demodulation and the centered-variance infimum is $\frac{\pi^2}{R^2}$). As $R \to 0^+$, the even and odd secular equations tend to $\tan\theta = \theta$ ($\theta_1 \approx 4.493409$) and $(3 - \theta^2)\sin\theta = 3\theta\cos\theta$ ($\theta_2 \approx 5.763459$), so the limiting ratio of the constrained uncentered second-moment floor to the unconstrained floor $\frac{\pi^2}{R^2}$ tends to $\frac{4\theta_1^2}{\pi^2} \approx 8.182994$ (with the corresponding limiting ratio in the odd sector tending to $\frac{4\theta_2^2}{\pi^2} \approx 13.462530$).

2. **Exact coprime decoupling and quantitative convergence of the reciprocal Möbius–Euler tensor kernel $G^{\mathrm{inv, AP}}(\sigma)$ and Gaussian-windowed $G^{\mathrm{inv}}(\sigma; W_T)$ (manual derivation $+$ literature)**: Let $W_T(t) := \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,T} e^{-t^2/(2T^2)}$ ($T > 0$) be the even Gaussian probability window on $\mathbb{R}$, and for $\sigma > \frac{1}{2}$ and integers $m, n \ge 1$ define the reciprocal and direct finite-height vertical-line Gram matrices (at $\sigma = 1,\; t = 0$ the reciprocal integrand takes its holomorphic value $1/\zeta(1) = 0$, whereas the direct diagonal diverges at the $s = 1$ pole):

   $$
   G^{\mathrm{inv}}(\sigma; W_T)_{mn} \;:=\; \int_{-\infty}^\infty W_T(t)\,\frac{m^{-\sigma - it} n^{-\sigma + it}}{\lvert\zeta(\sigma + it)\rvert^2}\,dt, \qquad
   G^{+}(\sigma; W_T)_{mn} \;:=\; \int_{-\infty}^\infty W_T(t)\,m^{-\sigma - it} n^{-\sigma + it}\lvert\zeta(\sigma + it)\rvert^2\,dt.
   $$

   Since $\lvert\zeta(\sigma - it)\rvert = \lvert\zeta(\sigma + it)\rvert$ and $W_T$ is even, the sine imaginary part integrates to zero, so both matrices are **real symmetric and strictly positive definite** whenever finite (distinct $\ln n$ exponential polynomials are linearly independent on positive-measure sets). Set $g := \gcd(m, n)$, $a := m/g$, and $b := n/g$ ($\gcd(a, b) = 1$); on $\sigma > \frac{1}{2}$, the reciprocal Möbius–Euler arithmetic limit kernel $G^{\mathrm{inv, AP}}(\sigma)_{mn}$ equals the expression below when both $a, b$ are squarefree and vanishes whenever $a$ or $b$ is divisible by a square:

   $$
   G^{\mathrm{inv, AP}}(\sigma)_{mn} \;=\; \frac{\zeta(2\sigma)}{\zeta(4\sigma)}\,\operatorname{lcm}(m, n)^{-2\sigma}\,\mu(ab)\prod_{p \mid ab}\bigl(1 + p^{-2\sigma}\bigr)^{-1}.
   $$

   On $\sigma > \frac{1}{2}$, the normalized correlation matrix $H^{\mathrm{inv, AP}}(\sigma)_{mn} := G^{\mathrm{inv, AP}}(\sigma)_{mn} / \sqrt{G^{\mathrm{inv, AP}}(\sigma)_{mm} G^{\mathrm{inv, AP}}(\sigma)_{nn}}$ extends via the prime-coordinate Kronecker product $H^{\mathrm{inv, AP}}(\sigma)_{mn} = \prod_{p \in \mathbb{P}} h^{(p)}_{\mathrm{inv}}(\sigma; v_p(m), v_p(n))$ to all $\sigma > 0$, where each local prime factor is itself **tridiagonal Toeplitz with nonzero adjacent diagonals**:

   $$
   h^{(p)}_{\mathrm{inv}}(\sigma; j, k) \;=\; \delta_{jk} - c_p(\sigma)\,\delta_{\lvert j - k\rvert, 1}, \qquad
   c_p(\sigma) \;:=\; \frac{1}{p^\sigma + p^{-\sigma}} \;\in\; \left(0, \tfrac{1}{2}\right).
   $$

   - **Exact duality with $H^{(p)}(\sigma)$, positivity for all $\sigma > 0$, and exact coprime Schur decoupling (independent of RH)**: Set $H^{\mathrm{inv},(p)}(\sigma) := \bigl(h^{(p)}_{\mathrm{inv}}(\sigma; j, k)\bigr)_{j,k \ge 0}$. As in [@rem-ch34-laplace-tridiagonal-vs-nbb-separator] (item 1), take $r = p^{-\sigma} \in (0, 1)$; the bilateral geometric operator $H^{(p)}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\sigma)_{j,k} = r^{\lvert j - k\rvert}$ is explicitly invertible on $\ell^2(\mathbb{Z})$ in terms of the bilateral shift $U$: direct multiplication verifies that $\bigl[(1 + r^2)I - r(U + U^*)\bigr]/(1 - r^2)$ is its exact inverse; normalizing by its diagonal entry $(1 + r^2)/(1 - r^2)$ gives $I - c_p(\sigma)(U + U^*)$ (where $\frac{r}{1 + r^2} = c_p(\sigma)$), whose restriction to $j, k \ge 0$ is precisely the displayed tridiagonal Toeplitz factor $H^{\mathrm{inv},(p)}(\sigma)$ (finite or one-sided direct truncations have boundary corrections to their inverses). Its Toeplitz symbol $f_p(\theta; \sigma) = 1 - 2c_p(\sigma)\cos\theta = \frac{\lvert 1 - p^{-\sigma}e^{i\theta}\rvert^2}{1 + p^{-2\sigma}} > 0$ guarantees strict positive definiteness for all $\sigma > 0$ (including $\sigma = \frac{1}{2}$) with uniform factor condition bound $\kappa_2\bigl(H^{\mathrm{inv},(p)}(\sigma)\bigr) \le \bigl(\frac{1 + p^{-\sigma}}{1 - p^{-\sigma}}\bigr)^2$. As in [@rem-ch34-dilation-wavefront-nogo-and-euler-tensor] (item 3), let $P_1, P_2, P_3$ be pairwise disjoint, $I_0$ a nonempty finite $P_1$-smooth root set, $A$ consisting of $m = m_1 m_2$ ($m_1 \in I_0$, $m_2 > 1$ $P_2$-smooth), and $B$ consisting of $n = n_1 n_3$ ($n_1 \in I_0$, $n_3 > 1$ $P_3$-smooth); for all $\sigma > 0$, on the normalized tensor $H := H^{\mathrm{inv, AP}}(\sigma)$, prime factorization gives $H_{m,k} = H_{m_1, k} H_{m_2, 1}$, $H_{k,n} = H_{k, n_1} H_{1, n_3}$, and $H_{m,n} = H_{m_1, n_1} H_{m_2, 1} H_{1, n_3}$ (non-squarefree extensions $m_2$ are orthogonal to $I_0$ and $B$ even prior to elimination), so $H_{m, I_0} H_{I_0, I_0}^{-1} H_{I_0, n} = (H_{I_0, I_0})_{m_1, n_1} H_{m_2, 1} H_{1, n_3} = H_{m, n}$ and the cross-block Schur complement **vanishes identically**, $S^{\mathrm{inv, AP}}(\sigma)_{AB} \equiv 0$ (eliminating $I_0$ leaves a block-diagonal matrix, so successive elimination of any branch-internal subsets $I_A \subset A, I_B \subset B$ preserves the zero cross-block; the same holds on $\sigma > \frac{1}{2}$ for unnormalized $G^{\mathrm{inv, AP}}(\sigma)$). Conversely, on the numerical separator L1 ($\{1, 2, 3\}$, $I = \{2\}$), the normalized kernel $H^{\mathrm{inv, AP}}(\sigma)$ has cross-Schur entry $S^{\mathrm{inv, AP}}_{1,3} = -c_3(1 - c_2^2) < 0$ (strictly opposite in sign to $H^{\mathrm{AP}}$; for unnormalized $G^{\mathrm{inv, AP}}(\sigma)$ on $\sigma > \frac{1}{2}$, multiply by $3^{-\sigma}\zeta(2\sigma)/\zeta(4\sigma)$), with either scaling yielding exact canonical correlation $\cos\theta_{\min}(\{1\}, \{3\} \mid \{2\}) = c_3\sqrt{\frac{1 - c_2^2}{1 - c_2^2 c_3^2}} > 0$.

   - **Quantitative convergence and coprime-partition Schur decay of Gaussian finite-height kernels (unconditional on $\sigma > 1$; under external RH on $\frac{1}{2} < \sigma \le 1$)**: For $\sigma > 1$, $\zeta(s)^{-1} = \sum_{k=1}^\infty \mu(k) k^{-s}$ converges absolutely and uniformly; expanding term by term via $\int_{-\infty}^\infty W_T(t)(r/s)^{-it}\,dt = \exp\bigl(-\frac{T^2}{2}(\ln(r/s))^2\bigr)$, the diagonal $mk = n\ell$ ($k = br,\; \ell = ar$) yields $\sum_{\gcd(r, ab) = 1} \mu(ab)\mu(r)^2 (\operatorname{lcm}(m,n) r)^{-2\sigma} = G^{\mathrm{inv, AP}}(\sigma)_{mn}$, while splitting off-diagonal pairs $1 \le r < s$ into $r \ge s/2$ (using $\ln(s/r) \ge \frac{s-r}{s}$ and $\sum_{d=1}^\infty e^{-T^2 d^2/(2s^2)} \le \sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}\,\frac{s}{T}$) and $r < s/2$ (using $\ln(s/r) > \ln 2$) gives the unconditional explicit bound holding for each fixed $\sigma > 1$, uniformly in $m, n \ge 1$ and $T > 0$ (likewise for $G^+$):

     $$
     \left\lvert G^{\mathrm{inv}}(\sigma; W_T)_{mn} - G^{\mathrm{inv, AP}}(\sigma)_{mn}\right\rvert \;\le\; 2\!\left[2^\sigma\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}\,\frac{\zeta(2\sigma - 1) - 1}{T} + \zeta(\sigma)^2 e^{-T^2(\ln 2)^2 / 2}\right] \;=\; O_\sigma(T^{-1}).
     $$

     On $\sigma \in (\frac{1}{2}, 1]$, assuming external RH ([@conj-riemann-hypothesis], not frozen in `trureturing`), choose $c > \max(0, 1 - \sigma)$ and $\beta_0 = \frac{1}{2} + \eta \in (\frac{1}{2}, \sigma)$; by Littlewood's bound (E. C. Titchmarsh, 1986, Theorem 14.2), on $\operatorname{Re} s \ge \beta_0$ one has $\lvert\zeta(s)\rvert^{-1} \ll_{\eta} (1 + \lvert\operatorname{Im} s\rvert)^\eta$. Shifting the Mellin representation $M_X(s) := \sum_{k=1}^\infty \mu(k) e^{-k/X} k^{-s} = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(c)} \Gamma(w) X^w \zeta(s + w)^{-1}\,dw$ ($X \ge 2$) left to $\operatorname{Re} w = -(\sigma - \beta_0) \in (-1, 0)$ (crossing only the $w = 0$ residue $\zeta(s)^{-1}$; $\zeta^{-1}$ vanishes at $s = 1$ with no pole) and using exponential decay of $\Gamma$ yields the pointwise shifted-line error $M_X(\sigma + it) - \zeta(\sigma + it)^{-1} = O_{\sigma,\eta}\bigl(X^{1/2 - \sigma + \eta}(1 + \lvert t\rvert)^\eta\bigr)$; combining with $\lvert\zeta(\sigma + it)\rvert^{-1} \ll_\eta (1 + \lvert t\rvert)^\eta$ and the Gaussian $2\eta$-moment estimate $\int_{-\infty}^\infty W_T(t)(1 + \lvert t\rvert)^{2\eta}\,dt = O_\eta((1 + T)^{2\eta})$ ($T \ge 1$) yields the squared-reciprocal approximation error $O_{m,n,\sigma,\eta}(T^{2\eta} X^{1/2 - \sigma + \eta})$; applying the same diagonal-tail and Gaussian off-diagonal bounds to $M_X$, retaining the far-pair term before choosing polynomial $X$, leaves $O_{m,n,\sigma}\bigl(X^{1 - 2\sigma} + X^{2 - 2\sigma}/T + e^{-T^2(\ln 2)^2 / 2} X^{2 - 2\sigma}\bigr)$ ($\frac{1}{2} < \sigma < 1$) and $O_{m,n}\bigl(X^{-1}\ln X + T^{-1}\ln X + e^{-T^2(\ln 2)^2 / 2}\ln^2 X\bigr)$ ($\sigma = 1$). Inserting the polynomial balances $X = T^{2/(3 - 2\sigma)}$ ($\frac{1}{2} < \sigma < 1$) and $X = T^2$ ($\sigma = 1$) (where the super-exponential factor $e^{-T^2(\ln 2)^2 / 2}$ absorbs the far-pair terms) gives entrywise convergence on any fixed finite index set $V$, and for any fixed finite coprime partition satisfying the hypotheses of item 2 above, $V = I_0 \sqcup A \sqcup B$ (by smoothness of Schur complements at $G^{\mathrm{inv, AP}}_V \succ 0$ and local Lipschitz continuity of canonical correlation at the zero cross-block), and every $0 < \varepsilon < \beta(\sigma)$,

     $$
     \left\lvert G^{\mathrm{inv}}(\sigma; W_T)_{mn} - G^{\mathrm{inv, AP}}(\sigma)_{mn}\right\rvert \;=\; O_{m,n,\sigma,\varepsilon}\!\left(T^{-\beta(\sigma) + \varepsilon}\right), \qquad
     \cos\theta_{\min}\!\left(A, B \mid I_0;\; G^{\mathrm{inv}}(\sigma; W_T)\right) \;=\; O_{V,\sigma,\varepsilon}\!\left(T^{-\beta(\sigma) + \varepsilon}\right) \;\longrightarrow\; 0,
     $$

     with $\beta(\sigma) = 1$ ($\sigma > 1$, unconditional), $\beta(1) = 1$ (external RH), and $\beta(\sigma) = \frac{2\sigma - 1}{3 - 2\sigma} \in (0, 1)$ ($\frac{1}{2} < \sigma < 1$, external RH). For the direct kernel $G^+(\sigma; W_T)$ at each fixed $\sigma \in (\frac{1}{2}, 1)$, summing the dyadic-interval errors from [@rem-ch34-dilation-wavefront-nogo-and-euler-tensor] (item 3) down to height $1$ and absorbing the bounded initial interval $[0, 1]$ gives the cumulative error on $[0, Y]$ ($Y \ge 1$) of order $O_{m,n,\sigma}(Y^{2 - 2\sigma}\ln(2Y))$; integrating by parts against $-W_T'(t)$ using evenness then yields unconditionally the stronger entrywise and coprime-partition canonical-correlation rate $O_{V,\sigma}(T^{1 - 2\sigma}\ln(2T))$ (whereas at $\sigma = 1$ the $s = 1$ pole makes the Gaussian diagonal diverge).

3. **Off-line zero blow-up lower bounds, rank-$2$ separator absorption, and RH equivalence of finiteness (manual derivation: off-line bounds independent of RH; implanted limits under external RH)**: Unlike the Bohr-torus direct limit $G^{\mathrm{AP}}(\sigma)$, which is blind to off-line zeros, for any continuous strictly positive even window $W$ the reciprocal kernel $G^{\mathrm{inv}}(\sigma; W)$ exhibits unconditional blow-up lower bounds at every hypothetical right-half-strip zero $\frac{1}{2} < \operatorname{Re}\rho_0 < 1$.

   - **(3A) Root energy and post-$I = \{1\}$ two-prime extended variational Schur trace divergence at least of order $\delta^{-(2m_0 - 1)}$**: Let $W(t)$ be any continuous strictly positive even window on $\mathbb{R}$ (for example, for any fixed $T > 0$, the Gaussian $W_T$). If in $\frac{1}{2} < \operatorname{Re} s < 1$ there exists a zero $\zeta(\rho_0) = 0$ (with $\rho_0 = \sigma_0 + i\gamma_0$, multiplicity $m_0 \ge 1$, and $D_0 := \zeta^{(m_0)}(\rho_0) / m_0! \neq 0$), then starting for $\operatorname{Re} s > 1$ from the identity $\eta(s) := \sum_{n=1}^\infty (-1)^{n - 1} n^{-s} = (1 - 2^{1 - s})\zeta(s)$ established by absolute summation, rewrite it as the paired series $\eta(s) = \sum_{j \ge 1}\bigl((2j - 1)^{-s} - (2j)^{-s}\bigr)$: since $(2j - 1)^{-s} - (2j)^{-s} = s\int_{2j - 1}^{2j} u^{-s - 1}\,du$, on every compact subset of $\operatorname{Re} s > 0$ the summand is $O(j^{-1 - \epsilon})$ (for some $\epsilon > 0$), so the paired series converges locally uniformly to a holomorphic function on $\operatorname{Re} s > 0$, extending the identity by the identity theorem and removing the apparent singularity at $s = 1$; in particular, termwise positivity for all $\sigma \in (0, 1)$ gives $\eta(\sigma) > 0$, while $1 - 2^{1 - \sigma} < 0$ forces $\zeta(\sigma) < 0$; hence necessarily $\gamma_0 \neq 0$ and the conjugate pair $\{\rho_0, \overline{\rho_0}\}$ both vanish. At $\sigma = \sigma_0$, the non-integrable singularity $\lvert t \mp \gamma_0\rvert^{-2m_0}$ forces $G^{\mathrm{inv}}(\sigma_0; W)_{1,1} = +\infty$; setting $\delta := \sigma - \sigma_0 \to 0^+$, on disjoint neighborhoods $(\pm\gamma_0 - \varepsilon, \pm\gamma_0 + \varepsilon)$ substituting $t = \pm\gamma_0 + \delta u$ and defining the pair residue constant $R(\rho_0; W) := \frac{2 W(\gamma_0)\sqrt{\pi}\,\Gamma(m_0 - 1/2)}{\lvert D_0\rvert^2\,\Gamma(m_0)}$ (equal for $m_0 = 1, 2, 3$ to $2\pi,\; \pi,\; \frac{3\pi}{4}$ times $\frac{W(\gamma_0)}{\lvert D_0\rvert^2}$) gives $\liminf_{\delta \to 0^+} \delta^{2m_0 - 1} G^{\mathrm{inv}}(\sigma_0 + \delta; W)_{1,1} \ge R(\rho_0; W) > 0$. Furthermore, for the **root separator $I = \{1\}$** and any $m \ge 2$, define the extended variational Schur energy in $[0, \infty]$ by $E^{\mathrm{inv}}_m(\sigma; W) := \inf_{c \in \mathbb{C}}\int_{-\infty}^\infty W(t)\lvert m^{-\sigma - it} - c\rvert^2\lvert\zeta(\sigma + it)\rvert^{-2}\,dt$ (which by [@prop-ch34-hermitian-schur-variational-minimum] equals $S^{\mathrm{inv}}_{mm}(\sigma; W)$ whenever the Gram entries are finite). By the $c \in \mathbb{C}$-independent pointwise parallelogram bound $\lvert m^{-\sigma - it} - c\rvert^2 + \lvert m^{-\sigma + it} - c\rvert^2 \ge \frac{1}{2}\lvert m^{-\sigma - it} - m^{-\sigma + it}\rvert^2 = 2 m^{-2\sigma}\sin^2(t\ln m)$ (attained in the isolated-pair limit at $c = m^{-\sigma_0}\cos(\gamma_0\ln m)$), for any distinct primes $p, q$ and any pivot set $P \supseteq \{p, q\}$, having $\gamma_0 \neq 0$ with $\sin(\gamma_0\ln p) = \sin(\gamma_0\ln q) = 0$ would force $\frac{\ln p}{\ln q} \in \mathbb{Q}$, contradicting unique prime factorization; hence $\sin^2(\gamma_0\ln p) + \sin^2(\gamma_0\ln q) > 0$, forcing divergence at least of order $\delta^{-(2m_0 - 1)}$ (global equality additionally requires controlling the scaled background and other poles):

     $$
     \liminf_{\delta \to 0^+} \delta^{2m_0 - 1}\Bigl(E^{\mathrm{inv}}_p(\sigma_0 + \delta; W) + E^{\mathrm{inv}}_q(\sigma_0 + \delta; W)\Bigr) \;\ge\; R(\rho_0; W)\Bigl(p^{-2\sigma_0}\sin^2(\gamma_0\ln p) + q^{-2\sigma_0}\sin^2(\gamma_0\ln q)\Bigr) \;>\; 0,
     $$

     and in particular at $\sigma = \sigma_0$ one has $E^{\mathrm{inv}}_p(\sigma_0; W) + E^{\mathrm{inv}}_q(\sigma_0; W) = +\infty$.

   - **(3B) Rank-$2$ separator absorption, critical-line multi-pair lower bounds, Blaschke-implanted contrast, and RH equivalence of finiteness**:

     - *Rank-$2$ absorption when separator evaluations span the pair coordinates*: When $\operatorname{Re} s = \sigma_0$ carries only a single simple conjugate pair $\{\rho_0, \overline{\rho_0}\}$, its scaled leading residue matrix $\lim_{\delta \to 0^+}\delta G^{\mathrm{inv}}_{\mathrm{local}}$ has rank at most $2$ (and rank exactly $2$ precisely when the evaluation coordinates on the index set in question span the two-dimensional real coordinates of the conjugate pair). Whenever the evaluations of a separator $I$ at $\{\rho_0, \overline{\rho_0}\}$ span those two-dimensional real coordinates, for any target index $m$ there exist real coefficients $\{c_j\}_{j \in I}$ making the general interpolated residual $m^{-s} - \sum_{j \in I} c_j j^{-s}$ vanish at both $\rho_0$ and $\overline{\rho_0}$ (specializing to $m = 3$ and $I = \{1, 2\}$ with $\operatorname{Im}(2^{-\rho_0}) \neq 0$ yields a unique real pair $c_1, c_2$, whereas larger separators generally lack uniqueness), so its local $O(\lvert s - \rho_0\rvert)$ vanishing cancels the simple pole; defining the extended variational Schur energy $E^{\mathrm{inv}}_{m\mid I}(\sigma; W) := \inf_{(c_j)_{j \in I}}\int_{-\infty}^\infty W(t)\bigl\lvert m^{-\sigma - it} - \sum_{j \in I} c_j j^{-\sigma - it}\bigr\rvert^2\lvert\zeta(\sigma + it)\rvert^{-2}\,dt$ (equal to the matrix Schur entry $S^{\mathrm{inv}}_{m,m\mid I}(\sigma; W)$ wherever the Gram entries are finite), **under the hypothesis** that the weighted residual integral outside the two local neighborhoods stays uniformly bounded as $\delta \to 0^+$ (the Gaussian-window background and finite-Gram estimate under external RH belongs to the implanted example below), the variational minimum gives $E^{\mathrm{inv}}_{m\mid I}(\sigma_0 + \delta; W) = O(1)$ (in particular $E^{\mathrm{inv}}_{3\mid\{1,2\}}(\sigma_0 + \delta; W) = O(1)$, hence $S^{\mathrm{inv}}_{3,3\mid\{1,2\}}(\sigma_0 + \delta; W) = O(1)$ wherever the Gram is finite). Thus the root energy $G^{\mathrm{inv}}_{1,1}$ and the $I = \{1\}$ trace $E^{\mathrm{inv}}_p + E^{\mathrm{inv}}_q$ retain off-line zero sensitivity, whereas a remainder block after eliminating a larger separator can lose it.

     - *Multi-pair lower bounds for genuine $\zeta$ approaching the critical line*: As genuine $\zeta$ approaches $\sigma = \frac{1}{2} + \delta$ (taking any nonempty finite collection of simple critical-line zero pairs $\{\rho_k, \overline{\rho_k}\}_{k \le N}$ with pair residue constants $R_k := R(\rho_k; W)$), one obtains strictly positive lower bounds on $\liminf_{\delta \to 0^+} \delta G^{\mathrm{inv}}_{1,1}$ and $\liminf_{\delta \to 0^+} \delta(E^{\mathrm{inv}}_2 + E^{\mathrm{inv}}_3)$, and for the two-node separator $I = \{1, 2\}$ a nonnegative lower bound on $\liminf_{\delta \to 0^+} \delta E^{\mathrm{inv}}_{3\mid\{1,2\}}$ given by $\min_{c_1, c_2 \in \mathbb{R}} \sum_{k \le N} R_k \lvert 3^{-\rho_k} - c_1 - c_2\cdot 2^{-\rho_k}\rvert^2$ (wherever the Gram entries are finite, the two extended variational energies coincide respectively with the matrix Schur quantities $S^{\mathrm{inv}}_{2,2} + S^{\mathrm{inv}}_{3,3}$ and $S^{\mathrm{inv}}_{3,3\mid\{1,2\}}$). To prove this, evenness reduces the infimum to real coefficients; restrict the integral to the union of fixed disjoint local neighborhoods of the $N$ zero pairs together with a fixed compact subinterval away from those neighborhoods and $s = 1$, denoted $J$, and scale by $\delta$: splitting the real separator coefficient space into the kernel of the limiting evaluation map and its orthogonal complement, kernel residuals vanish at every selected zero and are $O(\delta + \lvert t - \gamma_k\rvert)$ there (with the conjugate estimate at $-\gamma_k$), so integrating $\delta(\delta + \lvert t - \gamma_k\rvert)/(\delta^2 + (t - \gamma_k)^2)$ bounds their scaled cross-Gram entries with the target and complement by $O(\delta\ln(1/\delta))$; meanwhile, on $J$ linear independence of the separator exponential polynomials and a uniform positive lower bound for $W\lvert\zeta\rvert^{-2}$ give the scaled kernel block a lower bound $c\delta I$ ($c > 0$), so eliminating the null directions first perturbs the target-and-complement Gram by only $O(\delta\ln^2(1/\delta))$, and since the complement block is positive definite in the limit, its Schur minimum converges to the displayed weighted least-squares minimum (for any collection containing the first pair $(\rho_1, \overline{\rho_1})$, since $\operatorname{Im}(2^{-\rho_1}) \neq 0$, the limiting $\{1, 2\}$ separator block is already positive definite with trivial kernel; the lower bound is strictly positive if and only if no real coefficient pair $(c_1, c_2)$ fits all collected pairs). In particular, the real pair determined uniquely by the first pair $(\rho_1, \overline{\rho_1})$, namely $(c_1, c_2)$, leaves at the second pair $\rho_2 = \frac{1}{2} + i\gamma_2$ the certified nonzero residual $r_2 = 3^{-\rho_2} - c_1 - c_2\cdot 2^{-\rho_2} \approx 0.452702 + 0.258896 i$, proving strict positivity of the $\liminf$ lower bound for every finite collection containing the first two pairs (a global equality to the infinite simple-pair sum requires simplicity and uniform scaled-tail control).

     - *Blaschke-implanted test function contrast*: On $\sigma \in (\frac{1}{2}, 1)$, set $\rho_0 := \frac{3}{4} + i\gamma_1$ ($\sigma_0 = \frac{3}{4},\; \gamma_0 = \gamma_1$) and define $\zeta_{\rho_0}(s) := B(s)\,\zeta(s)$ with $B(s) := \frac{(s - \rho_0)(s - \overline{\rho_0})}{(s - 1 + \overline{\rho_0})(s - 1 + \rho_0)}$. Reflection gives $\lvert B(\frac{1}{2} + it)\rvert \equiv 1$ on the critical line, while as $\lvert t\rvert \to \infty$ one has $B(\sigma + it) = 1 + O(\lvert t\rvert^{-1})$ and $\lvert B(\sigma + it)\rvert^2 = 1 + O(t^{-2})$; for fixed $\sigma \in (\frac{1}{2}, 1)$, combining with $\lvert\zeta(\sigma + it)\rvert = O_\sigma((1 + \lvert t\rvert)^{1 - \sigma})$ makes $(\lvert B\rvert^2 - 1)\lvert\zeta\rvert^2$ integrable, so the direct mock satisfies entrywise $\bigl\lvert\bigl(G^+_{\zeta_{\rho_0}}(\sigma; W_T) - G^+(\sigma; W_T)\bigr)_{mn}\bigr\rvert = O_{m,n,\sigma}(T^{-1})$, stays analytic across $\sigma = \frac{3}{4}$, and unconditionally shares the same RH-blind limit $G^{\mathrm{AP}}(\sigma)$; conversely, for the reciprocal kernel $G^{\mathrm{inv}}_{\zeta_{\rho_0}}(\sigma; W_T)$, via the isolated simple zero pair and the uniform boundedness of the Gaussian-weighted background outside the local neighborhoods supplied by external RH (which also verifies the outer-tail hypothesis of rank-$2$ absorption above, yielding $S^{\mathrm{inv}}_{3,3\mid\{1,2\}}(\frac{3}{4} + \delta; W_T) = O(1)$), as $\delta := \sigma - \frac{3}{4} \to 0^+$ the unscaled root entry diverges while the scaled quantities converge to the exact pair residue limits $\delta G^{\mathrm{inv}}_{1,1} \to R_{\zeta_{\rho_0}}$ and $\delta(S^{\mathrm{inv}}_{pp} + S^{\mathrm{inv}}_{qq}) \to R_{\zeta_{\rho_0}}\sum_{m \in \{p, q\}} m^{-2\sigma_0}\sin^2(\gamma_0\ln m)$ (where $R_{\zeta_{\rho_0}} := \frac{2\pi W_T(\gamma_1)}{\lvert\zeta_{\rho_0}'(\rho_0)\rvert^2}$).

     - *RH equivalence of finiteness*: Combining item 2 and (3A), for **each fixed $T > 0$**, **RH is equivalent** to the condition that for **every** $\sigma \in (\frac{1}{2}, 1)$ one has $G^{\mathrm{inv}}(\sigma; W_T)_{1,1} < \infty$ (and equivalently that for every $\sigma \in (\frac{1}{2}, 1)$ the extended variational trace satisfies $E^{\mathrm{inv}}_2(\sigma; W_T) + E^{\mathrm{inv}}_3(\sigma; W_T) < \infty$; forward by Littlewood's bound and Gaussian decay, converse by (3A) and [@thm-ch34-critical-strip-barrier-and-cayley-bridge], `golden_right_half_strip_implies_rh`). This isolates the non-circularity constraint keeping [@prob-ch34-arithmetic-pivot-area-law] open as an independent proof: although $G^{\mathrm{inv}}(\sigma; W_T)$ unites an exact coprime-decoupling tensor limit with genuine off-line zero sensitivity, establishing *a priori* that $G^{\mathrm{inv}}(\sigma; W_T)_{1,1} < \infty$ for all $\sigma \in (\frac{1}{2}, 1)$ without invoking external RH is logically equivalent to RH itself.

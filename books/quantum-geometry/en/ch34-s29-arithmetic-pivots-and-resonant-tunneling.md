## 34.29.1 The Arithmetic Sorting Barrier, the Turán–Zeno Spurious-Zero Trap ($\zeta_{19}$ Beyond $\sigma = 1$), and the $0.046191/\ln M$ Logarithmic Wall {#sec-ch34-29-1}

Return to the infinite-dimensional orthogonal comparison cone $\bigoplus_{p \in \mathbb{P}} \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}\,\mathbf{e}_p$ spanned by the primes. By the Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic, the prime logarithmic step lengths $\{\ln 2, \ln 3, \ln 5, \ln 7, \dots\}$ are linearly independent over $\mathbb{Q}$: each prime $p$ acts as an irreducible minimal circular-arc trihedron $\Delta\mathcal{T}_p$ of intrinsic action step $L_p = \ln p$, while each positive integer $n = \prod_i p_i^{a_i}$ encodes a unique composite path $\Gamma_n$ of additive total action

$$
S[\Gamma_n] = \ln n = \sum_i a_i \ln p_i.
$$

Now inspect the two workhorse finite approximations of classical analytic number theory: the truncated Dirichlet partial sum $\zeta_M(s) := \sum_{n=1}^M n^{-s}$, and the sequential Nyman–Beurling–Báez-Duarte (NBB) Gram matrix $G_M$ built in natural-number order $k = 1, 2, \dots, M$. Translated into the algorithmic language of [§34.26](ch34-s26-chord-tunneling-and-pivot-renormalization.md#sec-ch34-chord-tunneling-and-pivot-renormalization), both procedures literally run **classical Dijkstra global total-order sorting** on the prime multiplicative graph: regardless of how wildly two paths differ in prime support, exterior grade, or winding multiplicity, they are forced into a single one-dimensional queue $1 < 2 < 3 < \dots < M$ by real action magnitude $\ln n$ and guillotined by a sharp step gate $\mathbf{1}_{\ln n \le \ln M}$ at $\ln M$. Geometrically and spectrally, this seemingly innocent total ordering triggers two immediate disasters:

1. **Hard truncation acts as a midway Zeno measurement, breeding "Turán spurious zeros" inside the Euler safety zone $\operatorname{Re}(s) > 1$**:
   In 1948, Pál Turán noticed a tantalizing route toward the Riemann Hypothesis: since the full zeta function $\zeta(s)$ is strictly zero-free on $\sigma = \operatorname{Re}(s) > 1$ by absolute convergence of the Euler product ([@thm-riemann-zeta-euler-product]), might its finite partial sums $\zeta_M(s) = \sum_{n=1}^M n^{-s}$ likewise remain zero-free on $\sigma > 1$? Turán proved (literature result) that if $\zeta_M(s) \neq 0$ on $\sigma > 1$ held for all sufficiently large $M$ (even up to a mild decaying shift), the Riemann Hypothesis [@conj-riemann-hypothesis] would follow immediately! History soon delivered a dramatic reversal. Exploiting Bohr almost-periodic phase alignment among $\mathbb{Q}$-incommensurate prime logarithms, Haselgrove (1958) disproved the closely allied Pólya and Turán sign conjectures for Liouville sums; Montgomery (1983) then established the sharp asymptotic law for the maximal zero abscissa $\psi_M := \sup\{\operatorname{Re}(s) : \zeta_M(s) = 0\}$ at large $M$ (literature result):

   $$
   \psi_M = 1 + \left(\frac{4}{\pi} - 1 - o(1)\right)\frac{\ln\ln M}{\ln M}, \qquad \frac{4}{\pi} - 1 \approx 0.2732395447,
   $$

   with the constant $\frac{4}{\pi} - 1$ best possible! Building on earlier bounds of Spira (1966, 1968), Monach (1980), and van de Lune & te Riele, Platt and Trudgian (2016, Theorem 1.1) closed every remaining finite gap via rigorous interval arithmetic (literature result): **the ordinary Dirichlet partial sum $\zeta_M(s)$ is zero-free in $\operatorname{Re}(s) > 1$ if and only if $M \in \{1, \dots, 18, 20, 21, 28\}$; for every other positive integer $M$, $\zeta_M(s)$ possesses infinitely many zeros inside $\operatorname{Re}(s) > 1$!** In particular, $M = 19$ marks the very first breakdown threshold in natural-number order: near $\sigma \approx 1.0069878449,\; t \approx 155417750480610.1069058344$, a 100-digit interval Rouché certificate encloses a genuine simple zero strictly to the right of $\sigma = 1$ (see [@ex-ch34-turan-schur-and-cayley-calculus] below).

   Through the lens of minimal arc-trihedra and the $90^\circ$ blindspot analysis of [§34.27](ch34-s27-imaginary-chord-tunneling-and-temporal-double-slit.md#sec-ch34-imaginary-chord-tunneling-and-temporal-double-slit), the origin of Turán's spurious zeros becomes transparent. Look at three consecutive integers in the one-dimensional queue: $1021$ opens a brand-new prime axis $\mathbf{e}_{1021}$ (a primitive rank-1 arc), $1023 = 3 \times 11 \times 31$ is a square-free fermionic exterior 3-blade $\mathbf{e}_3 \wedge \mathbf{e}_{11} \wedge \mathbf{e}_{31}$ carrying Möbius chirality $\mu(1023) = -1$, and $1024 = 2^{10}$ winds ten consecutive times around the single prime $\mathbf{e}_2$ ($\mu(1024) = 0$). Topologically, these three paths live in completely unrelated sectors of the multiplicative tree—yet one-dimensional size sorting jams them shoulder to shoulder and drops a hard blade across $\ln n \le \ln M$! Under Mellin–Fourier duality, the sharp indicator $\mathbf{1}_{[0, \ln M]}(u)$ transforms into an oscillating sign-indefinite Dirichlet–$\mathrm{sinc}$ kernel $\frac{1 - M^{1-s}}{s - 1}$ rather than a positive-semidefinite self-convolution square. That sharp cutoff is literally a **midway Zeno projective measurement**: it slices open unclosed minimal arc-trihedra before their downstream Möbius partners can cancel, and at gigantic heights $t$ where prime phases align almost-periodically, its Gibbs edge ringing splashes magenta spurious zeros straight across the $\sigma = 1$ border into the Euler safety zone!

2. **The exponential "sorting tax" and the $0.0461914179/\ln M$ logarithmic wall**:
   Even when one moves to manifestly positive-semidefinite Hilbert-space approximations—such as the criterion established by Nyman (1950) and Beurling (1955) and discretized by Báez-Duarte (2003), whereby RH holds if and only if $\mathbf{1} \in L^2(0,1)$ lies in the closed span of the natural-number fractional-part dilations $\{u \mapsto \{1/(ku)\}\}_{k=1}^M$, i.e., $d_M^2 = 1 - \mathbf{c}_M^\top G_M^{-1}\mathbf{c}_M \to 0$—expanding the Gram matrix $G_M$ sequentially along $k = 1, 2, \dots, M$ still pays an exponential sorting tax. Because intrinsic geodesic action depth is $L = \ln M$, advancing action depth by a mere $L$ units along a 1D integer queue inflates the matrix dimension exponentially to **$M = e^L$ rows**! As proved unconditionally by Báez-Duarte, Balazard, Landreau & Saias (2000) for distinct critical-line zeros and sharpened unconditionally to squared multiplicities by Burnol (2002) (literature results), in the sequential natural-number basis the Gram Schur-complement squared residual $d_M^2 = 1 - \mathbf{c}_M^\top G_M^{-1}\mathbf{c}_M$ obeys the strict logarithmic lower bound

   $$
   \liminf_{M \to \infty} (\ln M)\,d_M^2 \;\ge\; \sum_{\operatorname{Re}(\rho) = 1/2} \frac{m_\rho^2}{\lvert\rho\rvert^2} \;>\; 0,
   $$

   summed over all distinct critical-line zeros $\rho$ of $\zeta(s)$ with multiplicity $m_\rho \ge 1$; when the Riemann Hypothesis holds, this sum equals (if all zeros are simple) or is bounded below by (since $m_\rho^2 \ge m_\rho$) the benchmark constant $C_{\mathrm{NBB}} := 2 + \gamma - \ln(4\pi) \approx 0.0461914179$ (where $\gamma \approx 0.5772156649$ is the Euler–Mascheroni constant; whereas if RH failed, $d_M^2$ would not tend to $0$ at all), trapping $d_M^2$ above $\frac{C_{\mathrm{NBB}} + o(1)}{\ln M}$ at best.

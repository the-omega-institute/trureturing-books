   For any $\gamma > 0$ and $0 \le \delta < \frac{1}{2}$, set $A := \gamma^2 + \delta^2 + \frac{1}{4}$; rationalizing $u_\pm = 1 - (\frac{1}{2} \mp \delta + i\gamma)^{-1} = \frac{(A - \frac{1}{2}) + i\gamma}{A \mp \delta}$ yields identical numerator real and imaginary parts $(A - \frac{1}{2},\; \gamma)$, so the outer image $u_+$ and inner image $u_-$ share the exact same phase $\theta(\delta, \gamma) = \operatorname{Arg}((A - \frac{1}{2}) + i\gamma) \in (0, \pi)$ while carrying opposite log-radii $\pm\alpha(\delta, \gamma)$ with $\alpha(\delta, \gamma) := \ln\lvert u_+\rvert = \operatorname{artanh}(\frac{\delta}{A}) \ge 0$, namely $u_\pm = e^{\pm\alpha + i\theta}$. Treating $\mathcal{U}(\delta, \gamma) := (u_+, u_-, \overline{u_+}, \overline{u_-})$ as an indexed four-element family with multiplicity (at $\delta = 0$, retaining multiplicity $2$ for each unit-circle root, rather than deduplicating to a two-element set which would halve the power sums), its power-sum matrix $\mathbf{T}_N = [4\cosh((j - k)\alpha)\cos((j - k)\theta)]_{0 \le j,k \le N}$ is real symmetric and centrosymmetric (commuting with reversal $P_N$). With Vandermonde matrix $V := [v(u_+),\; v(u_-),\; v(\overline{u_+}),\; v(\overline{u_-})] \in \mathbb{C}^{(N+1)\times 4}$ ($v(z)_j = z^j$) and swap signature $J_4 := \operatorname{diag}(\sigma_x, \sigma_x)$ (eigenvalues $+1, +1, -1, -1$), we have $\mathbf{T}_N = V J_4 V^*$: at $\delta = 0$, $u_+ = u_- = e^{i\theta_0} \neq e^{-i\theta_0}$, giving for all $N \ge 1$ exact rank $2$ and inertia $(2, 0, N - 1)$; for $0 < \delta < \frac{1}{2}$ and $N \ge 3$, the four images are pairwise distinct, so $\operatorname{rank}(V) = 4$ and Sylvester's law of inertia locks **exact inertia $(n_+, n_-, n_0) = (2, 2, N - 3)$**.

   Setting $L := N + 1$ and centered index $m := j - \frac{N}{2}$, expanding $\cosh(\alpha(m - m'))\cos(\theta(m - m'))$ splits $\mathbf{T}_N$ orthogonally into $P_N$-even ($\sigma = +1$) and $P_N$-odd ($\sigma = -1$) two rank-at-most-$2$ real symmetric blocks $4(u_\sigma u_\sigma^\top - w_\sigma w_\sigma^\top)$, with $u_{\mathrm{e}}(m) = \cosh(\alpha m)\cos(\theta m)$, $w_{\mathrm{e}}(m) = \sinh(\alpha m)\sin(\theta m)$, $u_{\mathrm{o}}(m) = \cosh(\alpha m)\sin(\theta m)$, and $w_{\mathrm{o}}(m) = \sinh(\alpha m)\cos(\theta m)$. Defining $S_h := \frac{\sinh(L\alpha)}{\sinh\alpha}$ (equal at $\alpha = 0$ to $L$), $S_c := \frac{\sin(L\theta)}{\sin\theta}$, and $C + iS := \frac{\sinh(L(\alpha + i\theta))}{\sinh(\alpha + i\theta)}$, the two $2\times 2$ reductions give all four nonzero eigenvalues in **exact closed form** (for all $N \ge 3$ and $0 < \delta < \frac{1}{2}$):

   $$
   \lambda_{\sigma, \pm}(N; \delta, \gamma) \;=\; (L + \sigma C) \;\pm\; \sqrt{(S_h + \sigma S_c)^2 - S^2} \qquad (\sigma \in \{+1, -1\}),
   $$

   collapsing at $\delta = 0$ to $\lambda_{\sigma, +} = 2(L + \sigma S_c) > 0$ and $\lambda_{\sigma, -} = 0$. This closed form resolves three structural points:

   - **Low-order thresholds and sharpness of the general four-image criterion**: Put $t_d := 4\cosh(d\alpha)\cos(d\theta)$. At $(\gamma_1, \delta = 0.25)$, the $2\times 2$ matrix $\mathbf{T}_1$ remains strictly positive definite ($\lambda_{\min}(\mathbf{T}_1) = 4 - \lvert t_1\rvert \approx 0.0099886 > 0$); by contrast, for every off-circle Cayley quartet ($0 < \delta < \frac{1}{2},\; \gamma > 0$), already at $N = 2$ ($3\times 3$) the even vectors $u_{\mathrm{e}}, w_{\mathrm{e}} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ are linearly independent (center entries $u_{\mathrm{e}}(0) = 1$, $w_{\mathrm{e}}(0) = 0$ and nonzero outer entries $w_{\mathrm{e}}(\pm 1) = \sinh\alpha\sin\theta \neq 0$), forcing a strictly negative even eigenvalue $\lambda_{\mathrm{e}, -}(\mathbf{T}_2) = 4 + \frac{1}{2}(t_2 - \sqrt{t_2^2 + 8t_1^2}) < 0$ (at $(\gamma_1, 0.25)$, $\approx -2.0838\times 10^{-8}$), alongside the single odd eigenvalue $\lambda_{\mathrm{o}}(\mathbf{T}_2) = 4 - t_2$ (which can already be negative for small $\gamma$ whenever $t_2 > 4$), whereas $N = 3$ is the first order that for all $(\delta, \gamma)$ **uniformly guarantees** both negative modes (inertia $(2, 2, 0)$). Yet for the **general** four-distinct-image family in the frozen theorem `toeplitz_posSemidef_iff_norm_eq_one`, the uniform threshold $N \ge 3$ ($N + 1 \ge 4$) is strictly sharp: the off-circle inverse-conjugate quartet $\{2, \frac{1}{2}, i, -i\}$ has $\mathbf{T}_2 \succ 0$ with exact eigenvalues $\frac{41 - \sqrt{881}}{8} \approx 1.4148$, $\frac{7}{4}$, and $\frac{41 + \sqrt{881}}{8}$.
   - **Weak-drift cubic window and exponential runaway regime**: At fixed $N \ge 3$ and interior phase $\theta \in (0, \pi)$ as $\alpha \to 0^+$, $\lambda_{\sigma, -} = -c_\sigma(N, \theta)\alpha^2 + O(\alpha^4)$ with $c_\sigma = 4\bigl(\lVert z_\sigma\rVert^2 - \frac{\langle u_\sigma(0), z_\sigma\rangle^2}{\lVert u_\sigma(0)\rVert^2}\bigr)$ (where $u_\sigma(0)$ is the $\alpha = 0$ value of $u_\sigma$, $z_{\mathrm{e}} = m\sin(\theta m)$, and $z_{\mathrm{o}} = m\cos(\theta m)$) and $c_\sigma = \frac{1}{6}N^3 + O_\theta(N^2)$. Thus throughout the weak-drift window $(\sin\theta)^{-1} \ll N \ll \alpha^{-1}$, $-\lambda_{\min}(\mathbf{T}_N) \sim \frac{1}{6}\alpha^2 N^3$ (for small $\delta$, $\alpha \sim \frac{\delta}{\gamma^2 + 1/4}$, giving $-\lambda_{\min} \sim \frac{\delta^2 N^3}{6(\gamma^2 + 1/4)^2}$), so reaching a $\Theta(1)$ negative mode inside this window requires $N \asymp \alpha^{-2/3} \sim \bigl((\gamma^2 + \frac{1}{4})/\delta\bigr)^{2/3}$ (for large $\gamma$, simplifying to $(\gamma^2/\delta)^{2/3}$; at $\gamma_1,\; \delta = 10^{-3}$, the ratio $-\lambda_{\min}/(\frac{1}{6}\alpha^2 N^3)$ rounds across $N = 200, 500, 1000, 2000$ to $1.2240, 1.0733, 1.0452, 1.0045$); at $N = 95,\; \gamma_1$, $\lambda_{\min}(\mathbf{T}_{95})$ for both $\delta = 0.25$ and $0.40$ arises in the odd sector ($\sigma = -1$), with certified values rounding to $-0.29317011$ and $-0.75017762$ confirming [@ex-ch39-riemann-polishing-quantitative-audit]. Entering the exponential runaway regime $N\alpha \gg 1$ (onset $N_{\mathrm{exp}} := \alpha^{-1} \sim \frac{\gamma^2 + 1/4}{\delta}$, which at $(\gamma_1, 0.25)$ is $\approx 800.41$), putting $\phi := \operatorname{Arg}\sinh(\alpha + i\theta)$, $\psi_N := L\theta - \phi$, $k := \frac{\sinh\alpha}{\sqrt{\sinh^2\alpha + \sin^2\theta}} \in (0, 1)$, and $H_N := \frac{e^{L\alpha}}{2\sinh\alpha}$ yields

     $$
     -\frac{\lambda_{\sigma, -}}{H_N} \;=\; \sqrt{1 - k^2\sin^2\psi_N} \;-\; \sigma k\cos\psi_N \;+\; O_{\alpha,\theta}\bigl(L e^{-L\alpha}\bigr),
     $$

     with leading amplitude confined to $[1 - k, 1 + k]$ (at $(\gamma_1, 0.25)$, exact-to-leading ratios for even/odd parities round at $N = 2048$ to $0.5965, 0.5998$, and at $N = 4096$ to $0.9382, 0.9392$, where $\lambda_{\min}(\mathbf{T}_{4096}) \approx -63461.72$).
   - **Structural contrast with full-spectrum canonical Li Toeplitz conditioning**: Whereas the isolated four-image model at $\delta = 0$ ($N \ge 1$) keeps constant rank $2$ and at $\delta > 0$ ($N \ge 3$) keeps exact inertia $(2, 2, N - 3)$, the full-spectrum Li Toeplitz matrix $T_N(c^{\mathrm{Li}})$ analyzed in [§31.3](ch34-s31-arc-calculus-riemann-and-hermite-biehler.md#rem-ch34-li-toeplitz-superexponential-collapse) [@rem-ch34-li-toeplitz-superexponential-collapse] aggregates infinitely many critical-line zeros whose Cayley images accumulate at $u = 1$; under RH it stays strictly positive definite for every $N \ge 1$ yet suffers superexponential Schur-pivot collapse $\ln\lambda_{\min}(T_{N+1}) \le \ln\pi_N \le -2N\ln N + 2N\ln\ln N + O(N)$ ($\pi_{100} \approx 4.45\times 10^{-406}$, $\lambda_{\min}(T_{101}) \approx 4.96\times 10^{-465}$). Thus the finite four-image glue faithfully isolates the sign-flip criterion of `toeplitz_posSemidef_iff_norm_eq_one`, not the numerical conditioning of the full arithmetic spectrum.

3. **Character Parseval Radial–Directional Split, Real-Quadratic Fourier Projection, and $X^{1/2}$ / $X^{1/3}$ Prime-Power Offsets (Unconditional Identities + External Dirichlet GRH, No LI)**:
   Fix a modulus $q \ge 3$, $G := (\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^\times$, and $\varphi := \varphi(q)$. For any residue-class counting function $F_a = F(X; q, a)$ with character transform $\widehat{F}(\chi) := \sum_{a \in G} F_a \overline{\chi(a)}$ and common radial baseline $M(X)$ (for $\theta, \psi$, $X$; for $\pi$, $\operatorname{Li}(X) := \operatorname{li}(X) - \operatorname{li}(2)$), character orthogonality gives the unconditional Pythagorean identity

   $$
   E_{\mathrm{tot}}^F(X) := \sum_{a \in G}\left(F_a - \frac{M(X)}{\varphi}\right)^2 \;=\; \underbrace{\frac{1}{\varphi}\bigl\lvert\widehat{F}(\chi_0) - M(X)\bigr\rvert^2}_{E_{\mathrm{rad}}^F(X)} \;+\; \underbrace{\frac{1}{\varphi}\sum_{\chi \neq \chi_0}\bigl\lvert\widehat{F}(\chi)\bigr\rvert^2}_{E_{\mathrm{dir}}^F(X)}.
   $$

   For the prime-counting functions $F \in \{\theta, \psi, \pi\}$ with their respective baselines $M(X)$ above, the radial term $E_{\mathrm{rad}}^F$ is governed by classical RH (up to primes $p \mid q$), whereas the directional anisotropy energy $E_{\mathrm{dir}}^F$ is governed by non-principal Dirichlet GRH (`DirichletLZerosOnLine`, [@conj-riemann-hypothesis]), yielding for $F = \psi$ and $q \le X$ the uniform directional bound $E_{\mathrm{dir}}^\psi(X) = O(X\ln^4(qX))$ (Parseval sums over the $\varphi - 1$ non-principal characters and divides by $\varphi$, yielding the factor $\frac{\varphi - 1}{\varphi} < 1$).

   Zooming from the macroscopic relative scale $O(X^{-1/2}\ln(qX))$ down to the fine $\sqrt{X}$ scale exposes deterministic prime-power biases. Define the Riemann prime-power counter $\Pi(X; q, a) := \sum_{p^k \le X,\, p^k \equiv a \pmod q} \frac{1}{k}$ and the $k$-th power residue multiplicity $c_k(q; a) := \#\{b \in G : b^k \equiv a \pmod q\}$ ($k = 2, 3$).    $$

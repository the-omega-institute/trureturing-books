::: {.remark #rem-ch39-anomaly-free-hypercharge-lines title="The anomaly-free solution set of one fermion generation and the number of colours (manual derivation and literature result)"}
**Manual derivation; that anomaly cancellation yields two hypercharge assignments at $N_c = 3$, Witten's global anomaly, and the charges for general $N_c$ are literature results, not formalized in this book.** Write one generation of left-handed Weyl fields as $Q_L : (\mathbf{N}_c, \mathbf{2}, y_Q)$, $u_R^c : (\overline{\mathbf{N}}_c, \mathbf{1}, y_u)$, $d_R^c : (\overline{\mathbf{N}}_c, \mathbf{1}, y_d)$, $L_L : (\mathbf{1}, \mathbf{2}, y_L)$, $e_R^c : (\mathbf{1}, \mathbf{1}, y_e)$, with $N_c \ge 2$ and no right-handed neutrino (or only a gauge singlet of hypercharge $0$, which enters no anomaly). With Dynkin index $\frac{1}{2}$ for the fundamental and the antifundamental, the four perturbative anomaly conditions (each up to a positive normalization) are

$$
\begin{aligned}
&[\mathrm{SU}(N_c)]^2\mathrm{U}(1)_Y : && 2y_Q + y_u + y_d = 0, \\
&[\mathrm{SU}(2)_L]^2\mathrm{U}(1)_Y : && N_c y_Q + y_L = 0, \\
&\mathrm{grav}^2\,\mathrm{U}(1)_Y : && 2N_c y_Q + N_c y_u + N_c y_d + 2y_L + y_e = 0, \\
&[\mathrm{U}(1)_Y]^3 : && 2N_c y_Q^3 + N_c y_u^3 + N_c y_d^3 + 2y_L^3 + y_e^3 = 0;
\end{aligned}
$$

$[\mathrm{SU}(N_c)]^3$ cancels between the two copies of $\mathbf{N}_c$ and the two of $\overline{\mathbf{N}}_c$, and $[\mathrm{SU}(2)_L]^3$ vanishes identically.

1. The first three give $y_d = -2y_Q - y_u$, $y_L = -N_c y_Q$ and $y_e = 2N_c y_Q$. With $t = y_u + y_Q$ the fourth becomes $6N_c\,y_Q\,(N_c y_Q - t)(N_c y_Q + t) = 0$. Over the rationals or the reals the solution set is therefore exactly three lines through the origin: $a\bigl(1, -(N_c + 1), N_c - 1, -N_c, 2N_c\bigr)$; the same line with the labels of $u_R^c$ and $d_R^c$ exchanged; and $(0, b, -b, 0, 0)$. On the last line neither the quark doublet nor the leptons carry hypercharge, so the electron charge cannot be $-1$. The hypotheses matter: for $N_c = 1$ the first condition is absent and $(1, 1, -1, -1, 0)$ satisfies the other three without lying on any of the lines; if a right-handed neutrino may carry nonzero hypercharge, the solution set is also larger.
2. On the first line with $y_L = -\frac{1}{2}$ one gets $Q_u = \frac{N_c + 1}{2N_c}$, $Q_d = -\frac{N_c - 1}{2N_c}$, $Q_\nu = 0$, $Q_e = -1$, which is curve I above. For every $N_c \ge 2$ the four anomalies vanish, the total charge of a generation $N_c(Q_u + Q_d) + Q_\nu + Q_e$ is $0$, and all charges are integer multiples of $\frac{1}{2N_c}$. Witten's global anomaly requires the total number of $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L$ doublets to be even; there are $N_c + 1$ per generation, so with an odd number of generations (three, for instance) $N_c$ must be odd. For odd $N_c$ the baryon made of $\frac{N_c + 1}{2}$ up-type and $\frac{N_c - 1}{2}$ down-type quarks has charge $1$, and its isospin mirror has charge $0$.
3. Fix only the quark charges $(+\frac{2}{3}, -\frac{1}{3})$, i.e. $(y_Q, y_u, y_d) = (\frac{1}{6}, -\frac{2}{3}, \frac{1}{3})$, and leave the lepton hypercharges free. The first condition then holds automatically, the second and third give $y_L = -\frac{N_c}{6}$ and $y_e = \frac{N_c}{3}$, and the left-hand side of the fourth becomes $\frac{N_c(N_c - 3)(N_c + 3)}{36}$.

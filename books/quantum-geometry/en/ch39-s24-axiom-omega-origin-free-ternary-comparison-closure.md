## 39.1.2 The Grand Unified Mother Axiom (AXIOM-Ω): Origin-Free Ternary Comparison Transport and Bi-Invariant Positivity {#sec-ch39-24-2}

Starting from zero external background, zero privileged coordinates, and zero presupposed dimension "$3$," we state $\mathrm{AXIOM}\text{-}\Omega$ with the fewest assumptions, aligned with the `H1–H3` bridges of [Appendix E](appendix-e.md) (the axiom itself is `MODEL`; its clauses match the Lean formalization [@def-ch39-ternary-comparison-structure] one by one): the carrier is merely a nonempty set $\mathcal{M}$, taken after `H1` (`T1`) comparison-indistinguishability quotienting ([`partition_inseparable_iff_kernel`](lean:D5/S3/ConceptDynamics/ObservationTopology/PartitionTopologyKernel.partition_inseparable_iff_kernel); [Appendix E §E.2](appendix-e-s02-logic-and-quotients.md#sec-appe-logic)), with no topology, manifold, or differentiable structure presupposed, and no base point or binary operation. It carries two primitives: **origin-free ternary comparison transport** $[-, -, -] : \mathcal{M} \times \mathcal{M} \times \mathcal{M} \to \mathcal{M}$ ("using $y$ as a local reference bridge, transport the comparison relation of $x$ relative to $y$ losslessly onto $z$") and a **symmetric fidelity reading** $\kappa : \mathcal{M} \times \mathcal{M} \to \mathbb{R}$, neither of which is required to be continuous or differentiable. The carrier need only meet two conditions, both written in terms of the reading itself:

- **Chain connectedness**: however high the "close enough" threshold $1 - \varepsilon$, any two points are joined by a finite chain whose consecutive readings all clear it,

  $$
  \forall\, \varepsilon > 0,\ \forall\, x, y \in \mathcal{M},\ \exists\, x = x_0, x_1, \dots, x_k = y:\qquad \kappa(x_{i-1}, x_i) \;>\; 1 - \varepsilon \quad (1 \le i \le k);
  $$

- **Reading completeness**: sequences that are Cauchy in the sense of the reading converge in the sense of the reading, that is, for every sequence $(x_n)_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$ in $\mathcal{M}$,

  $$
  \bigl(\forall\, \varepsilon > 0\ \exists\, N\ \forall\, m, n \ge N:\ \kappa(x_m, x_n) > 1 - \varepsilon\bigr) \;\implies\; \exists\, a \in \mathcal{M}\ \forall\, \varepsilon > 0\ \exists\, N\ \forall\, n \ge N:\ \kappa(x_n, a) > 1 - \varepsilon.
  $$

  This is exactly the `H3` Cauchy completion of [Appendix E](appendix-e.md) written for the chordal distance $d_\kappa(x, y) := 2\sqrt{1 - \kappa(x, y)}$: since $\kappa(x_m, x_n) > 1 - \varepsilon$ means $d_\kappa(x_m, x_n) < 2\sqrt{\varepsilon}$, reading completeness is word for word "$(\mathcal{M}, d_\kappa)$ is a complete metric space" ($d_\kappa$ is a metric; see Item 4 of [@rem-ch39-axiom-omega-formalization-bridge]).

The two primitives obey three indivisible intrinsic laws (`MODEL`, with Law II enforcing `H2` Gram positivity):

1. **Law (I) Origin-Free Cancellation and Associativity**: For all $x, y, z, u, v \in \mathcal{M}$,

   $$
   [x, x, z] \;=\; z, \qquad [x, z, z] \;=\; x, \qquad \bigl[[x, y, z],\, u,\, v\bigr] \;=\; \bigl[x,\, y,\, [z, u, v]\bigr].
   $$

2. **Law (II) Bi-Invariant Gram Positivity and Zero-Infimum Separation**: $\kappa(x, y) = \kappa(y, x)$, $\kappa(x, y) = 1 \iff x = y$, $\kappa(x, y) \ge 0$, the reading **touches zero** (the orthogonal-mismatch limit $\inf_{x, y \in \mathcal{M}}\kappa(x, y) = 0$: for every $\varepsilon > 0$ some pair of points reads below $\varepsilon$), and for all $n \ge 1$, $x_1, \dots, x_n, u, v \in \mathcal{M}$, and real $c_1, \dots, c_n$, two-sided transport invariance and Gram positivity ($\mathbf{G}_n = \bigl[\kappa(x_i, x_j)\bigr]_{i,j=1}^n \succeq 0$) hold:

   $$
   \kappa\bigl([x, u, v],\, [y, u, v]\bigr) \;=\; \kappa\bigl([u, v, x],\, [u, v, y]\bigr) \;=\; \kappa(x, y), \qquad \sum_{i,j=1}^{n} c_i c_j\,\kappa(x_i, x_j) \;\ge\; 0.
   $$

   The upper bound $\kappa \le 1$ need not be assumed: coefficients $(1, -1)$ on the pair $(x, y)$ give $2 - 2\kappa(x, y) \ge 0$, so the reading lies in $[0, 1]$ automatically (`manual derivation`).

3. **Law (III) Minimal Non-Abelian and Minimal-Rank Lock**: transport hosts nontrivial order sensitivity, and the **feature rank** $\operatorname{rk}\kappa$ of the reading—the least $r$ for which the reading is realizable as an inner product in Euclidean space $\mathbb{R}^r$ ($\operatorname{rk}\kappa = \infty$ when there is no such $r$)—is minimal among all non-commutative, chain-connected structures obeying Laws (I)–(II):

   $$
   \exists\, x, y, z \in \mathcal{M},\quad [x, y, z] \neq [z, y, x], \qquad \operatorname{rk}\kappa \;:=\; \min\bigl\{ r \in \mathbb{N} : \exists\, \varphi : \mathcal{M} \to \mathbb{R}^r,\ \kappa(x, y) = \langle\varphi(x), \varphi(y)\rangle \bigr\} \;\to\; \min.
   $$

This minimum is exactly $5$: every non-commutative chain-connected structure obeying Laws (I)–(II) has feature rank at least $5$ (Item 4 of [@thm-ch39-axiom-omega-rank-five-unit-quaternion-embedding], `LEAN ✓`); the unit-quaternion model belongs to this class and its reading is realizable in $\mathbb{R}^5$ (Item 3 of the same theorem, `LEAN ✓`); together these give "the minimum is exactly $5$" (`manual derivation`). A structure satisfying the mother axiom is therefore non-commutative, chain connected, reading complete, touches zero, and has feature rank $5$, and it is isomorphic to the unit-quaternion model: for every base point $o \in \mathcal{M}$ there is a bijection $u : \mathcal{M} \to \mathrm{Sp}(1)$ sending $o$ to $1$ and preserving transport and reading, under which transport becomes $u(x)u(y)^{-1}u(z)$ and the reading becomes $\frac{1 + \langle u(x), u(y)\rangle}{2}$ (Item 2 of [@thm-ch39-axiom-omega-complete-rank-five-isomorphism], `LEAN ✓`). The unit-quaternion group $\mathrm{Sp}(1)$ is the unit sphere $S^3(1)$ of $\mathbb{R}^4$ ([`coe_unitary_eq_sphere`](lean:D5/S3/Quantum/AURICOmega/QuaternionComparisonModel.coe_unitary_eq_sphere), `LEAN ✓`); writing $d_{S^3}(x, y) := \arccos\langle u(x), u(y)\rangle \in [0, \pi]$ for the great-circle distance of the two images, the reading is $\kappa(x, y) = \cos^2\frac{d_{S^3}(x, y)}{2}$ (`manual derivation`).

Now take any candidate structure $\mathcal{M}$ obeying Laws (I)–(II) and non-commutativity, pick any local observer slice $o \in \mathcal{M}$, and define $x \star_o z := [x, o, z]$ with local inverse $x^{-1_o} := [o, x, o]$.    $$

## 39.1.4 Unified Degeneration Spectrum, Formal Anchors, and Candidate-Dimension Audit {#sec-ch39-24-4}

We record below the formal definitions of ternary comparison structures and of $\mathrm{AXIOM}\text{-}\Omega$, the two kernel-verified main theorems cited in the concluding sentence of §39.1.2, the eleven earlier frozen Lean 4 anchors, classical literature, the correspondence between the mother axiom and the manifold form, and the candidate-dimension audit. Ternary comparison structures and their predicates come first; they match the clauses of §39.1.2 word for word, and the mother axiom is then the conjunction of these predicates:

::: {.definition #def-ch39-ternary-comparison-structure title="Ternary Comparison Structures and Their Non-Commutativity, Zero-Touching, Chain-Connectedness, Feature-Rank, and Reading-Completeness Predicates" lean="D5/S3/Quantum/AURICOmega/TernaryComparisonGroup.TernaryComparison,D5/S3/Quantum/AURICOmega/TernaryComparisonGroup.TernaryComparison.Noncommutative,D5/S3/Quantum/AURICOmega/TernaryComparisonGroup.TernaryComparison.TouchesZero,D5/S3/Quantum/AURICOmega/TernaryComparisonGroup.TernaryComparison.ChainConnected,D5/S3/Quantum/AURICOmega/TernaryComparisonGroup.TernaryComparison.GramRankLE,D5/S3/Quantum/AURICOmega/TernaryComparisonGroup.TernaryComparison.HasFeatureRank,D5/S3/Quantum/AURICOmega/TernaryComparisonIsomorphism.ReadingComplete"}
A **ternary comparison structure** on a set $M$ consists of a transfer $[-, -, -] : M \times M \times M \to M$ and a reading $\kappa : M \times M \to \mathbb{R}$ such that for all $x, y, z, u, v \in M$: $[x, x, z] = z$, $[x, z, z] = x$, $[[x, y, z], u, v] = [x, y, [z, u, v]]$; $\kappa(x, y) = \kappa(y, x)$; $\kappa(x, y) = 1 \iff x = y$; $\kappa(x, y) \ge 0$; $\kappa([x, u, v], [y, u, v]) = \kappa(x, y)$ and $\kappa([u, v, x], [u, v, y]) = \kappa(x, y)$; and for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$, $x_1, \dots, x_n \in M$, and $c_1, \dots, c_n \in \mathbb{R}$, $\sum_{i,j=1}^{n} c_i c_j\,\kappa(x_i, x_j) \ge 0$. For such a structure and $r \in \mathbb{N}$ we define:

1. **Non-commutative**: there are $x, y, z \in M$ with $[x, y, z] \neq [z, y, x]$;
2. **Touches zero**: for every $\varepsilon > 0$ there are $x, y \in M$ with $\kappa(x, y) < \varepsilon$;
3. **Chain connected**: for every $\varepsilon > 0$ and all $x, y \in M$ there is a finite sequence $x = x_0, x_1, \dots, x_k = y$ ($k \ge 0$) with $\kappa(x_{i-1}, x_i) > 1 - \varepsilon$ for $1 \le i \le k$;
4. **Gram rank at most $r$**: for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$ and $x_1, \dots, x_n \in M$, the matrix $[\kappa(x_i, x_j)]_{i,j=1}^n$ has rank at most $r$;
5. **Feature rank at most $r$** (the reading is realizable in Euclidean $r$-space): there is $\varphi : M \to \mathbb{R}^r$ with $\kappa(x, y) = \langle\varphi(x), \varphi(y)\rangle$ for all $x, y \in M$;
6. **Reading complete**: for every sequence $(x_n)_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$ in $M$ such that for every $\varepsilon > 0$ there is $N \in \mathbb{N}$ with $\kappa(x_m, x_n) > 1 - \varepsilon$ for all $m, n \ge N$, there is $a \in M$ such that for every $\varepsilon > 0$ there is $N \in \mathbb{N}$ with $\kappa(x_n, a) > 1 - \varepsilon$ for all $n \ge N$.
:::

**The kernel-verified route: why feature rank is at least five, and why rank five means unit quaternions.** First the most transparent case, by hand. The unit-quaternion model has feature map $\varphi(q) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(1, q_0, q_1, q_2, q_3) \in \mathbb{R}^5$, with $\langle\varphi(p), \varphi(q)\rangle = \frac{1 + \langle p, q\rangle}{2} = \kappa(p, q)$ ([`inner_feature`](lean:D5/S3/Quantum/AURICOmega/QuaternionComparisonModel.inner_feature), `LEAN ✓`). For the antipodal pair $\pm 1$:

$$
\varphi(1) = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(1, 1, 0, 0, 0), \qquad \varphi(-1) = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(1, -1, 0, 0, 0), \qquad \langle\varphi(1), \varphi(-1)\rangle = 0,

so the reading touches zero; the point of their segment nearest the origin is the midpoint $m = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(1, 0, 0, 0, 0)$, with $\lVert m\rVert^2 = \frac{1}{2}$. Every feature vector has the same component along $m$, $\langle m, \varphi(q)\rangle = \frac{1}{2} = \lVert m\rVert^2$, and $\varphi(q) - m = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(0, q_0, q_1, q_2, q_3)$ is just the quaternion's own four coordinates. None of the five columns can be dropped: for the five points $1, -1, i, j, k$ the table of readings has $1$ on the diagonal, $\kappa(1, -1) = 0$, and $\frac{1}{2}$ in every other off-diagonal entry; its determinant is $\frac{1}{8} \neq 0$, so its rank is $5$ and the reading of this model cannot be realized in $\mathbb{R}^4$ (`manual derivation`, in agreement with Item 2 of [@thm-ch39-axiom-omega-rank-five-unit-quaternion-embedding]). - **Unit vectors and two families of isometries**: $\kappa(x, x) = 1$ and $\kappa \ge 0$ make the feature vectors unit vectors pairwise within $90^\circ$, so their closed convex hull avoids zero and its point $m$ nearest the origin is nonzero. Fix a base point $o$; left translations $x \mapsto [g, o, x]$ and right translations $x \mapsto [x, o, h]$ preserve the reading (Law II), extend to linear isometries of the feature span (a Gram-preserving permutation of a family spanning the whole space extends uniquely to a linear isometry: [`existsUnique_linearIsometryEquiv_of_gram_preserving`](lean:D5/S3/Quantum/AURICOmega/GramIsometryExtension.existsUnique_linearIsometryEquiv_of_gram_preserving), `LEAN ✓`), and commute by associativity $[[g, o, x], o, h] = [g, o, [x, o, h]]$; left translations permute the feature vectors, hence fix $m$ (for a family of unit vectors with pairwise nonnegative inner products, the point of least norm in the closed convex hull is nonzero and is fixed by every linear isometry mapping the family into itself: [`exists_fixed_ne_zero_of_pairwise_inner_nonneg`](lean:D5/S3/Quantum/AURICOmega/NonobtuseFamilyFixedVector.exists_fixed_ne_zero_of_pairwise_inner_nonneg), `LEAN ✓`), so every $\varphi(x)$ has component $\lVert m\rVert^2$ along $m$.
- **Quaternionic coordinates**: on the orthogonal complement $E$ of $m$ (of dimension at most $r - 1$), the centered vectors $\varphi(x) - m$ have Gram kernel $\kappa - \lVert m\rVert^2$. Suppose $\dim E \le 4$. Chain connectedness makes left translations generated by translations close to the identity that send no nonzero vector to its negative, so an invariant line would be fixed pointwise (contradicting that left translations have no common nonzero fixed vector); if $E$ split into two invariant planes, only mutually commuting rotations would remain on them, and the whole group would commute (contradicting non-commutativity). So left translations act irreducibly on $E$ ([`invariant_submodule_eq_bot_or_top`](lean:D5/S3/Quantum/AURICOmega/SmallInvariantSubspaceExclusion.invariant_submodule_eq_bot_or_top), `LEAN ✓`), and the right translations, commuting with them but not with each other, force quaternionic coordinates on $E$ (the classical algebra of Schur and Frobenius), with $\dim E = 4$ ([`exists_quaternion_coordinates`](lean:D5/S3/Quantum/AURICOmega/CommutantQuaternionCoordinates.exists_quaternion_coordinates), `LEAN ✓`). In all cases $\dim E \ge 4$, hence $r \ge 5$.
- **The coefficient locks at rank five**: for $r = 5$ the reading is $\kappa(x, y) = a + (1 - a)\langle q(x), q(y)\rangle$ with $a = \lVert m\rVert^2 < 1$ and $q$ injective into the unit quaternions. Touching zero with $\langle q(x), q(y)\rangle \ge -1$ gives $a \le \frac{1}{2}$; a unit quaternion $p \neq 1$ has a power with real part at most $-\operatorname{Re}p$, so readings between distinct points are at most $2a$, while chain connectedness supplies such readings arbitrarily close to $1$, giving $a \ge \frac{1}{2}$—exactly the number computed by hand above.

Item 7 gives an upper bound on the feature rank of a product reading. For independently variable unit quaternions, five sample points per factor suffice to determine the exact dimension.

::: {.theorem #thm-ch39-quaternion-product-feature-rank title="Least real feature dimension of a product of unit-quaternion readings" lean="D5/S3/Quantum/AURICOmega/Products/QuaternionProductFeatureRank.least_feature_rank"}
Let $N\in\mathbb N$ and define the product reading on $(S^3)^N$ by

$$
K_N(q,p)=\prod_{a=1}^N\frac{1+\langle q_a,p_a\rangle}{2}.
$$

The least $r\in\mathbb N$ for which there exists a map $\Phi:(S^3)^N\to\mathbb R^r$ satisfying $K_N(q,p)=\langle\Phi(q),\Phi(p)\rangle$ for all $q,p$ is $5^N$.
:::

::: proof
For one factor, set $f(q)=(1,q)/\sqrt{2}\in\mathbb R^5$. Then $\langle f(q),f(p)\rangle=K_1(q,p)$. Sample the five points $Q=(1,-1,i,j,k)$. The matrix with rows $(1,q)$ in this order has upper left block $\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\1&-1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$, lower right block $I_3$, and zero upper right block. Its determinant is $-2$. After scaling, the Gram matrix $G$ of the five vectors $f(q)$ therefore has $\det G=(-2)^2/2^5=1/8$.

For $N$ factors, the vectors $f(q_1)\otimes\cdots\otimes f(q_N)$ realize $K_N$ by inner products in $5^N$ real coordinates. To see that all these coordinates are necessary, sample every configuration in $Q^N$. Its Gram matrix is the invertible Kronecker power $G^{\otimes N}$. Any realization in $\mathbb R^r$ must therefore accommodate $5^N$ linearly independent vectors, so the least possible $r$ is exactly $5^N$. The empty product has a single configuration with reading $1$ and feature dimension $1$. The full proof is checked by the Lean kernel.

::: {.remark #rem-ch39-independent-central-flips title="How many three-dimensional cells do independent central flips determine?"}
**Changing the class over which rank is minimized (model).** Retain laws (I) and (II), chain connectedness and completeness of the readings. Choose a temporary origin to form a group $G$ and take closures in the comparison chordal metric. Specify $k\ge1$ independent central flips and require the group $C\cong(\mathbb Z/2)^k$ they generate to lie in the closure of the commutator subgroup $[G,G]$. Every continuous one-dimensional unitary representation therefore sends these flips to the identity. The least real feature rank in this class is $4k+1$. At equality, $G\cong\mathrm{SU}(2)^k$ and the full family of minimizing comparison kernels, in unit quaternion coordinates, is

$$
\kappa(x,y)=\sum_{j=1}^k p_j\frac{1+\operatorname{Re}(x_j^{-1}y_j)}{2},\qquad p_j>0,\qquad \sum_{j=1}^k p_j=1.
$$

**Lower bound and realization (manual derivation).** For each finite-rank competitor the minimal real Gram feature map embeds the carrier in a finite-dimensional unit sphere. Completeness makes its image closed and hence compact. A compact chain-connected metric space is connected. The bi-invariant chordal metric makes multiplication and inversion continuous. Thus $f(g)=\kappa(e,g)$ is a continuous, nonnegative, positive-definite class function on a compact connected group, with $f(e)=1$. The finite-rank character expansion of $f$ is a positively weighted sum of normalized irreducible complex characters $\chi_\lambda/d_\lambda$, with weights summing to one. The matrix coefficients of each supported representation belong to the complex span of the continuous sections $x\mapsto f(xy^{-1})$, making the representations continuous in the comparison topology. [Character expansion for a finite-rank positive-definite class function](lean:D5/S3/FiniteGroups/GroupAlgebra/PositiveDefiniteClassFunction.exists_sum_character_of_finiteDimensional)

The feature rank is $\sum_\lambda d_\lambda^2$ over the support, since complexification preserves the rank of a real kernel. [Rank of a character kernel](lean:D5/S3/FiniteGroups/GroupAlgebra/CharacterKernelRank.finrank_span_sum_character_kernel)

The Haar mean of $f$ is strictly positive, so the trivial representation contributes one dimension. The central group $C$ acts on each irreducible by a sign character. If these signs failed to span the dual space $C^*$, a nonidentity flip would act trivially on the entire support and give a reading of one, violating separation. We can therefore choose $k$ independent signs, each carried by a representation of dimension at least two. Write $r$ for the number of nontrivial one-dimensional representations in the support and $d_j\ge2$ for the dimensions of the remaining $m$ representations. The signs outside the trivial term are jointly injective on $C$, with the first $r$ entries constant. The [dimension bound for finite sign readings](lean:D5/S3/Fourier/CharacterSelection/CentralSignDimensionCost.central_sign_dimension_cost) gives $k\le m$ and $1+r+\sum_j d_j^2\ge4k+1$. Equality holds precisely when $m=k$, $r=0$, all $d_j=2$, and the remaining sign readings give a bijection on the binary cube. Conversely, in the quaternion product displayed above, each coordinate flip $-1$ is the commutator $[i,j]$ within its factor. Positive weights ensure separation, and taking all signs negative attains zero. A constant feature and the four real coordinates in each factor realize the kernel in $4k+1$ dimensions, attaining the bound.

**Why does equality give the whole product? (Manual derivation.)** Only the trivial representation and exactly $k$ two-dimensional representations can occur, with independent central signs. Since $f$ is real, the support contains the complex conjugate of each representation. Conjugation preserves its central sign, so each two-dimensional representation is equivalent to its conjugate. Its determinant is real and of unit modulus, hence identically one by connectedness. Separation makes the combined map $G\to\mathrm{SU}(2)^k$ faithful. Its compact connected projection to each factor acts irreducibly and therefore fills the factor, since every proper connected closed subgroup of $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ is contained in a one-dimensional torus. The Lie algebra of the image maps onto each $\mathfrak{su}(2)$, and its center projects to zero in every factor. Decompose this compact semisimple Lie algebra into simple ideals. Each factor receives an isomorphism from exactly one ideal, and each ideal occurs at least once. If one ideal supplied two factors, the two group projections would differ by an automorphism of $\mathrm{SU}(2)$, because a Lie algebra automorphism integrates on the simply connected factor and the source group is connected. That automorphism fixes $-1$, giving both projections the same central sign, contrary to independence. The image thus has the Lie algebra of the whole product and fills the connected product.

Now $f=a_0+\sum_j a_j\operatorname{Re}(q_j)$. All $a_j$ are positive, and $f$ takes its minimum with all signs negative. Attainment of zero and normalization give $a_0=\sum_j a_j=1/2$, yielding the displayed weights $p_j=2a_j$. Invariance under every factor permutation further forces $p_j=1/k$. The given number of flips $k$ yields $3k$ local operational parameters. If the flips need only be central, then for $k\ge2$ the nonabelian group $\mathrm{SU}(2)\times(S^1)^{k-1}$ admits a positive mixture of one quaternion kernel and $k-1$ circle kernels $(1+\cos\theta)/2$, of rank only $1+4+2(k-1)=2k+3$. Spatial propagation, composition of quantum instruments and interactions still require dynamical input. [Central flips, rank and classification at equality](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing-books/issues/987)

::: {.remark #rem-ch39-two-rotor-energy-scale title="Do the comparison readings of two rotors determine their excitation energies?"}
Take two independently variable, labeled unit rotors. The comparison distinguishes $q$ from $-q$, so the state of each rotor is a point of the double cover $\mathrm{Spin}(3)$. The joint internal configuration space is $S^3\times S^3$. The product of the two three-dimensional manifolds has six real configuration parameters. Two rotors at the same point of ordinary three-dimensional space still require these six parameters to record their orientations. By [@thm-ch39-quaternion-product-feature-rank], the least real inner-product feature dimension of $K_2$ is $25$. This result uses the individual comparison readings and the rule that multiplies them. This rank exceeds the minimum of five selected by Law (III) of Ω. The unique zero partner of a single rotor also becomes a whole family. Given $(q_1,q_2)$, setting $p_1=-q_1$ gives $K_2(q,p)=0$ for every $p_2\in S^3$.

**Choosing a kinetic energy (model and manual derivation).** On $L^2(S^3\times S^3)$ consider a local second-order scalar kinetic operator invariant under independent left and right translations in each factor. Take it to be self-adjoint with positive definite principal symbol and set the energy of the constant state to zero. At the group identity the symbol is invariant under the independent adjoint rotations $\mathrm{SO}(3)\times\mathrm{SO}(3)$. An invariant symmetric form on either three-dimensional tangent factor is a scalar multiple of its round form. Rotating one factor independently forces the mixed term to vanish. These rotations also have no invariant first-order direction. Thus $H=a(-\Delta_1)+b(-\Delta_2)$ for the unit round factors, with independent positive coefficients $a,b$.

Scalar spherical harmonics of degree $\ell$ on the unit round $S^3$ have eigenvalue $\ell(\ell+2)$ for the negative Laplacian, with multiplicity $(\ell+1)^2$. A product mode has energy $E_{\ell m}=a\ell(\ell+2)+bm(m+2)$ and multiplicity $(\ell+1)^2(m+1)^2$. Add the mode multiplicities when energies coincide. Order the distinct energy values and take $0<a\le b$. For this mode formula, [@thm-ch39-rotor-product-levels] identifies the first two positive levels. For $b,c\ge a$, it also characterizes when the second energies coincide. For $a<b$ the first positive value is $3a$ and the second is $\min(3b,8a)$. The first comes from $(1,0)$, the two candidates for the second from $(0,1)$ and $(2,0)$. Exciting both factors costs at least $3a+3b$. At $b=8a/3$ the two candidates coincide, with multiplicity $4+9=13$. For $b>8a/3$ the first two positive levels stay at $3a,8a$, with multiplicities four and nine. These two levels and their multiplicities give the same readings throughout this range of $b$. Take $a=1$ and choose $b=3/2$ or $b=2$. The two systems have identical $K_2$ readings, while the ratio of the second positive energy to the first is $3/2$ or $2$, respectively.

**How does exchange symmetry affect the prediction?** Exchanging the factors preserves $K_2$. Requiring it also to preserve $H$ gives $a=b$. The modes $(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$ then share the first positive energy $3a$, of multiplicity eight. The second is $6a$, from $(1,1)$, with multiplicity sixteen. Another prescription uses the metric defined by the quadratic part of $K_2$ near the diagonal. For geodesic separations $d_1,d_2$ in the two factors, $1-K_2=(d_1^2+d_2^2)/4+O((d_1^2+d_2^2)^2)$. Choosing the scalar kinetic energy to be a common positive multiple of the negative Laplacian of this scaled product metric also gives $a=b$. The equal-scale model and the model with $b=2a$ both give an energy ratio of $2$, with first-level multiplicities eight and four, respectively. Testing these predictions requires resolving distinct energies and their degeneracies. [Low energies and dynamical assumptions for two rotors](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing-books/issues/925)

One can also prescribe comparison operations that generate diffusion (model and manual derivation). Propose a new orientation for each rotor independently with uniform Haar probability. Given the two proposals, run $m$ comparisons per factor. Each trial succeeds with probability $K_1(q_i,p_i)$ for that factor, and all $2m$ trials are conditionally independent. Accept the pair if all comparisons succeed and draw a fresh proposal after any failure. On a clock counting accepted steps, the one-factor transition density is $K_1(q,p)^m/Z_m$, where its Haar normalization $Z_m$ is independent of the current state, and its degree-one harmonic multiplier is $m/(m+3)$. For fixed degree $j$ and $m\ge j$, the one-step multiplier is $\prod_{k=0}^{j-1}(m-k)/(m+k+3)$. For fixed $t>0$, its $\lfloor mt\rfloor$-th power tends to $e^{-tj(j+2)}$ as $m\to\infty$. Finite sums of harmonics are dense and all operators are contractions, giving the strong $L^2$ limit $\exp[t(\Delta_1+\Delta_2)]$. Changing only the second factor to $\lfloor rm\rfloor$ comparisons per proposal for $r>0$ gives a relative diffusion rate $1/r$ on the same clock. This experiment specifies a heat semigroup. Interpreting $-(\Delta_1+r^{-1}\Delta_2)$ as a physical quantum Hamiltonian requires a dynamical assumption and a time unit. [Spherical means](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF03322314) · [Harmonic multipliers and limiting dynamics](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing-books/issues/925#issuecomment-7075628219)

For a relative orientation $p^{-1}q\ne\pm1$, run the same protocol on the circle of rotors commuting with it. Read the return density at the starting point after $\lfloor\pi mt\rfloor$ accepted steps. In the limit $m\to\infty$, each integer Fourier mode $k$ contributes $e^{-\pi t k^2}$ with unit weight. Poisson summation gives scale inversion for their sum, yielding the [circle heat spectrum of §39.2](ch39-s25-axiom-omega-five-tier-deduction-cascade.md#sec-ch39-25-2). [Derivation of the circle return density](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing-books/issues/925#issuecomment-7075769017)

The joint update can also change while preserving both single-rotor transition laws. Identify the axes for right increments of the two rotors. Make a fresh independent choice at each accepted step. With probability $c\in[0,1]$ draw a common increment from the one-rotor law above, and otherwise draw two independent increments from that law. Start from equal orientations and read the relative orientation $D(q,p)=q\cdot p$. A shared right increment preserves $D$ exactly. Independent increments multiply its conditional mean by $[m/(m+3)]^2$. In the diffusion limit, when the single-rotor first harmonic has decayed to half its initial value, the mean relative reading is $1/4$ for $c=0$ and $1/2$ for $c=1/2$. This classically correlated update uses a shared frame for the right increments. For $c>0$ the transition law is invariant under simultaneous right rotations of both factors. General independent right rotations change the joint law. [Finite-step mean relative orientation](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing-books/issues/925#issuecomment-7075637035)

::: {.theorem #thm-ch39-rotor-product-levels title="First two distinct positive values of the product rotor ladder" lean="D5/S3/Quantum/Rotors/ProductBranchLevels.first_two_positive_levels,D5/S3/Quantum/Rotors/ProductBranchLevels.second_level_fiber"}
For $\ell,m\in\mathbb N$, let $E_{\ell m}=a\ell(\ell+2)+bm(m+2)$. Define $S_a(b)=6a$ for $b=a$ and $S_a(b)=\min(3b,8a)$ otherwise. If $0<a\le b$, the least positive member of $\{E_{\ell m}\}$ is $3a$, and its least member strictly above $3a$ is $S_a(b)$.

For any $a>0$ and real $b,c$, the equality $S_a(b)=S_a(c)$ holds exactly when at least one of three conditions holds. Either $b=c$, or $\{b,c\}=\{a,2a\}$, or both $3b\ge8a$ and $3c\ge8a$.
:::

::: proof
For a natural index $n$, the value $n(n+2)$ is zero, three or at least eight. With $a\le b$, separate the zero modes, first excitations and higher excitations. The pair $(1,0)$ attains $3a$. For $a=b$, $(1,1)$ attains $6a$. For $a<b$, the lower of the two modes $(0,1)$ and $(2,0)$ attains $S_a(b)$. The remaining modes leave no value strictly between those two consecutive levels. Expanding the equal-scale case and the two branches of the minimum gives all the conditions for equal outputs of $S_a$. The full proof is checked by the Lean kernel.
:::

For $N$ independently variable rotors in the multiple-field interpretation of [§32.3.3](ch39-s20-ternion-field-composition-and-hilbert-reprojection.md#sec-ch39-20-3), $3N$ is the dimension of the configuration manifold and $5^N$ is the feature rank of this particular comparison kernel. The kernel compares rotors. For instance, $K_1(1,-1)=0$, while $1$ and $-1$ acting on a fixed reference spinor give the same ray, with ray reading $1$ by [@thm-ch39-axiom-omega-ray-kernel-born-weight]. Applying this description to a physical system requires identifying its degrees of freedom and their joint reading.

Item 5 gives only an injection because a structure may occupy just part of the unit quaternions: the countable subgroup of rational unit quaternions satisfies every hypothesis of Item 5 yet cannot be put in one-to-one correspondence with $\mathrm{Sp}(1)$ (`manual derivation`; that a countable dense subgroup satisfying these hypotheses without reading completeness exists is kernel-checked as [`exists_subgroupModel_not_readingComplete`](lean:D5/S3/Quantum/AURICOmega/TernaryComparisonCompletion.exists_subgroupModel_not_readingComplete), `LEAN ✓`), although the part it occupies is always dense in $\mathrm{Sp}(1)$ ([`denseRange_iff`](lean:D5/S3/Quantum/AURICOmega/TernaryComparisonCompletion.denseRange_iff), `LEAN ✓`; applying it to the injection of Item 5 is a `manual derivation`; see §39.1.2). ::: {.theorem #thm-ch39-axiom-omega-complete-rank-five-isomorphism title="Reading-Complete Rank-Five Ternary Comparison Structures Are Isomorphic to the Unit Quaternions" lean="D5/S3/Quantum/AURICOmega/UnitQuaternionClosedSubgroup.eq_top_of_isClosed_of_chainConnected,D5/S3/Quantum/AURICOmega/TernaryComparisonIsomorphism.exists_unitQuaternion_equiv"}
Keep the notation of [@thm-ch39-axiom-omega-rank-five-unit-quaternion-embedding], and for $p, q \in \mathrm{Sp}(1)$ write $\kappa_{\mathbb{H}}(p, q) := \frac{1 + \langle p, q\rangle}{2}$.

1. Let $H$ be a subgroup of $\mathrm{Sp}(1)$ that is closed in $\mathrm{Sp}(1)$ (with the subspace topology from $\mathbb{H}$), contains $x, y$ with $xy \neq yx$, and is such that for every $\varepsilon > 0$ and all $x, y \in H$ there is a finite sequence $x = h_0, h_1, \dots, h_k = y$ in $H$ ($k \ge 0$) with $\kappa_{\mathbb{H}}(h_{i-1}, h_i) > 1 - \varepsilon$ for $1 \le i \le k$. Then $H = \mathrm{Sp}(1)$.
2. Let the ternary comparison structure $(M, [-, -, -], \kappa)$ ([@def-ch39-ternary-comparison-structure]) be non-commutative, chain connected, and reading complete, let it touch zero, and let its reading be realized by some $\varphi : M \to \mathbb{R}^5$. Then for every $o \in M$ there is a bijection $u : M \to \mathrm{Sp}(1)$ with $u(o) = 1$, $u([x, y, z]) = u(x)\,u(y)^{-1}\,u(z)$ for all $x, y, z \in M$, and $\kappa(x, y) = \kappa_{\mathbb{H}}(u(x), u(y))$ for all $x, y \in M$.
:::

::: proof
Item 1 takes three steps. First, pick an element of $H$ other than $1$; a chain from $1$ to it must contain a step from $1$ to an element other than $1$, so $H$ contains nontrivial elements $h$ whose reading $\kappa_{\mathbb{H}}(1, h)$ is arbitrarily close to $1$. Second, write these in polar form $h = \cos\theta + \sin\theta\,n$ with $n$ a unit imaginary quaternion and $\theta \to 0$; the unit imaginary quaternions form a compact two-sphere, so along a subsequence $n$ tends to some $a$. By de Moivre's formula $(\cos\theta + \sin\theta\,n)^N = \cos N\theta + \sin N\theta\,n$, the powers with exponent $\lfloor t/\theta\rfloor$ tend along that subsequence to $\cos t + \sin t\,a$, and since $H$ is closed the whole circle $\{\cos t + \sin t\,a\}$ about the axis $a$ lies in $H$. Third, $H$ is non-commutative, so some element fails to commute with this axis (the elements that do commute lie in the plane spanned by $1$ and $a$ and commute with each other); a chain from $1$ to that element at reading threshold $\frac{1}{2}$ has a step from an element commuting with the axis to one that does not, and the quotient $g$ of that step does not commute with the axis and has $\kappa_{\mathbb{H}}(1, g) > \frac{1}{2}$. Conjugating the axis by $g$ gives a second axis that is neither $a$ nor $-a$; conjugating one axis by the circle about another reaches more and more axes, until every unit imaginary quaternion is an axis. Every unit quaternion is $\cos t + \sin t\,n$ for some axis $n$, so $H = \mathrm{Sp}(1)$.

Item 2: take the injection of Item 5 of [@thm-ch39-axiom-omega-rank-five-unit-quaternion-embedding] and multiply on the left by $u(o)^{-1}$ so that $o$ goes to $1$; left multiplication by a unit quaternion preserves the real inner product, so transfer and reading are still preserved. The image $H$ is a subgroup: $[x, o, y]$ goes to the product of the two images and $[o, x, o]$ to the inverse of the image. On unit quaternions $\kappa_{\mathbb{H}}(p, q) = 1 - \frac{\lVert p - q\rVert^2}{4}$ ([`kappa_eq_one_sub_dist_sq`](lean:D5/S3/Quantum/AURICOmega/TernaryComparisonIsomorphism.kappa_eq_one_sub_dist_sq)), so a sequence in $H$ that converges in $\mathbb{H}$ has preimages whose readings tend to $1$ uniformly; reading completeness supplies a point of $M$ whose image is the limit of the sequence, so $H$ is closed. Chains in $M$ map to chains in $H$; by Item 1 of [@thm-ch39-axiom-omega-rank-five-unit-quaternion-embedding] the group based at $o$ contains two non-commuting points, and their images do not commute. Item 1 then gives $H = \mathrm{Sp}(1)$, so the normalized injection is also surjective. The full proof is checked by the Lean kernel.
:::

The classical route of the manifold form (Step 1 of §39.1.3), by contrast, eliminates low-dimensional non-commutative Lie algebras through an $\operatorname{Ad}$-invariant positive-definite inner product, and the purely algebraic part of that step is kernel-checked by the following theorem; it serves only the classical route, and the rank route does not need it.

::: {.theorem #thm-ch39-axiom-omega-invariant-form-low-dimension title="Low-Dimensional Lie Algebras with an Invariant Form" lean="D5/S3/Quantum/Algebra/InvariantMetric/LowDimensionInvariantMetric.isLieAbelian_of_finrank_le_two,D5/S3/Quantum/Algebra/InvariantMetric/LowDimensionInvariantMetric.exists_basis_bracket_eq_smul_cross"}
Let $L$ be a Lie algebra over a field $K$ and $\Phi : L \times L \to K$ a bilinear form (not assumed symmetric). Call $\Phi$ **invariant** if $\Phi([x, y], z) = -\Phi(y, [x, z])$ for all $x, y, z \in L$, and **left-separating** if, for $x \in L$, $\Phi(x, y) = 0$ for all $y \in L$ implies $x = 0$. For a basis $b_0, b_1, b_2$ of $L$ and $v \in L$, write $v_b \in K^3$ for the coordinates of $v$ in that basis.

1. ([`isLieAbelian_of_finrank_le_two`](lean:D5/S3/Quantum/Algebra/InvariantMetric/LowDimensionInvariantMetric.isLieAbelian_of_finrank_le_two)) If $L$ is finite-dimensional with $\dim_K L \le 2$ and $\Phi$ is invariant and left-separating, then $L$ is Abelian: $[x, y] = 0$ for all $x, y \in L$.
2. ([`exists_basis_bracket_eq_smul_cross`](lean:D5/S3/Quantum/Algebra/InvariantMetric/LowDimensionInvariantMetric.exists_basis_bracket_eq_smul_cross)) Let $K = \mathbb{R}$, $\dim_{\mathbb{R}} L = 3$, $L$ non-Abelian, $\Phi$ invariant, and $\Phi(x, x) > 0$ for all $x \in L$ with $x \neq 0$. Then there are a basis $b_0, b_1, b_2$ of $L$ and a real number $c > 0$ such that $\Phi(b_i, b_j) = \delta_{ij}$ for all $i, j \in \{0, 1, 2\}$ and $[x, y]_b = c\,(x_b \times y_b)$ for all $x, y \in L$, where $\times$ is the cross product on $\mathbb{R}^3$.
:::

::: proof
Both items start from one observation: for an invariant form, the bracket $[x, y]$ is orthogonal to $x$ and to $y$ in both slots of $\Phi$. By invariance and $[x, x] = 0$, $\Phi([x, y], x) = -\Phi(y, [x, x]) = 0$ and $\Phi(x, [x, y]) = -\Phi([x, x], y) = 0$; using $[x, y] = -[y, x]$ as well, $\Phi([x, y], y) = \Phi(x, [y, y]) = 0$ and $\Phi(y, [x, y]) = -\Phi([x, y], y) = 0$.

Item 1: suppose $[x, y] \neq 0$ for some $x, y$. Then $x \neq 0$, and $x, y$ are linearly independent: if $s x + t y = 0$, bracketing with $x$ gives $t\,[x, y] = 0$, so $t = 0$, and then $s x = 0$ gives $s = 0$. The dimension is at most two, so $x, y$ span $L$. The linear functional $\Phi([x, y], \cdot)$ vanishes on $x$ and on $y$, hence on all of $L$, and left separation gives $[x, y] = 0$, a contradiction.

Item 2: pick $x, y$ with $[x, y] \neq 0$; then $x \neq 0$, and invariance gives $\Phi([x, [x, y]], y) = -\Phi([x, y], [x, y]) < 0$, so $[x, [x, y]] \neq 0$. Thus $x$, $[x, y]$, and $[x, [x, y]]$ are all nonzero and, by the observation above, pairwise orthogonal in both slots. Rescale $x$ and $[x, y]$ by positive factors to $\Phi$-unit vectors $e_1, e_2$; then $[e_1, e_2]$ is a positive multiple of $[x, [x, y]]$, and normalizing it to $e_3$ gives $[e_1, e_2] = c\,e_3$ with $c > 0$. Three pairwise orthogonal unit vectors are linearly independent, so in dimension three they form a basis (the $b_0, b_1, b_2$ of the theorem), and the $k$-th coordinate of any vector is its $\Phi$-value against $e_k$. By invariance, $\Phi([e_2, e_3], e_1) = -\Phi(e_3, [e_2, e_1]) = c\,\Phi(e_3, e_3) = c$, and $[e_2, e_3]$ is orthogonal to $e_2$ and $e_3$, so $[e_2, e_3] = c\,e_1$; likewise $\Phi([e_3, e_1], e_2) = -\Phi(e_1, [e_3, e_2]) = c$ gives $[e_3, e_1] = c\,e_2$. Together with antisymmetry, every bracket of basis vectors is $c$ times the corresponding cross-product structure constant; both sides are bilinear in $x, y$, so agreement on the basis means agreement everywhere. The full proof is checked by the Lean kernel.
:::

The Peter–Weyl kernel selection kept in Step 2 of §39.1.3 is a classical explanation, and two of its ingredients can be handed to the kernel: how the rank of a character kernel is counted, and whether a compact group has enough finite-dimensional unitary representations. ::: {.theorem #thm-ch39-axiom-omega-character-kernel-rank title="The Rank of a Character Kernel" lean="D5/S3/FiniteGroups/GroupAlgebra/CharacterKernelRank.finrank_span_sum_character_kernel"}
Let $k$ be an algebraically closed field, $G$ a group (not assumed finite and carrying no topology), and $\iota$ a finite index set. For each $i \in \iota$ let $W_i$ be a finite-dimensional $k$-vector space, $\rho_i$ a representation of $G$ on $W_i$ (a group homomorphism $G \to \mathrm{GL}(W_i)$) with character $\chi_i(g) := \operatorname{tr}\rho_i(g)$, and $c_i \in k$. Assume:

1. each $\rho_i$ is irreducible: $W_i \neq 0$, and the only subspaces of $W_i$ invariant under every $\rho_i(g)$ are $0$ and $W_i$;
2. the $\rho_i$ are pairwise inequivalent: for $i \neq j$ there is no linear isomorphism $\varphi : W_i \to W_j$ with $\varphi \circ \rho_i(g) = \rho_j(g) \circ \varphi$ for all $g \in G$;
3. $c_i \neq 0$ for every $i \in \iota$.

Then the $k$-subspace of the space of all $k$-valued functions on $G$ spanned by the family $\{\, x \mapsto \sum_{i \in \iota} c_i\,\chi_i(x y^{-1}) \;:\; y \in G \,\}$ has dimension $\sum_{i \in \iota} (\dim_k W_i)^2$.
:::

::: proof
Regard each $W_i$ as a module over the group algebra $k[G]$ and form the product module $\prod_i W_i$. Let $\phi$ be a $k[G]$-module endomorphism of it. By Schur's lemma (this is where algebraic closedness of $k$ enters), the component of $\phi$ from the $i$-th factor to itself is a scalar $a_i$; a nonzero intertwining map between irreducible representations is an isomorphism, and pairwise inequivalence rules isomorphisms out, so the component from the $j$-th factor to the $i$-th ($i \neq j$) vanishes. Hence $\phi$ acts on each factor by a scalar, and every $(A_i)_i \in \prod_i \operatorname{End}_k(W_i)$ commutes with $\phi$. The product module is finitely generated over its endomorphism ring, and the Jacobson density theorem shows that every $(A_i)_i$ is the action of some element of the group algebra; thus the operators $\{(\rho_i(g))_i : g \in G\}$ span $\prod_i \operatorname{End}_k(W_i)$ (Burnside's theorem for several representations).

Define the linear map $\Phi : \prod_i \operatorname{End}_k(W_i) \to k^G$ by $\Phi(A)(x) := \sum_i c_i \operatorname{tr}\bigl(\rho_i(x) A_i\bigr)$. Since $\chi_i(x y^{-1}) = \operatorname{tr}\bigl(\rho_i(x)\rho_i(y^{-1})\bigr)$, the function indexed by $y$ is exactly $\Phi\bigl((\rho_i(y^{-1}))_i\bigr)$; as $y \mapsto y^{-1}$ is a bijection of $G$, the span of these functions is the image of $\Phi$. If $\Phi(A) = 0$, the weighted trace pairing $(B, A) \mapsto \sum_i c_i \operatorname{tr}(B_i A_i)$ vanishes on the operators $B = (\rho_i(x))_i$, which span the whole space, hence on every $B$; taking $B$ nonzero only in the $j$-th slot and equal to the rank-one operator $w \mapsto \psi(w)\,v$ gives the value $c_j\,\psi(A_j v)$, and the nonzero weight forces $A_j = 0$. So $\Phi$ is injective and its image has dimension $\dim_k \prod_i \operatorname{End}_k(W_i) = \sum_i (\dim_k W_i)^2$. The full proof is checked by the Lean kernel.
:::

::: {.theorem #thm-ch39-axiom-omega-compact-group-unitary-kernel title="Finite-Dimensional Unitary Representations of a Compact Group Have Arbitrarily Small Kernels" lean="D5/S3/Quantum/Analysis/CompactGroups/UnitaryRepresentationKernel.exists_unitaryRep_ker_subset,D5/S3/Quantum/Analysis/CompactGroups/ContinuousKernelCompact.exists_isCompactOperator_kernel"}
1. ([`exists_isCompactOperator_kernel`](lean:D5/S3/Quantum/Analysis/CompactGroups/ContinuousKernelCompact.exists_isCompactOperator_kernel)) Let $X$ and $Y$ be compact topological spaces, let the measurable structure on $Y$ contain all open sets, and let $\mu$ be a finite measure on $Y$; let the scalar field $\mathbb{K}$ be $\mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$ (an `RCLike` field in Lean), $p \in [1, \infty]$, and $K : X \times Y \to \mathbb{K}$ continuous. Write $C(X)$ for the space of continuous $\mathbb{K}$-valued functions on $X$ with the sup norm. Then there is a continuous linear map $T : L^p(\mu) \to C(X)$ that is a compact operator (some neighbourhood of $0$ is mapped into a compact set) and satisfies $(Tf)(x) = \int_Y K(x, y)\,f(y)\,d\mu(y)$ for all $f \in L^p(\mu)$ and $x \in X$.
2. ([`exists_unitaryRep_ker_subset`](lean:D5/S3/Quantum/Analysis/CompactGroups/UnitaryRepresentationKernel.exists_unitaryRep_ker_subset)) Let $G$ be a compact topological group (no separation axiom is assumed) and $U \subseteq G$ a neighbourhood of the identity. Then there are a natural number $n$ and a continuous group homomorphism $\rho : G \to \mathrm{U}(n)$ (the group of $n \times n$ complex unitary matrices) such that, for every $g \in G$, $\rho(g) = I_n$ implies $g \in U$.
:::

::: proof
Item 1: since $\mu$ is finite, every $f \in L^p(\mu)$ is integrable with $\int_Y \lvert f\rvert\,d\mu \le C\,\lVert f\rVert_p$, where $C = \mu(Y)^{1 - 1/p}$. Hence $\lvert (Tf)(x)\rvert \le \lVert K\rVert_\infty\,C\,\lVert f\rVert_p$ and $\lvert (Tf)(x) - (Tf)(x')\rvert \le \sup_{y} \lvert K(x, y) - K(x', y)\rvert \cdot C\,\lVert f\rVert_p$. Because $K$ is continuous and $Y$ is compact, $x \mapsto K(x, \cdot)$ is continuous from $X$ into the continuous functions on $Y$ with the sup norm, so $Tf$ is continuous and $T$ is a bounded linear map; the image of the unit ball is uniformly bounded and equicontinuous, and the Arzelà–Ascoli theorem makes its closure compact.

Item 2: take a Haar measure $\mu$ and the left regular representation $(\pi(g)f)(x) = f(g^{-1}x)$ on $L^2(\mu)$, which preserves norms and is strongly continuous. Choose a neighbourhood $V$ of the identity such that $v w^{-1}$ ($v, w \in V$) always lies in the interior of $U$, and a continuous real function $\varphi$ with $\varphi(1) = 1$ that vanishes outside $V$, so that $\int \varphi^2\,d\mu > 0$. By Item 1 the integral operator with kernel $\varphi(x^{-1}y)$ is a compact operator from $L^2(\mu)$ to $C(G)$; composing with the inclusion into $L^2(\mu)$ gives a compact operator $T$ that commutes with every $\pi(g)$. The continuous function $T\varphi$ takes the value $\int \varphi^2\,d\mu \neq 0$ at $1$ and the value $0$ at any $g$ outside the interior of $U$, so such a $\pi(g)$ moves $T\varphi$. The operator $S = T T^\dagger$ is compact, self-adjoint, and also commutes with every $\pi(g)$. By the spectral theorem the eigenspaces of $S$ span a dense subspace, so a continuous operator that fixes every eigenspace of $S$ with nonzero eigenvalue pointwise fixes the closure of the range of $S$ pointwise, and that closure contains the range of $T$. Hence for each $c$ outside the interior of $U$, some eigenspace with nonzero eigenvalue — finite-dimensional and invariant under translation — contains a vector moved by $\pi(c)$, and by strong continuity that vector is moved by every element of some neighbourhood of $c$. The complement of the interior of $U$ is compact, so finitely many such eigenspaces suffice; their sum $W$ is finite-dimensional and translation-invariant, and the matrix of $\pi$ on $W$ in an orthonormal basis is the required continuous unitary representation $\rho$: if $\rho(g) = I_n$, then $\pi(g)$ fixes $W$ pointwise, $g$ lies in none of those neighbourhoods, and so $g \in U$. Item 2 is the separation form of the Peter–Weyl theorem, not the full Peter–Weyl theorem: density of matrix coefficients, the decomposition of $L^2(\mu)$ into irreducible representations, and the character expansion of positive-definite class functions all lie outside this theorem. The full proof is checked by the Lean kernel.

::: {.theorem #thm-ch39-axiom-omega-duplication-infinitesimal-classification title="Infinitesimal Classification under the Duplication Law" lean="D5/S3/Quantum/Algebra/Comparison/RoundTripPeriodMetric.comparison_classification_of_continuous_rays"}
Let $A$ be a finite-dimensional (hence complete) real normed algebra with Lie bracket the commutator $[X, Y] := XY - YX$; let $L \subseteq A$ be a real linear subspace closed under the commutator, with the norm of $A$; let $G$ be a subgroup of the group of units of $A$; and let $\exp X := \sum_{n \ge 0} X^n/n!$. Let $\varphi : G \to \mathbb{R}$. Assume:

1. $\exp X \in G$ for every $X \in L$, and $g X g^{-1} \in L$ for every $g \in G$ and $X \in L$;
2. $0 \le \varphi(g) \le 1$ for every $g \in G$, and $\varphi(g) = 1 \iff g = 1$;
3. duplication: $\varphi(g^2) = \bigl(2\varphi(g) - 1\bigr)^2$ for every $g \in G$;
4. conjugation invariance: $\varphi(k g k^{-1}) = \varphi(g)$ for all $g, k \in G$;
5. continuity along rays: for every $X \in L$, the function $t \mapsto \varphi(\exp tX)$ is continuous on $\mathbb{R}$.

Then there is a symmetric real bilinear form $h$ on $L$ such that:

1. $h(X, X) \ge 0$ for every $X \in L$, and $h(X, X) = 0$ implies $X = 0$;
2. $\varphi(\exp tX) = \cos^2\bigl(t\sqrt{h(X, X)/2}\bigr)$ for all $t \in \mathbb{R}$ and $X \in L$;
3. for all $t \in \mathbb{R}$ and $X \in L$, $\exp tX = 1$ if and only if $t\sqrt{h(X, X)/2} = n\pi$ for some integer $n$;
4. if $X, Y \in L$ and $[X, Y] = 0$, then $X = 0$ or $Y = cX$ for some real $c$;
5. $h([C, X], Y) + h(X, [C, Y]) = 0$ for all $C, X, Y \in L$;
6. $\dim_{\mathbb{R}} L \in \{0, 1, 3\}$;
7. if $\dim_{\mathbb{R}} L = 3$, there are a basis $b_0, b_1, b_2$ of $L$ and a real $\mu > 0$ with $h(b_i, b_j) = \delta_{ij}$ for all $i, j$ and $[b_0, b_1] = \mu\,b_2$, $[b_1, b_2] = \mu\,b_0$, $[b_2, b_0] = \mu\,b_1$;
8. for every nonzero $X \in L$ there is $T > 0$ such that, for all $t \in \mathbb{R}$, $\exp tX = 1$ if and only if $t = kT$ for some integer $k$, and $h(X, X) = 2\pi^2/T^2$.
:::

::: proof
First solve the duplication equation on a ray. Fix a nonzero $X \in L$ and put $p(t) := 2\varphi(\exp tX) - 1$. From $\exp\bigl((s + t)X\bigr) = \exp(sX)\exp(tX)$ and duplication, $p$ is continuous, $\lvert p\rvert \le 1$ and $p(2t) = 2p(t)^2 - 1$; by faithfulness $p(t) = 1$ exactly when $\exp tX = 1$, these $t$ form an additive subgroup of $\mathbb{R}$, and each of its elements is a period of $p$. The exponential map is injective near $0$ (inverse function theorem), so near $0$ the subgroup contains only $0$ and is not dense; and no continuous bounded function on the line satisfying the duplication formula takes the value $1$ only at $0$, so the subgroup is not $\{0\}$. Hence it is the set of integer multiples of some $T > 0$, and from this $p(t) = \cos(2\pi t/T)$.

Next fix $h$. Complexify the left regular representation of $A$ on itself, call it $\rho$, and put $B(X, Y) := -\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{tr}\bigl(\rho(X)\rho(Y)\bigr)$, a symmetric bilinear form. Since $\exp TX = 1$, $\rho(X)$ is diagonalizable with eigenvalues $2\pi i k/T$ ($k$ integers, not all zero), so $B(X, X) = (2\pi/T)^2\sum k^2 > 0$. Normalize the nonzero directions for $B$: in dimension greater than one, $L$ minus the origin is connected, and the characteristic polynomial of the normalized operator is continuous on it and takes only countably many values, hence is the same everywhere, so all normalized directions have the same least period $T_0$; in dimension one there are only two normalized directions, negatives of each other. Taking $h := 2(\pi/T_0)^2 B$ gives Item 8, and $p(t) = \cos(2\pi t/T)$ with $h(X, X) = 2\pi^2/T^2$ gives Item 2, hence $\varphi(\exp X) = 1 - \frac{1}{2}h(X, X) + o\bigl(\lVert X\rVert^2\bigr)$ as $X \to 0$. Item 1 follows from $B(X, X) > 0$ for $X \neq 0$. Item 3: by faithfulness, $\exp tX = 1$ means $\cos^2 = 1$, that is, the sine vanishes.

Item 5: $\exp\bigl(e^{tC}Xe^{-tC}\bigr) = e^{tC}(\exp X)e^{-tC}$, so conjugation invariance makes $\varphi \circ \exp$ invariant under this conjugation, and hence so is its second-order coefficient $h$; differentiating in $t$ at $0$ gives $\operatorname{ad}$-invariance.

Item 4: let $X \neq 0$ with $[X, Y] = 0$, and let $S$ be the span of $X$ and $Y$. Elements of $S$ commute, so $\exp$ is a homomorphism from addition to multiplication on $S$, and its kernel $K = \{u \in S : \exp u = 1\}$ is an additive subgroup. Use $h$ as the inner product on $S$ and put $r := \sqrt{2}\,\pi$. By Item 2, every vector of length $r$ lies in $K$, while at every vector of length $r/2$ one has $\varphi = 0 \neq 1$, so none of them lies in $K$. If $\dim S \ge 2$, a vector of length $r/2$ is the sum of two vectors of length $r$ (an isosceles triangle) and would lie in $K$, a contradiction; so $\dim S = 1$ and $Y$ is a multiple of $X$.

Items 6 and 7: $h$ is an $\operatorname{ad}$-invariant inner product on $L$, and the centralizer of each nonzero element is the line it spans. For a unit vector $a$, $\operatorname{ad}_a$ is skew-adjoint for $h$; when $\dim L > 1$ it is nonzero, which yields orthogonal unit vectors $u, v$ and $\mu > 0$ with $[a, u] = \mu v$, $[a, v] = -\mu u$, $[u, v] = \mu a$, and no dimension remains outside the span of $a$, $u$, $v$ (that would contradict the centralizers being lines). So $\dim L$ is $0$, $1$ or $3$, and in dimension three $a, u, v$ is the required basis. The full proof is checked by the Lean kernel.

::: {.remark #rem-ch39-axiom-omega-formalization-bridge title="How the Mother Axiom Corresponds to the Manifold Form"}
**Correspondence and status.** The clauses of the mother axiom in §39.1.2 and in [@def-ch39-axiom-omega-ternary-comparison-closure] are, one by one, the axioms and predicates of [@def-ch39-ternary-comparison-structure], with only two rewritings: the $\mathbf{G}_n \succeq 0$ of Law (II) is nonnegativity of the Gram quadratic form (a real symmetric matrix is positive semidefinite precisely when its quadratic form is nonnegative, and the case $n = 0$ is trivial), and, because $\kappa \ge 0$, $\inf\kappa = 0$ is touching zero. The items below explain how the earlier manifold form of this section (end of §39.1.2) relates to the mother axiom, and what the status of each step is.

1. **Clauses shared by both statements (direct correspondence).** The three identities of Law (I); the symmetry, extensional separation $\kappa(x, y) = 1 \iff x = y$ (the outcome of `H1` quotienting), nonnegativity, two-sided transport invariance, Gram positivity, and zero touching of Law (II); and the order sensitivity $\exists\, x, y, z,\; [x, y, z] \neq [z, y, x]$ of Law (III) are the same in both. The manifold form writes the reading as taking values in $[0, 1]$; the upper end is not a separate condition, since the Gram form of two points $x, y$ with coefficients $(1, -1)$ gives $2 - 2\kappa(x, y) \ge 0$.
2. **Feature rank and $\operatorname{rank}\mathcal{H}_\kappa$ agree (`manual derivation` plus `LEAN ✓`).** The manifold form's $\operatorname{rank}\mathcal{H}_\kappa = \dim_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{H}_\kappa$ equals the supremum of the ranks of finite Gram matrices, because $\mathcal{H}_\kappa$ is the completed span of the kernel functions $\kappa(x, \cdot)$, whose mutual inner products are the readings (`manual derivation`); together with Item 2 of [@thm-ch39-axiom-omega-rank-five-unit-quaternion-embedding] (`LEAN ✓`), $\operatorname{rank}\mathcal{H}_\kappa \le r$ holds exactly when the reading is realizable in $\mathbb{R}^r$, so $\operatorname{rank}\mathcal{H}_\kappa$ is the feature rank, and the two are finite or infinite together.
3. **A connected carrier with each $\kappa(x, \cdot)$ continuous at $x$ is chain connected (`LEAN ✓`).** Fix $\varepsilon > 0$ and $x \in \mathcal{M}$, and let $A$ be the set of points reachable from $x$ by finite chains whose consecutive readings exceed $1 - \varepsilon$. If $a \in A$, continuity of $\kappa(a, \cdot)$ at $a$ and $\kappa(a, a) = 1$ let every point of some neighborhood of $a$ be reached in one more step, so $A$ is open. If $b \notin A$, every point $c$ of some neighborhood of $b$ has $\kappa(c, b) > 1 - \varepsilon$; were such a $c$ in $A$, $b$ would be too, so that neighborhood misses $A$ and the complement of $A$ is open as well. Only continuity of each $\kappa(a, \cdot)$ at $a$, the symmetry of $\kappa$ and $\kappa(a, a) = 1$ are used: a ternary comparison structure on a connected space is chain connected as soon as each $\kappa(x, \cdot)$ is continuous at $x$ ([`chainConnected_of_connected`](lean:D5/S3/Quantum/AURICOmega/FiniteRankDimensionBound.chainConnected_of_connected), `LEAN ✓`; connectedness is taken in Lean as preconnectedness, which does not require the space to be nonempty). 4. **`H3` Cauchy metric completeness is reading completeness when the metric is the chordal one (`manual derivation`).** `H3` in [Appendix E](appendix-e.md) has two parts, parallelogram polarization and metric Cauchy completion (summary table of [§E.7](appendix-e-s07-intermediate-hypotheses-audit.md)), and only the second bears on reading completeness. What remains `Literature` plus `manual derivation` is: that a topological group on a manifold is automatically a Lie group (Montgomery–Zippin), the passage from the group to a Lie algebra with an invariant positive-definite form (the $\operatorname{Ad}$-invariance of the Hessian form and the Lie group–Lie algebra correspondence), and the coverage of infinite-rank candidates. The finite-rank theorem above does not cover infinite-rank candidates, so the step as a whole is still `Literature` plus `manual derivation`. Given $d_{\min} = 3$, the least rank in that candidate class is $5$.
6. **Manifold form $\Rightarrow$ mother axiom.** A structure satisfying the manifold form satisfies the shared clauses by Item 1, is chain connected by Item 3, is reading complete by Item 4 (when the completion uses the chordal distance), and has feature rank $5$ by Item 5; since $5$ is exactly the minimum required by Law (III) of the mother axiom (Items 4 and 3 of the theorem, `LEAN ✓`), it satisfies the mother axiom and is therefore isomorphic to the unit-quaternion model by Item 2 of [@thm-ch39-axiom-omega-complete-rank-five-isomorphism]. The status of this direction is that of the weakest link among Items 3–5, namely `Literature` plus `manual derivation`.
7. **Mother axiom $\Rightarrow$ manifold form.** A structure satisfying the mother axiom is isomorphic to the unit-quaternion model by the bijection of Item 2 of [@thm-ch39-axiom-omega-complete-rank-five-isomorphism] (`LEAN ✓`). The unit-quaternion model meets the clauses of the manifold form: $S^3(1)$ is a connected simply connected three-manifold (`manual derivation`), the transport $x y^{-1} z$ is continuous ([`continuous_tri_quaternionModel`](lean:D5/S3/Quantum/AURICOmega/DimensionRankRealization.continuous_tri_quaternionModel), `LEAN ✓`), $\kappa = \frac{1 + \langle x, y\rangle}{2}$ is smooth with single-slot second-order Hessian equal to $-\frac{1}{2}$ times the unit form, hence strictly negative definite and nondegenerate, and $S^3$ is compact and therefore complete for the chordal distance (`manual derivation`); its "$(\dim\mathcal{M}, \operatorname{rank}\mathcal{H}_\kappa) = (3, 5)$ is lexicographically minimal" again depends on $d_{\min} = 3$ from Item 5 (`Literature` plus `manual derivation`). Carrying these structures over to $\mathcal{M}$ along the bijection is a `manual derivation`; since the bijection preserves the reading, the topology carried over is the metric topology of the chordal distance $d_\kappa$. The bijection is unique up to left and right multiplication: writing $u$ for it, the maps $\mathcal{M} \to \mathrm{Sp}(1)$ preserving transport and reading are exactly $x \mapsto p\,u(x)\,q$ ([`preserves_iff_exists_mul_mul`](lean:D5/S3/Quantum/AURICOmega/QuaternionModelAutomorphism.preserves_iff_exists_mul_mul), `LEAN ✓`), and $(p, q)$ is determined up to a common sign ([`mul_mul_eq_mul_mul_iff`](lean:D5/S3/Quantum/AURICOmega/QuaternionModelAutomorphism.mul_mul_eq_mul_mul_iff), `LEAN ✓`, together with the `manual derivation` step that passes through $u$ being onto; see §39.1.2).
8. **Clauses that are no longer assumptions.** The finite-dimensional manifold, simple connectivity, $C^2$ regularity, nondegeneracy of the single-slot second-order Hessian, and continuity of the transfer appear neither among the hypotheses of [@thm-ch39-axiom-omega-rank-five-unit-quaternion-embedding] and [@thm-ch39-axiom-omega-complete-rank-five-isomorphism] nor in the mother axiom; under the mother axiom they are the consequences described in Item 7.

**Smoothing and classifying continuous point reflections with round-trip comparison (`manual derivation`, using `Literature`).** Let $M$ be a connected boundaryless topological $n$-manifold, $n\ge1$. Assume $(x,w)\mapsto s_xw$ is jointly continuous, with $s_xx=x$ and $s_x^2=\mathrm{id}$. Let $\kappa:M^2\to[0,1]$ be continuous, symmetric, and faithful, meaning $\kappa(x,y)=1$ exactly when $x=y$. Each $s_x$ preserves $\kappa$, and the round-trip law is $\kappa(w,s_xw)=(2\kappa(w,x)-1)^2$. At a fixed point $w$ of $s_x$ this forces $\kappa(w,x)\in\{0,1\}$. In the neighborhood with $\kappa(w,x)>1/2$, only $x$ is fixed.

In coordinates centered at $o$, the local mod-$2$ degree of $s_o(z)-z$ is $1$. To see this, take a small $s_o$-invariant neighborhood $U$, let the group of order $2$ act by $s_o$ on $U$ and by negation on $\mathbb R^n$, and use the difference map as an equivariant map of punctured pairs. Excision puts their mod-$2$ Borel relative spectral sequences in a single row, of degree $n$. The actions on the punctured spaces are free, so their Borel cohomology is that of the quotient manifolds. Compact-support Poincaré duality and universal coefficients over a field give vanishing in degrees $>n$ on those quotients. In these degrees the target relative groups therefore have classes restricting nontrivially to the group classifying space by the fixed-origin section. Degree zero would make the pullback on relative cohomology zero, contradicting naturality of that restriction. The local homotopy $z\mapsto s_{tz}((t-1)z)-(t-1)z$ for $0\le t\le1$ vanishes only at the origin. Thus $z\mapsto s_z o$ also has local mod-$2$ degree $1$. For the displacement group $G=\langle s_xs_y:x,y\in M\rangle$, the identity $s_xs_o(o)=s_xo$ shows that the $G$-orbit of $o$ contains a neighborhood of $o$. The argument works at each base point, so every $G$-orbit is open. Connectedness makes $G$ transitive.

Give the full group $H$ of $\kappa$-preserving homeomorphisms the compact-open topology. The infimum of path diameters measured with $1-\kappa$ controls small neighborhoods. Paths inside small coordinate balls have small diameter, and leaving such a ball has a positive comparison cost on its boundary. For a compactly supported continuous function $f$ with a unique maximum at $o$, the formula $\sup_{g\in H}|f(gx)-f(gy)|$ defines a compatible invariant metric. Transitivity and path control give uniformly compact small balls. Diagonal subsequences of maps taking $o$ into a compact small ball, and of their inverses, give local compactness of $H$ and compactness of $H_o$. The continuous map $x\mapsto s_xs_o$ evaluates at $o$ onto a neighborhood of $o$, so the orbit map is open and $H/H_o\cong M$. The Hofmann–Kramer theorem applies to this faithful action with connected, locally contractible quotient (Literature). The quotient carries a compatible smooth structure, and averaging over $H_o$ gives an invariant Riemannian metric. Each $s_x$ is then an isometric involution, whose isolated fixed point gives derivative $-I$ in exponential coordinates. An isometry is determined by its value and derivative at a point, so $g s_xg^{-1}=s_{gx}$. Smooth local sections of the quotient map give joint smoothness of $s$.

For a unit-speed geodesic $\gamma$ from $o$, the comparison angle $u(t)=\arccos(2\kappa(o,\gamma(t))-1)$ satisfies $u(2t)=\min(2u(t),2\pi-2u(t))$. At a fixed small positive time its values on the unit tangent sphere have a uniform positive lower bound. Doubling and the intermediate value theorem give a uniformly bounded $\tau$ with $u(\tau)=\pi/2$, hence $u(4\tau)=0$ and $\gamma(4\tau)=o$. The geodesics are orbits of one-parameter isometry groups in the symmetric space, so return gives a period. Completeness and Hopf–Rinow turn the uniform period bound into compactness. Two independent commuting tangent generators would give a discrete return subgroup meeting every line through the origin nontrivially, which is impossible. The rank is therefore one. Loos's symmetric space structure and Cartan's compact rank-one classification give round spheres, real, complex and quaternionic projective spaces, and the Cayley plane (Literature). Isotropy gives all geodesics the same least positive period $L$. The zeros of $u$ occur exactly at the period endpoints, so $u(L/2)=\pi$. The doubling law gives $u(t)=\pi/2$ within one period exactly at $t=L/4,3L/4$. Thus $u(t)$ and $u_0(t)=2\pi\operatorname{dist}(t/L,\mathbb Z)$ always lie on the same branches of the doubling law. Their difference doubles under each iteration and stays bounded by $\pi$, so $u=u_0$. Take shortest geodesics with $0\le t\le L/2$ and rescale distances by $\pi/L$ to obtain $\kappa(x,y)=\cos^2 d(x,y)$ and $\operatorname{diam}(M,d)=\pi/2$. Under the isometry with the standard metric model, $s_x$ becomes geodesic symmetry at the image of $x$. Projective lines are already represented among the round spheres. Mod-$2$ duality enters the local degree argument, and the forced geodesic returns give compactness. See Paper I's *Local degree of an isolated involution*, *classification with continuous reflections*, and the smooth classification it invokes.

::: {.remark #rem-ch39-axiom-omega-round-trip-closing-law title="A Single-Structure Form of Law (III): the Round-Trip Law and d'Alembert's Functional Equation"}
**Round-trip law (`manual derivation`).** Write $s_x(w) := [x, w, x]$ for the reflection of $w$ in $x$. On $S^3(1)$ one has $s_x(w) = 2\langle x, w\rangle x - w$, hence $\langle w, s_x(w)\rangle = 2\langle x, w\rangle^2 - 1$, and the master kernel $\kappa_{S^3}$ satisfies

$$
\kappa\bigl(w,\, [x, w, x]\bigr) \;=\; \bigl(2\kappa(w, x) - 1\bigr)^2 \qquad (\forall\, w, x \in \mathcal{M}).
$$

The minimal feature rank of Law (III) (the "minimal dimension, minimal rank" of the manifold form) minimizes over a whole class of structures; the identity above is a property of a single structure and can replace it as the closing law. On the carrier of the manifold form, on top of Law (I) and the symmetry, faithfulness $\kappa(x, y) = 1 \iff x = y$ and two-sided transport invariance clauses of Law (II) (continuity of $\kappa$ suffices; $C^2$ regularity is not part of the definition, it follows: real-analyticity of $\kappa$ comes from the group structure), require that every $x$ have exactly one zero partner $x^\perp$ ($\kappa(x, x^\perp) = 0$). Then the structures satisfying the round-trip law are, up to isomorphism, exactly two: the unit circle $S^1$ and the unit 3-sphere $S^3$, both with $[x, y, z] = x y^{-1} z$ and $\kappa(x, y) = \lVert (x + y)/2\rVert^2$. Only $S^3$ has non-commutative transport, and the dimension, compactness and Gram positive-semidefiniteness of $\kappa$ (of rank $\dim\mathcal{M} + 2$, i.e. $1 + 4 = 5$ on $S^3$) all follow. With the non-commutativity clause of Law (III) (or the simply connected carrier of the manifold form), $S^1$ drops out and the only solution is $S^3(1)$ with $\kappa_{S^3}$. Conversely, every structure satisfying the mother axiom satisfies the round-trip law and the interference law below: the rank-five embedding (Item 5 of [@thm-ch39-axiom-omega-rank-five-unit-quaternion-embedding], `LEAN ✓`) carries it into the unit quaternions, where $x w^{-1} x = 2\langle x, w\rangle x - w$ (`manual derivation`). Sketch of proof: fix a base point $o$ and put $\varphi(g) := \kappa(o, g)$; the round-trip law becomes the duplication formula $\varphi(g^2) = \bigl(2\varphi(g) - 1\bigr)^2$; along every one-parameter subgroup it gives $\varphi(\exp tX) = \cos^2\bigl(\lVert X\rVert t/\sqrt{2}\bigr)$, so all one-parameter subgroups close after the same length, Abelian subalgebras have dimension at most one, and the Lie algebra is $\mathbb{R}$ or $\mathfrak{su}(2)$. The infinitesimal part of these steps is kernel-checked by [@thm-ch39-axiom-omega-duplication-infinitesimal-classification] (`LEAN ✓`) in the setting where $G$ is a subgroup of the units of a finite-dimensional real normed algebra and $\varphi$ is continuous along every one-parameter subgroup, the symmetric bilinear form $h$ that serves as the cosine's coefficient being a conclusion rather than an assumption: the cosine along one-parameter subgroups, the common closing length and Abelian subalgebras of dimension at most one are its Items 2–4, that each one-parameter subgroup returns to the identity exactly at the integer multiples of a period $T$, with $h(X, X) = 2\pi^2/T^2$, is Item 8, and the Lie algebra having dimension $0$, $1$ or $3$, with the bracket a positive multiple of the cross product in some $h$-orthonormal basis in dimension three, is Items 6 and 7 (the dimension step also holds in a form that assumes only an invariant inner product and that the centralizer of one nonzero element is a line: [`finrank_eq_one_or_three`](lean:D5/S3/Quantum/Algebra/RankOneInvariantMetric.finrank_eq_one_or_three), `LEAN ✓`). Passing from the manifold carrier to this setting (realizing the group as a linear group; continuity of $\varphi$ along one-parameter subgroups follows directly from continuity of $\kappa$), obtaining the duplication formula and conjugation invariance from the round-trip law, and recognizing the three-dimensional case as $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ remain `manual derivation`. On the real line the duplication formula $f(2t) = 2f(t)^2 - 1$ has continuous solutions other than the cosine, and the characterization of the cosine uses the group structure; one-dimensional characterizations of the cosine by duplication may have precursors (M. Kuczma, *Ann. Polon. Math.* **16**, 53–57, 1964; A. N. Sharkovskii, *Aequationes Math.* **9**, 121–128, 1973), and that part is not claimed as new.

**d'Alembert's functional equation (`Literature`).** Put $\psi := 2\varphi - 1$. The two-variable identity $\psi(ab) + \psi(ab^{-1}) = 2\psi(a)\psi(b)$ is d'Alembert's functional equation on a group; T. M. K. Davison (*Aequationes Math.* **76**, 33–53, 2008; *Publ. Math. Debrecen* **75**, 41–66, 2009) proved that its nonzero continuous solutions are $\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{tr}\circ\rho$ with $\rho : G \to \mathrm{SL}(2, \mathbb{C})$ a continuous homomorphism, and that a compact group carrying a faithful solution embeds in $\mathrm{SU}(2)$. If the two-variable form (the interference law below) is taken as the axiom, only Law (I) and the symmetry, faithfulness and two-sided invariance clauses of Law (II) are needed, neither the unique-zero-partner clause nor the $C^2$ clause, and the solutions are exactly $S^1$ and $S^3$ (`manual derivation`; it also follows from the literature just cited); the round-trip law is its diagonal case $a = b$. The one-variable round-trip law alone does not exclude $\mathrm{SO}(3)$: there $\psi = \cos\alpha$ ($\alpha$ the rotation angle) is faithful, smooth and satisfies the duplication formula, yet for the rotations $a, b$ by $\pi/2$ about two orthogonal axes $\psi(ab) + \psi(ab^{-1}) = -1 \ne 0 = 2\psi(a)\psi(b)$, and the corresponding $\kappa = \cos^2(\alpha/2)$ vanishes on every rotation by $\pi$, so the zero partner is not unique (this structure is the model on $\mathrm{Sp}(1)/\{\pm 1\}$ with reading $\langle x, y\rangle^2$, in which every point has at least two distinct zero partners: [`projectiveModel_featureRank_ten`](lean:D5/S3/Quantum/AURICOmega/ProjectiveQuaternionModel.projectiveModel_featureRank_ten), `LEAN ✓`; matching the two descriptions is a `manual derivation`). The unique-zero-partner clause therefore cannot be dropped.

**Operational form: two-readout determinacy (`manual derivation` + `LEAN ✓`).** Write $c := 2\kappa - 1$ for the correlation. On $S^3(1)$ the round-trip law holds together with the interference law

$$
c\bigl(y,\, [x, w, x]\bigr) \;=\; 2\,c(w, x)\,c(y, x) \;-\; c(y, w) \qquad (\forall\, w, x, y \in \mathcal{M}).
$$

Operationally: watching any test point $y$ along a uniform transport $\dots, w, x, [x, w, x], \dots$, the reading of $y$ against the next point is fixed affinely by its two readings against the previous two points. Under Law (I) and Law (II) (including the unique-zero-partner clause), the statement "for every pair $w, x$ there are reals $a, b, d$ depending only on $(w, x)$ such that $\kappa(y, [x, w, x]) = a\,\kappa(y, x) + b\,\kappa(y, w) + d$ for all $y$" is equivalent to the round-trip law, and then $a = 2c(w, x)$, $b = -1$, $d = 1 - c(w, x)$, which is the interference law above. Two steps of this are kernel-checked: for an even, conjugation-invariant, faithful ($\psi(g) = 1 \iff g = 1$) function $\psi$ on a group, if $\psi(zu^{-1}) = a\,\psi(z) + b\,\psi(zu) + d$ for all $z$ and some $u$ with $u^2 \neq 1$, then $b = -1$ ([`coefficient_eq_neg_one`](lean:D5/S3/Quantum/Algebra/Comparison/TwoReadoutNormalization.coefficient_eq_neg_one), `LEAN ✓`); and if $\psi(1) = 1$, $\psi$ is even, conjugation-invariant, bounded by $\lvert\psi\rvert \le 1$ and not identically $1$, and $\psi(zu) + \psi(zu^{-1}) = a(u)\,\psi(z) + d(u)$ for all $z, u$, then there is $m < 1$ such that $q := (\psi - m)/(1 - m)$ satisfies $\lvert q\rvert \le 1$ and the bounded d'Alembert equation $q(zu) + q(zu^{-1}) = 2q(z)q(u)$ ([`bounded_dalembert_of_affine_law`](lean:D5/S3/Quantum/Algebra/Comparison/TwoReadoutNormalization.bounded_dalembert_of_affine_law), `LEAN ✓`). Taking the group based at $x$ and $\psi := c(x, \cdot)$, rewriting the affine determinacy law as the hypotheses of these two theorems (the elements with $u^2 = 1$ treated separately), as well as the equivalence itself and the values of $a$ and $d$, remain `manual derivation`. The unique-zero-partner clause cannot be dropped here either: on $S^3$, $\kappa'(x, y) = \frac{3}{4} + \frac{1}{4}\langle x, y\rangle$ satisfies the affine determinacy law but has no zero and violates the round-trip law. Conversely, if the interference law above is required for all $w, x, y$, neither the unique-zero-partner clause nor the $C^2$ clause is needed: with $\kappa$ merely continuous, the solutions are exactly $S^1$ and $S^3$ (`manual derivation`, see the previous paragraph).

**Three closing laws select the same Lie algebra (`manual derivation`).** In the manifold form, under Law (I) and the symmetry, faithfulness, two-sided transport invariance and $C^2$ regularity clauses of Law (II), each of the following forces the Lie algebra of a structure with non-commutative transport to be $\mathfrak{su}(2)$: the round-trip law; the minimal dimension of §39.1.3 (minimal within the class with Gram positive-semidefinite $\kappa$ and $\inf\kappa = 0$; it selects the same model as the minimal feature rank of Law (III)); and completeness of comparison, i.e. whenever $\kappa(x, y) = \kappa(x', y')$ some automorphism preserving $\kappa$, and preserving or reversing transport, carries $(x, y)$ to $(x', y')$.

::: {.remark #rem-ch39-axiom-omega-heap-rigidity title="Rigidity of the Heap: Counterexamples to Weakened Associativity, Roundness without Associativity, One-Sided Invariance, Dimensions 1, 2, 4, 8 and the Solutions on Each Sphere, the Moufang Property, and the Excluded Quotient"}
**Weakened associativity does not give di-associativity (`manual derivation`).** The Mal'cev identities $[x, x, z] = z$, $[x, z, z] = x$ together with the third identity on subsystems generated by two elements do not imply that $x \cdot y := [x, o, y]$ is a di-associative loop, nor that $[x, y, z] = (x \cdot y^{-1}) \cdot z$. Two counterexamples: (A) the Mal'cev term $p(x, y, z) = y \circ (x \circ z)$ of the Steiner quasigroup on the seven points of the Fano plane ($x \circ x = x$, $x \circ y$ the third point on the line through $x, y$); Steiner quasigroups form a congruence-permutable variety (R. W. Quackenbush, *Canad. J. Math.* **28**, 1187–1198, 1976, `Literature`); (B) $[a, b, x] = a\bar{b}a\bar{b}x\bar{a}b$ on $S^3$, which is the standard heap on great circles and satisfies the round-trip law, and for which the standard $\kappa$ is invariant under all left transports but not preserved by the right transports.

**Roundness without associativity (`manual derivation`; the results it rests on are `Literature`).** Let the carrier be a connected manifold with a ternary operation satisfying only the two Mal'cev identities (continuous in its outer arguments), and let $\kappa$ be continuous, symmetric, equal to $1$ exactly on the diagonal, with a unique zero partner for each point, invariant under all left and right transports, and satisfying the round-trip law; no form of the third (para-associative) identity is assumed. Then the carrier is compact and isometric to a round sphere $S^1$, $S^3$ or $S^7$ with $\kappa = \cos^2\frac{d}{2}$. Proof outline: the $\kappa$-preserving homeomorphisms form a locally compact group acting transitively, which by the theorem of Hofmann–Kramer (K. H. Hofmann & L. Kramer, *J. Reine Angew. Math.* **702**, 227–243, 2015) is a Lie group, and this gives an invariant Riemannian metric; left and right transports give Killing fields of constant length (V. N. Berestovskiĭ & Yu. G. Nikonorov, *J. Differential Geom.* **82**, 467–500, 2009) whose integral curves are geodesics, along which $\kappa$ satisfies the duplication law and is therefore a cosine; all geodesics from a point meet at its antipode, which forces constant curvature; Hurwitz's theorem then gives the dimensions $1, 3, 7$. What is new is obtaining a Riemannian structure from a merely continuous comparison and selecting the round factor through the duplication lemmas; the nearest precursor is the classification by the Killing property in Berestovskiĭ–Nikonorov. The third identity then does only two things: it selects $S^1$ and $S^3$, and it fixes the operation; for the operations on $S^7$ see item 4 below. Hypotheses that cannot be dropped: without the unique zero partner, $\mathrm{SO}(3)$ satisfies everything else; without the round-trip law, a torus and a cylinder satisfy everything else.

**One-sided invariance (`manual derivation`; Adams' theorem and Kinyon 2000 are `Literature`).** Invariance under left translations alone does not suffice. On $S^3$ the smooth operation $B(x, y) = x\,h_x\,y\,h_x^{-1}$ ($h_x = e^{\hat{\mathbf{i}}\phi(x)/2}$ with $\phi$ a smooth function of $x$), with a comparison $\kappa$ built from the fixed point of a contraction, satisfies every hypothesis of the roundness theorem except invariance under right translations, and $\kappa$ is not round: for $x = (\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}, \frac{1}{2}, \frac{1}{2}, 0)$ the Gram matrix of $c$ on $(1, -1, x)$ has negative determinant, and $\arccos c$ violates the triangle inequality on $1, x, -1$. The scope of the example: the corresponding ternary operation fails the third identity on a subsystem generated by two elements ($B$ is not power-associative either); $\kappa$ is continuous but, by an uncertified numerical computation, not twice differentiable on the diagonal; and $\kappa$ is invariant under the group generated by the left transports and one clock (isomorphic to $\mathrm{U}(2)$). What survives under one-sided invariance: the $\kappa$-preserving homeomorphisms form a transitive Lie group and the carrier has a complete invariant Riemannian metric; the carrier is a round sphere if and only if all Gram matrices of $c$ are positive semidefinite, equivalently if and only if a four-point Gram test holds, and then Adams' theorem (J. F. Adams, *Ann. of Math.* **72**, 20–104, 1960) gives dimension $1$, $3$ or $7$. Kinyon constructed on the unit sphere of every real Hilbert space an operation with orthogonal left translations that is the circle product on every plane through the unit; in dimension at least $3$ its right translations have a nonremovable discontinuity (M. K. Kinyon, *Comment. Math. Univ. Carolin.* **41**, 325–346, 2000). Still open: with invariance under left transports only, is the carrier a round sphere if in addition the third identity holds on two-generated subsystems or the operation is power-associative, or if the comparison is twice differentiable?

**Dimensions and the solutions on each sphere (`Literature`: an elementary consequence of Hurwitz's theorem; the reduction is a `manual derivation`; odd $m > 1$ is also excluded by the zero-divisor theorem, `LEAN ✓` plus `manual derivation`).** Take the standard $\kappa$ on the unit sphere of an $m$-dimensional Euclidean space. Any map $T$ (not assumed continuous) satisfying the two Mal'cev identities and whose left and right transports preserve $\kappa$ exists only for $m \in \{1, 2, 4, 8\}$, i.e. only on $S^0$, $S^1$, $S^3$, $S^7$, and the round-trip law then holds automatically. Reduction: at a fixed base point $b$ the left and right translations are $\kappa$-isometries, so $B_b(a, x) := \lvert a\rvert\lvert x\rvert\,T(\hat{a}, b, \hat{x})$ is a norm-preserving bilinear product with unit $b$, and Hurwitz's theorem leaves the dimensions $1, 2, 4, 8$. Excluding odd $m > 1$ does not need Hurwitz's theorem: a bilinear map $U \times V \to W$ over a field, with $U$ and $W$ finite-dimensional, $\dim W \le \dim U$ and $V$ of dimension greater than $1$, and such that every polynomial of degree $\dim U$ has a root whenever $\dim W = \dim U$, vanishes at some pair of nonzero $a \in U$, $x \in V$ ([`exists_zero_divisor`](lean:D5/S3/QuadraticForms/BilinearZeroDivisor.exists_zero_divisor), `LEAN ✓`). Take $U = V = W = \mathbb{R}^m$: for odd $m$ every real polynomial of degree $m$ has a real root, while a norm-preserving product, $\lvert B_b(a, x)\rvert = \lvert a\rvert\lvert x\rvert$, has no zero divisors, so $m$ cannot be odd and greater than $1$ (`manual derivation`); excluding even $m \notin \{2, 4, 8\}$ still rests on Hurwitz's theorem and is `Literature`. This dimension statement differs from the topological statement in Step 3: there continuity and Adams' theorem are used; here no topology is used, at the price of the metric hypotheses (a round sphere, the standard $\kappa$, both transports preserving $\kappa$). The solutions on each sphere are:

1. $S^0$: only the heap $a\bar{b}x$.
2. $S^1$: only $a\bar{b}x$, with no continuity hypothesis.
3. $S^3$ (rigidity): let $T\colon (S^3)^3 \to S^3$ be continuous, satisfy the two Mal'cev identities, and let the standard $\kappa$ be invariant under all left and all right transports. Then either $T(a, b, x) = a\bar{b}x$ for all $a, b, x$, or $T(a, b, x) = x\bar{b}a$ for all $a, b, x$ (the latter isomorphic to the former via $x \mapsto \bar{x}$). Reduction: at a fixed base point the left and right translations are $\kappa$-isometries, which gives a norm-preserving bilinear product on $\mathbb{R}^4$ with two-sided unit $1$; by Hurwitz's theorem (A. Hurwitz, *Nachr. Ges. Wiss. Göttingen*, 309–316, 1898) it is isometrically isomorphic to the product of $\mathbb{H}$ or its opposite. Continuity cannot be dropped: for any set $E \subset S^3$, taking $a\bar{b}x$ when the base point $b$ lies in $E$ and $x\bar{b}a$ otherwise satisfies all the algebraic conditions but is discontinuous on the boundary of $E$.
4. $S^7$ (an infinite family, `manual derivation`): on $S^7$ the continuous operations satisfying the same conditions are exactly $T(a, b, x) = b\,\mu_b(\bar{b}a, \bar{b}x)$ with $b \mapsto \mu_b$ a continuous map from $S^7$ into the space $\mathrm{O}(7)/G_2$ of unital composition products: an infinite-dimensional family. The identity $T(x, y, z) = T\bigl(T(x, o, T(o, y, o)), o, z\bigr)$ holds for one base point $o$ if and only if it holds for all; it singles out the octonionic operation $(a\bar{b})x$ up to isometry. The identity at every base point is the relation by which a ternary Moufang loop is recovered from its loops at each origin (W. Bertram & M. Kinyon, [arXiv:1206.2222](https://arxiv.org/abs/1206.2222), 2012, `Literature`); what is added here is that under the metric hypotheses one base point suffices and only the unit octonions occur.

**The Moufang property is a consequence (`Literature`: a consequence of Mal'cev 1955 and Kuz'min 1971).** A connected real-analytic di-associative loop whose tangent algebra is a Malcev algebra is a Moufang loop: in canonical coordinates the product is given by the Campbell–Hausdorff series, which satisfies the formal Moufang identity (E. N. Kuz'min, *Algebra i Logika* **10**, 3–22, 1971), and analytic continuation extends it to the whole loop.

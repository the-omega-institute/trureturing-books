It carries two primitives: **origin-free ternary comparison transport** $[-, -, -] : \mathcal{M} \times \mathcal{M} \times \mathcal{M} \to \mathcal{M}$ ("using $y$ as a local reference bridge, transport the comparison relation of $x$ relative to $y$ losslessly onto $z$") and a **symmetric fidelity reading** $\kappa : \mathcal{M} \times \mathcal{M} \to \mathbb{R}$, neither of which is required to be continuous or differentiable. The carrier need only meet two conditions, both written in terms of the reading itself:

- **Chain connectedness**: however high the "close enough" threshold $1 - \varepsilon$, any two points are joined by a finite chain whose consecutive readings all clear it,

  $$
  \forall\, \varepsilon > 0,\ \forall\, x, y \in \mathcal{M},\ \exists\, x = x_0, x_1, \dots, x_k = y:\qquad \kappa(x_{i-1}, x_i) \;>\; 1 - \varepsilon \quad (1 \le i \le k);
  $$

- **Reading completeness**: sequences that are Cauchy in the sense of the reading converge in the sense of the reading, that is, for every sequence $(x_n)_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$ in $\mathcal{M}$,

  $$
  \bigl(\forall\, \varepsilon > 0\ \exists\, N\ \forall\, m, n \ge N:\ \kappa(x_m, x_n) > 1 - \varepsilon\bigr) \;\implies\; \exists\, a \in \mathcal{M}\ \forall\, \varepsilon > 0\ \exists\, N\ \forall\, n \ge N:\ \kappa(x_n, a) > 1 - \varepsilon.
  $$

  This is exactly the `H3` Cauchy completion of [Appendix E](appendix-e.md) written for the chordal distance $d_\kappa(x, y) := 2\sqrt{1 - \kappa(x, y)}$: since $\kappa(x_m, x_n) > 1 - \varepsilon$ means $d_\kappa(x_m, x_n) < 2\sqrt{\varepsilon}$, reading completeness is word for word "$(\mathcal{M}, d_\kappa)$ is a complete metric space" ($d_\kappa$ is a metric; see Item 4 of [@rem-ch39-axiom-omega-formalization-bridge]).

The two primitives obey three indivisible intrinsic laws (`MODEL`, with Law II enforcing `H2` Gram positivity):

1. **Law (I) Origin-Free Cancellation and Associativity**: For all $x, y, z, u, v \in \mathcal{M}$,

   $$
   [x, x, z] \;=\; z, \qquad [x, z, z] \;=\; x, \qquad \bigl[[x, y, z],\, u,\, v\bigr] \;=\; \bigl[x,\, y,\, [z, u, v]\bigr].
   $$

2. **Law (II) Bi-Invariant Gram Positivity and Zero-Infimum Separation**: $\kappa(x, y) = \kappa(y, x)$, $\kappa(x, y) = 1 \iff x = y$, $\kappa(x, y) \ge 0$, the reading **touches zero** (the orthogonal-mismatch limit $\inf_{x, y \in \mathcal{M}}\kappa(x, y) = 0$: for every $\varepsilon > 0$ some pair of points reads below $\varepsilon$), and for all $n \ge 1$, $x_1, \dots, x_n, u, v \in \mathcal{M}$, and real $c_1, \dots, c_n$, two-sided transport invariance and Gram positivity ($\mathbf{G}_n = \bigl[\kappa(x_i, x_j)\bigr]_{i,j=1}^n \succeq 0$) hold:

   $$
   \kappa\bigl([x, u, v],\, [y, u, v]\bigr) \;=\; \kappa\bigl([u, v, x],\, [u, v, y]\bigr) \;=\; \kappa(x, y), \qquad \sum_{i,j=1}^{n} c_i c_j\,\kappa(x_i, x_j) \;\ge\; 0.
   $$

   The upper bound $\kappa \le 1$ need not be assumed: coefficients $(1, -1)$ on the pair $(x, y)$ give $2 - 2\kappa(x, y) \ge 0$, so the reading lies in $[0, 1]$ automatically (`manual derivation`).

3. **Law (III) Minimal Non-Abelian and Minimal-Rank Lock**: transport hosts nontrivial order sensitivity, and the **feature rank** $\operatorname{rk}\kappa$ of the reading—the least $r$ for which the reading is realizable as an inner product in Euclidean space $\mathbb{R}^r$ ($\operatorname{rk}\kappa = \infty$ when there is no such $r$)—is minimal among all non-commutative, chain-connected structures obeying Laws (I)–(II):

   $$
   \exists\, x, y, z \in \mathcal{M},\quad [x, y, z] \neq [z, y, x], \qquad \operatorname{rk}\kappa \;:=\; \min\bigl\{ r \in \mathbb{N} : \exists\, \varphi : \mathcal{M} \to \mathbb{R}^r,\ \kappa(x, y) = \langle\varphi(x), \varphi(y)\rangle \bigr\} \;\to\; \min.

A **ternary comparison structure** on a set $M$ consists of a transfer $[-, -, -] : M \times M \times M \to M$ and a reading $\kappa : M \times M \to \mathbb{R}$ such that for all $x, y, z, u, v \in M$: $[x, x, z] = z$, $[x, z, z] = x$, $[[x, y, z], u, v] = [x, y, [z, u, v]]$; $\kappa(x, y) = \kappa(y, x)$; $\kappa(x, y) = 1 \iff x = y$; $\kappa(x, y) \ge 0$; $\kappa([x, u, v], [y, u, v]) = \kappa(x, y)$ and $\kappa([u, v, x], [u, v, y]) = \kappa(x, y)$; and for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$, $x_1, \dots, x_n \in M$, and $c_1, \dots, c_n \in \mathbb{R}$, $\sum_{i,j=1}^{n} c_i c_j\,\kappa(x_i, x_j) \ge 0$. For such a structure and $r \in \mathbb{N}$ we define:

1. **Non-commutative**: there are $x, y, z \in M$ with $[x, y, z] \neq [z, y, x]$;
2. **Touches zero**: for every $\varepsilon > 0$ there are $x, y \in M$ with $\kappa(x, y) < \varepsilon$;
3. **Chain connected**: for every $\varepsilon > 0$ and all $x, y \in M$ there is a finite sequence $x = x_0, x_1, \dots, x_k = y$ ($k \ge 0$) with $\kappa(x_{i-1}, x_i) > 1 - \varepsilon$ for $1 \le i \le k$;
4. **Gram rank at most $r$**: for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$ and $x_1, \dots, x_n \in M$, the matrix $[\kappa(x_i, x_j)]_{i,j=1}^n$ has rank at most $r$;
5. **Feature rank at most $r$** (the reading is realizable in Euclidean $r$-space): there is $\varphi : M \to \mathbb{R}^r$ with $\kappa(x, y) = \langle\varphi(x), \varphi(y)\rangle$ for all $x, y \in M$;
6. **Reading complete**: for every sequence $(x_n)_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$ in $M$ such that for every $\varepsilon > 0$ there is $N \in \mathbb{N}$ with $\kappa(x_m, x_n) > 1 - \varepsilon$ for all $m, n \ge N$, there is $a \in M$ such that for every $\varepsilon > 0$ there is $N \in \mathbb{N}$ with $\kappa(x_n, a) > 1 - \varepsilon$ for all $n \ge N$.

::: proof

By de Moivre's formula $(\cos\theta + \sin\theta\,n)^N = \cos N\theta + \sin N\theta\,n$, the powers with exponent $\lfloor t/\theta\rfloor$ tend along that subsequence to $\cos t + \sin t\,a$, and since $H$ is closed the whole circle $\{\cos t + \sin t\,a\}$ about the axis $a$ lies in $H$. ::: {.remark #rem-ch39-axiom-omega-formalization-bridge title="How the Mother Axiom Corresponds to the Manifold Form"}
**Correspondence and status.** The clauses of the mother axiom in §39.1.2 and in [@def-ch39-axiom-omega-ternary-comparison-closure] are, one by one, the axioms and predicates of [@def-ch39-ternary-comparison-structure], with only two rewritings: the $\mathbf{G}_n \succeq 0$ of Law (II) is nonnegativity of the Gram quadratic form (a real symmetric matrix is positive semidefinite precisely when its quadratic form is nonnegative, and the case $n = 0$ is trivial), and, because $\kappa \ge 0$, $\inf\kappa = 0$ is touching zero. 1. **Clauses shared by both statements (direct correspondence).** The three identities of Law (I); the symmetry, extensional separation $\kappa(x, y) = 1 \iff x = y$ (the outcome of `H1` quotienting), nonnegativity, two-sided transport invariance, Gram positivity, and zero touching of Law (II); and the order sensitivity $\exists\, x, y, z,\; [x, y, z] \neq [z, y, x]$ of Law (III) are the same in both. If $a \in A$, continuity of $\kappa(a, \cdot)$ and $\kappa(a, a) = 1$ let every point of some neighborhood of $a$ be reached in one more step, so $A$ is open. ::: {.remark #rem-ch39-axiom-omega-round-trip-closing-law title="A Single-Structure Form of Law (III): the Round-Trip Law and d'Alembert's Functional Equation"}
**Round-trip law (`manual derivation`).** Write $s_x(w) := [x, w, x]$ for the reflection of $w$ in $x$. On $S^3(1)$ one has $s_x(w) = 2\langle x, w\rangle x - w$, hence $\langle w, s_x(w)\rangle = 2\langle x, w\rangle^2 - 1$, and the master kernel $\kappa_{S^3}$ satisfies

$$
\kappa\bigl(w,\, [x, w, x]\bigr) \;=\; \bigl(2\kappa(w, x) - 1\bigr)^2 \qquad (\forall\, w, x \in \mathcal{M}).
$$

The minimal feature rank of Law (III) (the "minimal dimension, minimal rank" of the manifold form) minimizes over a whole class of structures; the identity above is a property of a single structure and can replace it as the closing law. On the carrier of the manifold form, on top of Law (I) and the symmetry, faithfulness $\kappa(x, y) = 1 \iff x = y$ and two-sided transport invariance clauses of Law (II) (continuity of $\kappa$ suffices; that form's $C^2$ regularity clause can be dropped, since real-analyticity of $\kappa$ follows from the group structure), require that every $x$ have exactly one zero partner $x^\perp$ ($\kappa(x, x^\perp) = 0$). Then the structures satisfying the round-trip law are, up to isomorphism, exactly two: the unit circle $S^1$ and the unit 3-sphere $S^3$, both with $[x, y, z] = x y^{-1} z$ and $\kappa(x, y) = \lVert (x + y)/2\rVert^2$. **d'Alembert's functional equation (`Literature`).** Put $\psi := 2\varphi - 1$. The two-variable identity $\psi(ab) + \psi(ab^{-1}) = 2\psi(a)\psi(b)$ is d'Alembert's functional equation on a group; T. M. K. Davison (*Aequationes Math.* **76**, 33–53, 2008; *Publ. Math. If the two-variable form (the interference law below) is taken as the axiom, only Law (I) and the symmetry, faithfulness and two-sided invariance clauses of Law (II) are needed, neither the unique-zero-partner clause nor the $C^2$ clause, and the solutions are exactly $S^1$ and $S^3$ (`manual derivation`; it also follows from the literature just cited); the round-trip law is its diagonal case $a = b$. The one-variable round-trip law alone does not exclude $\mathrm{SO}(3)$: there $\psi = \cos\alpha$ ($\alpha$ the rotation angle) is faithful, smooth and satisfies the duplication formula, yet for the rotations $a, b$ by $\pi/2$ about two orthogonal axes $\psi(ab) + \psi(ab^{-1}) = -1 \ne 0 = 2\psi(a)\psi(b)$, and the corresponding $\kappa = \cos^2(\alpha/2)$ vanishes on every rotation by $\pi$, so the zero partner is not unique. The unique-zero-partner clause therefore cannot be dropped.

**Operational form: two-readout determinacy (`manual derivation`).** Write $c := 2\kappa - 1$ for the correlation. On $S^3(1)$ the round-trip law holds together with the interference law

$$
c\bigl(y,\, [x, w, x]\bigr) \;=\; 2\,c(w, x)\,c(y, x) \;-\; c(y, w) \qquad (\forall\, w, x, y \in \mathcal{M}).
$$

Operationally: watching any test point $y$ along a uniform transport $\dots, w, x, [x, w, x], \dots$, the reading of $y$ against the next point is fixed affinely by its two readings against the previous two points. Under Law (I) and Law (II) (including the unique-zero-partner clause), the statement "for every pair $w, x$ there are reals $a, b, d$ depending only on $(w, x)$ such that $\kappa(y, [x, w, x]) = a\,\kappa(y, x) + b\,\kappa(y, w) + d$ for all $y$" is equivalent to the round-trip law, and then $a = 2c(w, x)$, $b = -1$, $d = 1 - c(w, x)$, which is the interference law above. The unique-zero-partner clause cannot be dropped here either: on $S^3$, $\kappa'(x, y) = \frac{3}{4} + \frac{1}{4}\langle x, y\rangle$ satisfies the affine determinacy law but has no zero and violates the round-trip law. **Weakened associativity does not give di-associativity (`manual derivation`).** The Mal'cev identities $[x, x, z] = z$, $[x, z, z] = x$ together with the third identity on subsystems generated by two elements do not imply that $x \cdot y := [x, o, y]$ is a di-associative loop, nor that $[x, y, z] = (x \cdot y^{-1}) \cdot z$. **Rigidity on $S^3$ (`Literature`: an elementary consequence of Hurwitz's theorem; the reduction is a `manual derivation`).** Let $T\colon (S^3)^3 \to S^3$ be continuous, satisfy the two Mal'cev identities, and let the standard $\kappa$ be invariant under all left and all right transports.

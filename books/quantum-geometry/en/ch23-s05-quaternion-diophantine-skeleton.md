# 23.5 From Binary Quadratic Forms to Quaternion Norm Spheres: Defining $\mathbb{Z}$ in $\mathbb{Q}$ {#sec-ch23-3}

How does modern arithmetic geometry scale this norm-and-trace locking mechanism up from two-dimensional quadratic fields to four-dimensional **quaternion algebras** over $\mathbb{Q}$, isolating $\mathbb{Z}$ inside $\mathbb{Q}$?

In a four-dimensional quaternion algebra over $\mathbb{Q}$ with nonzero parameters $a, b \in \mathbb{Q}^\times$,

$$
H_{a, b}(\mathbb{Q}) \;=\; \mathbb{Q} \oplus \mathbb{Q}\mathbf{i} \oplus \mathbb{Q}\mathbf{j} \oplus \mathbb{Q}\mathbf{k} \qquad (\mathbf{i}^2 = a,\; \mathbf{j}^2 = b,\; \mathbf{i}\mathbf{j} = -\mathbf{j}\mathbf{i} = \mathbf{k}),
$$

every quaternion $\alpha = x_0 + x_1\mathbf{i} + x_2\mathbf{j} + x_3\mathbf{k} \in H_{a,b}(\mathbb{Q})$ carries a **reduced trace** and a quaternary quadratic **reduced norm**:

$$
\operatorname{Trd}(\alpha) = \alpha + \bar{\alpha} = 2x_0, \qquad
\operatorname{Nrd}(\alpha) = \alpha\bar{\alpha} = x_0^2 - a x_1^2 - b x_2^2 + ab x_3^2.
$$

::: {.figure src="ch23-quaternion-norm"}
**Four dimensions, the same lock.** Take $a = b = -1$ (Hamilton quaternions), so $\operatorname{Nrd}(\alpha) = x_0^2 + x_1^2 + x_2^2 + x_3^2$. The horizontal axis is the scalar part $x_0$ (half the trace), the vertical axis the length $\lvert v\rvert$ of the vector part; the four cyan half circles are $\operatorname{Nrd} = 1, 2, 3, 4$, and the number beside a point counts the integer quaternions there, $8, 24, 32, 24$ on the four half circles (counted by exhaustive search). No point lies between the circles, and the trace $\operatorname{Trd} = 2x_0$ takes only the even numbers from $-4$ to $4$.
:::

How quaternion reduced norms $\operatorname{Nrd}(\alpha)$ enforce local-global locking over $\mathbb{Q}$, together with the boundary among existential ($\exists$), universal ($\forall$), and universal-existential ($\forall\exists$) first-order quantifier prefixes, is summarized in the literature remark below ([@rem-diophantine-z-in-q-literature]). Whether $\mathbb{Z}$ inside $\mathbb{Q}$ admits a purely existential $\exists$ definition remains open. In §23.5.1, we then develop explicit derivations and exact witnesses for Hurwitz–Lipschitz norm spheres, Hilbert-symbol ramification reciprocity, and rational Cayley norm-one trace locks.

::: {.remark #rem-diophantine-z-in-q-literature title="Koenigsmann (2016) on Defining ℤ in ℚ, Quantifier Complexity, Quaternion Norm Locks, and H10(ℚ) (Literature Result, Not Formalized in This Book)"}
**Literature result, not formalized in this book.** Quaternion reduced norms succeed globally over $\mathbb{Q}$ where binary quadratic forms alone do not suffice through two classical mechanisms of arithmetic geometry: (a) **the Hasse–Minkowski local-global principle and controlled ramification** — a quaternary quadratic form has a nontrivial zero over $\mathbb{Q}$ if and only if it has a nontrivial zero over $\mathbb{R}$ and every $p$-adic completion $\mathbb{Q}_p$. The ramification sets obtained by varying nonzero $(a, b)$ obey reciprocity constraints; for instance, taking $a, b > 0$ makes the algebra split at the real place, and its finite-prime ramification set $\Delta_{a,b}$ has even cardinality; and (b) **quadratic reciprocity and trace sums sweeping across prime locks** — in Poonen's (2009) construction, take the reduced-trace set $S_{a,b}$ of rational elements of reduced norm one, then add two traces and one of finitely many integer shifts. The resulting $T_{a,b}$ equals $\bigcap_{p \in \Delta_{a,b}} \mathbb{Z}_{(p)}$, retaining the real-split condition $a, b > 0$. Varying these parameters covers every prime's integrality lock, closing onto the integers through $\bigcap_p \mathbb{Z}_{(p)} = \mathbb{Z}$. This local-global route runs through the work of Robinson (1949), Poonen (2009), and Koenigsmann (2016). To align this literature landscape with our formal verification contract, three distinctions are essential:

1. **Existential Diophantine ($\exists$) vs. first-order ($\forall, \forall\exists$) definability and Hilbert's Tenth Problem over $\mathbb{Q}$**:
   - Strictly speaking, a subset $S \subseteq \mathbb{Q}$ is **Diophantine (existentially definable, $\exists$)** over $\mathbb{Q}$ if there exists a polynomial $P(t, x_1, \dots, x_m) \in \mathbb{Z}[t, x_1, \dots, x_m]$ such that

     $$
     t \in S \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \exists x_1, \dots, x_m \in \mathbb{Q},\; P(t, x_1, \dots, x_m) = 0.
     $$

   - A subset defined using both universal ($\forall$) and existential ($\exists$) quantifiers is **first-order definable** in the language of fields. The quantifier pattern is crucial: by the Matiyasevich–Robinson–Davis–Putnam theorem (1970), Hilbert's Tenth Problem over $\mathbb{Z}$ ($\mathrm{H10}(\mathbb{Z})$) is undecidable. If $\mathbb{Z}$ had a purely existential ($\Sigma_1$, i.e., $\exists$) Diophantine definition in $\mathbb{Q}$, any polynomial system over $\mathbb{Z}$ would translate into an existential polynomial system over $\mathbb{Q}$, immediately proving that $\mathrm{H10}(\mathbb{Q})$ is undecidable! Conversely, as explained in Bjorn Poonen's survey *"Mazur's work relating logic and arithmetic geometry"*, **Mazur's conjecture on the real topology of rational points** (which predicts that for any variety $V/\mathbb{Q}$, the topological closure of $V(\mathbb{Q})$ inside $V(\mathbb{R})$ has at most finitely many connected components) would **rule out** any existential Diophantine ($\Sigma_1$) definition of $\mathbb{Z}$ in $\mathbb{Q}$ (since $\mathbb{Z} \subset \mathbb{R}$ has infinitely many real connected components)! **Whether $\mathbb{Z}$ is existentially ($\exists$) Diophantine in $\mathbb{Q}$ — and whether $\mathrm{H10}(\mathbb{Q})$ is decidable — remains OPEN.**
2. **The quantifier-compression milestones of Robinson (1949), Poonen (2009), Koenigsmann (2016), and subsequent papers**:
   - **Julia Robinson (1949, *Journal of Symbolic Logic* 14(2), 98–114, "Definability and decision problems in arithmetic")**: gave the first first-order definition of $\mathbb{Z}$ in $\mathbb{Q}$ using quadratic forms and Hasse–Minkowski local-global conditions, with a $\Pi_3$ ($\forall\exists\forall$, three-block alternating) quantifier prefix;
   - **Bjorn Poonen (2009, *American Journal of Mathematics* 131(3), 675–682, "Characterizing integers among rational numbers with a universal-existential formula")**: used quaternion algebras to compress the definition of $\mathbb{Z}$ in $\mathbb{Q}$ to a $\Pi_2$ ($\forall^2\exists^7$) formula with $2$ universal and $7$ existential quantifiers;
   - **Jochen Koenigsmann (2016, *Annals of Mathematics* 183(1), 73–93, "Defining $\mathbb{Z}$ in $\mathbb{Q}$", [arXiv:1011.3424](https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.3424))**: using quaternary quadratic forms / quaternion algebras, the Hasse–Minkowski local-global principle, existential definability of the **Jacobson radical** (§2 Step 3 of his proof), and quadratic reciprocity, proved **two distinct results**:
     1. **Theorem 1 ($\Pi_1$ universal definition of $\mathbb{Z}$ in $\mathbb{Q}$ / $\Sigma_1$ Diophantine definition of $\mathbb{Q} \setminus \mathbb{Z}$)**: the non-integers $\mathbb{Q} \setminus \mathbb{Z}$ are Diophantine ($\Sigma_1$; the $418$-variable count follows the early preprint [arXiv:1011.3424v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.3424v1), which explicitly lists the universal formula below) over $\mathbb{Q}$; taking its logical negation in the standard first-order language of rings (where negated equality $\neq$ is directly permitted) shows that **$\mathbb{Z}$ is universally ($\Pi_1$, one universal quantifier block) definable in $\mathbb{Q}$**:

        $$
        t \in \mathbb{Z} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \forall x_1, \dots, x_{418} \in \mathbb{Q},\; g(t, x_1, \dots, x_{418}) \neq 0.
        $$

     2. **Corollary 21 (separate one-universal $\forall^1\exists$ construction)**: by a separate arithmetic encoding that compresses the prime-indexed local conditions into a single universally quantified variable (rather than a mere syntactic rewrite of $\neq 0$), $\mathbb{Z}$ is definable in $\mathbb{Q}$ by a $\forall\exists$ formula with **a single universal quantifier** ($\forall y\,\exists z_1, \dots, \exists z_M$).
   - **Subsequent named advances (Dittmann 2018; Daans 2021; Zhang–Sun 2022; Daans 2023/2024)**:
     1. **Philip Dittmann (2018, *Compositio Mathematica* 154(4), 761–772, "Irreducibility of polynomials over global fields is Diophantine", [10.1112/S0010437X17007977](https://doi.org/10.1112/S0010437X17007977), [arXiv:1601.07829](https://arxiv.org/abs/1601.07829))**: extended the norm-and-trace method to central simple algebras of prime degree, combining local-global principles with class field theory to prove that irreducibility of polynomials over global fields is Diophantine;
     2. **Nicolas Daans (2021, *Documenta Mathematica* 26, 1851–1869, "Universally defining finitely generated subrings of global fields", [10.4171/dm/858](https://doi.org/10.4171/dm/858), [arXiv:1812.04372](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.04372))**: established universal definitions of finitely generated subrings of global fields in their fraction fields and reduced the number of universal quantifiers needed to define $\mathbb{Z}$ in $\mathbb{Q}$ to **$37$** ($\forall^{37}$);
     3. **Geng-Rui Zhang and Zhi-Wei Sun (2022, *Bulletin of the Polish Academy of Sciences: Mathematics* 70(2), 93–106, "$\mathbb{Q}\setminus\mathbb{Z}$ is diophantine over $\mathbb{Q}$ with $32$ unknowns", [10.4064/ba221231-19-3](https://doi.org/10.4064/ba221231-19-3), [arXiv:2104.02520](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.02520))**: proved that $\mathbb{Q}\setminus\mathbb{Z}$ is Diophantine over $\mathbb{Q}$ with $32$ unknowns, equivalently reducing the universal definition of $\mathbb{Z}$ in $\mathbb{Q}$ to **$32$** universal quantifiers ($\forall^{32}$);
     4. **Nicolas Daans (2023 preprint / 2024, *Journal of the London Mathematical Society* 109(2), e12864, "Universally defining $\mathbb{Z}$ in $\mathbb{Q}$ with $10$ quantifiers", [10.1112/jlms.12864](https://doi.org/10.1112/jlms.12864), [arXiv:2301.02107](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.02107))**: combined quadratic-form norm equations with refined local-global reciprocity to compress the universal ($\Pi_1$) definition of $\mathbb{Z}$ in $\mathbb{Q}$ down to **only $10$ universal quantifiers** ($\forall^{10}$). These advances sharpen the $\Pi_1$ and $\Pi_2$ boundaries without resolving the open existential ($\Sigma_1$) Diophantine question for $\mathbb{Z} \subset \mathbb{Q}$.
3. **Exact scope of the four frozen Lean declarations of §23.4**: The four kernel-verified declarations in [§23.4](ch23-s04-eisenstein-and-golden-pell.md) — [@thm-qform-eq-eisnorm] (`Qform_eq_eisNorm`), [@lem-qform-discriminant] (`Qform_discriminant`), [@cor-qform-range-eq-eisnorm] (`Qform_range_eq_eisNorm`), and [@prop-pell-form-eq-four-mul-norm] (`pell_form_eq_four_mul_norm`) — certify the exact integer quadratic norm-form identities for the $\Delta = -3$ Eisenstein crossing family and the $\Delta = +5$ Golden Pell form. They exhibit the concrete algebraic mechanism by which quadratic norms and traces lock integer lattices, **without claiming to formalize Koenigsmann's theorem on $\mathbb{Z} \subset \mathbb{Q}$**.
:::

## 23.5.1 Hurwitz–Lipschitz Norm Spheres, Hilbert-Symbol Ramification Reciprocity, and Rational Cayley Norm-One Trace Locks {#sec-ch23-5-hurwitz-jacobi-hilbert-ramification-trace-locks}

How does this local-global arithmetic locking mechanism unfold through concrete, exact examples—from the sphere counts and Euclidean covering radius of a single four-dimensional order, through the ramification reciprocity of local Hilbert symbols, to a prime-by-prime sieve excluding prime factors from reduced denominators via rational Cayley traces of finite families of real-split algebras? The three subsections below carry out this progression with core written derivations and representative exact witnesses, separating the chapter's written arguments (`ARGUED`) from cited literature theorems ([Serre 1973](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1007/978-1-4684-9884-4), [Conway & Smith 2003](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1201/9781439864180), [Poonen 2009](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1353/ajm.0.0057), [Koenigsmann 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.3424), [Voight 2021](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1007/978-3-030-56694-4), and [Daans 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.02107)) and the finite numerical checks in the companion archive (`NUM`).

### 23.5.1.1 Lipschitz Lattice $\mathcal{L}$ vs. Hurwitz Maximal Order $\mathcal{H}$: Jacobi's Four-Square Law, Half-Integer Odd-Norm Parity, and Covering Radii {#sec-ch23-5-1-lipschitz-hurwitz-jacobi}

Set $a = b = -1$, let $\mathcal{L} = \mathbb{Z} + \mathbb{Z}\mathbf{i} + \mathbb{Z}\mathbf{j} + \mathbb{Z}\mathbf{k}$ be the Lipschitz integer order, and let $\mathcal{H} = \mathcal{L} \cup \bigl(\mathcal{L} + h\bigr)$ be the Hurwitz maximal order with $h = \frac{1 + \mathbf{i} + \mathbf{j} + \mathbf{k}}{2}$. In doubled coordinates $2\alpha \in \mathbb{Z}^4$, the two cosets of $\mathcal{H}$ consist respectively of all-even and all-odd integer quadruples, and the first doubled coordinate equals the reduced trace $\operatorname{Trd}(\alpha)$. Because every odd square satisfies $(2k + 1)^2 \equiv 1 \pmod 8$, the sum of four odd squares satisfies $\sum_{j=0}^3 (2k_j + 1)^2 \equiv 4 \pmod 8$, so dividing by $4$ leaves remainder $1 \pmod 2$—hence **every strictly half-integer Hurwitz quaternion has odd reduced norm**.

For a positive integer $n$, define its odd-divisor sum $\sigma_{\mathrm{odd}}(n) = \sum_{d \mid n,\; 2 \nmid d} d$; by Jacobi's classical four-square theorem ([Voight 2021](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1007/978-3-030-56694-4), Eq. $(1.4.2)$), the Lipschitz norm-sphere count is $r_4(n) = 8 \sum_{d \mid n,\; 4 \nmid d} d$ (writing $n = 2^s m$ with $m$ odd gives for $s = 0$ the value $8\sigma(m)$, and for $s \ge 1$ the value $24\sigma(m)$). Moreover, right multiplication by $1 + \mathbf{i}$ sends $(w, x, y, z)$ to $(w - x, w + x, y + z, z - y)$, doubling the reduced norm and mapping both cosets of $\mathcal{H}$ into $\mathcal{L}$; conversely, for any $\beta \in \mathcal{L}$ satisfying $\operatorname{Nrd}(\beta) = 2n$, the sum of its four integer coordinates is even, so its front and back coordinate pairs share the same pair-sum parity, ensuring the inverse map $\beta \mapsto \beta(1 - \mathbf{i})/2$ produces four all-integer or four all-half-integer coordinates inside $\mathcal{H}$. Combined with Jacobi's theorem, this bijection directly proves that for every positive integer $n \ge 1$,

$$
r_{\mathcal{H}}(n) \;=\; r_4(2n) \;=\; 24\,\sigma_{\mathrm{odd}}(n), \qquad \frac{r_{\mathcal{H}}(n)}{r_4(n)} \;=\; \begin{cases} 3, & 2 \nmid n, \\ 1, & 2 \mid n. \end{cases}
$$

The table below compares the sphere counts for $n = 1, \dots, 8$ (matching `ch23-quaternion-norm` at $n = 1, 2, 3, 4$ with counts $8, 24, 32, 24$; the companion archive verifies exhaustively through $n = 24$):

| $n$ | $r_4(n)$ | $r_{\mathcal{H}}(n)$ | $r_{\mathcal{H}}(n)/r_4(n)$ |
| --------: | --------------: | ----------------------: | ------------------------: |
| $1$ | $8$ | $24$ | $3$ |
| $2$ | $24$ | $24$ | $1$ |
| $3$ | $32$ | $96$ | $3$ |
| $4$ | $24$ | $24$ | $1$ |
| $5$ | $48$ | $144$ | $3$ |
| $6$ | $96$ | $96$ | $1$ |
| $7$ | $64$ | $192$ | $3$ |
| $8$ | $24$ | $24$ | $1$ |

For trace distributions, $\mathcal{L}$ realizes only even reduced traces whereas $\mathcal{H}$ supplies odd reduced traces at odd norms: at $n = 1$, the $8$ Lipschitz units (the quaternion group $Q_8$) distribute across $\operatorname{Trd} \in \{-2, 0, 2\}$ as $1, 6, 1$, whereas the $24$ Hurwitz units (the binary tetrahedral group $\mathcal{H}^\times \cong Q_8 \rtimes C_3 \cong 2T$, where $(-1 + \mathbf{i} + \mathbf{j} + \mathbf{k})/2$ has order $3$ and cycles the three imaginary axes by conjugation) distribute across $\operatorname{Trd} \in \{-2, -1, 0, 1, 2\}$ as $1, 8, 6, 8, 1$; at $n = 3$, $\mathcal{L}$ distributes across $\operatorname{Trd} \in \{-2, 0, 2\}$ as $12, 8, 12$, whereas $\mathcal{H}$ distributes across $\operatorname{Trd} \in \{-3, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, 3\}$ as $8, 12, 24, 8, 24, 12, 8$; and at even norms $n = 2, 4$ both trace histograms agree (at $n = 2$, across $\operatorname{Trd} \in \{-2, 0, 2\}$, $6, 12, 6$; at $n = 4$, across $\operatorname{Trd} \in \{-4, -2, 0, 2, 4\}$, $1, 8, 6, 8, 1$). In the basis $\{1, \mathbf{i}, \mathbf{j}, h\}$, the conjugation-trace Gram matrix has determinant $2^4 (1/2)^2 = 4$, so $\mathcal{H}$ has reduced discriminant $2$, matching the algebra discriminant and confirming that $\mathcal{H}$ is a maximal order ([Voight 2021](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1007/978-3-030-56694-4), §§11.2–11.3; [Conway & Smith 2003](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1201/9781439864180)).

The arithmetic divisibility divide between the two orders stems from four-dimensional Euclidean covering radii: for any real quadruple with coordinate distances $d_j \in [0, 1/2]$ to the nearest integers, the squared Euclidean distances to the integer and half-integer cosets are $A = \sum_{j=0}^3 d_j^2$ and $B = \sum_{j=0}^3 (1/2 - d_j)^2$. The elementary inequality $d^2 + (1/2 - d)^2 \le 1/4$ proves the upper bound $\min(A, B) \le (A + B)/2 \le 1/2$, and at $(1/2, 1/2, 0, 0)$ exact equality $A = B = 1/2$ holds (the companion archive checks its $8$ closest Hurwitz points $0, 1, \mathbf{i}, 1 + \mathbf{i}$ and $(1 + \mathbf{i} \pm \mathbf{j} \pm \mathbf{k})/2$), so $\mathcal{H}$ has exact covering radius $\sqrt{1/2}$ (rounding from the archived Arb enclosure to six decimal places as $0.707107$) and supports both left- and right-Euclidean division with remainder norm at most half the divisor norm by norm multiplicativity; by contrast, $A \le 1$ with equality at $h$ gives $\mathcal{L}$ covering radius $1$, and dividing $1 + \mathbf{i} + \mathbf{j} + \mathbf{k}$ by $2$ forces every Lipschitz remainder to have norm at least $4 = \operatorname{Nrd}(2)$, breaking Euclidean division. The map $(w, x, y, z) \mapsto (w + x, w - x, y + z, y - z)$ scales lengths by $\sqrt{2}$ and identifies $\mathcal{H}$ with the root lattice $D_4 = \{u \in \mathbb{Z}^4 : \sum u_j \equiv 0 \pmod 2\}$, so $\mathcal{H}$ has covolume $2/(\sqrt{2})^4 = 1/2$ (while $\mathcal{L}$ has covolume $1$); both lattices have shortest nonzero vector length $1$ (packing radius $1/2$), so the four-ball volume $\frac{\pi^2}{2} r^4$ yields exact four-dimensional sphere-packing densities $\Delta(\mathcal{H}) = \frac{\pi^2}{16}$ (matching $D_4$, rounding from the archived enclosure to six decimal places as $0.616850$) versus $\Delta(\mathcal{L}) = \frac{\pi^2}{32}$ (rounding to six decimal places as $0.308425$), with exact density ratio $2$.

### 23.5.1.2 Local Hilbert Symbols, Ramification Reciprocity, Explicit Splitting Isomorphisms, and Definite Eichler Mass {#sec-ch23-5-2-hilbert-symbols-ramification-splitting}

For any nonzero rational pair $(a, b) \in (\mathbb{Q}^\times)^2$, the quaternion algebra $H_{a,b}(\mathbb{Q})$ splits at a place $v$ if and only if its local Hilbert symbol satisfies $(a, b)_v = +1$ ([Serre 1973](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1007/978-1-4684-9884-4), Ch. III; [Voight 2021](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1007/978-3-030-56694-4), §§12.4, 14.2). At the real place $\infty$, $(a, b)_\infty = -1$ if and only if $a < 0$ and $b < 0$; at a prime $p$, writing $a = p^m u$ and $b = p^n v$ with $p$-adic units $u, v$, the odd-prime $p$ local symbol is given by Legendre symbols evaluated on $u, v$ reduced modulo $p$:

$$
(a, b)_p \;=\; (-1)^{m n (p - 1)/2}\left(\frac{u}{p}\right)^n \left(\frac{v}{p}\right)^m,
$$

while at $p = 2$ one has $(a, b)_2 = (-1)^e$ with exponent $e = \frac{(u - 1)(v - 1)}{4} + m\frac{v^2 - 1}{8} + n\frac{u^2 - 1}{8}$ read modulo $2$. For every odd prime $p$ satisfying $v_p(a) = v_p(b) = 0$ (which for $a, b \in \mathbb{Z} \setminus \{0\}$ reduces to $p \nmid ab$), one has $(a, b)_p = +1$; bilinearity on rational squareclasses together with quadratic reciprocity and its two supplements on the generators $-1, 2$, and positive odd primes gives the classical **Hilbert reciprocity law** $\prod_v (a, b)_v = +1$, so the ramification set $\Delta_{a,b} = \{v : (a, b)_v = -1\}$ always has finite even cardinality (with reduced discriminant $D_{a,b} = \prod_{p \in \Delta_{a,b} \setminus \{\infty\}} p$). Three representative presentations exhibit this parity constraint transparently: the definite Hamilton presentation $(-1, -1)$ ramifies at $\Delta_{-1,-1} = \{2, \infty\}$ ($D = 2$); the real-split ramified presentation $(2, 3)$ splits at infinity and at $p = 2, 3$ takes sign $-1$ ($\Delta_{2,3} = \{2, 3\}$, $D = 6$), admitting no primitive norm zeros modulo $16$ or modulo $9$, which certifies local anisotropy at both primes directly by elementary congruences (since any nonzero $p$-adic norm zero scales to a primitive $p$-adic integral vector); and the globally split presentation $(2, 7)$ takes sign $+1$ at every place ($\Delta_{2,7} = \emptyset$, $D = 1$). The companion archive tabulates the complete local Hilbert symbols across $13$ quaternion presentations spanning all three regimes and checks by prime-power congruences across all $17$ finite ramified places that the primitive zero count is $0$.

For the globally split presentation $(2, 7)$ (as well as the isomorphic split presentations $(3, 13)$ and $(5, 11)$), setting $c = 3$ (and $c = 4$ in the other two cases) satisfies $c^2 = a + b$, directly yielding the primitive integral norm zero $(c, 1, 1, 0)$ and an explicit rational matrix isomorphism $\phi : H_{a,b}(\mathbb{Q}) \xrightarrow{\sim} M_2(\mathbb{Q})$:

$$
\phi(\mathbf{i}) \;=\; I \;=\; \begin{pmatrix} 0 & a \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \phi(\mathbf{j}) \;=\; J \;=\; \begin{pmatrix} c & -a \\ 1 & -c \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \phi(\mathbf{k}) \;=\; I J \;=\; \begin{pmatrix} a & -ac \\ c & -a \end{pmatrix}.
$$

Direct multiplication confirms $I^2 = a E$, $J^2 = (c^2 - a)E = b E$, and $I J = -J I$; the basis $\{E, I, J, I J\}$ forms a $4 \times 4$ row-major matrix of determinant $4ab$ ($56, 156, 220 \neq 0$ respectively), and for symbolic $x_0 + x_1\mathbf{i} + x_2\mathbf{j} + x_3\mathbf{k}$ one has identically $\operatorname{Tr}(\phi(\alpha)) = 2x_0 = \operatorname{Trd}(\alpha)$ and $\det(\phi(\alpha)) = \operatorname{Nrd}(\alpha)$. Furthermore, for a maximal order in a definite quaternion algebra over $\mathbb{Q}$ of reduced discriminant $D$, summing over right ideal classes $[I]$ with left order $\mathcal{O}_L(I) = \{x \in H_{a,b}(\mathbb{Q}) : x I \subseteq I\}$, the Eichler mass formula ([Voight 2021](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1007/978-3-030-56694-4), Theorem 25.3.15) gives $\sum_{[I]} \lvert\mathcal{O}_L(I)^\times / \{\pm 1\}\rvert^{-1} = \frac{1}{12}\prod_{p \mid D}(p - 1)$; at $D = 2, 3, 5$, realized by $(-1, -1)$, $(-1, -3)$, and $(-2, -5)$, this yields exact masses $\frac{1}{12}, \frac{1}{6}, \frac{1}{3}$ (rounding from archived Arb enclosures to six decimal places as $0.083333, 0.166667, 0.333333$). The archive explicitly verifies multiplicative closure for three finite norm-one unit groups: at $D = 2$ the $24$ Hurwitz units; at $D = 3$, taking $u = \mathbf{j}$ ($u^2 = -3$), the order-$6$ norm-one element $(1 + u)/2$ together with $\mathbf{i}$ generating a group of order $12$; and at $D = 5$, taking $u = (\mathbf{i} + \mathbf{k})/2$ ($u^2 = -3$), the element $(1 + u)/2$ alone generating a group of order $6$. For any finite group $G \subset H_{a,b}^1(\mathbb{Q})$ and any full $\mathbb{Z}$-lattice $\Lambda_0$, the sum lattice $\Lambda = \sum_{g \in G} g\Lambda_0$ is a $G$-invariant full lattice, so $G$ lies inside the left order $\mathcal{O}_L(\Lambda)$ and hence inside the unit group of a maximal order; combined with the literature classification at $D = 2, 3, 5$ giving class number $1$ ([Voight 2021](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1007/978-3-030-56694-4), Table 25.4.4), the three unit-group orders modulo $\{\pm 1\}$, namely $12, 6, 3$ (full orders $24, 12, 6$), exhaust the entire mass.

### 23.5.1.3 Generalized Rational Cayley Chart, Hensel Proof of the $p$-Adic Trace Lock, and Finite Prime-Lock Intersections {#sec-ch23-5-3-cayley-trace-locks-and-sieve}

To express this local-global ramification mechanism by explicit rational formulas, we analyze the rational norm-one trace set $S_{a,b} = \{\operatorname{Trd}(\alpha) : \alpha \in H_{a,b}^1(\mathbb{Q})\}$ introduced in [@rem-diophantine-z-in-q-literature]. For any pure quaternion $v = x_1\mathbf{i} + x_2\mathbf{j} + x_3\mathbf{k} \in H_{a,b}^0(\mathbb{Q})$, set $N = \operatorname{Nrd}(v) = -a x_1^2 - b x_2^2 + ab x_3^2$. Since $v^2 = -N$, we have $(1 - v)(1 + v) = 1 + N$, so on the domain $1 + N \neq 0$ the **generalized rational Cayley chart** expresses norm-one elements and their reduced traces by four rational operations:

$$
\alpha(v) \;=\; (1 + v)(1 - v)^{-1} \;=\; \frac{1 - N + 2v}{1 + N}, \qquad \operatorname{Nrd}(\alpha(v)) \;=\; \frac{(1 - N)^2 + 4N}{(1 + N)^2} \;=\; 1, \qquad \operatorname{Trd}(\alpha(v)) \;=\; \frac{2(1 - N)}{1 + N}.
$$

Conversely, for any norm-one element with $\alpha + 1$ invertible (i.e., $\operatorname{Nrd}(\alpha + 1) = 2 + \operatorname{Trd}(\alpha) \neq 0$), the element $\alpha$ maps to a pure quaternion $v = (\alpha - 1)(\alpha + 1)^{-1}$; whenever $H_{a,b}(\mathbb{Q})$ is a division algebra (in particular for every algebra with nonempty ramification set), $1 + N(v) = \operatorname{Nrd}(1 - v)$ never vanishes on rational pure quaternions and the chart complement is the single point $-1$ (since $\operatorname{Trd}(\alpha) = -2$ forces $(\alpha + 1)^2 = 0$, hence $\alpha = -1$ without zero divisors).

This yields a self-contained **proof of the ramified-prime trace integrality lock** ([Poonen 2009](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1353/ajm.0.0057), Lemma 2.1): let $p \in \Delta_{a,b} \setminus \{\infty\}$ be a finite ramified prime so that $H_{a,b}(\mathbb{Q}_p)$ is a division algebra, and first take any local norm-one element $\alpha \in H_{a,b}^1(\mathbb{Q}_p)$ with $t = \operatorname{Trd}(\alpha)$, so $\alpha$ satisfies $X^2 - t X + 1 = 0$. Suppose for contradiction that $v_p(t) < 0$; then $f(X) = X^2 - (1 - 4/t^2) \in \mathbb{Z}_p[X]$ satisfies at $X_0 = 1$ the inequality $v_p(f(1)) = 2 v_p(2) - 2 v_p(t) > 2 v_p(2) = 2 v_p(f'(1))$, so Hensel's lemma makes $1 - 4/t^2$ a square in $\mathbb{Q}_p^\times$; hence the discriminant $t^2(1 - 4/t^2)$ is a nonzero square and $X^2 - t X + 1$ factors into two distinct linear factors over $\mathbb{Q}_p$. Because the division algebra $H_{a,b}(\mathbb{Q}_p)$ has no zero divisors, $\alpha$ must equal a scalar root $r \in \mathbb{Q}_p$; then $1 = \operatorname{Nrd}(r) = r^2$ forces $r = \pm 1$, giving $t = \operatorname{Trd}(\alpha) = \pm 2$ (where $v_p(\pm 2) \ge 0$), contradicting $v_p(t) < 0$! Hence for every finite ramified prime $p \in \Delta_{a,b} \setminus \{\infty\}$ and every local norm-one element one has $v_p(\operatorname{Trd}(\alpha)) \ge 0$ (under the convention $v_p(0) = +\infty$); restricting to rational norm-one elements proves for each individual algebra that $S_{a,b} \subseteq \bigcap_{p \in \Delta_{a,b} \setminus \{\infty\}} \mathbb{Z}_{(p)}$. Conversely, at any finite unramified prime $\ell \notin \Delta_{a,b}$, inside $H_{a,b}(\mathbb{Q}_\ell) \cong M_2(\mathbb{Q}_\ell)$ the diagonal matrix $\operatorname{diag}(\ell^{-k}, \ell^k)$ ($k \ge 1$) has determinant $1$, trace valuation $-k$, and invertible sum with the identity, corresponding to a local pure Cayley parameter $v_0 \in H_{a,b}^0(\mathbb{Q}_\ell)$; because both the nonvanishing denominator $1 + N(v) \neq 0$ and the exact valuation $v_\ell(\operatorname{Trd}(\alpha(v))) = -k$ persist on a neighborhood of $v_0$ in the $\ell$-adic topology, density of $H_{a,b}^0(\mathbb{Q})$ in $H_{a,b}^0(\mathbb{Q}_\ell)$ yields rational pure parameters $v$ emitting at $\ell$ arbitrarily deep trace poles $v_\ell(\operatorname{Trd}(\alpha(v))) = -k$!

The real-split ramified algebra $H_{2,3}$ ($\Delta_{2,3} = \{2, 3\}$) makes both sides of this constraint concrete by hand: substituting the integer triples $(1, 0, 1)$, $(0, 0, 2)$, and $(3, 6, 0)$ into the Cayley chart produces at the unramified prime $\ell = 5$ exact norm-one traces $\operatorname{Trd} = -\frac{6}{5}, -\frac{46}{25}, -\frac{254}{125}$ (with $v_5 = -1, -2, -3$), while preserving at both ramified primes $p = 2, 3$ the integrality bound $v_p \ge 0$. Notice that $-\frac{6}{5}$ lies in $H_{2,3}$'s own localization intersection $\mathbb{Z}_{(2)} \cap \mathbb{Z}_{(3)}$ yet falls outside any larger intersection locking $5$, because each algebra locks only its own ramified set $\Delta_{a,b}$. For a single real-split algebra $H_{a,b}$, [Poonen 2009](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1353/ajm.0.0057) (Lemma 2.5, summing two traces from the same $S_{a,b}$ plus an integer shift in $\{0, \dots, 2309\}$) and [Koenigsmann 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.3424) (Proposition 6, taking the sumset $S_{a,b} + S_{a,b}$ within the same algebra) prove that these trace-sum sets recover that algebra's localization intersection $\bigcap_{p \in \Delta_{a,b}} \mathbb{Z}_{(p)}$ exactly.

# Prelude Why Rebuild Zero-Axiom Mathematics {#sec-om-prelude}

> *"Infinity is merely the tear left at the end of an unclosed straight line, and $\pi$ and $e$ are merely the infinite-series shavings ground off when flat tangent rulers try to hug a curved arc; once we return to a closed sphere and finite rational ratios, mathematics needs no ghosts."*

For more than two millennia, mathematicians have treated three steep entry fees as non-negotiable before geometry, calculus, or physics can even begin. The first is **Cantor's actual infinity** ($\infty, \aleph_0, \aleph_1$), the supposition that infinitely many objects can be packed all at once into a static, completed container. The second is the **uncountable real and complex continuum** ($\mathbb{R}, \mathbb{C}$), the supposition that a line is filled by infinitely many uncomputable decimal tails. The third is the toolkit of **transcendental approximation constants** ($\pi, e, \varphi$) together with infinite series, limit filters, and the non-constructive Axiom of Choice.

This monograph develops the opposite program: **remove actual infinity, remove the uncomputable continuum, remove flat tangent approximation constants, and rebuild a complete rational mathematics directly atop the zero-axiom bare kernel of the Calculus of Inductive Constructions (CIC) and homogeneous rational arithmetic.** The result covers spherical geometry, origin-free nonlinear algebra, limit-free calculus, harmonic analysis on the three-sphere, adelic spectral arithmetic, and non-Abelian computation. Nothing essential is lost. Quite the reverse: once nineteenth-century linearization scaffolding is stripped away, every algebraic law computes, every rational readout is exact, and every rotation stays strictly on the sphere after finitely many integer operations.

## 0.1 Three Hidden Bills: Actual Infinity, Uncomputable Continua, and Flat Approximation Constants {#sec-om-prelude-three-bills}

Before building a new foundation, it pays to audit the three invoices that standard mathematics quietly signs on page one.

The first bill comes from **actual infinity born of an open straight line**. A Euclidean ruler runs off indefinitely in both directions without closing back on itself; as a result, slope crashes into division by zero ($1/0$) at the vertical direction, forcing an alien symbol $\infty$ to be taped onto the horizon. When that habit of unclosed extension hardens into static completed infinite sets $\aleph_0, \aleph_1$ paired with non-constructive Choice (`Classical.choice`), geometric measure fractures—producing paradoxes such as Banach–Tarski, where a solid unit ball in $\mathbb{R}^3$ is cut into five non-measurable pieces and reassembled by rigid motions into two identical balls. Replace the open line from the start with homogeneous integer pairs $[P : Q]$ on a closed projective circle, and "infinity" turns into an ordinary integer pair $[1 : 0]$ whose denominator $P^2 + Q^2 = 1$ never vanishes. The tear never opens.

The second bill is the **full-measure ghost population of the uncomputable continuum**. Only countably many finite programs or Turing machines exist over any finite alphabet, so in Lebesgue measure fully $100\%$ of points on the real line $\mathbb{R}$ possess no finite algorithmic description whatsoever. Classical textbooks pretend we can pick arbitrary real numbers at will and divide or extract roots freely. Inside a modern proof assistant such as Lean 4 with `Mathlib.Real`, that hidden debt is called due immediately: defining `Real` as a quotient of rational Cauchy sequences imports the three classical axioms `{propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound}`, spreading `noncomputable` across real division, square roots, matrix exponentials, and derivatives, blocking native execution and halting kernel reflection.

The third bill is the **residue of flat approximation constants forced onto curved closure**. The number $\pi$ appears when zero-curvature straight sides of a regular $N$-gon try to mimic a circle, leaving a positive sagitta on every chord until $N \to \infty$ spits out a transcendental remainder. Euler's number $e$ appears when straight tangent Euler steps $(1 + \theta\mathbf{i}/N)^N$ push outward past the unit sphere at every finite $N$, forcing infinitely many infinitesimal corrections to bend a tangent line back onto an arc. The golden ratio $\varphi$ emerges once three-dimensional equal-fidelity configurations hit their rigid four-vertex tetrahedral cap (rational fidelity $\kappa = 1/3$) and a flat fivefold polyhedral or five-step circular symmetry tears out a quadratic irrational residual. Write spherical geometry directly in rational fidelity $\kappa$, rational Cayley transforms, and integer Chebyshev recurrences, and rotations stay locked to the sphere in a single rational step—with zero truncation drift and no need for $\pi$, $e$, or $\varphi$ at the axiomatic floor.

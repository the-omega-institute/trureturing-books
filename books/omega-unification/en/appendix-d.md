# Appendix D Support Differences and Rank Bounds for Fourier Bases

[Chapter 8 §8.1](ch08-flavor-hierarchy-and-ckm.md#sec-ch08-cyclic-obstruction) uses cyclic differences in the support of the first readout to bound the minimum rank of a shared state. It also characterizes when a second readout can force that rank to equal the support size. Difference sets can attain the resulting dimension threshold. A subset with repeated differences can yield a tighter upper bound.

## D.1 Distinct Differences and Modular Golomb Rulers {#sec-ou-appd-golomb}

For a support of size $k$, forcing rank $k$ requires the $k(k-1)$ directed differences to occupy distinct nonzero residues of $\mathbb Z_d$. There are only $d-1$ such residues, so $d\ge k(k-1)+1$. The supports $\{0,1,3\}$ modulo $7$ and $\{0,1,3,9\}$ modulo $13$ attain the threshold. More generally, when $k=q+1$ for a prime power $q$, Singer's cyclic difference set gives a support at the threshold. Take the trace-zero projective points in $\mathbb F_{q^3}^\times/\mathbb F_q^\times$. Each nontrivial translate meets the original set in exactly one point. For prime $q$ and a generator $\alpha$ of $\mathbb F_{q^3}^\times$, [SingerTracePlane.result](lean:D5/S3/Geometry/FiniteGeometry/SingerTracePlane.result) gives exactly $q+1$ indices with $\operatorname{Tr}(\alpha^i)=0$ in one projective period $0\le i<q^2+q+1$, with multiplicity one for every nonzero cyclic difference. The prime-power construction is due to [James Singer, *A theorem in finite projective geometry and some applications to number theory* (1938), pp. 377–385](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9947-1938-1501951-4).

**Seven-site and eleven-site supports.** A support with distinct nonzero directed differences is a modular Golomb ruler. Buratti and Stinson's classification gives every dimension $d\ge48$ for seven supported sites. A witness modulo $48$ is $\{0,1,3,15,20,38,42\}$. The eleven-site dimensions are $120$, $133$, and every $d\ge135$. The support $\{0,1,4,9,23,30,41,43,58,68,74\}$ modulo $120$ gives $110$ distinct nonzero differences, omitting exactly $12,24,\ldots,108$. [Buratti and Stinson, *New Results on Modular Golomb Rulers, Optical Orthogonal Codes and Related Structures* (2020), Theorem 2.3(5), (9), and Table 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.01908v2)

## D.2 Bounds from Subsets (Hand Derivation) {#sec-ou-appd-subsets}

A subset with more collisions can tighten the bound. For nonempty $T\subseteq S$, let $m(T)$ count its distinct nonzero directed differences. Take an extreme point of the feasible set of rank $r$. Its range intersects the coordinate space on $T$ in dimension at least $r-(k-|T|)$. A nonzero invisible Hermitian perturbation on the intersection would violate extremality. Thus the same extreme point satisfies

$$
r\le\min_{\varnothing\ne T\subseteq S}\left(k-|T|+\left\lfloor\sqrt{|T|+m(T)}\right\rfloor\right).
$$

For $S=\{0,1,3,6,9\}$ modulo $12$, the subset $T=\{0,3,6,9\}$ lowers the bound from four to three. There are readouts with a unique shared state of rank three. [Subset bound and explicit witness](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing-books/issues/1676#issuecomment-7233048570)

## D.3 Fourteen- and Fifteen-Site Supports {#sec-ou-appd-fourteen-fifteen}

The smallest cyclic dimension for fourteen sites is $183$. Fourteen sites need $14\cdot13$ distinct nonzero differences, and the support $\{0,1,8,24,37,41,59,107,119,128,134,139,153,181\}$ in $\mathbb Z_{183}$ realizes them all. For fifteen sites, the smallest dimension lies in $[223,255]$. The upper witness in $\mathbb Z_{255}$ is $\{0,13,22,38,45,59,87,142,143,154,162,214,220,224,250\}$, with $210$ distinct directed differences.

The count already excludes dimensions below $211$ for fifteen sites. In dimension $211$ the missing-difference set is empty. At every nontrivial root in characteristic two, inversion is the $2^{105}$ Frobenius power, so the group-ring identity forces the support polynomial to vanish at every such root. A fifteen-term support polynomial of degree at most $210$ cannot do so. Evaluation at $-1$ excludes $212$ and $214$ by giving nonsquare integers. At $213=3\cdot71$, the two missing differences are $\pm a$. For one of the primes $3,71$ not dividing $a$, the cyclotomic norm has odd valuation at a prime acting as inversion, which is impossible.

For $215$ and $216$, project the group-ring identity to $\mathbb Z_{43}$ and $\mathbb Z_{27}$, respectively. For each missing-difference type, recover every binary occupancy option with finite-field norm equations, then check its integer lifts. All $13$ and $17$ types are impossible. At $217$, the projection to $\mathbb Z_{31}$ leaves only two missing-difference types. One fails the characteristic-three autocorrelation on $\mathbb Z_7$. For the other, a linear combination of projected difference sums forces two identical directed differences in $\mathbb Z_{31}\times\mathbb Z_7$. [Witnesses, group-ring obstructions, and complete finite certificates](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing-books/issues/1676#issuecomment-7236858851)

At $218$, the parity classes of a fifteen-site support would have sizes nine and six. The directed differences between the two classes would use all $108$ available odd residues. Projecting to $\mathbb Z_{109}$ and identifying each pair of opposite residues gives a necessary exact cover of $54$ classes by nine rows of size six. An exhaustive search over the $55{,}129$ affine classes of the six-site side finds no such cover. [Parity reduction and executable certificate](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing-books/issues/1676#issuecomment-7237141232)

The next two moduli are also excluded. At $219$, the three residue rows modulo $3$ have sizes $(7,5,3)$, $(7,4,4)$, or $(6,6,3)$. Write the normalized rows in cyclic order as $C,A,B$. Over $\mathbb Z_{73}$, disjointness of $A-C$ and $B-A$ forces $B\cap(A+A-C)=\varnothing$. The three complete enumerations leave just one candidate before the final shift test, and all $73$ shifts give repeated differences. At $220$, parity forces a $6+9$ split. Lifting the autocorrelation counts from modulus $11$ through $22$ to $44$ leaves no occupancy vector after testing $5{,}973{,}000$ candidates in the last lift. [Complete row and occupancy replays](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing-books/issues/1676#issuecomment-7237283786)

At $221$, the projections modulo $13$ and $17$ give $88{,}867$ possible affine classes for the five omitted difference pairs. The first two moments across the rows leave six systems with a possible subset in every row. In each system, points forced by the row options give a repeated directed difference. At $222$, parity permits only $(10,5)$ and $(9,6)$. After projection to $\mathbb Z_{111}$, neither split admits two rows with disjoint sets of internal circular differences. Modulus $225$ is excluded separately. Its balanced three-row case contradicts the sum of all differences congruent to $1$ modulo $3$. The six other size profiles give $93{,}350$ complete candidates after row screening, each with a repeated directed difference.

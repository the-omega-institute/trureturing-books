# Chapter 14 The 624 Internal Channels, Local Memory Rank, and Inverse-Identifiability Limits {#sec-ch14}

At the end of [Chapter 13](ch13-winding-ladder-and-joint-vacuum.md) we stood before a sharp puzzle: the bare 529-triangle dual-clock action admits a parts-per-million joint zero witness, yet demands $105$ non-monotone windings and retains $100$ continuous free parameters. Any framework reluctant to insert a hundred parameters by hand must interrogate its own microscopic geometry: **how many native internal channels and how many irreducible local memory states live inside a finite-radius neighborhood of the 19-point Behrend shell?**

Following that geometric thread triggers a striking collision at one exact integer—**$624$**: on one side, two ordered signed comparisons along the three nearest-neighbor chords of the 25-state lifted shell generate $12 \times 4^2 + 12 \times 6^2 = 624$ directed internal channels; on the other, inverting boson and fermion thresholds recovers the integer port gain $K = 624$. Along this staircase, holding the old 19-point block fixed forces $38$ auxiliary states, unconstrained edits drop the minimum to $19$, and every intermediate squared chord cutoff $D$ charges an exact integer toll in internal degrees of freedom. Meanwhile, asking how accurately finite-precision measurements identify a specific Riemann zero reveals a nuanced architecture of conditional identifiability—complete with an exact 3-copy quantum code protecting a qutrit against unread zero jitter and an instructive split in which distinct selection rules crown `#11431`, `#10875`, `#11737`, `#11488`, and `#11618` respectively.

## 14.1 The 624 Internal Channels, the 17-Tick Return Cycle, and the Same-Graph Mass Obstruction

We begin by formalizing the 624-channel construction on the lifted shell and the distance-constrained Schur completion problem that defines local memory rank.

::: {.definition #def-ou-ch14-memory-rank title="624 Directed Chord Comparison Channels and Distance-Constrained Local Memory Rank"}
Lift the 19-point Behrend shell to its order-9 **25-state lifted shell**, and perform two ordered signed comparisons along the three nearest-neighbor chords at the pole: twelve lifted states take $4$ distinct integer readings and the other twelve take $6$, producing

$$
12 \times 4^2 + 12 \times 6^2 \;=\; 192 + 432 \;=\; 624
$$

**directed nearest-neighbor chord comparison channels**. Subdividing onto the $1/3$-grid on the sphere ($d^2 \le 56$) defines the 76-point refined propagation graph, whose Schur reduction produces three full clock response matrices on the $19$ visible shell points. For a squared chord cutoff $D \ge 0$ restricting which entries of the old 19-point propagation block may be modified, the **local memory rank** $r(D)$ is the minimum auxiliary state dimension needed by a **distance-constrained positive semidefinite (PSD) Schur completion** to reproduce all three visible clock responses exactly.
:::

Four tightly interlocking facts govern this 624-channel geometry, followed immediately by a hard convexity barrier:

1. **Geometric-arithmetic intersection at $K = 624$:** Inverting boson thresholds, the three-generation fermion threshold product, and a labeled pair of outer squared thresholds on data generated by the 624-channel model across the six candidate zeros `#11485–#11490` yields continuous gain estimates strictly inside **$(623.91,\, 624.09)$** even under $1\;\mathrm{ppm}$ threshold errors and a five-digit Planck scale—recovering $K = 624$ cleanly (see [`ch39-independent-integer-gain.py`](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing-experiments/blob/ba0ac9539cdb641744054bf3a0398494f4cba1c8/experiments/ch39-omega-research/replay/ch39-independent-integer-gain.py) and [`ch39-intrinsic-shell-comparisons.py`](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing-experiments/blob/ba0ac9539cdb641744054bf3a0398494f4cba1c8/experiments/ch39-omega-research/replay/ch39-intrinsic-shell-comparisons.py)). In parallel, this intrinsic comparison model and the two-register realization have exact three-step self-return probabilities $10/13$ and $85/208$ respectively.
2. **Emergent weak contact and energy-moment identifiability:** Coupling the $624$ internal channels and one outer port with uniform reflection—setting both the negative-eigenspace energy and outer port contact to $1$—eliminates automatically into an effective weak contact of **$0.00098368575387$**, placing over $99.994\%$ of the full ground state on `#11431` (and still $> 99.9866\%$ on two local lifted shells hopping only along $d^2 \le 152$). The first two energy moments at internal clock positions distinguish $624$ channels from the nearest competitor $625$ at error $< 1.82565 \times 10^{-7}$ (while seven exact continuous moments on the double local shell recover each register's zero-energy port weight $1/25$ and all $624$ channels). Copying comparison readouts across two slots gives zero-sum squared spectra $4$ and $8$ (Mellin ratio $2^s$) whose half-height arrivals $1/4$ and $1/8$ combine with $624$ channels to generate the 4-term integer prime window $(18, -19, -3, 4)$ (requiring at least $169$ internal states per channel and step cutoff $d^2 \le 152$ on a single stationary reversible trajectory).
3. **Nine-step return and the 17-tick feedback cycle:** Requiring all $624$ channels to return along integer shell edges of squared chord $d^2 \le 56$ traps $216$ channels on isolated single edges. Allowing transit through the **$1/3$-subdivided sphere** unlocks a unified **9-step return** completing both the lifted permutation and a target triangular phase, with both denominator $3$ and step count $9$ achieving exact lower bounds (see [`ch39-local-shell-return.py`](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing-experiments/blob/ba0ac9539cdb641744054bf3a0398494f4cba1c8/experiments/ch39-omega-research/replay/ch39-local-shell-return.py)). Implementing reflection on the $1/3$-grid via $4$ inward hops and $4$ outward hops combines with the $9$-step internal return into a **17-tick feedback cycle** ($4{,}489$ finite input ticks produce six distinct mirror probabilities at tick $76{,}304$).
4. **Same-graph convex mass obstruction:** Reusing those same $12 \le d^2 \le 56$ transit edges for mass propagation while holding the old 19-point block fixed triggers a strict convexity obstruction: every contractive Hermitian dilation strictly **increases** the shell mass readout. Retaining the 9-step $1/3$-grid raises all six candidate readouts (`#11485–#11490`) by more than **$3.163 \times 10^{-5}$**, and the six candidates demand mutually incompatible full-edge rescalings (see [`ch39-refined-mass-obstruction.py`](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing-experiments/blob/ba0ac9539cdb641744054bf3a0398494f4cba1c8/experiments/ch39-omega-research/replay/ch39-refined-mass-obstruction.py)). Moreover, for any fixed positive edge weights and real diagonal terms on the 76-point graph, both the top eigenvalue and the cubic-chord sum of top three eigenvalues increase strictly with zero height, ruling out static spectral tops for selecting an intermediate zero.

## 14.2 Algebraic Foundations of Schur Response and Gram Isotropy (Lean 4 Anchors)

Why do distance-constrained Schur completions and multi-channel isometric embeddings organize themselves into sharp integer staircases? Beneath the shell-specific calculations sit two pure algebraic classification theorems, both formally frozen inside the Lean 4 kernel.

The first theorem gives the exact algebraic image of response quadruples $(\beta, \gamma, \varepsilon, \zeta)$ produced by eliminating **two coupled hidden scalar modes** over a nonsingular mixing set $T$ ($t^2 \ne 1$). Returning to a positive-definite hidden block ($K = \mathbb{R}, T = (-1, 1)$), it immediately enforces a rigid visibility constraint: forcing $\beta$ and $\varepsilon$ both nonzero makes $\gamma$ nonzero as well—limited hidden dimension forbids arbitrary zero patterns on visible ports.

::: {.theorem #thm-ou-ch14-two-hidden-response title="Exact Algebraic Characterization of Two-Hidden-Scalar Schur Responses" lean="D5/S3/Quantum/Matrix/Schur/TwoHiddenScalarResponse.response_iff"}
Let $K$ be a field and let $T \subseteq K$ satisfy $t^2 \ne 1$ for every $t \in T$. Given $(\beta, \gamma, \varepsilon, \zeta) \in K^4$, there exist $t \in T$ and $a, b, d, e, f \in K$ satisfying

$$
\beta = -b d t, \qquad
\gamma = -d(e + t f), \qquad
\varepsilon = -a(e + t f), \qquad
\zeta = -b(t e + f)
$$

if and only if at least one of the following two conditions holds:

1. $0 \in T$ and $\beta = 0$;
2. there exists $t \in T$ with $t \ne 0$, and both $(\beta \ne 0 \lor \gamma = 0 \lor \zeta = 0)$ and $(\gamma \ne 0 \lor \beta = 0 \lor \varepsilon = 0)$ hold.
:::

::: proof
Introduce the linear change of variables $h = e + t f$ and $k = t e + f$; because $t^2 \ne 1$ for all $t \in T$, its determinant $1 - t^2$ is nonzero, so $(h, k)$ recovers $(e, f)$ uniquely. When $t = 0$, the system becomes $\beta = 0$, $\gamma = -d e$, $\varepsilon = -a e$, and $\zeta = -b f$, solvable for arbitrary $\gamma, \varepsilon, \zeta$ by taking $e = b = 1$, matching condition 1. When $t \ne 0$, the four equations form adjacent products $\beta = -b d t$, $\gamma = -d h$, $\varepsilon = -a h$, $\zeta = -b k$ along the chain $k - b - d - h - a$: if $\beta = 0$ then $b = 0$ or $d = 0$, forcing $\zeta = 0$ or $\gamma = 0$; similarly $\gamma = 0$ forces $\beta = 0$ or $\varepsilon = 0$. Conversely, under condition 2 explicit case assignments construct a solution: for $\beta \ne 0$ set $d = 1$, $b = -\beta / t$, $h = -\gamma$ and solve for $a$ according to whether $h = 0$; for $\beta = \gamma = 0$ take $d = 0, b = h = 1$; for $\beta = 0, \gamma \ne 0$ we have $\zeta = 0$, so $b = 0, h = 1, d = -\gamma$ solves all four equations. The full proof is checked by the Lean kernel.
:::

The second theorem delivers the complete dimension classification for **width-three Kronecker-sum column Gram isotropy** $F^* F = \lambda I$: when three parallel channels couple left and right spaces isometrically, the admissible dimension quadruples $(a, b, c, d)$ split cleanly into an exact staircase of linear inequalities—explaining why compressing one side forces a quantized toll of auxiliary dimensions on the opposite side:

::: {.theorem #thm-ou-ch14-native-scalar-gram title="Full-Dimension Classification of Width-Three Kronecker-Sum Scalar Gram Isotropy" lean="D5/S3/Quantum/TensorNetworks/BridgeGraph/NativeScalarGramClassification.native_scalar_gram_iff"}
For all natural numbers $a, b, c, d \in \mathbb{N}$ and every real $\lambda > 0$, there exist complex matrices $M_r \in M_{a \times b}(\mathbb{C})$ and $N_r \in M_{d \times c}(\mathbb{C})$ ($r = 0, 1, 2$) whose Kronecker sum $F = \sum_{r=0}^2 M_r \otimes N_r$ satisfies the isotropic Gram identity

$$
F^* F \;=\; \left(\sum_{r=0}^2 M_r \otimes N_r\right)^* \left(\sum_{r=0}^2 M_r \otimes N_r\right) \;=\; \lambda I_{bc}
$$

if and only if `ScalarGramAdmissible a b c d` holds—namely, at least one of the following four conditions holds:

1. $b = 0$ or $c = 0$;
2. $b \le a$ and $c \le d$;
3. $a < b$, and one of the three staircase regimes holds: $(2b \le 3a \land 3c \le 2d)$, or $(3a < 2b \land b \le 2a \land 2c \le d)$, or $(2a < b \le 3a \land 3c \le d)$;
4. $d < c$, and one of the swapped dual regimes holds: $(2c \le 3d \land 3b \le 2a)$, or $(3d < 2c \land c \le 2d \land 2b \le a)$, or $(2d < c \le 3d \land 3b \le a)$.
:::

::: proof
If $b = 0$ or $c = 0$, both sides are empty matrices and zero factors suffice. Assume $b, c > 0$; since $\lambda > 0$, $F^* F = \lambda I_{bc}$ forces $F$ to be injective and hence $a, d > 0$. If $b \le a$ and $c \le d$, single-block coordinate embeddings scaled by $\sqrt{\lambda}$ establish sufficiency. When $a < b$, the stacked map $x \mapsto (M_0 x, M_1 x, M_2 x)$ must be injective, giving $b \le 3a$; placing the Gram residual matrices inside the Hermitian compression kernel and applying inertia and kernel-geometry bounds sequentially across $2b \le 3a$, $3a < 2b \le 4a$, and $2a < b \le 3a$ yields $3c \le 2d$, $2c \le d$, and $3c \le d$. Swapping the tensor factors conjugates $F^* F$ by a unitary permutation and exchanges $(a, b) \leftrightarrow (d, c)$, producing condition 4 for $d < c$. Conversely, the explicit width-three construction for $2b \le 3a$ together with disjoint $k$-block coordinate embeddings ($k \in \{1, 2, 3\}$) covers every admissible regime. The full proof is checked by the Lean kernel.
:::

## 14.3 The Local Memory-Rank Staircase vs Squared Chord Cutoff $D$

Bringing the Schur-and-Gram rigidity of [@thm-ou-ch14-two-hidden-response] and [@thm-ou-ch14-native-scalar-gram] directly onto the 19-point Behrend shell maps out one of the cleanest discrete phase diagrams in this entire investigation: **how many auxiliary states $r(D)$ are strictly required to reproduce all three clock responses of the 76-point refined shell on the 19 visible sites as a function of the allowed squared chord edit cutoff $D$?**

A single visible port illustrates the distinction between the smallest memory that reproduces the readings and the number of spectral lines visible in the system that produced them. Fix the visible block at zero. Take a diagonal hidden block with eigenvalues $t_j\in(-1,1)$ and squared couplings $b_j^2>0$. Its contribution to the Schur complement is $S(z)=\sum_j b_j^2/(z-t_j)$. Write $w_j=b_j^2/(1-t_j^3)$ and $m_\ell=\sum_j w_j t_j^\ell$. At $z^3=1$ the identity $(z-t)(z^2+zt+t^2)=1-t^3$ gives $S(z)=m_0z^2+m_1z+m_2$. The three readings determine these three coefficients by interpolation.

Take poles $(-1/4,1/4)$ with weights $16w=(1/2,1/2)$. Independently, take poles $(-1/2,0,1/2)$ with weights $16w=(1/8,3/4,1/8)$. Both give $(m_0,m_1,m_2)=(1/16,0,1/256)$. Symmetry gives $\sum_j b_j^2=1/16$, so the full coupled matrix has operator norm at most $1/2+1/4=3/4$. One pole would require $m_0m_2=m_1^2$. Here $m_0m_2>0$ and $m_1=0$, so the minimum memory for these readings is exactly two. All three poles in the second system have positive residues and appear in its full response. At a fourth frequency, $z=-1$, the two values of $S(-1)$ are already distinct, $-257/3840$ and $-53/768$ respectively. More generally, for $r$ distinct poles of nonzero weight, the $r$-by-$r$ Hankel matrix formed from $m_0,\ldots,m_{2r-2}$ has rank $r$. [Finite-moment rank theorem](lean:D5/S3/Analytic/GoldenTomography/FinitePronyHankelReconstruction.prony_hankel_rank)

With several visible ports, the three moments are matrices that need not commute. Fix the visible block $A=A^*$ and suppose the readings come from a finite-dimensional self-adjoint strict contraction with hidden block $H$ and coupling $B$ from visible to hidden space. Interpolate the three clock values of $S(z)=B^*(zI-H)^{-1}B$ as $M_0z^2+M_1z+M_2$. We can calculate the minimum hidden dimension directly. Set $Y=(I-H^3)^{-1/2}B$. The same cubic factorization gives $M_j=Y^*H^jY$, so every realization needs at least

$$
q=\operatorname{rank}
\begin{pmatrix}M_0&M_1\\M_1&M_2\end{pmatrix}
=\operatorname{rank}[Y,\ HY]
$$

hidden states. The block matrix is the Gram matrix of the columns on the right. To attain the bound while also retaining the given realization's reading at $z=-1$, set $p(t)=(1+t)(1-t^3)$ and $U=p(H)^{-1/2}B$. The columns of $U$ and $HU$ span a space of dimension $q$ because $Y=(I+H)^{1/2}U$ and the invertible factor commutes with $H$. Compress $H$ orthogonally to this space, obtaining $T$. Since $TU=HU$, we have $U^*T^jU=U^*H^jU$ for $0\le j\le3$. At degree three both sides equal $(HU)^*H(HU)$. At each clock and at $z=-1$, the quotient $p(t)/(z-t)$ is a polynomial of degree at most three. The new coupling $p(T)^{1/2}U$ therefore preserves all four readings. The compression has $\|T\|<1$, and the two Schur complement conditions for strict contraction of the full matrix, $I-A-S(1)\succ0$ and $I+A+S(-1)\succ0$, are preserved as well. This realizes $q$.

For a two-port example, take $A=0$, give $H$ the four diagonal entries $t=\pm1/2,\pm1/4$, and take the corresponding rows of $U$ to be $(1,t)/8$. Then $\|B\|\le\sqrt{27/16}/4<1/2$, so the full system is strictly contractive. The columns of $[U,HU]$ are $1,t,t,t^2$ evaluated at four points and divided by $8$, giving rank exactly three. All four lines have nonzero residues, yet three hidden states suffice to retain the three clocks and the given fourth reading.

When only the readings are given, we must also decide whether such a realization exists. Write $G$ for the block matrix above, $C=M_0-M_2$ and $D_1=M_0-M_1$. First check that the three moments are self-adjoint, $G\succeq0$, $C\succeq0$ and $\ker C=\ker M_0$. The last two tests follow from $C=Y^*(I-H^2)Y$. Let $C^\dagger$ invert $C$ on its range and vanish on its kernel. On the full visible space the two endpoint conditions are

$$
\begin{aligned}
I-A-M_0-M_1-M_2&\succ0,\\
I+A-L&\succ0,\qquad L=M_1-M_2+D_1C^\dagger D_1 .
\end{aligned}
$$

Together these conditions are sufficient. The first inequality comes from the positive endpoint. For the second, factor $G=[Y,Z]^*[Y,Z]$. In any realization $Z=HY$, and the negative endpoint costs $-S(-1)=M_1-M_2+Y^*(I-H)(I+H)^{-1}Y$. The last term and $C$ are the diagonal blocks of a Gram matrix with off-diagonal block $D_1$, giving $-S(-1)\succeq L$. Conversely, factor $G$ on a space of dimension $q$ and set $F=Y-Z$. On $\operatorname{ran}M_0$ we have $\|Zv\|<\|Yv\|$ for nonzero $v$, so $D_1$ is positive definite there. From $T_0=I-FD_1^\dagger F^*$ subtract a sufficiently small positive multiple of the projection onto $(\operatorname{ran}Y)^\perp$. The identities $F^*Y=D_1$ and $2D_1-F^*F=C$ give $I+T_0\succ0$ and ensure that the resulting $T$ satisfies $TY=Z$ and $-I<T<I$. The coupling $(I-T^3)^{1/2}Y$ recovers the three clocks. Since $2(I+T_0)^{-1}=I+FC^\dagger F^*$, the negative endpoint cost tends to $L$ as the perturbation tends to zero, so the strict margin in the second inequality gives a realization passing both tests.

For example, $(M_0,M_1,M_2)=(1/4,0,1/16)$ gives $q=2$ and $L=13/48$. With $A=-35/48$ the positive endpoint has slack, but $1+A=L$ precludes strict contraction in every dimension. With $A=-2/3$, take $Y=(1/2,0)^{\mathsf T}$ and let the rows of $T$ be $(0,1/2)$ and $(1/2,1/2)$. This gives $-S(-1)=23/80$ and two states suffice. On the 19-site shell the visible block also varies in the minimization, and every candidate must satisfy the support and contraction constraints. [Endpoint construction and rational examples](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing-books/issues/1671#issuecomment-7236014282)

With the fourth clock $-1$ fixed as well, every minimum-dimensional realization has the same response at any fifth clock. In fact, the four clock values interpolate the four moments $N_0,\ldots,N_3$ in the polynomial basis $p_4(t)/(z-t)$, with $p_4(t)=(1+t)(1-t^3)$. The block matrices $G_4=(N_{a+b})_{a,b=0}^1$ and $J_4=(N_{a+b+1})_{a,b=0}^1$ equal $X^*X$ and $X^*HX$ for $X=[U,HU]$. At dimension $q=\operatorname{rank}G_4$, a full-row-rank Gram factor makes the hidden matrix unique up to a unitary change of coordinates because $X^*TX=J_4$ uniquely specifies $T$. The first block of $X$ then determines the new coupling.

A reading at $i$ determines that at $-i$ by adjoint symmetry. Interpolating the six known values with $p_6(t)=(1+t^2)p_4(t)$ gives self-adjoint moments $P_0,\ldots,P_5$ and matrices $G_6=(P_{a+b})_{a,b=0}^2$ and $J_6=(P_{a+b+1})_{a,b=0}^2$. The exact feasibility conditions are $G_6\pm J_6\succeq0$, $\ker(G_6\pm J_6)=\ker G_6$, and the two strict visible Schur inequalities at $1$ and $-1$. The least hidden dimension is $\operatorname{rank}G_6$. To construct it, factor $G_6=X^*X$ and obtain $T$ from $X^*TX=J_6$. The common kernel makes this well defined and the two signs give $-I<T<I$. Comparing block columns gives $TX_0=X_1$ and $TX_1=X_2$. The coupling $p_6(T)^{1/2}X_0$ reproduces the six values and hence the five independent readings.

For a single port, the fifth clock can add at most one hidden state because $[V,HV,H^2V]$ differs from $[V,HV]$ by one column, where $V=(I+H^2)^{-1/2}U$. For an exactly feasible fifth target $s$, the minimum with closed tolerance $\varepsilon$ at $i$ is $q$ precisely when $\varepsilon\ge|s-S_{\min}(i)|$. Here $S_{\min}(i)$ is the unique four-clock minimum-rank prediction. When $\varepsilon$ is smaller the minimum is $q+1$. For example, with $A=0$, the pairs of nodes and four-clock weights $((-1/3,1/3),(1/16,1/16))$ and $((-1/2,0,1/2),(1/36,5/72,1/36))$ both give $(N_0,N_1,N_2,N_3)=(1/8,0,1/72,0)$. Their fifth readings are $-1/90-i/9$ and $-1/120-i/9$. The latter target needs three states for tolerance below $1/360$ and two states at or above $1/360$. [Exact Gram test and rational replay](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing-books/issues/1671#issuecomment-7236665129)

Two ports can have two different tolerance thresholds even if the hidden states may couple both ports. For any $d$-dimensional realization preserving the four fixed readings, $\operatorname{rank}(S(i)-S_{\min}(i))\le d-q$. To see this, put $K=\operatorname{ran}[U,HU]$, let $P$ project onto $K$, and let $T=PH|_K$ be the minimizing compression. Polynomial division of $p_4(t)/(i-t)$ cancels the moments of degree at most three when taking the response difference. If $R=(iI-H)^{-1}$, the remaining term comes from
$$
 PRU-(iI_K-T)^{-1}U=(iI_K-T)^{-1}PH(I-P)RU.
$$
After multiplication on the left by $p_4(i)U^*$ this factors the response difference through $K^\perp$, giving the rank bound. In the rational example above, use both scalar models on each port, but double all weights on the first port. Set $A=0$. The strict Schur margins remain positive. Four states predict the two-state response on both ports, and the two three-state models give a target whose difference from that prediction is $\operatorname{diag}(1/180,1/360)$. The rank bound rules out reaching operator-norm error below $1/360$ with five states, even with mixed couplings. Replacing just the first scalar subsystem attains that threshold with five states. The minimum is therefore four at tolerance at least $1/180$, five from $1/360$ inclusive to $1/180$ exclusive, and six below $1/360$. [Rational two-port construction](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing-books/issues/1671#issuecomment-7236791167)

The scalar two-port data give a closed description of all fifth-clock corrections, including nonnormal matrices. Set $A=0$, $D=dI_2$ with $0<d<72/11$, and use the four moments $(D/8,0,D/72,0)$. For $\Delta=S(i)-S_{\min}(i)$, set $T=\Delta/(9d/400)$. The strict unit cap is attainable exactly when
$$
\operatorname{Re}T\pm\operatorname{Im}T-\frac{81}{40}T^*T\succeq0
$$
for both signs, with strictness on $(\ker T)^\perp$. The resolvent of the four-state minimum reduces the correction to a coupling through the added states. On the range of $T$, the positive matrix $\operatorname{Re}T-\frac9{10}T^*T$ reconstructs a tail that attains the given response. The exact minimum is $4+\operatorname{rank}\Delta$. The positive semidefinite conditions depend on $T^*T$, so testing the eigenvalues of a nonnormal target alone loses information. [Hidden and full-matrix norm caps](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing-books/issues/1671#issuecomment-7237327085)

The same calculation applies to full-rank four-clock data with noncommuting blocks. Orthonormalizing the first two moment columns fixes the two-block Jacobi matrix $J$. Its second-block resolvent couples the fifth-clock correction to the added states. The two endpoint Schur inequalities can be written directly in terms of the four moments and the target correction, including the order of the noncommuting factors. For a feasible target under a strict cap at most one, or a closed cap below one, two ports need exactly $4+\operatorname{rank}\Delta$ hidden states. At the closed unit cap, a hidden endpoint can carry polynomial moment mass while the visible coupling vanishes. In a certified four-atom example the fifth-clock correction has rank two and the full matrix needs eight hidden states. [General block inequalities and the endpoint example](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing-books/issues/1671#issuecomment-7237750979)

For a feasible fifth target with singular four-clock Gram data, the largest admissible endpoint masses can be assigned simultaneously. Together the five readings determine the six moments of the measure divided by $1+t^2$. Minimizing each interval moment form at a fixed endpoint value gives its capacity $C_\pm$. Subtracting both capacities leaves two forms with the same kernel, and the multiplication compression on their Gram quotient has spectrum strictly inside the interval.

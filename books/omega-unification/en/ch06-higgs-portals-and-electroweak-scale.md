## 6.1 Electroweak Matching Targets, Two-Loop Gauge Thresholds, and Cross-Candidate Polynomial Portals {#sec-ou-ch06-s01}

We first record the electroweak matching targets, portal structures, and unitarity bounds, anchored by the Lean 4 block Schur minimum theorem that powers both scattering-matrix positivity checks and endpoint eliminations throughout this chapter.

::: {.definition #def-ou-ch06-higgs-portal title="Electroweak Higgs Matching Targets, Two-Loop Pure-Gauge Thresholds, and Contact Unitarity"}
For the Standard Model Higgs potential $V(H) = -m_H^2 H^\dagger H + \lambda(H^\dagger H)^2$ (with effective high-side quadratic mass term $+m^2 H^\dagger H$), fix four baseline targets and constraints:

1. **Low-energy electroweak and high-side quartic targets**: vacuum expectation value $v = 246.22\;\mathrm{GeV}$, physical Higgs mass $m_H = 125.25\;\mathrm{GeV}$ (tree-level low-side boundary $\lambda(M_Z) = m_H^2/(2v^2) \approx 0.12938$), and high-side reference quartic $\lambda_H = 0.02335$.
2. **Two-loop pure electroweak gauge mass threshold $\Delta_{\mathrm{pole}}$**: the finite shift to the light Higgs squared mass induced at two loops purely by electroweak gauge interactions of active heavy charged scalars and heavy Dirac multiplets in a fixed perturbative pole or $\overline{\mathrm{MS}}$ scheme (matching the broken electroweak phase requires a net negative hard mass parameter $-m_{\mathrm{low}}^2 < 0$).
3. **Species-traceless leading portal $(-F, 3F)$ vs. subleading common portal $b$**: on each $(4, 2, 1)$ heavy scalar layer ($12$ colored real components and $4$ uncolored real components), assigning Hermitian portal blocks $(-F, 3F)$ cancels the leading one-loop quadratic tadpole layer-by-layer via $12(-F) + 4(3F) = 0$ (**species-traceless**); releasing tracelessness adds a common one-loop-order portal $b$ (or positive operator $B \succeq 0$), producing physical species portals $(-F + B,\; 3F + B)$.
4. **Tree-level boundedness and $8\pi$ contact unitarity**: the combined light-heavy quartic potential is bounded below along every nonzero field ray, and the two-body $s$-wave radial contact scattering matrix $\mathcal{A}$ satisfies the perturbative partial-wave unitarity ceiling

   $$
   \|\mathcal{A}\|_{\mathrm{op}} \;<\; 8\pi \;\approx\; 25.13274.
   $$

A layer with a common mass shows how much information these two portal readings retain. Put $s=H^\dagger H$ and take real portals $b,f$ so that the colored and uncolored squared masses are $M^2+(b-f)s$ and $M^2+(b+3f)s$. If the one-loop potential per real component is the same smooth function $G$, summing the twelve colored and four uncolored contributions gives

$$
V_1'(0)=16bG'(M^2),\qquad V_1''(0)=(16b^2+48f^2)G''(M^2).
$$

Changing $f$ to $-f$ therefore preserves both the one-loop mass and quartic corrections. For example, the species slopes $(0.02,0.06)$ and $(0.04,0)$ both have weighted sum $0.48$ and weighted sum of squares $0.0192$. The derivative of the uncolored minus colored squared mass with respect to $s$ distinguishes them, giving $+0.04$ and $-0.04$, respectively. This degeneracy uses the common $M^2$ and $G$. Split thresholds or species-resolved probes require separate weights.

How much could one recover by knowing the whole one-loop potential near $s=0$? Suppose the number of layers $N$ and their common positive squared mass $M^2>0$ are known. Both squared masses in each layer are affine in $s$, and all real components have the same known normalization. Take the scalar determinant kernel $G(x)=x^2[\log(x/\mu^2)-3/2]$, suppressing the common loop factor. Let $C_t,U_t$ count the layers whose colored or uncolored slope, respectively, takes the real value $t$, and put $w_t=3C_t+U_t$. Since $G'''(x)=2/x$,
$$
V_1(s)=4\sum_t w_t G(M^2+ts),\qquad
V_1'''(s)=8\sum_{t\ne0}\frac{w_t t^2}{s+M^2/t}.
$$
Knowing this rational function near the origin determines its poles and residues uniquely. Each pole $p$ gives a slope $t=-M^2/p$, and its residue divided by $8t^2$ gives the weighted count. The zero slope is accounted for by $w_0=4N-\sum_{t\ne0}w_t$. Any counterterm of degree at most two in $s$ drops out on differentiating three times.

For one or two layers, $U_t\le2$, so the remainder and quotient on dividing $w_t$ by three give $U_t$ and $C_t$, respectively. With two layers the colored and uncolored slopes can still be paired differently across layers. With three layers even the species counts can differ. In units of a common positive slope $\epsilon$, take colored slopes $(1,1,1)$ and uncolored slopes $(2,2,2)$ in one model, and colored slopes $(1,1,2)$ and uncolored slopes $(1,1,1)$ in another. Both have exactly $36$ components of slope $\epsilon$ and $12$ of slope $2\epsilon$, giving identical one-loop potentials pointwise. The sums of slopes over colored components are $36\epsilon$ and $48\epsilon$. Three is therefore the smallest number of equal-mass layers for which the complete potential can leave the species counts ambiguous. Mass splitting shifts the poles, and species-weighted readings retain different information.

Now let the layers have known positive squared masses $m_i=M_i^2$, common to both species within each layer. At each normalized slope $\lambda=t/m_i$, let $d_i(\lambda)$ take the value $0,1,3,4$ according as neither species, only the uncolored species, only the colored species, or both have that normalized slope in layer $i$. A nonzero $\lambda$ produces a pole of $V_1'''$ at $-1/\lambda$ with residue
$$
8\lambda^2\sum_i m_i^2 d_i(\lambda).
$$
All contributions at that pole have the same sign. At $\lambda=0$ the weighted sum follows by subtracting all the nonzero-slope sums from $4\sum_i m_i^2$.

Thus the potential up to a polynomial of degree two determines the labelled slope of each species in every layer for **all** assignments exactly when the $4^N$ numbers $\sum_i m_i^2 d_i$, with $d_i\in\{0,1,3,4\}$, are distinct. Each decoded digit identifies the contributing species, and $t=m_i\lambda$ restores its slope. Conversely, if two distinct digit vectors have the same sum, assign the species they select in each model to one normalized slope and all remaining species to a second. The sums agree at both slopes, so the third derivatives agree identically. For two layers with $m_2>m_1$ the exceptional ratios $m_2^2/m_1^2=M_2^4/M_1^4$ are exactly $4/3,3/2,2,3,4$. Indeed, the positive digit differences are $1,2,3,4$, and the ratio must be a quotient of two such differences. For example, at ratio $4$, both species of layer one have the same weight as just the uncolored species of layer two.

For two distinct masses, a single additional reading $V_1'(0)$ with the linear counterterm fixed resolves even these exceptions. To see why, let $\delta_i$ be the difference between the two distributions of digits over normalized slopes in layer $i$. Equal residues and the known total give $m_1^2\delta_1=-m_2^2\delta_2$ also at zero. Each distribution difference comes from the four signed weights $+3,+1,-3,-1$. If three or four support points survive, at least one uncancelled unit weight remains, so the smallest nonzero absolute weight in each $\delta_i$ is one. Proportionality with unequal masses then fails. A nonzero ambiguity must therefore have $\delta_2=a(\delta_x-\delta_y)$ at two distinct slopes, where $\delta_x$ denotes unit mass at $x$. Using $G'(m)=2m[\log(m/\mu^2)-1]$, the two linear readings differ by $8m_2^2\log(m_2/m_1)\,a(x-y)\ne0$. This identifies all four labelled slopes from the exact readings, including assignments with vanishing slopes.

Three is the minimum number of **pairwise distinct known thresholds** for which the same fixed-counterterm potential can come from different labelled slopes. Take the mass weights $m_i^2=M_i^4$ proportional to $(1,2,4)$. Choose two distinct positive normalized slopes $a,b$. At $a$, assign the three layers digits $(4,0,1)$ in one model and $(0,4,0)$ in the other. Put all remaining species at $b$. The weighted difference at $a$ is $4-8+4=0$, and the difference at $b$ is its negative. Even the layer-dependent logarithms cancel: $4\log 1-8\log\sqrt2+4\log 2=0$. Indeed $G(m_i(1+\lambda s))$ separates into a common function of $\lambda s$ times $m_i^2$ and a term proportional to $m_i^2\log(m_i/\mu^2)(1+\lambda s)^2$. Both pieces agree, so the unadjusted potentials coincide for all $s\ge0$. The two-layer result above gives minimality.

More generally, for three mass weights proportional to $(1,r,r^2)$ with $r>1$, this ambiguity occurs exactly at $r=(1+\sqrt5)/2$, $2$ and $3$. To check the extra constraint from fixing the counterterms, let $\delta_i(\lambda)$ be the digit difference at normalized slope $\lambda$, with layer indices $i=0,1,2$. After the pole weights agree, the remaining difference is proportional to $\sum_\lambda (r\delta_1(\lambda)+2r^2\delta_2(\lambda))(1+\lambda s)^2$. Its constant, linear and quadratic coefficients must all vanish. For example, at the golden ratio take the two possible (colored, uncolored) normalized-slope pairs $(3,7)$ and $(5,1)$. Each has weighted moments $(4,16,76)$ through degree two. Switch the assignments in the same direction in the first two layers and in the opposite direction in the third. The relation $1+r=r^2$ cancels the pole weights, and the moments cancel the remaining polynomial. The classification includes positive-slope examples at all three ratios. Which portal assignments can a long-wavelength disturbance distinguish? In the same Gaussian scalar model, perturb the constant background with Euclidean momentum $p$. Subtract the two-point response at $p=0$ and divide out the known common normalization. The coefficient of $p^2$, measured as a function of the background $s$, gives
$$
B(s)=\sum_{\text{species}} \frac{w t^2}{m_i+t s},\qquad w\in\{3,1\}.
$$
In the equal-mass bubble integral, differentiating $\log[1+p^2 z(1-z)/(m_i+ts)]$ at $p^2=0$ and integrating the Feynman parameter $z$ from zero to one supplies a factor of $1/6$. The two source insertions supply $t^2$. With fixed local counterterms, knowing the potential and $B(s)$ near $s=0$ identifies all six signed slopes at any three distinct known thresholds. At each nonzero normalized slope, the pole in the third derivative of the potential weights the layers by $m_i^2$, while the pole in $B(s)$ weights them by $m_i$. The two weights restrict every difference between three-layer assignments to a common line. The layer logarithms retained by the potential remove the residual ambiguity through the strict convexity of $m\log m$.

Four layers already admit assignments that both measurements miss. Give every species normalized slope $1$ or $2$. At slope $1$, use the digits $(3,1,3,0)$ in one model and $(1,3,1,1)$ in the other. There is a set of four distinct positive squared masses $m_i$ for which the difference $(2,-2,2,-1)$ has zero sum against each of $m_i$, $m_i^2$ and $m_i^2\log(m_i/\mu^2)$. These two portal assignments then give the same potential and the same low-momentum coefficient for every $s\ge0$. **Hand derivation at fixed nonzero momentum.** Keep the same bubble integral and choose any one known Euclidean squared momentum $P=p^2>0$. With local terms fixed and the zero-momentum value subtracted, suppose the following function is known exactly near $s=0$.

$$
R_P(s)=\sum_{i,\alpha} w_\alpha t_{i,\alpha}^2
\int_0^1\log\!\left(1+\frac{Pz(1-z)}{m_i+t_{i,\alpha}s}\right)\,dz,
\qquad (w_C,w_U)=(3,1).
$$

For any finite number of known, pairwise distinct positive squared masses $m_i$, this background response alone determines the signed colored and uncolored slopes in each layer.

Consider a term with slope $t\ne0$. Its $s$ derivative is $wt^2$ times the average of $1/(s+v)$ at $v=(m_i+Pz(1-z))/t$ for uniform $z$, minus $wt^2/(s+m_i/t)$. Summing gives the transform of a signed measure with compact support away from zero. The expansion of the kernel at $s=0$ gives every positive power moment of $1/v$, and the total mass is zero. Polynomial approximation on the compact reciprocal support therefore determines the measure uniquely.

Its negative atoms mark the starts $v=m_i/t$, whose signs give the slope signs. On each half-axis, order the starts by increasing $a=|m_i/t|$. Write $\ell=P/(4|t|)$, so $|v|=a+4\ell z(1-z)$. Changing variables on the two branches of this quadratic gives the continuous density contribution at $|v|=a+h$,

$$
\frac{wt^2}{2\ell}(1-h/\ell)^{-1/2},\qquad 0<h<\ell .
$$

For example, $m_1=1,t_1=1$ and $m_2=2,t_2=2$ both start at $a=1$. At $P=4$ their density intervals have lengths $1$ and $1/2$, so their shapes separate the two layers.

After subtracting the contributions from earlier starts, only terms starting at $a$ enter the density for sufficiently small $h>0$. The candidate nodes $\ell_i^{-1}=4m_i/(Pa)$ are distinct. For $N$ layers, the first $N$ Taylor coefficients of the density, divided by their nonzero binomial factors, give the first $N$ power moments of the amplitudes at these nodes. These moments determine the amplitudes uniquely. [Finite Vandermonde moment injectivity](lean:D5/S3/Analytic/GoldenTomography/FiniteVandermondeTomography.finite_moment_readout_injective)

With $t_i=\operatorname{sgn}(v_0)m_i/a$ at the signed start $v_0$, dividing each amplitude by $t_i^2/(2\ell_i)$ gives the species weight $0,1,3,$ or $4$ at that slope in layer $i$. Once all starts have been decoded, every unassigned species has slope zero. If two masses coincide, exchanging the colored slopes of their layers preserves the entire response.

**Can the response calibrate an unknown background zero shared by the whole scan?** Let a command $s$ apply the physical background $s+c$. Assume the squared masses $m_i$ entering the Chapter 5 gauge heat traces have been independently calibrated at physical zero. At command zero a species with slope $t$ has squared mass $M=m_i+ct$. Assume all these $M$ are positive. Writing the denominator as $M+ts$ gives the signed measure derived above with $M$ in place of $m_i$, with each negative atom marking a signed start $v_0=M/t$. The interval length $\ell$ is now unknown. The density near the start determines it.

Put $q=1/\ell=4|t|/P$. Subtract the contributions of earlier starts and divide the coefficient of $h^k$ in the density by $\binom{2k}{k}/4^k$. The result is $b_k=\sum_q A_q q^k$ with $A_q=d_qt^2q/2$, where $d_q$ is the total species weight at that slope. At a common start, $t(v_0-c)=m_i$, so each layer contributes at most one node. By the [Hankel rank formula](lean:D5/S3/Analytic/GoldenTomography/FinitePronyHankelReconstruction.prony_hankel_rank), the rank $r$ of $(b_{i+j})_{0\le i,j<N}$ counts the distinct nodes, and the leading block of order $r$ is invertible. Set $p_r=1$ and solve $\sum_{j=0}^r p_j b_{k+j}=0$ for $0\le k<r$. The [annihilating recurrence](lean:D5/S3/Analytic/GoldenTomography/FinitePronyHankelReconstruction.prony_moment_linear_recurrence) shows that $\prod_q(X-q)$ satisfies this system, so the roots of the recovered polynomial give all the $q$. The Vandermonde system then gives each $A_q$. These density coefficients come from the reconstructed measure. They give the signed slope $t=\operatorname{sgn}(v_0)Pq/4$, the intercept $M=tv_0$, and the species weight $d_q\in\{1,3,4\}$ for each active component.

Each component restricts the offset to the finite set $\{(M-m_i)/t:1\le i\le N\}$. Intersect these sets. For each candidate $c$, assign the component to the layer $m_i=M-ct$ and use its weight to assign the uncolored species, the colored species, or both. Discard a candidate if it assigns a species twice in one layer. Every surviving assignment reproduces the curve on filling unused slots with zero, since $m_i+t(s+c)=M+ts$. In one layer any nonzero slope therefore calibrates $c$. At $P>0$ each nonzero slope contributes strictly positively at command zero. A zero response therefore forces all slopes to vanish and leaves every offset possible. Even one active component becomes hard to calibrate as $t\to0$. Writing the bubble integral as a function $K_P$ of squared mass, its contribution has offset derivative $wt^3K_P'(m_i+t(s+c))$. At fixed $P>0$ the responses for two fixed offsets therefore differ by $O(|t|^3)$ uniformly on a bounded command interval.

For $N\ge2$, let $\Delta$ be the minimum squared-mass gap and suppose $|t|\le L$ with $L>0$. If the offset is known to satisfy $|c|\le\eta$, the condition $2\eta L<\Delta$ guarantees unique calibration for every nonzero response. Changing the offset moves each inferred mass $M-ct$ by at most $2\eta L$. Equality already admits a collision. Choose two layers attaining the gap $\Delta$, assign their colored slopes $(L,-L)$ in order of increasing mass, and take $c=\Delta/(2L)$. Reverse both slopes and the offset together, keeping all other slopes zero. With $\bar m$ the midpoint of the two squared masses, both assignments give the colored mass curves $\bar m+Ls$ and $\bar m-Ls$ with the same weights and squared slopes. Their full local responses therefore agree at every positive momentum, and their Chapter 5 gauge heat traces agree term by term at physical zero.

**How accurately can one layer calibrate the background zero?** Let $m>0$ be its independently calibrated squared mass, and let $a,b$ be its colored and uncolored slopes. Suppose $|a|,|b|\le L$, $\max(|a|,|b|)\ge\lambda>0$, and $|c|\le\eta$. Take a common command interval on which all admissible squared masses have a known positive lower bound. Keep the normalization and local terms fixed. Choose any four distinct commands in the interval. Measure at the same sufficiently small positive squared momentum $P$ at all four commands, then at $2P$ at the first two. If the absolute error per reading is at most $\sigma$, any two models fitting all six readings differ by at most $C\sigma$ in offset and $C\sigma^{1/3}$ in each labeled slope. The momentum is fixed before taking data. The constant may depend on the prior bounds and the settings. These rates follow by hand from the bubble integral.

The second momentum distinguishes species weights that can coincide in the leading response. For each nonzero slope $t$ put $d=c+m/t$ and retain its species weight $w$. Expanding the logarithm gives

$$
\frac{6R_P(s)}{P}=A(s)-\frac{P}{10}B(s)+O(P^2),\qquad
A(s)=\sum\frac{wt}{s+d},\quad B(s)=\sum\frac{w}{(s+d)^2}.
$$

For example, at $m=1$ the assignments $(a,b,c)=(1,0,0)$ and $(3/4,3/4,-1/3)$ both give $A(s)=3/(s+1)$. The numerator of $B(s)$ is $3$ for the first and $4$ for the second. In general, clearing denominators in the difference of two candidates for $A$ leaves a polynomial of degree at most three. Four values determine $A$ by invertibility of the Vandermonde matrix at distinct nodes. [Finite moment injectivity](lean:D5/S3/Analytic/GoldenTomography/FiniteVandermondeTomography.finite_moment_readout_injective)

Slopes sharing a pole are equal, so their residues add with the same sign. With two poles, $(s+d_1)/(s+d_2)$ is strictly monotone and keeps its sign on the command interval. Two values of $B$ thus determine both weights. One value suffices for a single pole. Dividing each residue by its weight gives $t$, whence $c=d-m/t$.

To see why small positive $P$ preserves the rates, write $x=s+c$ and $f(t,x)=t^2/(m+xt)$, so $A=3f(a,x)+f(b,x)$. At distinct nonzero slopes each slope derivative changes its own residue, while varying $c$ gives only double poles. The three derivatives are independent. When a slope $u$ crosses zero, its quadratic and cubic coefficients are proportional to $1/m,-x/m^2$. They are independent of the two pole derivatives of the active species. When the slopes meet, write $a=q+u,b=q-3u$. The linear term cancels. The coefficients of $u^2,u^3$ are $6f_{tt},-4f_{ttt}$, with poles of orders three and four at the common pole.

In both singular cases the convergent series has the form $E(u^2)+u^3H(u^2)$, so one can temporarily vary $u^2,u^3$ independently. In each case the derivative columns have numerators of degree at most three over a common denominator. Evaluation at the four commands preserves their rank. The positive mass margin gives convergence of $6R_P/P$ and its derivatives in these coordinates to $A$ and its derivatives. The two-momentum difference $10(6R_P/P-6R_{2P}/(2P))/P$ converges to $B$. Full column rank gives a local Lipschitz inverse in the stated coordinates. Compactness and injectivity of the limiting readings separate parameter pairs outside these neighborhoods. This gives linear control of $c$, and $|u-u'|^3\le4|u^3-u'^3|$ gives the slope bound.

Both rates are sharp on a box allowing either sign of a weak slope and a nontrivial offset interval. Hold an active slope fixed and reverse the other slope from $u$ to $-u$. At every fixed setting the reading changes by $O(|u|^3)$, while the Chapter 5 gauge heat traces at physical zero agree exactly. Taking $|u|$ proportional to $\sigma^{1/3}$ allows the same noisy data for both signs. Likewise, moving only $c$ by a sufficiently small multiple of $\sigma$ inside the offset interval can be hidden by the noise, since each fixed reading has bounded offset derivative. For an instrument with a single prescribed $P>0$, the logarithmic singularity of the bubble at zero squared mass and the singularities of its derivatives give the same rates for some finite set of preset commands. This existence argument leaves the number of commands undetermined. The optimal rates with several mass layers and a shared offset remain open. **How many background derivatives are needed at the origin?** Return to the calibrated Gaussian scalar model and the $N$ known, distinct positive squared masses. In one layer take the colored and uncolored slopes $(a,b)=(1,-\sqrt[3]{3})$, then reverse both signs. The two assignments have the same weighted squares and fourth powers, and both have $3a^3+b^3=0$. In the $j$th background derivative of the bubble at $s=0$, the slopes enter only through $3a^{j+2}+b^{j+2}$. The readings therefore agree through background order two at every $P\ge0$. The zero-background gauge traces of Chapter 5 also agree term by term.

Third order separates all assignments using finitely many momentum coefficients. Write $M_{i,r}=3t_{i,C}^r+t_{i,U}^r$. Near the origin, expand the logarithm and then $(m_i+ts)^{-k}$. Positive squared masses give a neighborhood where both expansions converge uniformly for $z\in[0,1]$. Integrating with $\int_0^1[z(1-z)]^k\,dz=(k!)^2/(2k+1)!$ gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
[P^ks^j]R_P(s)&=A_{kj}\sum_i m_i^{-k-j}M_{i,j+2},\\
A_{kj}&=\frac{(-1)^{k+j+1}}{k}\binom{k+j-1}{j}
          \frac{(k!)^2}{(2k+1)!}.
\end{aligned}
$$

For each $j=1,2,3$, take $k=1,\ldots,N$. After division by $A_{kj}$, the readings are the first $N$ power moments of the amplitudes $m_i^{-1-j}M_{i,j+2}$ at the known nodes $1/m_i$. Vandermonde injectivity therefore recovers $M_3,M_4,M_5$ in each layer from these $3N$ coefficients.

How well do the three moments separate signed slopes? Choose slope units so both pairs under comparison lie in $[-1,1]$. Let $F$ be the difference of the cumulative masses of $3\delta_a+\delta_b$ and $3\delta_{a'}+\delta_{b'}$, and set $D=\max(|a-a'|,|b-b'|)$. If $D>0$, consider the interval between the two slopes of the species attaining $D$. Its cumulative weight differs by three or one throughout the interior, and the other species cannot cancel it. Thus $|F|\ge1$ there almost everywhere. The four slopes delimit at most three intervals on which $F$ can be nonzero, so its sign changes at most twice. Put a root at each change of sign and place any unused roots at $-1$. Choose the overall sign of $q(x)=\pm x^2(x-r_1)(x-r_2)$ so that $qF\ge0$. Removing neighborhoods of radius $D/12$ around $0,r_1,r_2$ leaves at least half of the interval of length $D$. Hence, with $\eta=\max_{r=3,4,5}|M_r-M'_r|$,

$$
\frac{D^5}{2\cdot12^4}\le\int q(x)F(x)\,dx\le 4\eta .
$$

The upper bound follows by integration by parts since $q$ contains only powers two through four and its coefficients have absolute sum at most four. Whenever we certify positivity of $8\pi I \pm \mathcal{A}$ on coupled Higgs–heavy-radial channels or eliminate high-potential shell vertices into an effective boundary operator, the exact matrix identity at work is the Lean-verified block Schur complement minimum theorem:

::: {.theorem #thm-ou-ch06-schur-minimum title="Block Schur Complement Quadratic Form as Exact Minimum" lean="D5/S3/Quantum/Matrix/SchurMinimum.schur_quadratic_is_least"}
Let $m, n$ be finite index types, $A \in M_m(\mathbb{C})$ a Hermitian matrix, $B \in M_{m \times n}(\mathbb{C})$ any complex matrix, and $C \in M_n(\mathbb{C})$ a positive-definite matrix. Set $S := A - B C^{-1} B^{\mathsf{H}}$. Then for every boundary vector $x \in \mathbb{C}^m$, the set of block quadratic values

$$
\left\{\, \begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix}^* \begin{pmatrix} A & B \\ B^{\mathsf{H}} & C \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix} \;\Bigg|\; y \in \mathbb{C}^n \,\right\}
$$

as $y$ ranges over $\mathbb{C}^n$ has exact least element (`IsLeast`) equal to the boundary Schur complement value $x^* S x = x^*(A - B C^{-1} B^{\mathsf{H}})x$, attained at the unique minimizer $y^* = -C^{-1} B^{\mathsf{H}} x$.
:::

::: {.proof}
Because $C$ is positive definite, it is Hermitian and invertible. Completing the square on the block quadratic form gives the exact algebraic identity

$$
\begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix}^* \begin{pmatrix} A & B \\ B^{\mathsf{H}} & C \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix} \;=\; x^*(A - B C^{-1} B^{\mathsf{H}})x \;+\; (C^{-1} B^{\mathsf{H}} x + y)^*\,C\,(C^{-1} B^{\mathsf{H}} x + y).
$$

Evaluating at $y^* = -C^{-1} B^{\mathsf{H}} x$ annihilates the second term, showing $x^* S x$ lies in the range; for every $y \in \mathbb{C}^n$, positive definiteness of $C$ guarantees the second term is nonnegative, bounding all values below by $x^* S x$. The full proof is checked by the Lean kernel.
:::

## 6.2 All-Inertia Spectral-Width Exclusion and the Exact Rank-Five Floor for Neutral Traceless Portals {#sec-ou-ch06-s02}

Turning to the two-flavor continuous-arc and edge-preserving finite-subdivision ($h \le 0.01$) heavy scalar system yields the two sharpest ultraviolet portal theorems of the volume.

### Spectral-Width Exclusion Across Arbitrary Positive Arc Inertia $\rho > 0$

Suppose all active heavy scalars lie below $Q$ with logarithmic spectral width bounded by $L \ge \log(Q/m_{\min})$. Under tree-level boundedness, $8\pi$ contact unitarity, and one-loop quartic matching, weighted Gaussian averaging over negative portal directions combined with square completion in $g_U^2$ (by the same algebra as [@thm-ou-ch05-rational-schur]) proves a multiplicity-independent **necessary spectral-width condition** for any species-traceless Hermitian portal $(-F, 3F)$ to make $\Delta_{\mathrm{pole}}$ negative:

$$
\log\!\left(\frac{Q}{m_{\min}}\right) \;>\; 3.45 \qquad \left(\frac{Q}{m_{\min}} \;>\; e^{3.45} \;>\; 31.50039\right).
$$

Yet scanning **all positive arc inertias $\rho > 0$** ($d = \pi^2/(\rho\theta_{804}) > 0$), **all clock angles and rotation axes**, and all $h \le 0.01$ finite grids traps every perturbative gauge crossing inside $26.845 < \log(Q/\mathrm{TeV}) < 26.889$, $W < 49.2$, and $\alpha_G^{-1} < 38.36$ (with $\log(Q/m_{\min}) < 3.272$ on the #35 zero window, and tightly pinched by the two-layer fermion floor across all angles). Substituting into the pole-mass inequality **excludes every species-traceless portal $(-F, 3F)$ across all perturbative crossings**:

$$
\Delta_{\mathrm{pole}} \;>\; 6.8122 \times 10^{23}\;\mathrm{GeV}^2 \qquad (\Delta_{\mathrm{pole}} > 1.0985 \times 10^{24}\;\mathrm{GeV}^2 \text{ on all \#35 perturbative crossings}).
$$

At high inertia ($0 < d \le \frac{1}{2}$, precisely $172$ active scalar layers), the width contracts to $\log(Q/m_{\min}) < 1.787583 < 1.794796$, ruling out even one-loop quartic matching alone for traceless portals.

**Releasing tracelessness and spectral-shape sensitivity**: Adding a common one-loop-order portal $b$ on the 18-layer low-inertia solution ($-fI + bI,\; 3fI + bI$) immediately opens a unique bounded, unitary solution ($0.09 C^2 + 0.005 S^2$, contact norm $< 22.167 < 8\pi$):

$$
f \;\in\; (0.0846092,\; 0.0863030) \quad (f \approx 0.085), \qquad b \;\in\; (0.0061493,\; 0.0071064) \quad (b \approx 0.0066),
$$

at the cost of a relative mass sensitivity $b S_b / m_{\mathrm{low}}^2 > 4.0444 \times 10^{21}$ ($> 4 \times 10^{21}$). In the high-inertia 172-layer regime, however, **even releasing tracelessness fails if $b$ is uniform across all 172 layers**: two-loop mass matching caps $b < 0.002148$ whereas one-loop quartic matching requires $b > 0.005$. Only a spectrally shaped portal weighted toward the lowest modes (such as the six modes below $y = 1/16$, or the heat-kernel portal below) restores joint matching.

### Exact Minimal Rank Five for Neutral Traceless Portals

Return to the spin-balanced all-scale equal-trace two-loop crossing of [Chapter 5](ch05-kinetic-trace-and-uv-ceilings.md) ($Q \in (5.268, 5.390) \times 10^{14}\;\mathrm{GeV}$, carrying $3$ gauge-neutral complex singlets and $58$ charged real scalars per layer). Keeping the subleading common portal $b$ on the charged scalars, restrict the leading traceless Hermitian portal $P$ to act on active neutral heavy scalars with rank $d$ and negative-eigenvalue diagonal block $N$ ($k$ negative directions, $K = \operatorname{tr}N^2$). Tracelessness bounds the positive squared eigenvalues by $(\operatorname{tr}N)^2 \le kK$, so one-loop quartic matching gives $D(\lambda_H - \lambda_{\mathrm{low}}) \le t_{\max}(k + 1)K$. Averaging over the negative subspace with complex Gaussian covariance $N$ under tree-level stability bounds the normalized radial contact diagonal from below by $(1 + 1/k)K/(2\lambda_H)$, canceling $K$ and $k$ into a basis-independent floor:

$$
\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{neg}} \;\ge\; \frac{D(\lambda_H - \lambda_{\mathrm{low}})}{2\lambda_H (d - 1) t_{\max}}.
$$

- **Rank $d \le 4$ is universally excluded**: on the #35 crossing rectangle, $d \le 4$ forces the contact norm strictly above **$31.97519 > 8\pi$** (and rank $1$ traceless matrices vanish identically), for arbitrary eigenvectors and non-commuting mass bases.
- **Exact rank $d = 5$ construction and Schur verification**: selecting any $5$ of the $6$ neutral directions across the two lowest mass levels (separated by a uniform gap from level 3) and setting $P = p(5vv^* - I)/4$ ($|v_i| = 1/\sqrt{5}$, eigenvalues $+p$ and fourfold $-p/4$) solves both matching equations at $p \in (1.90453, 1.93017)$ and $b \in (0.00172813, 0.00204114)$. The normalized negative radial diagonal lies in $(24.27098, 24.93094)$, and applying [@thm-ou-ch06-schur-minimum] to the $2 \times 2$ Schur checks of $8\pi I \pm \mathcal{A}$ certifies a positive unitarity margin $> 3.11842$ alongside all orthogonal channels.

The minimal neutral traceless portal rank is therefore **exactly $5$** on the #35 zero window (and rises to **exactly $6$** on the #78 and #288 zero windows, where rank $5$ exceeds $25.8094 > 8\pi$—a direct link between Riemann zero selection and Higgs portal rank).

## 6.3 The Heat-Kernel Portal $P_t = e^{-tK/d}$ and Parameter-Free Species-Response Tests {#sec-ou-ch06-s03}

Because uniform common portals fail in the high-inertia 172-layer regime, the most canonical geometric ansatz weights both leading and common portals by the shell's own **heat kernel**:

$$
P_t \;=\; \exp\!\left(-\frac{tK}{d}\right), \qquad F \;=\; f P_t, \qquad B \;=\; b P_t \qquad (p_i = e^{-t e_i},\; e_i = x_i/d).
$$

For any positive Hermitian common portal $B \succeq 0$ with operator norm $B_* = \|B\|_{\mathrm{op}}$ and mass-weighted mean $\bar{b} = \sum_i y_i(1 - l_i) B_{ii} / \sum_i y_i(1 - l_i)$, joint matching requires $B_*/\bar{b} > 2.15$. This cleanly partitions diffusion time $t$:

1. **Short-time exclusion ($0 \le t \le 0.49$)**: evaluating at $t = 0.49$ gives $B_*/\bar{b} < 2.145925 < 2.15$; by monotonicity, the entire interval $0 \le t \le 0.49$ is excluded for **every** choice of leading Hermitian portal $F$.

## 6.4 The Low-Energy Collider Pinch: Equal-Depth Vacua, Gluon Fusion, $Z$ Width, CLEO, and OPAL {#sec-ou-ch06-s04}

Every one of these partners confronts precision collider measurements directly:

1. **Equal-depth electroweak vacua: $3\times$ cubic Higgs self-coupling or a real singlet $\le 8.602\;\mathrm{TeV}$**.

Realizing equal-depth vacua via a renormalizable real singlet $S$ with $U(s, S) = \lambda(s - aS)^2 + g s(S - b)^2$ ($ab = s_0$) locks the product of mass eigenvalues to $2\lambda g v^4$: across $\lambda, g \le 4\pi$, the **second scalar mass is bounded above by $\le 8.602\;\mathrm{TeV}$** (e.g., $377.38\;\mathrm{GeV}$ and $1.298\times$ standard cubic coupling at $a = v, b = v/2, g = 2$, see [`ch39-two-electroweak-vacua.py`](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing-experiments/blob/ba0ac9539cdb641744054bf3a0398494f4cba1c8/experiments/ch39-omega-research/replay/ch39-two-electroweak-vacua.py)).

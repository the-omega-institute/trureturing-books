# Chapter 5 Gauge Kinetic Traces, Two-Scale Balancing, and Perturbative UV Ceilings {#sec-ou-ch05}

> "When you summon heavy states at high energy to balance the three gauge kinetic coefficients, never forget that those same states march straight back into the renormalization-group thresholds below—the ledger of quantum field theory never lets you book ultraviolet credits without paying infrared debits."

In [Chapter 4](ch04-gauge-crossings-and-monotone-barriers.md), ultraviolet coupling equality $g_1^2 = g_2^2 = g_3^2$ still functioned as an imposed boundary condition at a matching scale $Q$. We now test a much stronger, independently falsifiable hypothesis: **the gauge kinetic terms themselves are generated by a single positive heat-kernel trace of squared gauge generators over the matter spectrum.**

The moment this hypothesis is imposed, ultraviolet normalization and infrared running lock into tight feedback. Balancing the three gauge traces forbids keeping only left-handed doublets; complementary right-handed multiplets must be introduced, and whenever they sit below $Q$ they push back against the very RG convergence they were meant to underpin. This chapter follows that feedback loop to its sharpest boundaries—from Laplace uniqueness and exact charged-rank floors, through the spin-balanced tower achieving exact all-scale equality, to the rigid perturbative ultraviolet ceilings that terminate fixed-group continuation (full derivations and interval certificates reside in [the gauge kinetic-trace replay notes](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing-experiments/blob/ba0ac9539cdb641744054bf3a0398494f4cba1c8/experiments/ch39-omega-research/replay/ch39-%E8%A7%84%E8%8C%83%E5%8A%A8%E8%83%BD%E8%B0%B1%E8%BF%B9%E4%B8%8E%E9%98%88%E5%80%BC.md)).

## 5.1 Positive Spectral-Trace Generation, Laplace Uniqueness, and Single-Scale Thresholds {#sec-ou-ch05-s01}

We begin by setting down the trace-generation ledger and anchoring the exact rational Schur-completion engine used across finite-grid eliminations and positive-measure moment bounds.

::: {.definition #def-ou-ch05-kinetic-trace title="Positive Spectral-Trace Generation of Gauge Kinetics and Thermal Moments"}
Write $I = (I_1, I_2, I_3)$ for the canonically normalized generator-squared trace indices of $\sqrt{3/5}\,Y$, $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L$, and $\mathrm{SU}(3)_C$. Counting each complex scalar (or equally normalized Weyl multiplet) with unit weight, introduce four building blocks:

- left-handed doublet sets $L = (\frac{2}{5}, 2, 1)$ (colored quark-type and uncolored lepton-type doublets);
- complementary right-handed conjugate sets $R = (\frac{8}{5}, 0, 1)$ (optionally accompanied by gauge-neutral singlets);
- the Higgs doublet $H = (\frac{3}{10}, \frac{1}{2}, 0)$ and the $|Y| = \frac{1}{3}$ color-triplet weak singlet $C = (\frac{1}{5}, 0, \frac{1}{2})$.

Together they assemble complete equal-trace families $L + R = (2, 2, 2)$ and $H + C = (\frac{1}{2}, \frac{1}{2}, \frac{1}{2})$. Setting $y_i := m_i^2/Q^2$ and dimensionless inverse squared cutoff $u > 0$, define for each sector $X \in \{L, R, H, C\}$ the thermal trace (zeroth moment) and first thermal moment:

$$
S_X(u) \;:=\; \sum_{i \in X} e^{-u y_i}, \qquad M_{1,X}(u) \;:=\; -\frac{\mathrm{d}S_X}{\mathrm{d}u} \;=\; \sum_{i \in X} y_i e^{-u y_i}.
$$

Demanding that these four sectors (plus any complete families) generate three equal positive gauge kinetic traces at a given cutoff eliminates the common vector via weak-minus-color and hypercharge-minus-color projections, uniquely forcing the **trace-balance laws**

$$
W_L \;=\; W_R, \qquad W_H \;=\; W_C,
$$

where $W_X = S_X(u)$ denotes the total thermal weight of sector $X$ at that cutoff.
:::

Whenever continuous-arc spectra are compressed to finite subdivisions or quadratic moment inequalities are minimized over an internal variable, the core algebraic move is completing the square. Its exact rational form is certified in Lean 4:

::: {.theorem #thm-ou-ch05-rational-schur title="Rational Schur Completion and Attained Positive-Pivot Minimum" lean="D5/S3/Quantum/AURICOmega/OmegaQuadraticAndSchur.rational_schur_complete D5/S3/Quantum/AURICOmega/OmegaQuadraticAndSchur.rational_schur_min"}
For any finite rationals $a, b, c, x, y \in \mathrm{Omega.FiniteRatio}$, set $\mathrm{scalarForm}(a,b,c,x,y) := a x^2 + 2bxy + c y^2$, $\mathrm{schurValue}(a,b,c,x) := (a - b^2/c)x^2$, and $\mathrm{schurSquare}(b,c,x,y) := c(y + (b/c)x)^2$:

1. **Nonzero-pivot exact completion (`rational_schur_complete`)**: whenever the pivot is nonzero (`c.num ≠ 0`), under rational equivalence `FiniteRatio.Equivalent`,

   $$
   a x^2 + 2bxy + c y^2 \;\equiv\; \left(a - \frac{b^2}{c}\right)x^2 \;+\; c\!\left(y + \frac{b}{c}x\right)^2.
   $$

2. **Positive-pivot attained global minimum (`rational_schur_min`)**: whenever the pivot is strictly positive ($0 < c$), every rational $y$ satisfies

   $$
   \left(a - \frac{b^2}{c}\right)x^2 \;\le\; a x^2 + 2bxy + c y^2,
   $$

   with exact equality attained at the explicit minimizer $y^* = -(b/c)x$ (`schurMinPoint b c x`).
:::

::: {.proof}
For the first statement, `c.num ≠ 0` supplies the rational inverse of $c$; expanding the right-hand side by commutative-ring laws cancels $\pm (b^2/c)x^2$ identically under `FiniteRatio.Equivalent`. For the second statement, $0 < c$ together with nonnegativity of squares in `Omega.FiniteRatio` guarantees $c(y + (b/c)x)^2 \ge 0$ for all $y$, giving the global lower bound, while substituting $y^* = -(b/c)x$ annihilates the squared remainder and attains $(a - b^2/c)x^2$. The full proof is checked by the Lean kernel.
:::

Three sharp structural consequences follow immediately from [@def-ou-ch05-kinetic-trace]:

1. **Full-interval Laplace uniqueness $\implies$ zero differential one-loop running**.
   Demanding $S_L(u) = S_R(u)$ across **any open cutoff interval** $u \in (u_1, u_2)$ forces $L$ and $R$ to have **identical mass spectra with multiplicities**, by analyticity of the Laplace transform and uniqueness of positive spectral measures. For equal spins, every mass threshold then contributes only a complete family $L + R = (2, 2, 2)$, whose differential one-loop RG shift is identically zero.

   When one tower has $d\ge1$ distinct finite mass levels, finitely many equally spaced readings suffice. Take $u_j=u_0+jh$ with $u_0,h>0$. For a reference tower with positive masses, $2d+1$ readings suffice. A massless level in the reference tower reduces the count to $2d$. The competitor may be any positive tower with finite trace at $u_0$. These counts are sharp in the worst case for the stated reference towers. Set $x=e^{-hy}$ and multiply the weight of each level by $e^{-u_0y}$. The reading $S(u_j)$ becomes the $j$th moment of this measure on $(0,1]$. For reference nodes $x_i$, form $p(x)=\prod_i(x-x_i)^2$, replacing $(x-1)^2$ by $1-x$ if the reference has a node at $1$. This nonnegative polynomial integrates to zero on the reference tower. Equality of the readings confines the competing measure to its zeros, and the Vandermonde system for the first $d$ moments then fixes every multiplicity. [Boundary criterion for truncated moments, Theorem 3.5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.12698)

   One fewer reading can be ambiguous. For $d=1$ and $u_0=h$, put three states at $x=1/2$. A second tower puts three at $x=1/4$ and one at $x=3/4$. Both give $3/2,3/4$ for the first two readings, while their third readings are $3/8$ and $15/32$. More generally, write $m=\deg p$ and place signed weights $(-1)^j\binom mj$ on $m+1$ equally spaced rational nodes. Their sum against every polynomial of degree less than $m$ vanishes. Take all nodes in $(0,1)$, or end at $1$ in the massless case. The odd-indexed side then has exactly $d$ nodes, counting from $j=0$. Dividing by $x_j^{u_0/h}$ restores the thermal weights. The two sides agree at the first $m$ readings and differ at the next. When $u_0=h$, clearing denominators gives positive integer multiplicities on both sides. For any $a=u_0/h>0$, perturb the signed thermal weights $c_i$ to nearby rationals and hold the rightmost node fixed. The Jacobian of the first $m$ differences in the other $m$ nodes has entries $(a+j)c_i x_i^{a-1}x_i^j$, an invertible Vandermonde matrix up to nonzero row and column factors. The implicit function theorem preserves the equalities with nearby ordered nodes. The next reading still differs by continuity, and the rational weights can now be scaled to integers. If each reading differs by at most $\eta$, the competitor’s **tilted** mass on $p(x)\ge b>0$ is at most $\eta\sum_j|p_j|/b$, where $p_j$ are the coefficients of $p$. A heavy state still carries the factor $e^{-u_0 y}$ in this bound.

   When the equally spaced readings carry errors, how far must the thermal weights move to match the two towers? For each tower with $0<S(u_0)<\infty$, divide its tilted weights by $S(u_0)$ and use the coordinate $x=e^{-hy}$. Its $j$th moment is $M_j=S(u_0+jh)/S(u_0)$. Suppose the two normalized moments differ by at most $\varepsilon$ at every order $1\le j\le m$. The reference has $d$ distinct nodes and the comparison spectrum may be any positive spectrum. If the $m+1$ unnormalized readings, including the baseline, each differ by at most $\eta$, one can take $\varepsilon=2\eta/S_{\mathrm{ref}}(u_0)$. Their first Wasserstein distance $W_1$ in $x$, the minimum mean displacement required to transport all the probability, satisfies $W_1\le C_d\varepsilon^{1/m}$. Take $m=2d$ when all reference masses are positive. If the reference contains the massless endpoint $x=1$, only $m=2d-1$ is needed. The first bound follows directly from the moment comparison theorem of Wu and Yang. [Wu and Yang, Proposition 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.07237v2)

   Why does the endpoint save one moment? On the line, $W_1$ is the integral of the absolute difference of the two cumulative distribution functions. The reference cumulative distribution function minus the comparison one is nonincreasing between reference nodes and can jump upward only at these nodes. It changes sign at most $2d-1$ times. If the last reference node is $1$, no interval follows the last jump, leaving at most $2d-2$ sign changes. Place a simple polynomial root at each sign change and choose the overall sign to match the cumulative difference. Integration by parts bounds the integral of the polynomial times this difference by the errors in the first $m$ moments. Remove a neighbourhood of radius $\varepsilon^{1/m}$ around each root and divide by the absolute value of the polynomial on the complement to obtain the stated bound. The exponent is sharp when reference nodes can approach one another. Normalize the alternating binomial weights above separately on the odd and even nodes and space the nodes a distance $s$ apart. Then $W_1=s$, the first $m-1$ moments agree, and the absolute discrepancy at order $m$ is exactly $m!s^m/2^{m-1}$.

   For reference nodes with a fixed positive minimum spacing, the bound improves to $O(\sqrt{\varepsilon})$, with a constant depending on the spacing and the number of levels. Split the weight of one interior node equally between points a distance $s$ to either side. Each moment changes by $O(s^2)$, yet the split weight must move a distance $s$. Thus the square-root exponent is sharp whenever the reference has an interior node. For a reference concentrated at $x=1$, the exact identity $W_1=1-M_1^{\mathrm{comp}}\le\varepsilon$ holds, where $M_1^{\mathrm{comp}}$ is the comparison tower's first moment. If both towers satisfy $y\le Y$, converting to $y=-\log(x)/h$ multiplies the bound on transport by at most $e^{hY}/h$. On an unbounded mass range the heavy tail can escape detection. For example, moving $\varepsilon$ of the normalized thermal weight from a fixed $y$ to $y+\varepsilon^{-2}$ changes each $x$ moment by at most $\varepsilon$, yet increases the mean squared mass by $\varepsilon^{-1}$. Dividing by one exponential and differentiating lets Rolle's theorem prove this by induction on the number of terms.    With only $N$ readings, even a fixed reference tower with positive real weights admits a different competitor. Add a sufficiently small positive weight at any extra positive mass. The Jacobian is invertible because a left null vector would give an exponential sum vanishing at the reference levels and with zero first derivative at every movable one, for a total multiplicity of $N$. Worst-case ambiguity also occurs with positive integer multiplicities. Take $N+1$ ordered levels. Their maximal evaluation minors are nonzero and have a common sign, so the signed cofactors alternate. Split the positive and negative weights into two towers and use the side with $d$ levels as reference, selecting the endpoint side when it has a massless level. Fix the first location and vary the other $N$. The Jacobian of the reading differences is the invertible exponential evaluation matrix times nonzero row and column factors. This lets the signed weights be adjusted to nearby rationals while keeping the readings equal. Clearing denominators gives integer multiplicities.

   With errors in the readings, the spacing of the inverse cutoffs matters. Keep the coordinate $x=e^{-hy}$ fixed and take $m+1$ distinct cutoffs $u_0<u_1<\cdots<u_m$, with $m=2d$ for an interior $d$-level reference and $m=2d-1$ if the reference contains the massless level. Write $a_j=(u_j-u_0)/h$, so $a_0=0$. Each normalized reading is now the moment of $x^{a_j}$. ## 5.2 Exact Left-Rank Floors, Higher-Moment Positivity Obstructions, and Two-Scale Irreconcilability {#sec-ou-ch05-s02}

The $r = 400$ two-loop construction looks like a lifeline for trace-generated gauge kinetics. Yet probing three natural questions—how many charged left layers must survive, what happens at second and third thermal derivatives, and whether balance survives a second cutoff scale—exposes sheer mathematical cliffs.

Three structural barriers organize the pure-scalar trace regime:

1. **Exact charged left-rank floors: $23$ (prefix) and $20$ (skip-layer)**.
   Holding the $r = 400$ two-loop window and the lowest $18$ left layers fixed while allowing high left layers to be retained or neutralized, each above-threshold right state ($y \ge 1$) carries weight $w = e^{-y} \le c = 1/e$. Concavity fixes the minimal first moment at total weight $W$ as $kc - v\log v$ ($k = \lfloor W/c \rfloor$, $v = W - kc$) and the maximal first moment over at most $N$ components as $W\log(N/W)$. Retaining only the 18 low left layers gives $W > 0$ yet $M < 0$ (infeasible). Adding $1, 2, 3$, or $4$ consecutive layers from the first 12-dimensional heavy band (modes $19\text{–}30$, $1.2710 < y < 1.273$) still leaves the required first moment strictly below the minimum (4 layers fall short by $-0.01707$); the **5th** layer clears the floor by $> 0.06732$, establishing **exact minimal prefix left rank $18 + 5 = 23$** (keeping the entire 12-mode band gives the gap-selected rank $30$). Allowing non-consecutive skip-layer selection from modes $127\text{–}138$ ($2.150 < y < 2.158$), just $2$ eigenmodes suffice (margin $> 0.01938$), lowering the **exact minimal general left rank to $18 + 2 = 20$**.

2. **Copy-number explosion at 2nd derivative ($> 75{,}000$ copies) and positivity collapse at 3rd derivative (violation $> 4.899$)**.
   In the rank-20 skip-layer solution, normalized heavy right weights $p_j = w_j/W$ have Shannon entropy $\eta = M/W + \log W \in (0.06796, 0.11555)$, while matching the second thermal derivative $B$ requires information variance $V = B/W - (M/W)^2 \in (1.76013, 1.85057)$. For $0 < \eta < \log 2$, Lagrange multipliers prove that the maximum variance over $N$ weights is attained by one large weight $1 - p$ and $N - 1$ equal tail weights, giving variance $\frac{1-p}{p}[\eta + \log(1-p)]^2$. Reaching $V > 1.76013$ requires **more than $75{,}000$ heavy right copies**.

   At the third thermal derivative, positivity fails outright: every positive spectral measure on $y \ge 1$ obeys $\int (y-1)(y-t)^2 e^{-y}\,\mathrm{d}n \ge 0$ for all $t$, which by completing the square (the same mechanism as [@thm-ou-ch05-rational-schur]) forces $C \ge B + (B-M)^2/(M-W)$. Across the entire window, this lower bound exceeds the target third moment $C$ by **more than $4.89948$** ($> 4.899$)—**no finite or infinite collection of heavy right states can match derivatives $0$ through $3$ simultaneously.**

**Hand derivation (`ARGUED`).** Fix $u>1$, the first right-sector heat trace $S=S_R(1)>0$, and an exposure budget $B$. Normalize the spectral weights by $e^{-y}/S$ and write $b=B/S$. With no cap, a layer with $0<y\le1$ costs $x=e^y(-\log y)/2$ per unit of first heat weight. The ratio of its second to first heat weight is $f(x)=e^{-(u-1)y}$. Layers above threshold have zero cost, and the maximum ratio at zero cost is $q=f(0)=e^{1-u}$, attained at $y=1$. As $x$ increases, $f$ is first convex and then concave. The line through $(0,q)$ tangent to the strictly concave portion has a unique point of contact $x_*$ and slope $c_*=(f(x_*)-q)/x_*$. The line segment up to $x_*$ followed by the concave portion of $f$ gives an increasing concave majorant $F$. Jensen's inequality yields $S_R(u)/S\le F(b)$. Real spectral weights attain the bound by mixing the threshold and tangent layers for $b\le x_*$, or using the single layer with cost $b$ for $b>x_*$. At $u=8$, for instance, $x_*\approx1.69524$. At $b=2$, the best pointwise line gives about $0.90238$ and the sharp bound is about $0.87051$. **The integer gap on the critical chord (hand derivation, `ARGUED`).** At $\tau=\tau_0$ with $0\le B\le S\tau$, the real-weight bound is $qS+c_*B$. Count each copy of an integer multiplicity as one state with first heat weight $w=e^{-y}$ and exposure $t(w)=\min(\tau,\max(0,-\log(-\log w)/2))$. Write $y_*$ for the squared mass ratio of the tangent layer. The chord contacts the weights $w_1=e^{-1}$, $w_*=e^{-y_*}$, and the limit $1$, with respective exposures $0$, $t_*=t(w_*)$, and $\tau$. Let $d$ be the minimum Euclidean distance from $(S,B)$ to the following count pairs, with nonnegative integers $n_0,n_*,n_1$ satisfying $n_0+n_*+n_1\le \lfloor 2eS\rfloor$.

$$
(n_0+n_*w_*+n_1w_1,\ n_0\tau+n_*t_*).
$$

For every finite integer spectrum with positive masses, first heat trace $S$, and exposure at most $B$, the gap $\Delta=qS+c_*B-S_R(u)$ satisfies $\Delta\ge K_2d^2$. When the first trace $S$ is held exactly fixed, the bound strengthens to $\Delta\ge K_1d$. Both constants are positive, depend only on $u,S$, and apply throughout the budget interval.

The slack of one state, $qw+c_*t(w)-w^u$, is quadratic at the tangent weight $w_*$ and one-sided linear near $w_1$ and $1$. Assigning each state with $w\ge w_1/2$ to its nearest contact weight gives at most $\lfloor 2eS\rfloor$ copies. The heavier states, with $w<w_1/2$, contribute total first heat weight bounded linearly by the gap. If $(S',B')$ is the resulting count pair, $|S-S'|$ is at most a constant times $\sqrt{\Delta}$. Near the point of tangency, the exposure varies with the first heat weight at slope $t'(w_*)$. Subtracting this contribution makes $|B-B'-t'(w_*)(S-S')|$ at most a constant times $\Delta$. With $S$ fixed, the finite set of candidate traces forces $S'=S$ for a sufficiently small gap, so the budget residual gives the linear bound.

Varying both inputs along the single-state curve $(w,t(w))$ through $w_*$ realizes the quadratic order.

# Chapter 9 Dimension-Six Baryon Operators and Baryon Triality Selection Rules {#sec-ch09}

> "The deadliest gift of grand unification is not high-energy crossing, but proton decay; gauge orthogonality seats the charges at separate tables, yet it swears no oath to keep the proton whole."

The second half of Question 3 points straight at the oldest sword hanging over grand unification: **will the proton decay?** In standard $SU(5)$ unification, packing quarks and leptons into shared multiplets invites heavy gauge-boson exchange to generate the dimension-six operator $QQQL$, pushing the proton lifetime right up against experimental walls. One might hope that because the 19-point Behrend shell encodes color, weak isospin, and hypercharge via orthogonal block projections and arc phases, gauge orthogonality itself automatically bans $QQQL$.

This chapter shatters that illusion with an exact thirty-six-monomial Grassmann expansion: gauge orthogonality and perturbative threshold data leave the dimension-six Wilson coefficient $c_6$ completely unconstrained. We then build the exact defense from the ground up: classifying all generation-universal **baryon triality $\mathbb{Z}_3$** assignments, proving that triality lifts the lowest allowed baryon-violating operator from dimension $6$ to dimension $15$ (or $18$ with $B - L$ conserved), constructing an anomaly-free gauged $U(1)_X$ UV completion whose six anomaly traces vanish identically, and establishing the general generation-divisibility law $m \mid N_g$.

Yet installing that seemingly modest order-3 phase inside a full unified gauge group and the 19-point shell sets off an extraordinary algebraic and topological chain reaction: the Lie algebra closure inflates to the $123$-dimensional $\mathfrak{su}(5) \oplus \mathfrak{su}(10)$, discrete cubic anomaly inflow forces the **25-state lifted Behrend shell**, tension between spectral grading and site locality explodes the closure to the $1801$-dimensional $\mathfrak{su}(19)^5 \oplus \mathfrak{u}(1)$, and on the joint witness zero **#2,306,888,100,085** the very same 105-winding vacuum and tree spectrum admit two rival UV extensions—one keeping the proton eternally stable, the other activating $QQQL$ with Wilson factor at least $\approx 0.03144$.

## 9.1 Why Gauge Orthogonality Cannot Ban Dimension-Six Proton Decay: Thirty-Six Monomials and Simplicial Exactness {#sec-ch09-qqql-and-exactness}

Does isolating color, weak isospin, and hypercharge inside mutually orthogonal generator blocks seal off baryon-number violation? One explicit invariant tensor answers in the negative.

::: {.definition #def-ou-ch09-baryon-triality title="Dimension-Six QQQL Operator and Generation-Universal Baryon Triality Classification"}
On the Standard Model left-handed Weyl field content, define the gauge-invariant dimension-six four-fermion baryon-violating operator

$$
\mathcal{O}_6 := \epsilon_{abc}\,\epsilon_{ij}\,\epsilon_{kl}\,\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}\,\epsilon_{\gamma\delta}\,Q^{ai\alpha} Q^{bj\beta} Q^{ck\gamma} L^{l\delta},
$$

where $a, b, c \in \{0, 1, 2\}$ are $SU(3)_c$ color indices, $i, j, k, l \in \{0, 1\}$ are $SU(2)_L$ weak-isospin indices, and $\alpha, \beta, \gamma, \delta \in \{0, 1\}$ are left-handed spinor indices. Its total hypercharge is $3\cdot \tfrac{1}{6} - \tfrac{1}{2} = 0$, and it carries $\Delta B = \Delta L = 1$.

Moreover, on a low-energy spectrum with one Higgs doublet $H$, all standard Yukawa couplings, and the Weinberg neutrino-mass operator $(LH)^2$, let $(q, l, h)$ denote the generation-universal $\mathbb{Z}_3$ charges of the left quark doublet, left lepton doublet, and Higgs doublet. Fixing conjugate singlets by Yukawa invariance and enforcing $2(l + h) \equiv 0 \pmod 3$ (so $l \equiv -h$) from the Weinberg operator yields the complete classification of generation-universal $\mathbb{Z}_3$ charges:

$$
X \equiv h(2Y - B) + q(3B) \pmod 3.
$$

:::

### 9.1.2 Zero-Axiom Simplicial Exactness and Mod-5 Rank-One Splitting

Why do local flatness and algebraic splitting fail to obstruct global baryon violation on their own? Turning back to the zero-axiom core of Lean (`axioms: []`), two strictly axiom-free theorems illuminate both local cochain exactness and non-commutative mod-5 rank-one splitting at the foundation:

::: {.theorem #thm-ou-ch09-simplicial-exactness title="Zero-Axiom Simplicial 2-Cochain Exactness and Mod-5 Rank-One Hurwitz Splitting" lean="D5/S3/Quantum/AURICOmega/OmegaSimplicialExactness.d2_eq_zero_iff D5/S3/Quantum/AURICOmega/OmegaMatrixAndFractran.mod_five_rank_one"}
In the strictly zero-axiom kernel (`axioms: []`), both exact statements hold unconditionally:

1. **Simplicial 2-cochain closedness equals exactness** (`d2_eq_zero_iff`): let $(G, \mathrm{laws})$ be an additive Abelian group on $A$, let `base : V` be a base vertex in $V$, and let $F : C^2(V, A)$ be any $2$-cochain. Then $F$ is closed under the $2$-coboundary ($d^2 F(x, y, z, w) = 0$ for all $x, y, z, w \in V$) if and only if there exists a $1$-cochain $e : C^1(V, A)$ such that $F = d^1 e$ ($d^1 e(x, y, z) = F(x, y, z)$ for all $x, y, z \in V$).
2. **Mod-5 rank-one splitting of Hurwitz generators** (`mod_five_rank_one`): for each of the six norm-$5$ Hurwitz quaternion generators `g : Hurwitz.Generator` with explicit residue factors `f := g.factors`, its splitting image in $\mathrm{Mat}_2(\mathbb{F}_5)$ satisfies

   $$
   \mathrm{split5}(g.\mathrm{coordinates}) = \mathrm{Matrix5.outer}(f.u, f.v, f.s, f.t)
   \qquad \text{and} \qquad
   \mathrm{split5}(g.\mathrm{coordinates}).\mathrm{isZero} = \mathrm{false}.
   $$

:::

Can the independent partition reading certify independent magnitudes? In complex dimension two, give site $i$ exactly $r_i$ Grassmann copies that share $x_i=|c_i|^2$, so the joint reading is $\mathbb E\prod_i P(x_i)^{r_i}$. Fix any copy limit $m\ge1$. There is an exchangeable orientation law that agrees with the independent reading for every number of sites and every $r_i\le m$. To construct it, take $k=4m+1$ and $H_k(x)=(d/dx)^k[x^k(x-1)^k]/k!$. Draw one fair sign $S$ for the entire probe, then draw sites independently with density $1+S\varepsilon H_k(x)$ for sufficiently small $\varepsilon>0$. Their individual marginals are uniform. Integration by parts gives $\int_0^1x^jH_k(x)\,dx=0$ for $j<k$ and $(j!)^2/[(j-k)!(j+k+1)!]$ for $j\ge k$. This makes each conditional mean of $P^{r_i}$ independent of $S$ up to $r_i=m$. With $m+1$ copies at each of two sites the excess over the independent reading is $\varepsilon^2(\int_0^1P^{m+1}H_k)^2>0$. For $m=1$ and $\varepsilon=1/10$ the excess is $26569/6084$.

If the sites extend to an infinite exchangeable sequence, testing all replica orders at two sites suffices. Write $Y_i=P(x_i)$. When $\mathbb E[Y_1^rY_2^r]=(\mathbb E Y_1^r)^2$ for every $r\ge1$, the empirical mean of $Y_i^r$ on $N$ sites has variance $\operatorname{Var}(Y_1^r)/N$. Products of these bounded empirical means converge in expectation to products of marginal moments. For any fixed number of factors, the fraction of products with repeated site indices tends to zero. Exchangeability thus gives factorization of all mixed moments, which determines the joint law on a compact cube. Strict monotonicity of $P$ transfers independence to the magnitudes. Finite exchangeability alone permits a counterexample. Randomly permuting $(U,V,(U+V)\bmod 1)$ for independent uniform $U,V$ makes each pair independent, yet the triple lies in a finite union of planes. This test addresses magnitude correlations. When the phase can be absorbed and when it becomes a physical parameter can be stated in three layers. In the contact probe, as long as the rest of the action is invariant under rephasing $Q$ and the product $Fc$ is the same at every site, $Q \to e^{-i\arg(Fc)/3} Q$ is a field redefinition with Berezin Jacobian $1$ that turns $Fc$ into $|Fc|$; every readout built from operators of definite charge and invariant under a complex rescaling of each operator therefore depends only on $|Fc|$. For the phase to be physical there must be a vertex that does not respect this rephasing.

In the three-generation model, fix the flavour basis and write $\eta_I$ for the nonzero weak-instanton coupling. The contact and instanton operators have baryon numbers one and three, so an invariant containing the contact phase must take the form $k\Phi_I$ plus phases of flavour couplings, where $\Phi_I=3\arg(Fc)-\arg\eta_I$ and $k$ is a nonzero integer. For a first-generation contact operator the charge difference is $3(3Q_1+L_1)-\sum_{i=1}^3(3Q_i+L_i)$, where $Q_i,L_i$ denote field rephasing charges. Which $k$ are allowed depends on the nonzero Yukawa and Weinberg entries. For an example with three invertible Yukawa matrices, take all their diagonal entries nonzero and add just the two off-diagonal entries in the first column of the up-type matrix. The three rows share a right-handed field, so subtracting two operator charges in that column gives any difference of left-handed quark charges. On the lepton side the required charge is $b=2L_1-L_2-L_3$. If only the three diagonal Weinberg entries are present, each carries even lepton charge in every generation, so they cannot supply $b$. Writing $w_{ij}=L_i+L_j+2H$ for the operator charge gives $2w_{11}-w_{22}-w_{33}=2b$, allowing the second harmonic. With just $w_{11},w_{23}$ instead, $w_{11}-w_{23}=b$ already allows the first harmonic.

The same charge differences classify every sparse texture. Join two quark rows that have nonzero entries in a common column of the same up- or down-type Yukawa matrix. All three rows must be connected because no nonempty proper subset of the weights $(2,-1,-1)$ sums to zero. On the lepton side, join rows sharing a charged-lepton Yukawa column and contract each connected component to a vertex carrying the sum of its weights. Draw an edge for each supported $w_{ij}$ between the resulting vertices, allowing loops. Crossing an edge reverses the sign of the generator in the quotient lattice. In each bipartite component the two sides must have equal total weights, counting the other side of an isolated vertex as empty. If quark connectivity or any bipartite test fails, no positive harmonic is allowed. Otherwise a component with an odd cycle imposes $2x=0$, retaining just the parity of its total weight. An odd total in any such component allows exactly the positive even $k$. In all other cases passing the tests, every positive integer $k$ is allowed. This determines the lowest harmonic that can enter a rephasing invariant. A nonzero phase response in the full model still requires a dynamical calculation. Allowing arbitrary unitary changes of the three-generation weak basis also requires checking the transformations that preserve the contact directions. Write the rank-one contact tensor as $g\,q^{\otimes3}\otimes\ell$, with $g=Fc\ne0$ and unit quark and lepton flavour vectors $q,\ell$. Changing their representatives to $e^{ia}q,e^{ib}\ell$ changes the coefficient of the same tensor to $e^{-i(3a+b)}g$. Fix the three left Gram matrices $A=Y_uY_u^\dagger$, $B=Y_dY_d^\dagger$, $E=Y_eY_e^\dagger$ and a complex symmetric Weinberg matrix $W$. Take unitaries $U$ preserving $A,B$ and $V$ preserving $E$ with $VWV^T=W$. For transformations preserving the two contact lines, write $Uq=\alpha q$ and $V\ell=\beta\ell$. The instanton carries one quark flavour determinant per colour and one lepton flavour determinant. Its character relative to three powers of the contact term is therefore

$$
\chi(U,V)=\left(\frac{\det U}{\alpha^3}\right)^3\frac{\det V}{\beta^3}.
$$

Flavour symmetry allows harmonic $k$ precisely when $\chi^k=1$ for every transformation preserving the background and the two lines. Independent right-handed rotations make the three Gram matrices sufficient for the Dirac sector even when a Yukawa matrix is singular. On the quark side, apply all products of $A,B$ of length at most two to $q$, including the empty product. On their span $U$ acts as the scalar $\alpha$. If the span has dimension less than three, an arbitrary phase on its orthogonal complement makes $\chi$ range over the unit circle, ruling out every positive harmonic. Suppose henceforth that the quark vectors span the full space. For $W=0$ the same test on $\ell,E\ell,E^2\ell$ permits the fundamental harmonic if these vectors span the lepton space and rules out every positive harmonic otherwise.

If $W$ has three distinct positive Takagi singular values, take a basis in which $W$ is positive diagonal. Then $V$ can only change coordinate signs. Join $i,j$ when $E_{ij}\ne0$, and join all indices in the support of $\ell$ to each other. Each connected component carries one independent sign, with sign $\beta$ on the component containing the support. Flipping any other component contributes one minus sign per index to the determinant. Thus an odd-sized component disjoint from the support allows precisely the positive even harmonics. In all other cases the fundamental and all its positive multiples are allowed. For example, with $W=\operatorname{diag}(1,2,3)$, positive diagonal $E$ and $\ell=e_1$, flipping the second coordinate alone excludes the fundamental.

Two real linear systems also cover repeated or zero Takagi singular values. Put $P=\ell\ell^\dagger$ and take skew-Hermitian $X$ commuting with $E,P$ and satisfying $XW=W\overline{X}$. If some such $X$ has $\operatorname{tr}X-3\ell^\dagger X\ell\ne0$, the relative character of $e^{sX}$ ranges over the circle. Otherwise, precisely the even harmonics survive if $\ell^\perp$ contains a complex line preserved by both $E$ and $z\mapsto W\bar z$, since one can reverse its sign. To test for that line, solve $YE=EY$, $YW=W\overline{Y}$, $Y\ell=0$ for Hermitian $Y$. If some solution is nonscalar on the two-dimensional space $\ell^\perp$, a simple nonzero eigenvalue gives the required line. Conversely, the orthogonal projector onto such a line is a solution. The tests are exhaustive because each stabilizer element factors into a continuous rotation and a reflection on its $-1$ eigenspace. If neither test obstructs the fundamental harmonic, flavour symmetry permits it. For example, at $W=I$ and $E=I$, the line of $\ell=e_1$ permits even harmonics, while $\ell=(e_1+i e_2)/\sqrt2$ acquires a continuous phase under rotations of the same real plane, ruling out every positive harmonic.

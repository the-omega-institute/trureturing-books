> "When an elegant ultraviolet boundary misses low-energy data by three point six six inverse-coupling units, nature is not playing a prank on you—it is daring you to pinpoint which silent monotonicity assumption locked every path on the wrong side of the wall."

Volume I assembled the arithmetic and geometric toolkit on the workbench: the 19-point Behrend shell with its $135$ chord edges, the dual quaternion clocks driven by Riemann zero ordinates, and the candidate zero windows bounded by Ramanujan spectral gaps and Fejér collisions (see [Chapter 1](ch01-behrend-shell-and-frequencies.md) through [Chapter 3](ch03-riemann-zeros-fejer-and-landau.md)). When three running couplings narrowly miss each other, the oldest temptation in particle physics is to sprinkle in extra fields until the triangle closes. This chapter takes the opposite route: **seal off every impossible corridor with airtight no-go bounds first, then measure the exact structural price of each surviving conditional crossing.**

## 4.1 Magnetic Shell Mass Operators, Weak-Center Parity Sectors, and the Coupling Gap {#sec-ou-ch04-s01}

The baseline gauge algebra is deliberately austere. The Standard Model Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{SM}} = \mathfrak{u}(1)_Y \oplus \mathfrak{su}(2)_L \oplus \mathfrak{su}(3)_C$ has exactly $1 + 3 + 8 = 12$ generators and introduces no baryon-number-violating $(X, Y)$ leptoquark gauge bosons. At the ultraviolet arc-confluence scale $\mu_{\mathrm{arc}}$, if all three gauge factors read the same complete chiral Weyl fermion ledger with a single universal arc-trace stiffness, the quadratic trace ratios of one generation lock $g_3^2 = g_2^2 = \frac{5}{3}g_Y^2$, fixing the bare ultraviolet Weinberg angle at the rational value $\sin^2\theta_W(\mu_{\mathrm{arc}}) = \frac{3}{8}$.

Yet running straight up from the $Z$-pole experimental baseline at $M_Z = 91.1876\;\mathrm{GeV}$ ($\widehat{\alpha}^{-1} = 127.930$, $\sin^2\widehat{\theta}_W = 0.23129$, $\alpha_s(M_Z) = 0.1180$) with the minimal Standard Model one-loop slopes $b = (\frac{41}{10}, -\frac{19}{6}, -7)$ makes the three lines miss cleanly. Writing $A_i(\mu) = \alpha_i^{-1}(\mu)$ (with $\alpha_1^{-1} = \frac{3}{5}\alpha_Y^{-1}$), consider the linear combination that removes a common unified coupling and cancels the one-loop contribution of a complete $\mathrm{SU}(5)$ family at a common threshold.

$$
C(\mu) \;:=\; \alpha_1^{-1}(\mu) - 3\alpha_2^{-1}(\mu) + 2\alpha_3^{-1}(\mu).
$$

Under minimal Standard Model one-loop running, $C(\mu)$ drifts with slope only $\frac{1}{5\pi}$ in $\log(\mu/M_Z)$, remaining trapped in $-11.195 < C(\mu) < -10.6083$ across the entire unification window $10^{13} \le \mu/\mathrm{GeV} \le 10^{17}$. Since $C = 3\bigl[\frac{1}{3}(\alpha_1^{-1} + 2\alpha_3^{-1}) - \alpha_2^{-1}\bigr]$, the spread between the largest and smallest inverse couplings stays above $3.5361$ at one loop.

Solving for the pairwise crossings gives a tighter baseline. If $\mu_{ij}$ is the scale at which $A_i=A_j$, then $\log(\mu_{ij}/M_Z)=2\pi[A_i(M_Z)-A_j(M_Z)]/(b_i-b_j)$, giving $\mu_{12}<\mu_{13}<\mu_{23}$. At $\mu_{13}\approx 2.4095\times 10^{14}\;\mathrm{GeV}$, the normalized hypercharge and color lines meet, and the weak inverse coupling sits about $3.66403$ above them. Below that crossing, $A_2-A_3$ decreases with scale. Above it, $A_2-A_1$ increases with scale. The spread is at least the respective signed difference on each side, so $3.66403$ is also the approximate minimum spread. At the electroweak crossing $\mu_{12}\approx 1.0140\times 10^{13}\;\mathrm{GeV}$, the strong inverse coupling sits about $5.59689$ below the crossing value. With the low-energy electromagnetic and strong inputs held fixed, imposing a common crossing in this thresholdless model would require $\sin^2\widehat{\theta}_W(M_Z)\approx 0.2076$, below the measured input $0.23129$.

Why are those $3.66$ units so hard to erase? The projection shows which heavy multiplets leave the gap unchanged when their components share a threshold.

Write the minimal Standard Model one-loop beta-coefficient vector as $b = (\frac{41}{10}, -\frac{19}{6}, -7)$, evolving via $\frac{\mathrm{d}\alpha_i^{-1}}{\mathrm{d}\log\mu} = -\frac{b_i}{2\pi}$. Dot the projection vector $c_{\mathrm{proj}} = (1, -3, 2)$ into four key directions:

1. **Common unified coupling** $(1, 1, 1)$: $1 - 3 + 2 = 0$; the one-loop coefficients of a complete $\mathrm{SU}(5)$ matter family sum to a multiple of $(1, 1, 1)$. Its contribution **cancels identically** in $C(\mu)$ when its components share a threshold weight, as they do at a common mass.
2. **Neutral heavy vector index** $(0, 2, 3)$: $0 - 6 + 6 = 0$; **charged $(X, Y)$ heavy vector index** $(5, 3, 2)$: $5 - 9 + 4 = 0$. Provided each multiplet shares a common mass, arbitrary towers of heavy gauge vectors contribute **identically zero** to $C(\mu)$.
3. **Standard Model light-field slope** $b = (\frac{41}{10}, -\frac{19}{6}, -7)$:

   $$
   c_{\mathrm{proj}} \cdot b \;=\; \frac{41}{10} - 3\!\left(-\frac{19}{6}\right) + 2(-7) \;=\; \frac{41}{10} + \frac{95}{10} - \frac{140}{10} \;=\; -\frac{2}{5}.
   $$

   Hence $\frac{\mathrm{d}C}{\mathrm{d}\log\mu} = -\frac{c_{\mathrm{proj}} \cdot b}{2\pi} = +\frac{1}{5\pi} \approx +0.06366$. Climbing all the way from $M_Z$ to $10^{17}\;\mathrm{GeV}$ ($\log(\mu/M_Z) \approx 34.63$) lifts $C(\mu)$ by only $\approx 2.20$, nowhere near enough to erase its $-12.81$ starting deficit at $M_Z$.

In the one-loop step-threshold approximation, only the doublet is active between the two masses. The extra beta-coefficient projection is $(1,-3,2)\cdot(3/10,1/2,0)/3=-2/5$. Once the triplet becomes active, the slope contributions cancel and the accumulated positive shift $\log(m_C/m_H)/(5\pi)$ remains. To test whether the discrete heavy tower of the 19-point Behrend shell can generate such an asymmetric hierarchy and close the $\Delta\alpha^{-1} > 3.66$ gap on its own, we first define the charged magnetic Laplacian, its two-step mass operator, and the weak-center parity decomposition.

::: {.definition #def-ou-ch04-magnetic-laplacian title="The 19-Point Behrend Magnetic Laplacian, Two-Step Mass Operator, and Weak-Center Parity Sectors"}
On the 19-point Behrend shell ($19$ vertices, $135$ chord edges, comprising $18$ polar spokes and $12$ clock-bearing edges), assign each oriented edge $e = (u \to v)$ a positive stiffness $w_e > 0$ and a unit phase $z_e \in \mathrm{U}(1)$ determined by the chosen Riemann zero ordinate $\gamma$ and the shell's dual arc-angle differences. For a charged field of integer hypercharge label $q = 6Y \in \mathbb{Z}$, define the following four structures:

1. **Charged magnetic Laplacian $L_q$**: the Hermitian positive-semidefinite operator on the $19$-dimensional vertex fiber $\mathbb{C}^{19}$ given by

   $$
   (L_q \psi)_u \;=\; \sum_{v \sim u} w_{uv}\bigl(\psi_u - z_{uv}^q \psi_v\bigr),
   $$

   whose neutral sector $L_0$ has a unique constant zero mode (the four-dimensional massless gauge field) and $18$ positive eigenvalues with arithmetic mean $c := \operatorname{tr}(L_0)/18$ (for unit edge weights, $\operatorname{tr}(L_0) = 270$ and $c = 15$).
2. **Local two-step mass operator $D_q$**: the quadratic shell operator centered at the neutral positive spectral mean $c$, requiring at most two steps of shell-edge transport,

   $$
   D_q \;=\; \kappa\, L_q(L_q - c I), \qquad \kappa > 0.
   $$

   An eigenvalue $\lambda \ge 0$ of $L_q$ yields fermion physical mass equal to the singular value $m(\lambda) = \kappa h(\lambda)$, with dimensionless two-step readout $h(\lambda) := \lambda|\lambda - c|$, and bosonic squared-mass operator $D_q^\dagger D_q$.
3. **Weak-center parity sectors**: the decomposition of field multiplets under the center $\mathbb{Z}_2 = \{\pm I_2\} \subset \mathrm{SU}(2)_L$—weak doublets transforming by $-1$ form the **center-odd sector** ($Q_L$, $L_L$, and the Higgs doublet $H$), while weak singlets and adjoints transforming by $+1$ form the **center-even sector** ($u_R^c, d_R^c, e_R^c$, and heavy neutral gauge vectors).
4. **The $\sin^2\theta_W = \frac{3}{8}$ baseline gap**: under the $M_Z = 91.1876\;\mathrm{GeV}$ experimental electroweak and strong inputs with thresholdless minimal Standard Model one-loop running, the equal-stiffness boundary $\sin^2\theta_W = \frac{3}{8}$ leaves a minimum inverse-coupling spread $\Delta\alpha^{-1} > 3.66$, attained at the normalized hypercharge and color crossing (about $3.664$).
:::

The physical intuition behind the two-step operator $\kappa L_q(L_q - cI)$ is immediate: a one-step Laplacian $\kappa L_q$ orders every charged mode monotonically away from zero, making it hard to carve out three light generations across five hypercharges simultaneously; folding the spectrum around the neutral mean $c$ allows modes near $\lambda \approx c$ to dip back down to small singular values without hand-assigning masses charge by charge (see [Chapter 7](ch07-chiral-three-generations.md) for the universal cut $9$ chiral spectrum).

Before marching through the all-phase obstructions and conditional crossings, we anchor the chapter's two algebraic engines in Lean 4 kernel-verified theorems: the Lagrange–Gram sum-of-squares identity that turns every Cauchy–Schwarz defect and dual-projection bound into explicit nonnegativity, and the exact rational Ramanujan margin theorem on the norm-5 Lubotzky–Phillips–Sarnak (LPS) shell that certifies discrete spherical-sum spectral gaps without floating-point drift.

::: {.theorem #thm-ou-ch04-lagrange-gram title="Lagrange–Gram Sum-of-Squares Identity" lean="D5/S3/QuantumBounds/LagrangeGramIdentity.lagrange_gram_identity"}
Let $\iota$ be any index type, $s \subseteq \iota$ a finite subset, and $u, v : \iota \to \mathbb{R}$ real-valued functions. Then the Cauchy–Schwarz defect equals the manifestly nonnegative double sum of wedge squares:

$$
\left(\sum_{i \in s} u_i^2\right)\left(\sum_{i \in s} v_i^2\right) - \left(\sum_{i \in s} u_i v_i\right)^2 \;=\; \frac{1}{2}\sum_{i \in s}\sum_{j \in s} (u_i v_j - u_j v_i)^2.
$$

:::

::: {.proof}
Expanding $(u_i v_j - u_j v_i)^2 = u_i^2 v_j^2 + u_j^2 v_i^2 - 2 u_i v_i u_j v_j$ inside the finite double sum over $s \times s$ and swapping summation indices gives $\sum_{i,j} u_j^2 v_i^2 = \sum_{i,j} u_i^2 v_j^2$. The double sum therefore collapses to $2(\sum_i u_i^2)(\sum_j v_j^2) - 2(\sum_i u_i v_i)^2$; dividing by $2$ yields the identity. The full proof is checked by the Lean kernel.
:::

::: {.theorem #thm-ou-ch04-hecke-margins title="Norm-5 LPS Shell Integer Matrix Match and Strict Rational Ramanujan Margins" lean="D5/S3/Quantum/AURICOmega/OmegaChebyshevHecke.lps_ramanujan_margins"}
On the four low nonconstant symmetric-power shells `shell : S5.Shell` of the norm-5 LPS operator (degrees $d = 1, 2, 3, 4$, corresponding to spins $j = \frac{1}{2}, 1, \frac{3}{2}, 2$) and spectral branch label `branch : Bool`, both assertions hold simultaneously:

1. **Exact integer matrix match (`S5.MatrixMatches shell`)**: in the homogeneous monomial basis, the six-generator sum of unnormalized norm-5 Gaussian-integer matrices has vanishing imaginary part entry-by-entry and matches the pure integer matrix `S5.matrixEntry shell` ($6 I_2$, $-2 I_3$, $-18 I_4$ for $d = 1, 2, 3$, and the symmetric $5 \times 5$ integer matrix at $d = 4$ whose outer monomials couple via off-diagonal entries $64$).
2. **Strict rational Ramanujan margin**: writing $\lambda_{\mathrm{sc}} \in \{6, -2, -18, +54, -74\}$ for the scaled integer eigenvalues across the five branches, the squared spherical-sum eigenvalue $\lambda^2 = \lambda_{\mathrm{sc}}^2 / 5^d$ (namely $\frac{36}{5}, \frac{4}{25}, \frac{324}{125}, \frac{2916}{625}, \frac{5476}{625}$) satisfies the strict rational Ramanujan bound in `Omega.IntRatio.Lt`:

   $$
   20 - \lambda^2 \;>\; 0.
   $$

:::

::: {.proof}
For the first conjunct, `S5.matrices_match` verifies all $2^2 + 3^2 + 4^2 + 5^2 = 54$ Gaussian-integer entries across the four shells by case recursion and exact integer arithmetic reduction under conjugate generator pairing. For the second conjunct, rewriting $20 - \lambda_{\mathrm{sc}}^2 / 5^d$ over the common denominator $5^d$ as $(20 \cdot 5^d - \lambda_{\mathrm{sc}}^2)/5^d$ yields the five strictly positive rational margins $\frac{64}{5}, \frac{496}{25}, \frac{2176}{125}, \frac{9584}{625}$, and $\frac{7024}{625}$, each certified positive in `Omega.IntRatio.Lt` from its positive numerator and denominator. The full proof is checked by the Lean kernel.
:::

## 4.4 Complete Representation Floors, Independent Matter-Number Transport, and Two-Loop On-Shell Resonances {#sec-ou-ch04-s04}

Alongside the native 12-generator algebra, an instructive comparison model embeds the 19-point shell into a local $\mathrm{SU}(5)$ gauge background—preserving the 12 massless Standard Model zero modes while adding 12 heavy charged $(X, Y)$ vectors—on zero #11431 with the three-spoke adjustment and smooth Euclidean one-loop subtraction. This benchmark answers two crisp structural questions: what is the exact minimal heavy matter dimension needed for positive three-way crossing, and what happens when heavy vector loops enter at two loops?

### Exact Dimension Floor $75$ Under Shared Stiffness vs. Exact Floor $10$ With Matter-Number Transport

Under shared unit stiffness between gauge and matter connections (see [`ch39-unique-seventy-five-mass.py`](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing-experiments/blob/ba0ac9539cdb641744054bf3a0398494f4cba1c8/experiments/ch39-omega-research/replay/ch39-unique-seventy-five-mass.py)):

- **Total dimension $\le 74$ is universally excluded**: allowing arbitrary finite combinations of complete vector-like $\mathrm{SU}(5)$ Dirac representations, each with its own nonnegative bare mass squared, any content of total dimension $\le 74$ leaves a one-loop spread strictly $> 2.6826$ across $10^{13}\text{–}10^{17}\;\mathrm{GeV}$ (in particular, arbitrary copies of $\mathbf{5}$, $\mathbf{10}$, and $\mathbf{15}$ fail at positive coupling under any shared positive stiffness).
- **Exact floor $\dim = 75$ and the UV zero at $1.6795\times$ crossing**: the minimal total Dirac dimension is **exactly $75$**, uniquely realized by one irreducible $\mathbf{75} = (0, 1, 1, 0)$ (crossing uniquely at bare mass squared $x \in (0.02251147, 0.02251149)$, $\mu \approx 1.3681 \times 10^{16}\;\mathrm{GeV}$, $\alpha_G^{-1} \approx 17.2032$; or at zero bare mass with three-spoke phase $\phi \approx 0.43546464\;\mathrm{rad}$ and $\alpha_G^{-1} \approx 17.1596$). Crucially, continuing the same running above the crossing drives the **weak inverse coupling $\alpha_2^{-1}$ to zero between $1.679471$ and $1.679481$ times the crossing scale ($\approx 1.6795\times$)**, and by $2\times$ crossing all three inverse couplings have turned negative.

Relaxing the shared-connection rule to permit an independent scalar $\mathrm{U}(1)$ matter-number edge transport transforms both the dimension floor and the ultraviolet fate (see [`ch39-minimal-shifted-ten.py`](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing-experiments/blob/ba0ac9539cdb641744054bf3a0398494f4cba1c8/experiments/ch39-omega-research/replay/ch39-minimal-shifted-ten.py)):

- **Exact floor $\dim = 10$**: any content of total dimension $< 10$ (at most a single $\mathbf{5}$ or $\overline{\mathbf{5}}$) remains excluded over all magnetic phases ($C < -0.1972$). At dimension **$10$**, a single zero-bare-mass $\mathbf{10} = \wedge^2\mathbf{5}$ carrying inverse native transport $z_e^{-1}$ plus a tiny residual phase $\phi \in (2.2022, 2.2024) \times 10^{-9}\;\mathrm{rad}$ on the three spokes lifts its central quark-doublet zero mode to $\approx 5.69 \times 10^5\;\mathrm{GeV}$ and achieves exact three-way crossing at

  $$
  Q \;\approx\; 6.3574 \times 10^{15}\;\mathrm{GeV}, \qquad \alpha_G^{-1} \;\approx\; 53.9034.
  $$

- **Full asymptotic freedom above crossing**: fixing the physical masses of this 10-dimensional construction and running above $Q$, the 19-point heavy vector tower dominates the matter contribution uniformly, enforcing $\mathrm{d}\alpha_i^{-1}/\mathrm{d}\log p > 17.68$ for all three couplings. The $1.6795\times$ Landau-like zero disappears completely, and all three interactions remain asymptotically free above unification. The underlying physics is transparent: the irreducible $\mathbf{75}$ carries Dynkin index $25$, whose positive screening across all 19 shell levels overwhelms gauge anti-screening, whereas the $\mathbf{10}$ carries index only $3$, leaving the 19-point vector tower firmly in command. ### Two-Loop Charged-Vector Channels and Eight On-Shell Resonance Cascades

Going to two loops inside the equal-coupling 19-site $\mathrm{SU}(5)$ comparison action uncovers both stability and a sharp dynamical boundary (see [`ch39-shell-all-charged-cut-integral.py`](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing-experiments/blob/ba0ac9539cdb641744054bf3a0398494f4cba1c8/experiments/ch39-omega-research/replay/ch39-shell-all-charged-cut-integral.py) and [`ch39-shell-nonabelian-resonances.py`](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing-experiments/blob/ba0ac9539cdb641744054bf3a0398494f4cba1c8/experiments/ch39-omega-research/replay/ch39-shell-nonabelian-resonances.py)):

1. **60 nonzero charged-vector-pair + heavy-hypercharge channels**: below the matching scale, each complex gauge root has $172$ kinematically open three-particle cut channels pairing charged vectors with heavy hypercharge vectors; three-fold cyclic symmetry leaves exactly **$60$** nonzero channels. Including the full real spectral integral of all $60$ channels (the lightest charged pair plus 18 heavy hypercharge modes contributes $|d| \le 0.00793\,g_D^2$) preserves a partial two-loop positive crossing throughout $g_D^2 \le 2$.
2. **8 on-shell resonance cascades**: as soon as heavy weak or color vector radiation is included, **$8$** nonzero on-shell cascade decays open below the matching scale, starting at $0.77717182\,\mu$.

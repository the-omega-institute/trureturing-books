# Chapter 10 Four-Dimensional Simplicial Regge Action, Spin-2 Propagators, and Finite Hinge Loads {#sec-ch10-regge-gravity-and-spin2}

The first three volumes pushed the 19-point Behrend shell, dual quaternion clocks, and Riemann zero spectra to the edge of particle physics, uncovering sharp arithmetic thresholds for gauge unification, Higgs portals, three chiral generations, and baryon triality. We now turn the telescope from internal gauge space to spacetime geometry itself and confront Question 4: **how can discrete minimal arcs and simplicial angle deficits produce a massless spin-2 graviton, the Newton constant, and a tiny dark energy?**

If continuum general relativity resembles a taut rubber membrane, discrete Regge gravity is an origami polyhedron assembled from rigid flat four-simplices: every interior is flat, and all curvature sits in the dihedral angle deficits concentrated along two-dimensional triangular faces called **hinges**. That disarmingly concrete picture immediately poses four hard tests:

1. Can bare gravitational couplings be inverted from simplicial partition weights, and can matter fields sense geometric structure finer than the hinge-deficit histogram?
2. Does the long-wavelength limit genuinely propagate two positive-residue Lorentzian helicities $\pm 2$ with reflection positivity, rather than drowning in Euclidean conformal-mode instabilities or slicing artifacts?
3. If each hinge weight is built from finite integer counts or finite-dimensional Born measurements, does flat-space stationarity collide with rigid arithmetic barriers?
4. When moving from free propagators to cubic three-graviton vertices, do discrete hinge loads preserve gauge Ward identities and Lorentz covariance?

This chapter audits all four hurdles along that chain.

## 10.1 Euclidean Regge Action, Pachner Moves, and Full-Spectrum Matter Resolution {#sec-ch10-regge-pachner-matter}

We begin with the cleanest discrete laboratory: a closed four-dimensional equilateral simplicial manifold of uniform edge length $a$, where curvature is governed solely by the number $q_h$ of four-simplices meeting at each triangular hinge $h$.

::: {.definition #def-ou-ch10-regge-action title="Four-Dimensional Equilateral Simplicial Regge Action and Bare Gravitational Weight"}
Let $T$ be a closed four-dimensional equilateral simplicial manifold of uniform edge length $a$, with $N_4$ four-simplices and $N_2$ triangular faces (hinges). Each triangle has area $A = \sqrt{3}\,a^2/4$, each four-simplex has four-volume $v = \sqrt{5}\,a^4/96$, and the equilateral four-simplex dihedral angle is $\theta = \arccos(1/4)$. Writing $q_h$ for the valence (number of incident four-simplices) around hinge $h$, define the total four-volume $V(T)$ and total integrated deficit $K(T)$ by

$$
V(T) = v N_4, \qquad K(T) = A \sum_h (2\pi - q_h\theta) = A\bigl(2\pi N_2 - 10\theta N_4\bigr).
$$

Under the Euclidean Einstein–Regge bare-coupling convention (with $\hbar = c = 1$), each triangulation $T$ carries the partition weight formed from a reference weight $w(T)$ and the gravitational exponential factor

$$
W(T) = w(T)\exp\bigl[-s\,V(T) - b\,K(T)\bigr], \qquad b = -\frac{1}{8\pi G}, \qquad s = \frac{\Lambda}{8\pi G}.
$$

Topology-preserving local retriangulations that replace a cluster of four-simplices sharing interior faces by the complementary subdivision (such as $1\to 5$ and $2\to 4$ moves) are called four-dimensional **Pachner moves**; comparing partition probabilities across independent moves identifies the bare couplings $(b, s)$.
:::

[@def-ou-ch10-regge-action] immediately enforces an identifiability law that is easy to overlook: in any conditional ensemble with fixed $(V, K)$, the factor $\exp[-sV - bK]$ cancels identically in normalization. Even complete knowledge of all normalized matter spectra inside that conditional ensemble leaves $(s, b)$ completely unconstrained, and fixing only the volume still leaves $s$ unidentifiable. Extracting bare $G$ and $\Lambda$ therefore requires comparing partition weights across geometries with different $(N_4, N_2)$.

::: {.example #ex-ou-ch10-pachner-and-dual-walks title="Inverting Bare Couplings via Two Pachner Moves and Full-Spectrum Walk Resolution"}
Starting from the boundary of a five-simplex $T_0$ (a topological $4$-sphere $S^4$ with $(N_4, N_2) = (6, 20)$), apply a $1\to 5$ Pachner move to $\{0,1,2,3,4\}$ inserting vertex $6$ to obtain $T_1$ with $(10, 30)$, and then a $2\to 4$ move across the shared tetrahedron $\{0,1,2,3\}$ connecting vertices $5$ and $6$ to obtain $T_2$ with $(12, 34)$. The volume and deficit increments form the $2\times 2$ matrix

$$
\Delta = \begin{pmatrix} 4v & A(20\pi - 40\theta) \\ 2v & A(8\pi - 20\theta) \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \det \Delta = -8\pi vA \neq 0.
$$

Denoting the three probabilities by $p_0, p_1, p_2$ and the relative log-weight ratios by $d_1 = \log[(p_1/w_1)/(p_0/w_0)]$ and $d_2 = \log[(p_2/w_2)/(p_1/w_1)]$, we have $(d_1, d_2)^{\mathsf T} = -\Delta\,(s, b)^{\mathsf T}$. Subtracting twice the second row from the first eliminates both the volume increment $4v - 2(2v)$ and the dihedral angle term $-40\theta - 2(-20\theta)$, immediately yielding ([4D deficit and shell spectral weights](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing-experiments/blob/ba0ac9539cdb641744054bf3a0398494f4cba1c8/experiments/ch39-omega-research/replay/ch39-%E5%9B%9B%E7%BB%B4%E8%A7%92%E4%BA%8F%E4%B8%8E%E7%90%83%E5%A3%B3%E8%B0%B1%E6%9D%83%E9%87%8D.md))

$$
d_1 - 2d_2 = -4\pi A b \quad \Longrightarrow \quad G = \frac{A}{2(d_1 - 2d_2)},
$$

after which substituting back into $d_2$ gives $s$ and $\Lambda = -s/b$.
:::

Once matter fields from the Behrend shell are coupled to the triangulation, however, spectral weights resolve geometric distinctions far finer than $(N_4, N_2)$ or even the entire single-hinge deficit histogram. Place one real Gaussian scalar on each four-simplex, coupled across shared tetrahedra on the $5$-regular dual graph with Laplacian $L_T = 5I - \mathrm{Adj}_T$. A dimensionless squared mass $x > 0$ contributes $\det(xI + L_T)^{-1/2}$. Beneath all simplicial gauge and curvature variations lies the pure algebraic nilpotency of simplicial coboundaries—the discrete prototype of curl-of-gradient vanishing and the Bianchi identity—verified with an empty axiom closure in `OmegaInit`:

::: {.theorem #thm-ou-ch10-coboundary-zero title="Zero-Axiom Nilpotency of Simplicial Coboundary Operators" lean="D5/S3/Quantum/AURICOmega/OmegaSimplicialExactness.d1_d0 D5/S3/Quantum/AURICOmega/OmegaSimplicialExactness.d2_d1"}
Let $V$ be any vertex type and let $(A, G)$ be an additive commutative group satisfying the group laws `G.Laws`. Write $C^0(V, A) = V \to A$, $C^1(V, A) = V \to V \to A$, and $C^2(V, A) = V \to V \to V \to A$, with simplicial coboundary operators

$$
\begin{aligned}
(d^0 f)(x, y) &:= f(y) - f(x), \\
(d^1 e)(x, y, z) &:= \bigl(e(y, z) + e(x, y)\bigr) - e(x, z), \\
(d^2 F)(x, y, z, w) &:= \bigl(F(y, z, w) + F(x, y, w)\bigr) - \bigl(F(x, z, w) + F(x, y, z)\bigr).
\end{aligned}
$$

Then for all $f \in C^0(V, A)$, $e \in C^1(V, A)$, and all vertices $x, y, z, w \in V$, two zero-axiom identities hold:

$$
d^1(d^0 f)(x, y, z) = 0, \qquad d^2(d^1 e)(x, y, z, w) = 0.
$$

:::

::: proof
For the first identity, expanding $(d^1(d^0 f))(x, y, z) = \bigl((f(z) - f(y)) + (f(y) - f(x))\bigr) - (f(z) - f(x))$ cancels the intermediate term $f(y)$ pairwise in the abelian group. For the second, expanding the four face terms in $(d^2(d^1 e))(x, y, z, w)$ and regrouping by associativity and commutativity reduces both the positive and negative halves identically to $\bigl(e(z, w) + e(y, z) + e(x, y)\bigr) - e(x, w)$, whose difference is zero. Both declarations carry an empty axiom closure (`axioms: []`) in the Lean 4 kernel. The full proof is checked by the Lean kernel.
:::

[@thm-ou-ch10-coboundary-zero] anchors simplicial exactness; to determine whether discrete geometry yields a physical graviton, however, we must confront propagators, reflection positivity, and extrinsic shear.

## 10.2 Long-Wave Spin-2 Propagator, Reflection Positivity, and Shear Constraints {#sec-ch10-spin2-reflection-shear}

Leave fixed equilateral topologies behind and move to the periodic four-dimensional Freudenthal–Kuhn coordinate-chain triangulation with continuously variable squared edge lengths. Around the flat background metric $g = I - J/5$ (15 undirected edge types, 50 triangular hinges, and 24 four-simplices per unit cell, all with $\delta_h = 0$), adopt the bare Euclidean Regge action $S_R = \sum_f A_f\delta_f$ with standard linear source coupling. Within each unit cell, the 15 edge-length modes split into 10 slow metric directions $h$ and 5 non-metric directions that are strictly gapped at zero momentum.

### 10.2.1 Conserved-Source Poles, Two Helicities, and Ten Reflection-Positive Slices {#sec-ch10-propagator-slices}

Expanding the midpoint Fourier symbol and performing Schur elimination shows that integrating out the 5 heavy non-metric modes modifies the metric kinetic block only starting at $O(|k|^4)$. Adding the gauge-fixing term $2\|F(h)\|_g^2/20$ with $F(h) = h k^\sharp - \tfrac{1}{2}k\operatorname{tr}_g(h)$ reduces the leading quadratic form to the Fierz–Pauli tensor $k^2\bigl(h:h - \tfrac{1}{2}(\operatorname{tr}_g h)^2\bigr)/20$. Inverting yields the long-wave conserved-source propagator ([conserved-source propagator certificate](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing-experiments/blob/ba0ac9539cdb641744054bf3a0398494f4cba1c8/experiments/ch39-omega-research/replay/ch39-regge-conserved-source.py)):

$$
\frac{20}{k^2}\!\left(T:S - \frac{1}{2}\operatorname{tr}_g T\,\operatorname{tr}_g S\right) + O(1), \qquad k^2 = k^{\mathsf T} g^{-1} k.
$$

Performing standard Wick continuation to the Lorentzian metric $\eta = \operatorname{diag}(-1, 1, 1, 1)$ and evaluating on a null four-momentum $k \propto (1, 0, 0, 1)$ with source conservation $T^{0\nu} = T^{3\nu}$, the pole-residue numerator simplifies to

$$
\frac{1}{2}\lvert T^{11} - T^{22}\rvert^2 + 2\lvert T^{12}\rvert^2 \ge 0.
$$

It is positive semidefinite of exact rank $2$; under a transverse rotation by $\theta$, the pair $(T^{11} - T^{22}, 2T^{12})$ rotates by $2\theta$, identifying two positive-residue **massless spin-2 Lorentzian helicities $\pm 2$**. For a static unit mass-density source, the numerator equals $1/2$, producing the static kernel $10/|p|^2$ (or $10/(4\pi r)$ in position space). Given the Euclidean Regge action, the linearized long-wave spectrum indeed carries the two helicities of a graviton.

Constructing a positive Hilbert space on the Euclidean lattice, however, also demands **reflection positivity**. On the $g = I - J/5$ simplicial lattice, requiring every four-simplex to span only two adjacent time layers admits, up to sign, exactly **$10$** undirected primitive time slices $h(x) = g(v_i - v_j, x)$ ($1 \le i < j \le 5$, where $v_1,\dots,v_4$ are coordinate steps and $v_5 = -\sum_{m=1}^4 v_m$). For any reflection-symmetric nonnegative local bosonic weight, the two-layer normal transfer operator factors exactly as $T = AA^\dagger \ge 0$, covering compactly supported and non-integer zero-clock face weights ([ten reflection slices certificate](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing-experiments/blob/ba0ac9539cdb641744054bf3a0398494f4cba1c8/experiments/ch39-omega-research/replay/ch39-simplex-reflection-slices.py)). By contrast, for the cross-polarization mode along projected momenta $(t+s, t+s, t-s, t-s)$ with exact kinetic symbol $K = (12 - 2T^2 - TV)/20$ ($T = 2\cos t$, $V = 2\cos s$), the two transfer roots $a = (-V + \sqrt{V^2+96})/4 > 2$ and $b = (-V - \sqrt{V^2+96})/4 < -2$ produce $C_n = c x^{|n|} + d y^{|n|}$ ($x > 0$, $y < 0$): two-step transfer across even slices is positive and recovers massless propagation $\ell C_n \to (\sqrt{5}/|p|)\exp(-|p|\tau/\sqrt{5})$, yet on an 8-slice periodic lattice at $s = \pi/2$ the single-step half-slice reflection quadratic form takes the explicit rational negative value **$-5/3$** ([cross-polarization transfer certificate](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing-experiments/blob/ba0ac9539cdb641744054bf3a0398494f4cba1c8/experiments/ch39-omega-research/replay/ch39-regge-cross-transfer.py)). Moreover, unamplified single-copy zero-clock gauge weights on these slices suffer an enormous transfer gap $a\Delta E > 20.39$, lacking low-energy propagating states.

This negative value is not a counterexample to reflection positivity in the cross sector. The sector's field lives only on the checkerboard sublattice where the diagonal time $n = \sum_i x_i$ and the transverse coordinate $m = x_1 + x_2 - x_3 - x_4$ have equal parity, so transverse momenta $s$ and $s + \pi$ label the same slice momentum, and every quadratic form coupling only that sublattice obeys $C_n(s + \pi) = (-1)^n C_n(s)$. The negative-ratio branch is therefore the image of the positive one under the slice-dependent sign $(-1)^n$, with $y(s) = -x(s + \pi)$; the single-step test at fixed $m$ flips sign with that convention, while the two-step transfer roots $x^2$ and $y^2$ do not. Enumerating the order-$240$ point group of the triangulation and metric shows that the involutions preserving the sector and reversing $n$ fall into two types only, the site type $(n, m) \mapsto (c - n, m)$ and the PT type $(n, m) \mapsto (c - n, d - m)$. The fixed-$m$ half-slice reflection $(n, m) \mapsto (1 - n, m)$ behind the value $-5/3$ maps the sublattice off itself and is not a lattice map. Under site-type reflections the bare Regge cross sector is reflection positive for every $0 < s < \pi$; half-slice PT-type reflections fail on a transversally antiperiodic box for every theory with time propagation, so they carry no information specific to the Regge action. The bare Regge sector also has a second mode at each slice momentum, with long-wavelength gap $\operatorname{arcosh}(3/2)$. It can be removed by the sector quadratic form $K' = (6 + V^2)/10 - VT/4$, which couples only the same sublattice, is local, and keeps the long-wavelength leading term $t^2/2 + s^2/10$; whether $K'$ derives from a simplicial action is undecided. These statements concern this one-dimensional cross sector only, and reflection positivity of the full theory, with its unbounded conformal mode, remains open. This site positivity belongs to the cross sector alone, and only to the diagonal time $n = \sum_i x_i$. That slicing is not one of the $10$ primitive slicings above: every 4-simplex spans five layers along $n$, so the decomposition $T = AA^\dagger$ on primitive slicings does not apply here. On the same family of projected momenta, the pointwise symmetries $(12)$ and $(34)$ also split off two helicity-$\pm 1$ vector sectors that decouple from the cross sector. Their metric component $u \otimes k_\perp$ leaves only contact terms in the continuum limit; the physical helicities $\pm 2$ sit in the cross polarization and in the plus polarization $u_1 u_1 - u_2 u_2$. The two site-type reflections exchange these vector sectors and satisfy $\Theta(12)\Theta = (34)$, so for any theory whose covariance is invariant under the two pointwise permutations the site-reflection form on the vector sectors is purely off-diagonal: it is reflection positive if and only if all gauge-invariant correlations across the reflection plane vanish. The vector sectors of bare Regge are normalizable, with gauge-invariant determinant $c = (2T^2 + 7 - 2\sin t\sin s)/25 \ge 1/5$ and no massless pole, but at every transverse momentum they carry two gapped lattice modes at the cutoff scale, with gap $\ln 2 \le E \le \operatorname{arcosh}(3/2)$ and a negative two-step transfer eigenvalue. ### 10.2.2 Conformal Instability, Fixed-Volume Failure, and the Extrinsic Shear Test {#sec-ch10-conformal-shear}

Moving from linearized poles to non-perturbative path integrals triggers two severe Euclidean obstructions. The first is the **conformal-mode instability**: using squared-edge Lebesgue measure $\prod_e dq_e$ on the $4^4$ periodic lattice, fixing any total four-volume $V_0 > 0$ and any nonzero real Regge coupling $\beta$ leaves the truncated integral $Z_R = \int_{\max q_e \le R}\delta(V - V_0)e^{\beta S}\prod dq_e$ unbounded above—even inside arbitrarily narrow uniform local-volume bands and with the six zero-clock gauge weights included ([fixed-volume divergence certificate](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing-experiments/blob/ba0ac9539cdb641744054bf3a0398494f4cba1c8/experiments/ch39-omega-research/replay/ch39-regge-fixed-volume.py)). Exact integer characteristic polynomials across all $255$ nonzero Bloch momenta on $4^4$ pinpoint the mechanism: every nonzero momentum has exact Hessian inertia **(1 positive, 10 negative, 4 zero)** (and 5 negative, 10 zero at $k = 0$), with conformal variations $u_{xy} = q_{xy}(\phi_x + \phi_y)$ supplying the lone opposite-sign direction—reproducing the continuum Euclidean conformal-mode instability discretely ([conformal-mode inertia certificate](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing-experiments/blob/ba0ac9539cdb641744054bf3a0398494f4cba1c8/experiments/ch39-omega-research/replay/ch39-regge-conformal-mode.py)).

The second obstruction is the **clash between fixed volume and reflection positivity**: imposing a dimensionless local shape bound $\chi_\sigma = (24 V_\sigma)^2/Q_\sigma^4 \ge 1/16$ (with $Q_\sigma$ the largest squared edge in $\sigma$) together with $\Lambda > 0$ produces a normalizable, reflection-positive finite-lattice measure uniquely tunable to any desired mean four-volume $V_0$. Locking the total volume strictly to $V = V_0$, however, forces $F(\Theta F) = -(V_+ - V_0/2)^2$ on every configuration and breaks reflection positivity: in a four-configuration counting example keeping two reflected variable edges (half-volumes $v_0 < v_1$), the reflection expectation is **$-1$**, and the soft rational weight $w(V) = [1 + (V - v_0 - v_1)^2/(v_1 - v_0)^2]^{-1}$ still yields **$-1/3$** ([volume reflection certificate](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing-experiments/blob/ba0ac9539cdb641744054bf3a0398494f4cba1c8/experiments/ch39-omega-research/replay/ch39-regge-volume-reflection.py)). By Widder's bilateral Laplace representation theorem, a continuous total-volume weight $w(V)$ preserves reflection positivity if and only if it is a nonnegative mixture $\int_{(0,\infty)} e^{-\lambda v}\,\nu(d\lambda)$ of positive cosmological constants, with minimum variance attained by a single $\Lambda$ (relative variance $\ge 1/1920$ at $\beta = 0$; [volume fluctuation floor](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing-experiments/blob/ba0ac9539cdb641744054bf3a0398494f4cba1c8/experiments/ch39-omega-research/replay/ch39-volume-fluctuations.py)).

With the bare gravitational weight held fixed, how does the mean four-volume respond to a constant shift $b$ in the matter energy density? On the $4^4$ lattice above, take $\beta=0$ and retain only the dimensionless shape bound, squared-edge Lebesgue measure, and a mixture of positive cosmological coefficients. Scaling all $3840$ squared edge lengths by $a>0$ multiplies the four-volume by $a^2$ and the measure by $a^{3840}$. The shape-restricted region of volume below $v$ thus has measure proportional to $v^k$, with $k=1920$. At fixed $\lambda>0$ the normalized volume density is proportional to $v^{k-1}e^{-\lambda v}$, with mean $k/\lambda$.

**Hand derivation (`ARGUED`).** Assume that the unshifted partition function satisfies $0<Z(0)<\infty$. Let $X=1/\lambda$ have the component law weighted by each component's contribution to that partition function. Write $S(b)=\mathbb E_0[(1+bX)^{-k}]$ for the ratio of shifted to unshifted partition functions. The shifted mean is $m_b=-S'(b)/S(b)$ for $b>0$. Knowing $m_b$ throughout an open interval determines $S$. Two equal mean curves make their functions $S$ proportional. Analyticity extends the proportionality to all $b>0$, and $S(0)=1$ fixes the constant. Uniqueness of the Laplace transform then determines the law of $V$. Write $V=XG$ with independent unit-rate gamma $G$. The characteristic function $\Gamma(k+it)/\Gamma(k)$ of $\log G$ has no zeros, so the characteristic function of $\log V$ uniquely determines the law of $\log X$.

At finitely many $b_j\ge0$, each observed $m_{b_j}$ yields one linear constraint on the mixture probabilities after multiplying by its denominator. Choose $n+2$ distinct scales with strictly positive initial probabilities and record $n$ shifted means. Including normalization, there are at most $n+1$ linear constraints. A sufficiently small perturbation in either direction in their common kernel gives two different positive mixtures with the same recorded means. An interval of mean readings determines the normalized component law, with a common multiplicative factor in the partition function still free.

If $k$ is known and $X$ takes at most $r$ positive scale values, a measurement at $b=0$ suffices when it includes the mean and its derivatives through order $2r-2$. The equation $S'=-m_bS$ and the normalization $S(0)=1$ recover the derivatives of $S$ through order $2r-1$ successively. Differentiating $S(b)=\mathbb E_0[(1+bX)^{-k}]$ term by term gives
$$
S^{(j)}(0)=(-1)^j(k)_j\,\mathbb E_0[X^j],
\qquad (k)_j=k(k+1)\cdots(k+j-1).
$$
The difference between two candidate laws is a signed measure on at most $2r$ distinct scales. Its moments of orders zero through $2r-1$ all vanish. Taking as many of these equations, starting at order zero, as there are distinct scales gives an invertible Vandermonde matrix, so every weight in the difference is zero.

For $r\ge2$, one fewer derivative can leave the mixture ambiguous. Put $n=2r-1$ and split the scales $1+j$ ($j=0,\ldots,n$) by the parity of $j$. In each group assign weight $\binom{n}{j}/2^{n-1}$ to its scale. Each group has $r$ scales and total weight one. The $n$th finite difference vanishes on polynomials of degree less than $n$ and equals $n!$ on $x^n$. Thus the two scale laws match in moments through order $2r-2$ and differ at order $2r-1$. Their mean curves agree in derivatives through order $2r-3$ and differ at order $2r-2$. For $r=1$, the sole scale is simply $m_0/k$.

With the same bound of $r$ positive scales, one can also take one mean reading at each of any $2r-1$ predetermined distinct nonnegative shifts. Combine the scales of two candidates into $x_1<\cdots<x_M$, where $M\le 2r$. Write $\phi_i(b)=(1+bx_i)^{-k}$ and let $F=S_1+S_2$. The two means agree precisely at the zeros of $(S_1/F)'$. For any increasing list of scale indices, the Wronskian of the kernel functions is the determinant of their successive derivatives starting at order zero. Factoring its rows and columns leaves a Vandermonde determinant on the nodes $x_i/(1+bx_i)$. Consequently $W(F,\phi_1,\ldots,\phi_j)$ is nonzero for $0\le j<M$. Expanding the first column leaves only kernel indices greater than $j$, all terms have the same sign, and $F$ has positive weights.

Dividing all functions by $F$ multiplies an $m$-column Wronskian by $F^{-m}$. The first column now corresponds to the constant function $1$. Expansion along that column shows that every initial segment of $(\phi_1/F)',\ldots,(\phi_{M-1}/F)'$ also has a nonzero Wronskian. Any nonzero linear combination of these functions has at most $M-2$ distinct zeros. To see this, divide by the first function and differentiate, then apply Rolle's theorem inductively. Since $(S_1/F)'$ is in their span, $M-1$ distinct zeros force it to vanish identically. Normalization $S_1(0)=S_2(0)=1$ and linear independence of the kernels then make the two mixtures equal. For $M=1$, both candidates already have the same sole scale.

The number of shifts is minimal. Mixtures with exactly $r$ ordered positive scales and strictly positive weights form an open parameter domain of dimension $2r-1$. With fewer mean readings, the smooth observation map has smaller maximal Jacobian rank. The constant rank theorem near a point of maximal rank gives a positive-dimensional family with identical readings.

**Finite Readings at Fixed Curvature (Hand Derivation, `ARGUED`).** Fixing a common curvature coupling $\beta$ still allows identification from finitely many volume readings after the radial density changes. Let $\rho_\beta$ be the pushforward by total volume $V$ of $e^{\beta S_R}\prod_e dq_e$ with the shape bound, and write $L_\beta(t)=\int e^{-tv}\rho_\beta(dv)$. The shape bound gives $q_e\le\sqrt{96V}$ for every edge. Each triangular hinge area is at most $\max_e q_e/2$, and the absolute deficits are bounded on the fixed finite lattice. Thus $|S_R|\le C\sqrt V$ for a lattice constant $C$. At zero curvature coupling, the volume density is proportional to $v^{1919}$. The factor $e^{\beta S_R}$ bounds the new radial measure above and below by this density times $e^{\pm |\beta|C\sqrt v}$. Hence $L_\beta(t)$ is finite for all $t>0$, and the support of $\rho_\beta$ contains the whole positive half-line. At zero coupling, the density has a strictly positive normalization constant. On the flat coordinate-chain simplex the largest squared edge is $6/5$ and $(24V_\sigma)^2=1/5$, giving a shape ratio of $125/1296$, strictly above $1/16$. An open neighborhood of edge lengths is therefore admissible.

For mixtures of at most $r$ positive cosmological coefficients, mean four-volume at any $2r-1$ predetermined distinct nonnegative shifts identifies the coefficients and their normalized weights. Combine positive weights at repeated rates. If $c_i$ is the combined raw weight, the weight recovered is its unshifted partition share $p_i=c_iL_\beta(\lambda_i)/\sum_j c_jL_\beta(\lambda_j)$. The kernels are now $\phi_i(b)=L_\beta(b+\lambda_i)$. For increasing $\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_m$, express the kernel derivatives as integrals and average over permutations of the volume variables. This gives the Wronskian

$$
W(\phi_1,\ldots,\phi_m)(b)
=\frac{1}{m!}\int
\det[(-v_a)^j]_{j=0,\ldots,m-1;\,a=1,\ldots,m}
\det[e^{-\lambda_i v_a}]_{a,i=1,\ldots,m}
\prod_{a=1}^m e^{-bv_a}\rho_\beta(dv_a).
$$

On strictly increasing volumes, both the Vandermonde and exponential determinants have sign $(-1)^{m(m-1)/2}$. The zero bound for real exponential sums makes the exponential determinant nonzero. Its sign follows by bringing the volume nodes close together and using Taylor expansion. The determinant product is strictly positive on ordered volumes and invariant under their permutations, so the integral is positive.

The positive Wronskians for the pooled rates extend the preceding normalization and Rolle argument to $\rho_\beta$. Thus agreement at $2r-1$ shifts identifies the rate distribution, and the preceding constant-rank argument still proves the count sharp.

**Precision at a rate collision (`ARGUED`).** How far apart can two rate distributions be when their means differ only slightly? Consider four rates $\lambda_j=\lambda_0+jh$, $0\le j\le3$. Give $P$ the unshifted partition shares $(1,3)/4$ at $\lambda_0,\lambda_2$, and $Q$ the shares $(3,1)/4$ at $\lambda_1,\lambda_3$. Moving $P$ into $Q$ requires transporting masses $1/4,1/2,1/4$ across the three successive gaps. With cost equal to mass times distance moved in rate, the minimum total cost is exactly $W_1(P,Q)=h$. At zero curvature, putting the terms of $m_P(0)=k\int\lambda^{-1}dP$ over a common denominator gives
$$
m_P(0)-m_Q(0)=\frac{3kh^3}{2\lambda_0\lambda_1\lambda_2\lambda_3}.
$$
At any fixed shift, the differences of the partition functions and of their first derivatives are third finite differences in the rate, up to a constant factor. The two rate distributions are a distance $h$ apart while the mean readings differ by only $O(h^3)$.

There is a sharp bound on this loss of precision. With the same known common coupling $\beta$, write $\rho,L$ for $\rho_\beta,L_\beta$. The bound requires $\rho\ge0$, at least $2r$ distinct volumes in its support, and $L(t)<\infty$ for every $t>0$. Suppose $P,Q$ each have at most $r$ rates in a prescribed interval $[a,A]\subset(0,\infty)$ with $a<A$, weighted by their shares of the unshifted partition function. At any $n=2r-1$ predetermined distinct nonnegative shifts, let $\delta$ be the largest absolute difference between the two sets of mean readings. Then
$$
W_1(P,Q)\le C\delta^{1/n}.
$$
The constant depends on the interval, shifts, $\rho$ and $r$. Both the rate gaps and the positive weights may be arbitrarily small. If each reading has absolute error at most $\varepsilon$, any two mixtures consistent with the same observations have $\delta\le2\varepsilon$.

For a single rate the error bound has an explicit constant. Take $b=0$, $\lambda\in[1,2]$, and $\beta=1/(3840\pi)$. If $n_h$ four-simplices meet at a hinge $h$, then $|\delta_h|\le n_h\pi$, and every incident simplex gives $A_h\le Q_\sigma/2\le\sqrt{24V_\sigma}$. Counting the ten triangular faces of each simplex gives $|S_R|\le10\pi\sqrt{24}\sum_\sigma\sqrt{V_\sigma}\le3840\pi\sqrt V$, by Cauchy–Schwarz over the $6144$ simplices. Thus the shape variable $d=\beta S_R/\sqrt V$ satisfies $|d|\le1$.

Write $t=\lambda+b$ and $\mu=\mathbb E_t V$. A common dilation of the squared edge lengths preserves $d$, so volume scaling gives a radial density at each shape proportional to $v^{k-1}e^{-tv+d\sqrt v}$. Put $D=tv-d\sqrt v/2-k$. Integration by parts in $v$, followed by averaging over shapes, gives $\mathbb E_t[fD]=\mathbb E_t[v\,\partial_v f]$. Taking $f=1,v,D$ in turn gives $\mathbb E_t D=0$, $\mathbb E_t[(V-\mu)D]=\mu$, and $\mathbb E_t D^2=(k+t\mu)/2$. Cauchy–Schwarz then gives a lower bound for the magnitude of the slope.
$$
-\mu'(t)=\operatorname{Var}_t V\ge\frac{2\mu^2}{k+t\mu}.
$$
On the chosen interval the first identity gives $1920\le2\mu+\sqrt\mu/2$. Since $\sqrt{952}<32$, we have $\mu>952$, so $-\mu'(t)>2(952)^2/(1920+2\cdot952)>474$. Integrating shows that two rates whose mean volumes differ by $\delta$ can differ by at most $\delta/474$. At zero coupling the variance inequality is an equality and gives the exact slope of $\mu(t)=k/t$. [Single-rate constant for any known coupling and rate interval](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing-books/issues/1597#issuecomment-7234392256)

For the multiple-rate upper bound, interpolate the normalized component kernel $K(b,\lambda)=L(b+\lambda)/L(\lambda)$ in Newton form on the union of the rates. As rates merge, the divided differences extend by rate derivatives. Set $U=S_P-S_Q$. At a sampled shift, $U'+m_Q U=S_P(m_Q-m_P)$, so differences of means give a linear system for the coefficients of $U$ in this basis. Expanding the Wronskians in the volume variables gives exponential determinants with consecutive rate derivatives at each repeated rate. Counting zeros of exponential sums with multiplicity shows these determinants are nonzero. The preceding division and Rolle argument then makes the system nonsingular. Its inverse is uniformly bounded on the compact set that includes coincident nodes and zero weights. Rescale the rate interval to $[0,1]$. For any gap of width $g$, the function equal to one at the nodes on its left and zero on its right has divided differences of order $j\ge1$ bounded by $2^{j-1}g^{-j}$. The excess mass across the gap is therefore at most $\min(1,C_1\delta/g^n)$. Multiplying by $g$ and summing over at most $n$ gaps gives the bound. [Derivation of the confluent determinant and the transport bound](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing-books/issues/1597#issuecomment-7234188498)

To see why the exponent is optimal, put weights $\binom nj/2^{n-1}$ on the even and odd indices of $\lambda_0+jh$, $0\le j\le n$, in the two mixtures respectively. Each mixture has exactly $r$ rates. The masses transported across the gaps sum to one, so $W_1=h$. On the integer nodes $j$, the alternating binomial weights annihilate every monomial of degree less than $n$. Taylor expansion in the rate at each sampled shift therefore gives $O(h^n)$ differences for the mixtures of $K$ and of $\partial_b K$, while the partition functions stay bounded below by a positive constant. This gives positive mixtures with $\delta=O(h^n)$ at the same fixed curvature. The worst precision at a rate collision when the curvature coupling is also unknown remains open. [Integer-node divided-difference identity](lean:D5/S3/Quantum/Dynamics/ParityNodeDividedDifference.sum_eval_div_nodal_eq_coeff)

Whenever the unshifted measure is normalizable with finite mean, $m_0=k\mathbb E_0 X$ also bounds the shifted mean. A shift $b>0$ multiplies the volume weight by $e^{-bv}$. Each component's partition function acquires a factor $h(X)=(1+bX)^{-k}$, and its mean becomes $k g(X)$, where $g(X)=X/(1+bX)$. Since $h$ decreases and $g$ increases, $\mathbb E_0[hg]\le\mathbb E_0[h]\mathbb E_0[g]$. Strict concavity of $g$ then gives
$$
m_b=k\frac{\mathbb E_0[hg]}{\mathbb E_0[h]}
\le k g(\mathbb E_0 X)=\frac{m_0}{1+b m_0/k}.
$$
Equality holds precisely for a single value of $\lambda$. For instance, at $b=k/m_0$ even the least responsive weight halves the mean four-volume. For a shift $b=-s<0$, a finite mean requires $\lambda>s$ almost surely and hence $s<k/m_0$. More precisely, given $sX<1$ almost surely, the shifted partition function and mean are finite exactly when both $\mathbb E_0[(1-sX)^{-k}]$ and $\mathbb E_0[X(1-sX)^{-k-1}]$ are finite. In that domain $h$ and $g$ both increase and $g$ is strictly convex, giving $m_{-s}\ge m_0/(1-s m_0/k)$.

**Separating one scale from two under measurement error (`ARGUED`).** At zero curvature coupling, the mean and its initial slope distinguish these two classes under the separation condition below. The scale $X=1/\lambda$ still has the weights of the components in the unshifted partition function. The normalized volume is $V=XG$, with $G$ independent of $X$ and gamma-distributed with shape $k$ and unit rate. Exponential tilting gives $m_0=\mathbb E V$. If $\mathbb E X^2<\infty$, the right derivative at zero satisfies $-m'_{0+}=\operatorname{Var}(V)$. For such a mixture with scale mean $\mu$, these readings are $k\mu$ and $k\mu^2+k(k+1)\operatorname{Var}(X)$. The variance term vanishes for a single scale. For exactly two positive scales with each weight at least $\eta\in(0,1/2]$ and separation at least $d>0$, this term is at least $C=k(k+1)\eta(1-\eta)d^2$. A two-scale mean must satisfy $\mu>\eta d$. At every such mean, putting weight $\eta$ on the higher scale and the remaining weight on $\mu-\eta d$, with separation $d$, attains $C$.

Suppose both classes have scale means in $[A,B]$ with $0<A\le B$, and the absolute errors in the two readings are at most $e$ and $f$. The two-scale class is empty if $B\le\eta d$. Otherwise put $q=2e/k$, $h=\min\{B-\max(A,\eta d),q\}$, and $D=kh(2B-h)$. The classes have disjoint sets of possible readings if $C>D+2f$ and overlapping sets if $C<D+2f$. At equality they are still distinguishable precisely when $q>0$ and $B-h=\eta d$, since strict positivity excludes the critical two-scale mean. When the two observation sets are disjoint and the observation is known to come from one of the two classes, classify it as single-scale if its error rectangle intersects the curve $(k\nu,k\nu^2)$ for $\nu\in[A,B]$. Otherwise it is two-scale.

To derive the sharp threshold, let $\nu$ be the single scale and $\mu$ the two-scale mean. Their mean-reading intervals can overlap only if $|\nu-\mu|\le q$. Subject to this constraint, the supremum of $k(\nu^2-\mu^2)$ is $D$. At fixed $\mu$, the largest allowed $\nu$ is $\min(B,\mu+q)$. For $q>0$, the resulting squared difference increases up to $B-q$ and decreases thereafter. The worst case is $\mu=B-h,\nu=B$, approached from above in $\mu$ if the endpoint is excluded. Fixing $\nu=B$ and taking the least-variance two-scale mixture at $\mu=B-t$, the difference in the second reading decreases continuously from $C$ to $C-D$ or approaches that value as $t$ grows. Once its absolute value is at most $2f$, the coordinatewise midpoints give a common observation. If $q=0$, any shared feasible mean attains the gap $C$.

This finite-lattice response uses volume scaling at zero curvature coupling. A nonzero curvature coupling changes the radial density.

Fixing the mean four-volume also gives a comparison of the full distributions. Let $G$ have gamma shape $k$ and rate $1$, independently of the random scale $X=1/\lambda$. The mixture volume is $V=XG$. Write $m=k\mathbb E X$. The volume at the single rate $k/m$ is $V_*=(m/k)G$. Since $\mathbb E[V\mid G]=V_*$, conditional Jensen gives $\mathbb E\phi(V)\ge\mathbb E\phi(V_*)$ for every convex $\phi$ for which the expectations exist. If $\phi$ is strictly convex and its expectations are finite, equality holds exactly when $X$ is constant. For $t>0$, this gives the sharp bound $\mathbb E e^{-tV}\ge(1+tm/k)^{-k}$. Every shift with finite normalization and mean gives the same comparison using the shifted random scale and shifted mean.

**Unknown curvature coupling (hand derivation, `ARGUED`).** Use the same volume-shift experiment with a fixed shape law and a finite mixture of cosmological coefficients, and treat the common real curvature coefficient $\beta$ as unknown as well. Set $T=S_R/\sqrt V$. Multiplying all squared edge lengths by $a$ multiplies $S_R$ by $a$ and $V$ by $a^2$, so $T$ depends only on shape. In the shape-restricted domain without curvature weighting, the edge-length measure below volume $v$ scales as $v^{1920}$ on every measurable set of shapes. The pushforward to $(V,T)$ is therefore a constant times $v^{1919}dv\,P(dT)$, where $P$ is the normalized law of $T$ below volume one. The shape bound gives $q_e\le\sqrt{96V}$. Each hinge area is at most $\max_e q_e/2$, and deficits on the finite lattice are bounded, so $T$ is bounded.

Write $M(t)=\int e^{tT}P(dT)$. Combine all weights at the same cosmological coefficient. Let $\lambda_i$ be the distinct positive coefficients and $a_i$ the normalized exponential weight at each one. Factor $v^{1919}$ out of the normalized volume density. The remaining factor at $v=w^2>0$ is

$$
H(w)=M(\beta w)\sum_i a_i e^{-\lambda_i w^2},\qquad a_i>0.
$$

The exact mean-volume response on any nonempty open interval of positive $b$ determines the normalized volume density. The mean is the negative logarithmic derivative of the volume Laplace transform. Analytic continuation and normalization at zero fix the transform. Laplace uniqueness then determines the density and hence the real-analytic function $H$.

The exponential mixture depends only on $w^2$, so the odd coefficients of $\log H$ come from $\log M(\beta w)$. If $P$ is asymmetric about zero, some odd coefficient of $\log M$ is nonzero. If all of them vanished, all odd moments would vanish, and compact support would force $P$ to be symmetric. The corresponding coefficient of $\log H$ determines an odd power of $\beta$ and therefore its magnitude and sign. If $P$ is symmetric and $T$ is nonzero with positive probability, set $a_*=\operatorname{ess\,sup}|T|>0$. As $w\to+\infty$, $\log H(w)=-\lambda_{\min}w^2+a_*|\beta|w+o(w)$. The smallest $\lambda_i$ controls the finite positive sum, and $\log M(\beta w)/w$ tends to $\operatorname{ess\,sup}(\beta T)$. The quadratic and linear terms give $\lambda_{\min}$ and $|\beta|$, respectively. Both signs then give the same $M$. The tail bound below verifies the asymmetric case for the periodic lattice.

Divide out the determined factor $M(\beta w)$. Linear independence of the exponential functions identifies the distinct $\lambda_i$ and their combined weights $a_i$, up to ordering. Multiply each $a_i$ by the partition function for its rate and normalize to obtain the total partition share at that rate. For two components with the same rate, every split into positive weights $c_1,c_2$ with the same sum gives the same response.

If $T=0$ almost surely, the response still identifies the mixture and is independent of the curvature coefficient. On this periodic $4^4$ lattice both curvature signs occur with positive probability. Write each shared edge as $y=x+\mathbf1_J$, where $J$ is a nonempty subset of coordinate directions and $k=|J|$. For the first shape, let $q_{xy}$ depend only on the step count $k$, taking the successive values $(1,1,3242533/10^7,1)$. For the second, vary the lengths according to the vertex parity $\epsilon(x)=(-1)^{\sum_i x_i}$ by setting

$$
q_{xy}=b_k\left(1+\frac{11}{100}[\epsilon(x)+\epsilon(y)]\right),\qquad
(b_1,b_2,b_3,b_4)=(1,13/10,59/40,6/5).
$$

Both prescriptions give each shared edge a consistent length. The first has congruent chain simplices, and the second has two types distinguished by the parity of the starting vertex. A triangle spanning successive coordinate blocks of sizes $p,q$ belongs to $n=p!q!(5-p-q)!$ simplices. **Local Finite Readings at Unknown Curvature (Hand Derivation, `ARGUED`).** If the common curvature coupling $\beta$ is unknown, how many exact means at positive shifts distinguish curvature from the cosmological coefficients near a given parameter? The second column comes from the Gamma covariance $\operatorname{Cov}(V,\sqrt V)$ multiplied by $\mathbb ET$. When $C\ne0$, the column ratio varies with $\sqrt t$, so any two distinct shifts distinguish the parameters locally.

In general there are $2r$ free parameters. Generic sets of $2r$ readings suffice when $\beta\ne0$ and $T$ is nonzero with positive probability. If every mean has zero first variation, the logarithmic variation of the unnormalized partition function is independent of the shift. Laplace uniqueness gives a constant logarithmic variation of the unnormalized volume weight. At large volume, varying the slowest rate produces a term linear in $v$. Once this term vanishes, the coupling score divided by $\sqrt v$ tends to the selected shape endpoint. Invariance of domain rules out injectivity on an open neighborhood with fewer readings.

**Choosing Shifts at Zero Coupling with Nonzero Shape Mean (Hand Derivation, `ARGUED`).** The freedom to choose the shifts extends from one rate to several. Write the weight and rate variations of the partition function in terms of $(b+\lambda_i)^{-k}$ and its rate derivative. The paired columns give a confluent Vandermonde determinant. The curvature variation contributes a factor $\sqrt v$. ## 10.3 Arithmetic Obstructions of Finite Hinge Loads, Integer Ground States, and Cubic Curvature Tests {#sec-ch10-finite-hinge-loads}

The previous two sections treated the Regge action as a given input. What happens if we return to the native arc-partition ambition—replacing continuous hinge areas $A_h$ by finite nonnegative integer **hinge loads** $N_h$ generated by discrete counting or finite quantum measurements in $(8\pi)^{-1}\sum_h N_h\delta_h$? An extraordinarily sharp sequence of arithmetic and geometric obstructions emerges.

### 10.3.1 The $\sqrt{3}$ Rationality Barrier, Degree-4 Minimal Fields, and Four-Copy Sharpness {#sec-ch10-sqrt3-obstruction}

A shape variation at fixed hinge incidences changes both triangle areas and deficit angles. In the derivative of the curvature sum, the area-weighted deficit-angle derivative terms cancel within each four-simplex. The hinge-load calculation below uses this cancellation.

::: {.theorem #thm-ou-ch10-euclidean-regge-variation title="First Variation of the Four-Dimensional Euclidean Regge Curvature Sum" lean="D5/S3/Geometry/Lorentz/ReggeSchlafli4D.regge_variation"}
Let $\Sigma$ and $H$ be finite index sets for four-simplices and global hinges. Each $s\in\Sigma$ follows a path of nondegenerate Euclidean four-simplices with differentiable vertex positions parametrized by $t\in\mathbb R$. For each $s$, a fixed injection $i_s$ sends its local triangles into $H$. Each global hinge has a differentiable area function $A_h(t)$ that agrees with the local area of $k$ whenever $i_s(k)=h$. With dihedral angles $\theta_{s,k}(t)$, define

$$
\delta_h(t)=2\pi-\sum_{s,k:\,i_s(k)=h}\theta_{s,k}(t),
\qquad
K(t)=\sum_{h\in H} A_h(t)\,\delta_h(t).
$$

Then $K$ is differentiable at every $t\in\mathbb R$, with

$$
K'(t)=\sum_{h\in H} A_h'(t)\,\delta_h(t).
$$
:::

::: proof
The product rule also contributes $\sum_h A_h(t)\delta_h'(t)$. Reindexing this term by local triangles gives, for each four-simplex, the negative of the sum of its areas times the corresponding dihedral-angle derivatives. Area compatibility supplies the local areas, and the Euclidean Schläfli identity makes the sum vanish for each simplex. The full proof is checked by the Lean kernel.
:::

On the flat four-dimensional coordinate-chain cell (30 four-valent hinges, 20 six-valent hinges), shift every short squared edge ($4/5$) uniformly by $\eta$ while holding the long edges fixed. Dihedral differentiation yields $d\delta/d\eta = 3$ on every four-valent hinge and $d\delta/d\eta = -3\sqrt{3}$ on every six-valent hinge, so the first variation along this shape direction (omitting $1/(8\pi)$) is exactly ([hinge-load stationarity certificate](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing-experiments/blob/ba0ac9539cdb641744054bf3a0398494f4cba1c8/experiments/ch39-omega-research/replay/ch39-regge-hinge-load.py))

$$
3\left(\sum_{h\ \text{4-val}} N_h - \sqrt{3}\sum_{h\ \text{6-val}} N_h\right).
$$

This single equation triggers two immediate algebraic barriers:

- **No positive rational solution and second-variation sign flip**: no nonzero nonnegative rational counts can satisfy the $\sqrt{3}$ ratio, even after combining with uniform scaling to hold the total four-volume fixed. Moreover, if differentiable loads satisfy $N_h = C A_h$ and $dN_h = C r\,dA_h$ at the flat point, differentiating the cancellation $\sum_h A_h\,d\delta_h=0$ in the proof of [@thm-ou-ch10-euclidean-regge-variation] yields the second variation $C(2r - 1)R''_{q_0}$: frozen flat-area loads ($r = 0$) invert the sign of the gravitational kinetic term to $-R''$, while $r = 1/2$ annihilates the entire quadratic kinetic term!
- **Minimal Born-amplitude number-field degree is $4$**: if nonnegative integer loads arise from a finite-dimensional Born measurement whose unnormalized amplitudes have real and imaginary parts in any **totally real number field** $F$ (every embedding maps $F$ into $\mathbb{R}$), then $A = \sqrt{3}B$ is impossible—some embedding sends $\sqrt{3} \mapsto -\sqrt{3}$, contradicting the positivity of sums of squares under every real embedding. Conversely, the degree-4 real algebraic amplitude $(1, \beta)$ with $\beta > 0$ and $4\beta^4 = 3$ ($2\beta$ a root of irreducible $x^4 - 12$) achieves exact flat balance with $p_4 = 4 - 2\sqrt{3}$ and $p_6 = 2\sqrt{3} - 3$ ($p_4/p_6 = 2/\sqrt{3}$). Since any real field of degree $< 4$ containing $\sqrt{3}$ is totally real quadratic, **degree $4$ is strictly minimal** ([finite Born hinge certificate](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing-experiments/blob/ba0ac9539cdb641744054bf3a0398494f4cba1c8/experiments/ch39-omega-research/replay/ch39-finite-born-hinge.py)).

Notice how sharply this degree-4 wall separates flat four-dimensional hinge geometry from quadratic spinor arithmetic. In the constructive quadratic extension `QSqrt2` ($\delta^2 = 2$), no transcendental functions or fourth roots are needed to construct an exact unit right-angle spinor closing in eight beats ($u_{90}^8 = 1$); yet balancing four-dimensional simplicial hinge loads via $4\beta^4 = 3$ forces an irreversible jump from degree $2$ to degree $4$.

# Style preservation

This skill changes factual scope without replacing the book’s voice.

- Preserve author epigraphs and quoted manuscript wording exactly, except for the repository’s permitted punctuation, whitespace, and mathematical typography normalization.
- Keep the chapter’s explanatory rhythm: concrete geometric or physical picture, transparent hand calculation, then the general formal statement. Do not remove the author’s metaphors merely because they are rhetorical; bound them when they could be mistaken for a theorem.
- Keep theorem, definition, proof, remark, example, conjecture, and problem boundaries intact. Formal boxes and proofs use restrained mathematical language; explanatory prose may remain vivid. Do not add a `lean` attribute to a literature result or an open problem.
- Treat the Chinese version as authoritative. Preserve its intended terminology and sentence cadence where accurate. Write English as idiomatic academic prose with the same assumptions, strength, order, and examples; never use a literal translation that changes the claim.
- Preserve stable `#id` values, `lean` GIDs, chapter order, table columns, media sequence, and the standard proof closing sentences.
- When a sentence needs a scope condition, place it where a reader sees what the formula or physical quantity actually means. Typical conditions include pure versus mixed states, equal weights, balanced recombination, a scanned reference phase, bounded operators, finite baths, or a named protocol.
- Do not upgrade a metaphor into a physical law, an existence statement into an algorithm, a counterexample into a minimal counterexample, or a state-dependent bound into a uniform one.

If a proposed correction would change the author’s argument, structure, or rhetorical voice rather than fix a confirmed accuracy issue, record it as advisory and leave it for author review.
